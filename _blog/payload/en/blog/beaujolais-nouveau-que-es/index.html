Beaujolais Nouveau: what it is and why it is celebrated in November

Every third Thursday of November the first wine of the harvest goes on sale. How it is made, what it tastes like and how to serve it.

On the third Thursday of November, which falls on the 19th in 2026, Beaujolais Nouveau goes on sale around the world: the first wine of that same year's harvest. For decades its arrival has been celebrated with parties in bars and restaurants, especially in France, Japan and elsewhere.

A wine just a few weeks old

It is made in the Beaujolais region, south of Burgundy, from the Gamay grape. The grapes are picked at the end of summer and the wine is bottled just a few weeks later. To obtain such a young, fruity red, carbonic maceration is often used: whole bunches ferment in tanks filled with carbon dioxide, which gives intense aromas of red fruit, banana or strawberry sweets and very little tannin.

What it tastes like

It is a light, purple-hued red, fresh and very fruity, easy to drink. It is not meant for ageing: it is best drunk in the months after its release.

How to serve it

  • Slightly chilled, at 12 to 14 °C: half an hour in the fridge does it good.
  • With charcuterie, pâtés, mild cheeses, roast chicken or informal nibbles.
  • In simple glasses, with no need to decant.

Beyond Nouveau

Beaujolais also makes more serious wines, the crus, named after villages such as Morgon, Fleurie or Moulin-à-Vent. Made from the same Gamay grape, they offer more depth and can age for several years. If you enjoy Nouveau, they are the natural next step.
