Cava, Champagne, Prosecco and Crémant: what is the difference?

The four best-known sparkling wines look alike in the glass, but differ in how they are made, the grapes and the style. A quick guide to choosing one for the holidays.

More sparkling wine is sold in December than in the rest of the year put together. Most bottles look similar, but behind each name there is a region, a set of grapes and, above all, a different way of making the bubbles.

Two ways to make bubbles

In the traditional method, the base wine undergoes a second fermentation inside the bottle and then rests on its lees for months or years. This gives fine bubbles and aromas of toast and pastry. Champagne, Cava and Crémant are made this way.

In the Charmat or tank method, the second fermentation takes place in large sealed tanks. It is quicker and better preserves the fruity and floral aromas of the grape. It is the usual method for Prosecco.

Champagne

Only sparkling wine from the Champagne region of France can use the name. Its main grapes are Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier. It has high acidity and a minimum lees ageing longer than most sparkling wines, which gives it complexity.

Cava

Spain's great traditional-method sparkling wine, mostly produced in Penedès, Catalonia. The classic grapes are Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada, although Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and others are also used. There are young, fresh Cavas and others with long ageing.

Prosecco

Made in north-east Italy, mainly in Veneto and Friuli, from the Glera grape. It is usually made by the Charmat method, so it is lighter and fruitier (apple, pear, white flowers), often with a touch of sweetness. It is ideal as an aperitif and for cocktails.

Crémant

These are traditional-method sparkling wines from other French regions, such as Alsace, Burgundy or the Loire, and also from Luxembourg. They often offer a Champagne-like style at a gentler price.

Which one to choose?

  • For an aperitif or an informal toast: Prosecco or a young Cava.
  • To go with a whole meal: an aged Cava, a Crémant or a brut Champagne.
  • With shellfish: a very dry brut nature or extra brut.
  • With dessert: an off-dry or sweet sparkling wine, never a brut.

Serve them well chilled, at 6 to 8 °C, and open the bottle at an angle, holding the cork so it does not fly out.
