Which wine to serve with Epiphany cakes: roscón, galette des rois and more

Roscón in Spain, galette des rois in France, Befana sweets in Italy: the desserts of 6 January and the wines that suit them best.

On 6 January, the feast of the Epiphany, many countries end the holidays with a special cake. In Spain it is the roscón de Reyes; in France, the galette des rois; in Portugal, bolo rei; and in Italy the Befana brings children sweets and sugar coal. In the English-speaking world, Twelfth Night once had its own Twelfth cake.

Roscón de Reyes

A brioche-like ring flavoured with orange-blossom water and citrus zest, topped with candied fruit and often filled with cream or chocolate truffle. It suits aromatic sweet wines that are not too heavy:

  • Moscatel (from Valencia, Málaga or Alicante): its floral and orange aromas match the orange blossom.
  • An off-dry or sweet sparkling wine: refreshing and good with cream.
  • If filled with truffle or chocolate, a Pedro Ximénez or sweet oloroso.

Galette des rois

Puff pastry filled with almond cream (frangipane). Its butter and almond call for a demi-sec Champagne, a Vouvray moelleux or a light Muscat. A brut Crémant also works if you prefer less sweetness.

Bolo rei and fruit-and-nut cakes

With plenty of candied fruit and nuts, they go well with tawny Port, sweet Madeira or Vin Santo.

And for children

In Spain, roscón is traditionally eaten with hot chocolate. For those who do not drink alcohol, sparkling grape juice is a good alternative.

As always with desserts, the rule is that the wine should be at least as sweet as the cake. Serve it cool and in small glasses.
