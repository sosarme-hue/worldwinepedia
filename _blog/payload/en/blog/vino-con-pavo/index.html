Which wine goes with roast turkey

Turkey is a mild meat, but stuffings and sauces change the pairing a great deal. Light reds, full-bodied whites and a few surprises.

Roast turkey is the star of Thanksgiving in the United States at the end of November and of Christmas in many countries. Its meat is lean and mild, so the wine should not overpower it. What matters most are the trimmings: stuffing with nuts, cranberry sauce, chestnuts or gravy.

Light, fruity reds

They are the safest bet. A Pinot Noir, a Gamay from Beaujolais, a young Grenache or a Mencía have soft tannins and plenty of red fruit, which suits cranberry sauce and the bird's crisp skin. Serve them slightly cool, at 14–16 °C.

Full-bodied whites

If you prefer white, choose one with some weight: an oaked Chardonnay, an aged white Rioja or a Godello. They work very well if the turkey comes with creamy sauces or mushroom stuffing.

Depending on the stuffing

  • With nuts and prunes: Grenache or a red with ripe fruit.
  • With chestnuts and mushrooms: Pinot Noir or an aged white.
  • With cranberry sauce: fruity, low-tannin reds.
  • With spices: a dry or off-dry Riesling balances the heat.

What to avoid

Very powerful, tannic, heavily oaked reds tend to overwhelm turkey and taste drying with white meat. If the table needs something more structured for other dishes, keep that wine for them.

A wild card

An aged sparkling wine or a full-bodied rosé goes with almost the whole menu, from the nibbles to the turkey, and is a good choice if guests help themselves.
