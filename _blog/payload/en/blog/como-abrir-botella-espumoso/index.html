How to open a bottle of Champagne or Cava safely

A sparkling wine cork comes out with great force and can injure eyes. A step-by-step guide to opening it without accidents or losing half the wine.

A bottle of sparkling wine holds a pressure of around 5 or 6 atmospheres, several times that of a car tyre. That is why the cork can shoot out at great speed, and every year there are eye injuries from Champagne and Cava corks during the holidays. Opening it properly is easy.

Before opening

  • Chill it well, to 6–8 °C. A cold bottle has less pressure and foams less.
  • Do not shake it or open it right after bringing it home in the car: let it rest.
  • Dry the bottle so it does not slip.

Step by step

  1. Remove the foil.
  2. Put your thumb on the cork and keep it there from now on.
  3. Loosen the cage (the wire muzzle) with about six turns, without removing it: let it keep covering the cork.
  4. Tilt the bottle at about 45 degrees, pointing away from people, lamps and windows.
  5. Hold the cork and cage firmly with one hand and, with the other, turn the bottle slowly, not the cork.
  6. When you feel the cork starting to come out, hold it back so it eases out with a soft sigh rather than a bang.

Pouring without spilling

Keep the bottle tilted for a few seconds after opening so the foam does not overflow. Pour a little into each glass first, wait for the foam to settle and then fill to two thirds.

What about sabrage?

Opening a bottle with a sabre is a spectacle that needs practice and carries a risk of cuts and broken glass. At home it is best left to professionals.

If a cork hits an eye and there is pain, blurred vision or bleeding, go to A&E without delay.
