D.O. Toro · Zamora

Toro

Old vines on sandy soils by the Duero, the collegiate church with its painted portal and Zamora's Romanesque close by.

The town of Toro looks down on the Duero from a height. Its wine, made from Tinta de Toro (a local adaptation of Tempranillo), has a historic reputation for power and deep colour. Phylloxera barely took hold in the appellation's sandy soils, so very old ungrafted vines on their own roots still survive.

The collegiate church and the town

The Romanesque collegiate church of Santa María la Mayor holds the Portico of Majesty, a Gothic portal that keeps much of its original paint. From the Espolón viewpoint you look over the Duero plain. Beneath the old town are cellars dug into the rock, testimony to wine's role in local life.

Around Toro

The villages of the appellation (Morales de Toro, Valdefinjas, Venialbo, San Román de Hornija…) have both family and modern wineries. Thirty kilometres away, Zamora is one of the European cities with the most Romanesque churches.

Towns and villages

Toro, Morales de Toro, Valdefinjas, Venialbo, San Román de Hornija, Zamora.

What to see

  • Collegiate church and Portico of Majesty.
  • Espolón viewpoint.
  • Cellars beneath the old town.
  • Zamora's Romanesque.

When to go

Spring and autumn; the harvest runs from September to October.

Food

Milk-fed lamb, Fuentesaúco chickpeas, Zamorano cheese and bacalao a la tranca.

Tip

Visit Toro and Zamora together: they combine wine, Romanesque and walks along the Duero.
