Argentina · Malbec at the foot of the Andes

Mendoza

High-altitude vineyards watered by snowmelt, the snowy cordillera behind and the National Grape Harvest Festival.

Mendoza is Argentina's largest wine region. In an almost desert climate, vines depend on water from the Andes, which arrives through a network of irrigation channels partly inherited from the Indigenous peoples. Altitude, often between 800 and over 1,000 metres, brings cool nights and deeply coloured grapes. Malbec, brought from France in the nineteenth century, is its emblem.

Luján de Cuyo and Maipú

Near the city of Mendoza, Luján de Cuyo and Maipú bring together historic and modern wineries. Maipú is known for its bike tours among wineries, olive groves and oil mills.

Uco Valley

To the south, the Uco Valley (Tupungato, Tunuyán and San Carlos) has some of the highest vineyards and most spectacular winery architecture, with the cordillera as a backdrop. From Mendoza you can also head up towards Aconcagua, the highest mountain in the Americas.

The Vendimia

At the end of the southern summer, between February and March, the city holds the National Grape Harvest Festival, with parades and a large show in an amphitheatre at the foot of the hills.

What to see

  • Wineries of Luján de Cuyo and Maipú.
  • Uco Valley.
  • General San Martín Park (Mendoza).
  • The road to Aconcagua.

When to go

February to April (harvest) and the southern spring (October–November).

Food

Asado, Mendoza empanadas, kid goat and local olive oil.

Tip

Many wineries offer long paired lunches: book ahead and don't plan more visits that afternoon.
