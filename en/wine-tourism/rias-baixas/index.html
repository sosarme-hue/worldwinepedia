D.O. Rías Baixas · Atlantic Galicia

Rías Baixas

Albariño on pergolas by the sea, manor houses, Cambados and the shellfish of the rías: a wine route that smells of the Atlantic.

Rías Baixas covers south-western Galicia, in the province of Pontevedra and part of A Coruña. The mild, humid Atlantic climate and granite soils shape the wines of Albariño, an aromatic, fresh white grape. Traditionally the vines are trained on high pergolas on stone posts, which keep the bunches away from ground moisture.

Val do Salnés and Cambados

Val do Salnés, around the Ría de Arousa, is the sub-zone with most vineyards. Cambados, regarded as the capital of Albariño, holds the Albariño Festival in early August, one of the oldest in Galicia. Its Fefiñáns quarter, with its manor house and square, is one of the classic sights of the route.

Condado do Tea and O Rosal

To the south, by the Miño and the Portuguese border, the Condado do Tea and O Rosal sub-zones blend Albariño with Loureira, Treixadura and Caíño, in landscapes of terraces above the river.

Manor houses and sea

Many wineries occupy old pazos, manor houses with camellia gardens. The route is rounded off by the Cíes Islands, in the Atlantic Islands National Park, and the fishing villages of the rías.

Towns and villages

Cambados, Meaño, Meis, Ribadumia, O Rosal, Salvaterra de Miño, Pontevedra.

What to see

  • Cambados and the Fefiñáns manor.
  • Albariño pergolas in Salnés.
  • Miño terraces in O Rosal.
  • Cíes Islands.

When to go

June to October; the Albariño Festival in early August; the harvest in September.

Food

Shellfish from the rías, pulpo á feira, empanada, grilled fish.

Tip

Combine a winery in the morning with a seafood platter at the harbour: Albariño makes most sense by the sea.
