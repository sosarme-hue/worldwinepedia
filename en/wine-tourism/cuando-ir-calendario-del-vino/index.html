When to go: the wine calendar

What happens in the vineyard and cellar each season, and which time to choose depending on what you want to see, in the northern and southern hemispheres.

Vines follow a yearly cycle, and each season offers a different experience. The dates below are a guide for Europe and the northern hemisphere; in the southern hemisphere (Argentina, Chile, South Africa, Australia, New Zealand) the calendar shifts by about six months.

Winter: pruning and the cellar

From December to February the vines rest and are pruned. It is a good time for quiet visits focused on the cellar and tasting, and for seasonal food. In Catalonia, for example, it coincides with calçotades.

Spring: budburst and flowering

Between March and May the vines bud and flower, and the landscape turns green. Temperatures are ideal for walking or cycling among the vineyards.

Summer: véraison

In July and August the grapes change colour (véraison). It is festival season and time for fresh wines, though in hot areas it is best to visit early in the day or at dusk.

Autumn: harvest

Between late August and October, depending on the area and the variety, comes the harvest, the liveliest time of the year: festivals, the smell of fermenting must and wineries in full swing. Some offer harvest activities for visitors; others cut back on tours. Afterwards, the red and golden colours of the vines make late autumn especially photogenic.
