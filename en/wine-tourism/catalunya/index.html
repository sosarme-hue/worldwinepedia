Catalonia: planning a wine trip

Twelve appellations in a small territory: how to combine cellars, coast, mountains and heritage in one or several trips through Catalonia.

Catalonia has eleven territorial appellations, plus the Cava D.O., which it shares with other regions, and the Catalunya D.O., created in 1999, which covers all Catalan vineyards and allows wines to be made from grapes grown in different areas. In a few hours by car you go from seaside vines to mountain terraces.

Three trips from Barcelona

  • Half a day or a day: Alella, or Penedès and Sant Sadurní d'Anoia by train.
  • A weekend: Priorat and Montsant based in Falset, or Empordà combined with the Costa Brava.
  • A week in the south: Penedès, Conca de Barberà and Poblet, Priorat, Terra Alta and the wine cathedrals, ending in the Ebro Delta.

Local grapes

It is worth seeking out the native grapes: Xarel·lo, Macabeo and Parellada in Penedès; Grenache and Carignan in Priorat, Montsant and Empordà; white Grenache in Terra Alta; Trepat in Conca de Barberà; Picapoll in Pla de Bages; Pansa Blanca in Alella; and Sumoll, Malvasia or Muscat in small quantities.

Catalan wine tourism

Many family cellars open by appointment, and many districts organise fairs, harvest festivals and open days. District tourist offices and the regulatory councils of each appellation publish their calendars of events.

When to go

September and October for the harvest; spring for walking; winter for calçotades and cava cellars.

Tip

Pair each area with nearby heritage: Poblet, Tarraco, Montserrat, Sant Pere de Rodes or the Seu Vella.
