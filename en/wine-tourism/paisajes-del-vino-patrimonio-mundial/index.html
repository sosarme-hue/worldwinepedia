World Heritage wine landscapes

The vineyards UNESCO has recognised as World Heritage, from the Douro to Tokaj and from Saint-Émilion to the Prosecco hills.

UNESCO has inscribed several cultural landscapes on the World Heritage List in which the vine takes centre stage. They are places where centuries of work have shaped the land, and all of them can be visited.

  • Jurisdiction of Saint-Émilion (France, 1999).
  • The Loire Valley between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes (France, 2000).
  • Wachau Cultural Landscape (Austria, 2000).
  • Alto Douro Wine Region (Portugal, 2001).
  • Fertő/Neusiedlersee Cultural Landscape (Austria and Hungary, 2001).
  • Tokaj Wine Region Historic Cultural Landscape (Hungary, 2002).
  • Upper Middle Rhine Valley (Germany, 2002).
  • Landscape of the Pico Island Vineyard Culture (Azores, Portugal, 2004).
  • Val d'Orcia (Italy, 2004).
  • Lavaux, Vineyard Terraces (Switzerland, 2007).
  • Vineyard Landscape of Piedmont: Langhe-Roero and Monferrato (Italy, 2014).
  • Champagne Hillsides, Houses and Cellars (France, 2015).
  • The Climats, terroirs of Burgundy (France, 2015).
  • Le Colline del Prosecco di Conegliano e Valdobbiadene (Italy, 2019).

Several of these areas have their own route guide in this section. Other World Heritage sites, such as Poblet, Tarraco or San Millán de la Cogolla, lie right in wine country and pair well with a wine route.
