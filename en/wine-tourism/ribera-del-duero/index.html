D.O. Ribera del Duero · Castile and León

Ribera del Duero

Castles above the Duero, medieval underground cellars and Tinto Fino on the Castilian plateau, with roast milk-fed lamb as its great ally.

Ribera del Duero follows the river for about a hundred kilometres across Burgos, Valladolid, Soria and Segovia, at more than 700 metres above sea level. Summers are hot and nights cold, and Tempranillo, known here as Tinta del País or Tinto Fino, makes deeply coloured, well-structured reds.

Peñafiel

Peñafiel castle, long and narrow like a ship on a hilltop, houses the Valladolid Provincial Wine Museum. Below it, the Plaza del Coso, ringed by wooden balconies, is one of the best-preserved medieval squares in Castile. Around the town are some of the appellation's best-known wineries.

Aranda de Duero and its underground cellars

Beneath the old town of Aranda de Duero runs a network of cellars dug between the twelfth and eighteenth centuries, where residents made and stored their wine. Several can be visited. In the riverside villages, hills honeycombed with cellars and their zarceras (ventilation chimneys) are part of the landscape.

Monasteries and river

The route can include monasteries such as Santa María de Valbuena or Abadía Retuerta, now linked to wine, and walks along the banks of the Duero.

Towns and villages

Peñafiel, Aranda de Duero, Roa, Gumiel de Izán, La Horra, Pesquera de Duero, Quintanilla de Onésimo.

What to see

  • Peñafiel castle and Provincial Wine Museum.
  • Plaza del Coso.
  • Aranda's underground cellars.
  • Hills of traditional cellars.

When to go

Spring and autumn. Winters are cold, but it is a good time for roast lamb and underground cellars.

Food

Milk-fed lamb roasted in a wood oven, Burgos black pudding, sheep's cheeses.

Tip

Many large wineries require booking; combine a big one with a small village cellar.
