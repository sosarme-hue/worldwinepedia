D.O. Conca de Barberà · Trepat and the Cistercian Route

Conca de Barberà

A valley ringed by mountains, with Poblet monastery, the walled town of Montblanc and a grape of its own, Trepat, ideal for rosé and sparkling wines.

Conca de Barberà is a basin surrounded by ranges (Prades, Miramar, Comalats), with a cooler climate than the coast. A large share of its grapes has traditionally gone into sparkling wine, and the area is known for Trepat, a local red variety with pale colour and lively acidity used for rosés, light reds and rosé cava.

Poblet and the Cistercian Route

The monastery of Santa Maria de Poblet, a UNESCO World Heritage site, was the burial place of the kings of the Crown of Aragon and is still home to a Cistercian community. With Santes Creus and Vallbona de les Monges it forms the Cistercian Route, combining monasteries, medieval villages and vineyards.

Montblanc and L'Espluga

Montblanc keeps much of its medieval wall and, according to tradition, is where Saint George slew the dragon, a legend the town re-enacts every spring. In L'Espluga de Francolí, the great early twentieth-century cooperative winery is one of the wine cathedrals, and nearby is the one at Barberà de la Conca, by Cèsar Martinell.

Towns and villages

Montblanc, L'Espluga de Francolí, Vimbodí i Poblet, Barberà de la Conca, Sarral, Rocafort de Queralt.

What to see

  • Poblet monastery.
  • The walls and old town of Montblanc.
  • The cooperative winery of L'Espluga de Francolí.
  • The cooperatives of Barberà de la Conca and Rocafort de Queralt.

When to go

Spring (with Montblanc's medieval festival) and autumn.

Food

Calçots with romesco sauce in winter and spring, cured meats, mushrooms and mountain cooking from Prades.

Tip

Try a Trepat rosé or sparkling wine: it is the best way to get to know the Conca's personality.
