South Africa · Stellenbosch, Franschhoek, Paarl and Constantia

Cape Winelands

Cape Dutch farmsteads among mountains, century-old oaks and Pinotage, a grape born in South Africa.

Less than an hour from Cape Town, the Cape Winelands bring together vineyard valleys ringed by mountains. Wine arrived with Dutch settlers in the seventeenth century, and many estates keep homesteads in the characteristic white, curved-gabled Cape Dutch style.

Stellenbosch

Stellenbosch, a university town of oak-lined streets, is the centre of the region and of winemaking education in the country. Around it are dozens of wineries with tasting rooms and restaurants. It was at the local university, in the 1920s, that Pinotage, a cross of Pinot Noir and Cinsault, was created.

Franschhoek, Paarl and Constantia

Franschhoek, founded by French Huguenots in the late seventeenth century, is known for its food and for a tourist tram between wineries. Paarl lies at the foot of a great granite massif. And Constantia, on the Cape Town peninsula itself, has some of the country's oldest wine estates, famous for a historic sweet wine.

What to see

  • Historic Stellenbosch.
  • Franschhoek Wine Tram.
  • Constantia's historic estates.

When to go

October to April (southern summer); the harvest is in February and March.

Food

Braai, bobotie, biltong and chef-driven cooking in Franschhoek.

Tip

Hop-on hop-off buses run between wineries; the easiest way to avoid driving.
