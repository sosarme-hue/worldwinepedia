Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG)

Franciacorta

Franciacorta, in the Lombard province of Brescia beside Lake Iseo, produces traditional-method sparkling wines based on Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. In 1995 it became the first Italian bottle-fermented sparkling wine to obtain DOCG status.

History

Franciacorta is an area of hills between the city of Brescia and the southern shore of Lake Iseo, with an ancient tradition of vine-growing linked in part to medieval monasteries. Its modern history as a sparkling-wine area began in 1961, when Guido Berlucchi and the oenologist Franco Ziliani made a bottle-fermented sparkling wine that was sold as Pinot di Franciacorta. Many producers followed in the years after. The DOC was recognised in 1967, and in 1995 Franciacorta obtained DOCG status, the first for an Italian traditional-method sparkling wine. Since 2003 the name Franciacorta has designated the territory, the method and the wine at once, without the need to add the word spumante.

Territory and climate

The designation covers nineteen municipalities in the province of Brescia. The hills form a morainic amphitheatre created by the ancient glacier that carved out the basin of Lake Iseo. The lake moderates temperatures, and the Alps to the north offer shelter from cold winds. The climate is moderately continental, with good ventilation and temperature swings that favour the acidity needed for sparkling base wines.

Soils

Glacial moraine soils predominate, with gravel, sand, silt and clay in highly variable proportions, generally well drained. Some areas also have deeper, clay-rich soils and limestone outcrops.

Grapes

The authorised varieties are Chardonnay, the most widely planted, Pinot Noir and Pinot Bianco, joined since 2017 by Erbamat, a late-ripening, high-acid local white variety, in limited proportion.

Types and ageing rules

Harvesting must be done by hand and is usually early, to preserve the acidity of the grapes. All Franciacorta is made by second fermentation in bottle. Non-vintage Franciacorta must spend at least 18 months on the lees; Satèn, made only from white grapes, at lower pressure and always as Brut, and Rosé require 24 months; Millesimato, from a single vintage, 30 months; and Riserva, 60 months. Depending on sugar content, the wines may be Dosaggio Zero, Extra Brut, Brut, Extra Dry, Sec or Demi-Sec. The area's still wines fall under the Curtefranca DOC.

Style

Franciacorta usually shows a fine mousse, aromas of white fruit, citrus and flowers and, with ageing, notes of toast, pastry and nuts.
