Designation of Origin (DO)

Binissalem

Binissalem, officially Binissalem-Mallorca, was the first designation of origin in the Balearic Islands. Located in the centre of Mallorca, at the foot of the Serra de Tramuntana, its emblem is the native red grape Manto Negro.

History

Vines have been grown in Mallorca since Antiquity, and the town of Binissalem, in the interior of the island, has been one of its main wine centres for centuries. In the nineteenth century Mallorcan viticulture enjoyed a period of prosperity, partly because phylloxera reached the island later than France, which made it possible to export wine to the mainland for a number of years. When the plague finally reached Mallorca at the end of that century, it destroyed much of the vineyard and the planted area shrank drastically. In the last decades of the twentieth century a group of local wineries drove the revival of quality viticulture, and in 1991 the designation of origin was recognised, the first in the Balearic archipelago.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises the municipalities of Binissalem, Santa Maria del Camí, Consell, Santa Eugènia and Sencelles, in the centre of the island. The vineyards lie on a plain and gentle hills at low altitude, at the foot of the Serra de Tramuntana, which shelters them from cold northerly winds. The climate is Mediterranean, with long, hot, dry summers, mild winters and rainfall concentrated in autumn. The influence of the mountains moderates temperatures somewhat compared with other parts of the island.

Soils

The soils are calcareous, brown or reddish in colour, with a variable proportion of clay and, in some vineyards, abundant limestone on the surface, and they drain well.

Grape varieties

The emblematic variety is Manto Negro, a native Mallorcan red grape that gives wines of moderate colour, aromas of ripe red fruit and soft tannins; the regulations require it to make up a significant share of the designation's reds. Another local red is Callet. Tempranillo, Monastrell, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah are also grown. The main white is Prensal Blanc, also called Moll, accompanied by Macabeo, Parellada, Moscatel and Chardonnay.

Styles

Production centres on reds based on Manto Negro, often blended with Callet or with non-native varieties, young or barrel-aged. Whites from Prensal Blanc, fresh and delicately aromatic, are also made, together with rosés and some sparkling wines. Much of the wine is drunk on the island itself, where tourism is an important market, although several wineries have gained a presence beyond the Balearics. The Binissalem Grape Harvest Festival, held every September, is one of the town's most deeply rooted traditions.
