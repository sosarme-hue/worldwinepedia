Russian River Valley

An AVA in Sonoma County, California, cooled by fog flowing in from the Pacific. It is one of America’s benchmark areas for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, and also preserves old Zinfandel vines.

Territory

The Russian River Valley occupies the central and southern part of the Russian River valley in Sonoma County, north of San Francisco. The AVA extends roughly from Healdsburg in the north to the area around Sebastopol and Santa Rosa in the south, and includes communities such as Forestville, Windsor, Graton and Guerneville, close to the coast. Within it lies a smaller AVA, Green Valley of Russian River Valley, in its south-western sector, one of the coolest parts.

Fog as the driving force

The defining feature of the area is fog. On hot summer days the inland air warms up and draws in cool, moist air from the Pacific, which flows in along the lower course of the river and through the so-called Petaluma Gap. In the evening the fog blankets much of the valley and does not burn off until mid-morning. This produces a large difference between day and night temperatures and a long growing season, in which the grapes ripen slowly and keep their acidity. The lower areas nearest the coast are the coolest; the northern and eastern parts are warmer.

Soils

The soil most associated with the region is Goldridge, a fine, well-drained, pale sandy loam that tends to restrain vine vigour and is often linked with wines of delicate texture. Elsewhere there are more clay-rich and alluvial soils, such as those of the Huichica series, and gravels near the river.

Grapes and styles

Pinot Noir is the star variety. Its wines typically show ripe red and black fruit, cherry, spicy and sometimes earthy notes, with a silky texture and acidity that keeps them fresh; many producers make them from individual vineyards. Chardonnay gives whites ranging from rich styles, with malolactic fermentation and barrel ageing, to tauter, more mineral versions. Both varieties are also used for traditional-method sparkling wines. In the warmer areas old Zinfandel vineyards survive, often planted by Italian immigrants, giving spicy, concentrated reds, and Syrah and Sauvignon Blanc are grown to a lesser extent.

Recent history

For a long time the area was known for apple orchards and bulk wine. From the 1970s onwards a group of pioneering producers showed that its cool climate was ideal for the Burgundian varieties, and since then the Russian River Valley has become one of the most prestigious names in Californian wine.
