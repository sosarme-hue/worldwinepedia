Geographical Indication (GI)

Mudgee

A historic region of New South Wales, on the western side of the Great Dividing Range. With a continental climate and elevated vineyards, it is known for Cabernet Sauvignon and Shiraz reds and for its Chardonnay.

Territory

Mudgee lies in central-eastern New South Wales, a few hours’ drive north-west of Sydney, in the uplands west of the Great Dividing Range. The town of Mudgee, with its nineteenth-century architecture, is the centre of a farming and grazing region in which vineyards are spread over valleys and slopes around the Cudgegong river. The name comes from a word of the local Aboriginal peoples usually translated as “nest in the hills”, referring to a valley surrounded by high ground. The GI is part of the Central Ranges zone, together with other elevated regions such as Orange and Cowra.

History

Mudgee is one of Australia’s oldest wine regions. In the mid-nineteenth century settlers of German origin planted the first vineyards, and the local gold rush boosted demand for wine. After a long decline in the early twentieth century, winegrowing revived from the 1960s and 1970s with the arrival of new wineries, some of them pioneers of organic farming in Australia. For a long time Mudgee’s vineyards were also an important source of Chardonnay planting material for the rest of the country.

Climate and soils

Lying on the western side of the range, Mudgee receives less maritime influence and less humidity than the Hunter Valley to the east. Its climate is continental, with warm, sunny summer days, cool nights and cold winters, and spring frosts are a risk on valley floors. Rainfall is spread throughout the year, with some concentration in summer. The vineyards lie at moderate to high altitudes for Australia, which lengthens ripening. The soils are varied: sandy and silty loams over clay subsoils, with areas of stony and granite-derived soils.

Grapes and styles

Reds are the region’s most recognised wines. Cabernet Sauvignon gives structured wines with black fruit, herbal notes and firm tannins, and Shiraz medium- to full-bodied reds that are spicy and ripe-fruited; the two are often blended. Merlot is also grown, as are, increasingly, Italian varieties such as Sangiovese and Barbera. Among the whites, Chardonnay has a long tradition in Mudgee and gives wines with ripe fruit and a broad texture, while Semillon and Riesling are also made.
