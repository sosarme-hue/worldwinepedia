DAC (Districtus Austriae Controllatus)

Kremstal

The Kremstal surrounds the historic town of Krems, where the Wachau ends, and combines primary-rock terraces in the west with broad loess terraces in the east. It produces outstanding dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling.

History

Krems is one of the oldest towns in Austria and, together with neighbouring Stein, was for centuries an important centre of the Danube wine trade. The monasteries of the area, especially the Benedictine abbey of Göttweig, set on a hill south of the river, owned extensive vineyards. Krems is home to one of the country’s best-known viticultural schools, and the town also hosts a large cooperative winery. The historic centre of Krems and Stein is part of the Wachau cultural landscape inscribed by UNESCO in 2000. The Kremstal DAC, created in the 2000s, applies to the area’s dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling; other varieties are sold under the Niederösterreich designation.

Territory and climate

The region extends around Krems and the lower course of the river Krems, which flows into the Danube, and also includes vineyards on the south bank of the Danube, around Furth and Göttweig. Its climate is similar to that of the Wachau: warm Pannonian influence from the east combines with cool air arriving from the Waldviertel, producing cool nights during ripening. Senftenberg, Stein, Rohrendorf, Gedersdorf and Furth are among the best-known villages.

Soils

In the west, in the Krems valley and on the slopes near Stein and Senftenberg, primary rocks such as gneiss and crystalline schists predominate, on often steep terraces; vineyards such as the Pfaffenberg, at Stein, are among the most highly regarded for Riesling. East of the town there are thick layers of loess, forming broad, deep terraces particularly suited to Grüner Veltliner. Loess, a fine sediment laid down by the wind at the end of the ice ages, holds water well and is easy to dig, so the area is full of sunken lanes between steep banks and cellars dug into the hillsides. South of the Danube, around Furth, there are also gravel and sandy soils.

Grapes and styles

Grüner Veltliner is the dominant variety and produces wines ranging from fresh, spicy styles to full-bodied single-vineyard wines, especially on loess soils. Riesling reaches its best expression on the primary-rock terraces, with taut wines showing stone fruit and a marked mineral character. Weissburgunder, Chardonnay, Zweigelt and other varieties are also grown. Like other Austrian DACs, the Kremstal DAC distinguishes between regional, village and single-vineyard wines.
