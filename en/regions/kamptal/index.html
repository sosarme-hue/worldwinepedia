Kamptal

The Kamptal follows the valley of the river Kamp, a tributary of the Danube, around the town of Langenlois. Its Grüner Veltliners from loess and its Rieslings from the Heiligenstein vineyard are among Austria’s most admired whites.

History

Winegrowing in the Kamp valley has been documented since the Middle Ages, and, as in other areas along the Danube, monasteries played an important part in its development. Gobelsburg castle, in the south of the region, belonged for a long time to the Cistercian abbey of Zwettl and houses one of the oldest wine estates in the area. Langenlois, the main town, is one of Austria’s largest wine-growing municipalities and is home to the Loisium, a centre devoted to wine culture with a contemporary building and old cellar tunnels. The Kamptal DAC, created in the 2000s, applies to dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling; wines from other varieties are sold as Niederösterreich.

Territory and climate

The river Kamp flows down from the Waldviertel and crosses the region before joining the Danube; upstream of Zöbing the valley narrows and enters wooded country, and the vineyards are concentrated in the lower, more open and sunnier stretch. The vineyards are spread between the slopes of the valley, around Zöbing, Langenlois and Gobelsburg, and the gentle hills that open out to the south and east. As in the Wachau and Kremstal, the climate combines the warm, dry influence of the Pannonian plain with cool air that descends at night from the Waldviertel; this difference between day and night temperatures helps to preserve acidity and aromatic clarity.

Soils

The region’s most famous vineyard is the Heiligenstein, at Zöbing, a hill of Permian sandstone and conglomerates with volcanic components, an unusual soil in Austria, which produces Rieslings of great finesse. The neighbouring Gaisberg lies on crystalline schists. Around Langenlois and to the south, deep and fertile loess terraces predominate, in vineyards such as Lamm and Loiserberg, which are very well suited to Grüner Veltliner.

Grapes and styles

Grüner Veltliner is the dominant variety and produces everything from fresh, spicy wines for drinking young to full-bodied single-vineyard wines with ageing potential. Riesling occupies the stony slopes and gives taut, floral, mineral wines. Zweigelt, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Weissburgunder are also grown, and some estates make traditional-method sparkling wines. Several of the country’s best-known wineries are based in the Kamptal, and the region has stood out for the vineyard classification work promoted by the producers themselves.
