Naoussa

An appellation in Greek Macedonia, on the slopes of Mount Vermio, devoted exclusively to Xinomavro reds: wines with marked acidity and tannins, suited to long ageing.

Naoussa lies in northern Greece, in the regional unit of Imathia, in Central Macedonia. Its vineyards cover the eastern and south-western slopes of Mount Vermio, around the town of Naoussa and villages such as Yiannakohori, Trilofo and Fytia. It is one of the first designations of origin recognised in Greece, in the early 1970s, and for more than a century it has been the benchmark for the great reds of the north of the country.

History

Viticulture in the area is ancient and continued through the Ottoman period. In the late nineteenth century some of Greece’s first modern commercial wineries were founded in Naoussa, among them that of the Boutari family, in 1879. Phylloxera arrived in the early twentieth century and caused a sharp decline in the vineyard area, which was slowly replanted on American rootstocks. From the 1980s and 1990s, alongside the historic houses, medium-sized wineries and small producers working their own vineyards emerged and brought the diversity of the area’s sites to wider attention.

Climate and soils

By Greek standards the climate is relatively cool and wet, continental with Mediterranean influence: winters are cold, sometimes with snow, and spring frosts and hail are a risk. Summers are warm, but cool nights and altitude allow slow ripening, and harvest usually takes place in October. Soils are highly varied, from clay and loam to sandy, limestone and marl areas, which gives rise to noticeable differences in style between villages and plots.

Wines and rules

The Naoussa PDO is reserved for dry red wines made entirely from Xinomavro. This variety, whose name means “acid black”, gives wines that are often moderate in colour, quickly evolving towards brick hues, with lively acidity and firm tannins; because of its profile it is often compared with Piedmont’s Nebbiolo. When young it recalls tart red fruit, sun-dried tomato, black olive and spice; with ageing in wood and bottle it develops notes of leather, dried herbs and forest floor. The wines are aged in barrels or larger casks, and the terms Reserve and Grande Reserve indicate longer ageing before release. Outside the PDO, Xinomavro from the area is also used for rosés, sparkling wines and blends with other varieties, sold as geographical indication wines.
