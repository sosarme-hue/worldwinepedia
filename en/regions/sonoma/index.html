Sonoma

Sonoma County, between the Pacific and Napa, brings together a wide range of climates and AVAs, from the cool Russian River Valley and Sonoma Coast to the warm Dry Creek and Alexander valleys.

History

Sonoma is one of the cradles of Californian viticulture. The Franciscan mission of San Francisco Solano, founded in 1823 in what is now the town of Sonoma, planted vines, and in 1857 Agoston Haraszthy established the Buena Vista winery nearby. In the late nineteenth century Italian immigrants settled in the north of the county and planted Zinfandel vineyards, some of which are still in production. After Prohibition the county took longer than Napa to turn to quality wine, but since the 1970s it has established itself with a highly diverse profile, built on a multitude of small wineries and growers.

Climate and soils

Sonoma is far larger and more varied than Napa. Its climate depends above all on distance from the Pacific and on the gaps through which fog and sea wind penetrate, such as the Russian River valley and the so-called Petaluma Gap. Areas near the coast and the low-lying Russian River lands are cool, with foggy mornings, while the inland valleys, sheltered by hills, are warm and sunny. Soils include sands and silts of marine origin, such as the Goldridge soil of the Russian River, alluvial deposits on the valley floors and volcanic soils in the Mayacamas Mountains.

Sub-regions

The county contains numerous AVAs. Among the best known are Russian River Valley, renowned for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay; Sonoma Coast and its sub-zone Fort Ross-Seaview, on the heights near the sea; Petaluma Gap, defined by wind; Dry Creek Valley, classic Zinfandel country; Alexander Valley, warmer and known for Cabernet Sauvignon; Sonoma Valley, next to the town that gives the county its name; Knights Valley, Chalk Hill and the part of Los Carneros that it shares with Napa. Some AVAs, such as Northern Sonoma, in turn encompass several of those listed above, which illustrates the overlapping nature of the American appellation system.

Styles

The range of climates allows a broad palette: Pinot Noir and Chardonnay from cool areas, both still and as a base for sparkling wine; old-vine Zinfandel, often with Petite Sirah and Carignan; Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot from the warmer valleys; and Sauvignon Blanc. As a county with several AVAs, labels may show Sonoma County or a more precise AVA.
