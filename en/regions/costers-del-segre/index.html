Designation of Origin (DO)

Costers del Segre

Costers del Segre is a designation of origin in inland Catalonia, mainly in the province of Lleida, made up of several scattered subzones along the Segre river and its tributaries. Its dry continental climate gives rise to a wide variety of reds, whites and rosés.

History

Viticulture in the lands of Lleida has ancient roots, but the modern history of Costers del Segre is tied to the Raimat estate, acquired in the early twentieth century by the Raventós family, owners of Codorníu. In what was then an arid area, the arrival of water from the Aragón and Catalonia canal made it possible to transform the land and plant vineyards, first with traditional varieties and later with international ones such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Chardonnay. Building on this experience and on the work of cooperatives and family wineries in other districts, the designation of origin was established, bringing together wine areas that are very different from one another.

Territory and subzones

The designation does not form a continuous territory but a collection of separate areas in the province of Lleida, with some extension into Tarragona. Its subzones include Raimat, on the plain west of Lleida; Segrià, around the provincial capital; Les Garrigues, to the south-east, drier and also known for its olive oil; Valls del Riu Corb; Artesa de Segre, to the north; and Pallars Jussà, in the pre-Pyrenees, with vineyards at higher altitude.

Climate

The climate is dry and continental, with little rain, cold winters, often with persistent fog on the plain, and hot summers. In the low-lying areas irrigation has been decisive for the development of the vineyards, while in the mountain subzones, such as Pallars Jussà, altitude brings cooler temperatures and a wider day-night range, which favours acidity and aroma.

Soils

Calcareous soils predominate, with a loamy or sandy texture, poor in organic matter and well drained, with notable variations between subzones.

Grapes and styles

The diversity of conditions is reflected in a wide range of varieties. Red grapes include Tempranillo (Ull de Llebre), Grenache, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah, Pinot Noir, Monastrell and Trepat, the last associated with the rosés of some areas. White grapes include Macabeo, Parellada, Xarel·lo, Chardonnay, White Grenache, Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling. Producers make young and oak-aged reds, often with international varieties, fresh or barrel-fermented whites, and rosés. Part of the area's production also goes into sparkling wine, as some municipalities are included in the Cava zone.
