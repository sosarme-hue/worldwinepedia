Sussex

Sussex, on the south coast of England, is one of the birthplaces of English traditional-method sparkling wine. Its vineyards are spread between the chalk hills of the South Downs and the clay and sandstone soils of the Weald.

History

The historic county of Sussex, now divided into West Sussex and East Sussex, plays a central role in the recent history of English wine. Although there were already small vineyards in the area in the 1960s and 1970s, the great change came at the end of the 1980s, when Nyetimber planted Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier in West Sussex with the aim of making sparkling wine in the Champagne style. Shortly afterwards, in the 1990s, Ridgeview set up in East Sussex, near Ditchling, with the same purpose. The recognition their wines earned encouraged many other producers, and in recent decades numerous estates have been created in the county, some of them large, such as Rathfinny, near Alfriston, or Wiston, at the foot of the South Downs.

Sussex was also the first English area to obtain its own designation of origin, which allows wines that meet its rules to state Sussex on the label.

Climate

The climate is cool and maritime, with a long growing season. The proximity of the English Channel moderates temperatures and reduces the risk of frost in coastal areas, although spring frosts remain a danger in inland valleys. Sussex is among the sunniest parts of the United Kingdom, but grapes ripen slowly and retain high acidity, ideal for sparkling wine. Variation between vintages is considerable.

Soils

The county has two main types of terrain. To the south, the South Downs range is made of chalk, the same limestone rock that outcrops in Champagne, and its south-facing slopes offer thin, well-drained soils. To the north lies the Weald, with clay, sand and sandstone soils, including greensand formations. Both types of soil produce quality wines: broadly, chalk is associated with taut wines of marked acidity, while clays and sands sometimes bring more body and fruit.

Varieties and styles

The dominant style is traditional-method sparkling wine, made from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier, with a second fermentation and lees ageing in bottle. Producers make non-vintage cuvées, vintage wines, Blanc de Blancs, Blanc de Noirs and rosés. Some wineries also make still wines from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Bacchus. The Sussex designation sets rules for sparkling wines, which must be made by the traditional method with a minimum period of lees ageing, and also for still wines.
