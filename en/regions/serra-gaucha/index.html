Serra Gaúcha

The Serra Gaúcha, in the north-east of the state of Rio Grande do Sul, is Brazil’s leading wine region. Settled by Italian immigrants from 1875, it is known above all for its sparkling wines and is home to the Vale dos Vinhedos, the country’s first designation of origin.

History

The Serra Gaúcha is an area of plateaux and valleys on the edge of the great basalt plateau of southern Brazil. From 1875 thousands of immigrants from northern Italy, mainly from Veneto and Trentino, arrived there and received plots of land in the colonies created by the government. On them they planted vines and made wine for their own consumption and for sale, and these colonies gave rise to towns such as Bento Gonçalves, Caxias do Sul, Garibaldi and Flores da Cunha. The humid climate favoured the spread of American varieties such as Isabel, which still occupy a large part of the vineyard area and are used for grape juice and table wines.

In the twentieth century large cooperatives were formed, and from the 1970s the arrival of foreign companies drove the planting of European varieties and technical modernisation, particularly in sparkling wine production. From the 1990s, many grape-growing families of Italian origin created their own wineries, such as Miolo and Casa Valduga, and the region has become an important wine tourism destination.

Climate

The climate is temperate and humid, with cool winters, mild summers and abundant rain throughout the year, including during ripening. Altitude, of several hundred metres, moderates temperatures. Humidity requires careful control of fungal diseases and close attention to harvest timing; as a result the grapes often reach moderate sugar levels and retain lively acidity, conditions well suited to sparkling wine. The quality of the reds depends heavily on the vintage.

Soils

The soils derive from the basalt rocks of the plateau. They are usually clayey, acidic and sometimes shallow, with rock fragments, in rugged terrain of slopes and narrow valleys that often means working vineyards on steep ground. The drainage and exposure of each plot matter greatly in such a rainy climate.

Geographical indications

The Serra Gaúcha is home to several geographical indications. The Vale dos Vinhedos, between Bento Gonçalves, Garibaldi and Monte Belo do Sul, was the first recognised area in the country, first as an Indicação de Procedência (IP) and later as a Denominação de Origem (DO); its rules give pride of place to Merlot among reds and to Chardonnay and Pinot Noir in sparkling wines. Other indications are Pinto Bandeira, focused on traditional-method sparkling wine; Altos Montes; Monte Belo; and Farroupilha, the last associated with Muscat varieties.

Styles

Sparkling wines are the region’s speciality: they are made by the traditional method, with a second fermentation in bottle, and in sealed tanks, from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Riesling Itálico, with a fresh, fruity profile. Sweet sparkling Moscatel, light and aromatic, is another local tradition. Among still wines, reds from Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Tannat and Teroldego and whites from Chardonnay stand out. Table wines and juices from American varieties still carry great economic weight.
