AOC (Pouilly-Fumé or Blanc Fumé de Pouilly), Central Loire

Pouilly-Fumé

Pouilly-Fumé, on the right bank of the Loire opposite Sancerre, is one of the benchmark appellations for Sauvignon Blanc. Its dry whites combine freshness, aromatic intensity and notes often described as flinty or smoky.

Location

Pouilly-Fumé lies in the Nièvre department, in the Central Loire, around the small town of Pouilly-sur-Loire. It occupies the right bank of the river, opposite the hills of Sancerre, visible across the valley. The appellation covers seven communes, including Pouilly-sur-Loire, Saint-Andelain, Tracy-sur-Loire and Saint-Martin-sur-Nohain. The landscape consists of gentle hills, less steep than those of Sancerre, with hamlets such as Les Loges, by the river, or Saint-Andelain, on one of the heights.

Name and appellations

The name comes from the term Blanc Fumé, by which Sauvignon Blanc is known locally, and which is usually linked either to the greyish bloom covering ripe grapes or to the smoky notes attributed to the wine; both explanations are traditional. The appellation may appear on the label as Pouilly-Fumé or Blanc Fumé de Pouilly. In the same area there is the Pouilly-sur-Loire AOC, reserved for whites made from Chasselas, a variety that was formerly widely grown here, including as a table grape, and that is now very much a minority.

Climate and soils

The climate is semi-continental, with cold winters, warm summers and a risk of spring frost, far from the influence of the Atlantic. The soils fall mainly into three types, which they share with Sancerre: Kimmeridgian calcareous marls, rich in fossils of small oysters; hard limestone, known as caillottes; and clay with flint, especially around Saint-Andelain. Much of the gunflint character associated with the appellation is attributed to the last of these.

Grape and style

The only variety of Pouilly-Fumé is Sauvignon Blanc. Its wines are dry whites with lively acidity, with aromas of citrus, white currant, grass and white flowers and, depending on the soil, notes of stone, flint or smoke. They usually have somewhat more breadth and less herbaceous exuberance than many New World Sauvignons. Most are made and aged in tank to preserve the fruit; some producers use barrels or large casks and make single-plot wines with greater ageing potential.

Producers

The name most closely associated with the appellation's renewal is that of Didier Dagueneau, a grower from Saint-Andelain who, from the 1980s until his death in 2008, demonstrated Sauvignon Blanc's potential to make age-worthy wines. Also notable are Château de Tracy and the Ladoucette family, owners of Château du Nozet, alongside many family growers.
