Manchuela

Manchuela is a designation of origin in Castilla-La Mancha that stretches across the east of the provinces of Cuenca and Albacete, between the rivers Júcar and Cabriel. Its main variety is Bobal, used for rosés and reds, alongside the revived Moravia Agria.

History

La Manchuela is a historic district lying between the plain of La Mancha and the lands of Valencia, sharing features of both. Vines have been grown there for centuries, and in the late nineteenth century and throughout the twentieth the area specialised in bulk wines, many of them from Bobal, which were sold to other regions to add colour and body to their wines. For decades the village cooperatives accounted for almost all production. At the end of the twentieth century some wineries began to make quality bottled wines, and the area was recognised as a designation of origin at the beginning of the twenty-first century.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises municipalities in the east of the province of Cuenca and the north-east of Albacete, bordering the Valencian Community to the east, near Utiel-Requena. It is a territory of plateaus and plains cut by the deep valleys of the rivers Júcar and Cabriel. The vineyards lie roughly between 600 and 1,000 metres above sea level. The climate is continental, with cold winters and hot summers, though partly softened by Mediterranean influence coming in from the east, above all through the breezes of summer afternoons. Rainfall is low and the difference between day and night temperatures is marked.

Soils

Light-coloured soils rich in limestone predominate, with variable proportions of clay and sand, generally poor and well drained. In some areas the soils are stonier.

Grape varieties

The most widely planted variety is Bobal, a red grape probably of Valencian origin that gives deeply coloured wines with notable acidity and aromas of red and black fruit. Alongside it, Moravia Agria has been revived, a local variety that had almost disappeared, with high acidity. Tempranillo, Garnacha, Garnacha Tintorera, Monastrell, Syrah, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Petit Verdot are also grown. The whites include Macabeo, Albillo, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and Moscatel.

Styles

Manchuela produces fresh Bobal rosés, bright in colour and with red fruit, and young and aged reds, notably those from old Bobal bush vines, which have helped to raise the standing of this variety. Whites and wines from international varieties are also made. Alongside the large cooperatives, a growing number of family wineries and small projects work with old vineyards and native varieties, seeking wines that express the character of this area of transition between La Mancha and the Levante.
