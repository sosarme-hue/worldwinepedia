Valtellina

Valtellina is an Alpine valley in Lombardy, in the province of Sondrio, where Nebbiolo, known here as Chiavennasca, is grown on terraces on the sunny slope. It produces fine reds and Sforzato, made from dried grapes.

An Alpine valley

Valtellina is the valley of the upper Adda river, in northern Lombardy, very close to the Swiss border. Unlike most Alpine valleys, it runs from east to west, so that one of its slopes, the one facing south, receives sun for much of the day. The vineyards are concentrated on that slope, known locally as the sunny side, in a narrow strip that stretches for several dozen kilometres. To farm it, terraces supported by dry-stone walls were built over the centuries, forming one of the most spectacular vineyard landscapes in Europe and requiring largely manual work. Between 1512 and 1797 the valley was ruled by the Three Leagues of the Grisons, and Valtellina wine was traditionally exported to Switzerland and the German-speaking lands.

Climate and soils

The climate is Alpine, with cold winters, but the southern exposure, the heat stored by the rocks and walls and the shelter the mountains provide from northern winds create favourable conditions for ripening. The difference between day and night temperatures is marked. The soils are shallow, sandy and mainly of granitic and metamorphic origin, low in organic matter, and soil often had to be carried up to build the terraces.

Appellations

The main variety is Nebbiolo, known here as Chiavennasca, which must make up the great majority of every wine. The Valtellina Superiore DOCG, recognised in 1998, includes five subzones that may appear on the label: Maroggia, Sassella, Grumello, Inferno and Valgella. The Sforzato di Valtellina DOCG, or Sfursat, covers a dry red made from grapes left to dry for several months after the harvest, which concentrates sugars and aromas; the result is a powerful wine with high alcohol. The Rosso di Valtellina DOC is intended for younger, simpler reds.

Style

Valtellina Nebbiolo is usually lighter in colour and body than that of Piedmont, with aromas of red berries, rose, spice and Alpine herbs, lively acidity and fine tannins. Valtellina Superiore from the best subzones can age for many years, while Sforzato combines the structure of Nebbiolo with notes of dried fruit and spice. Taken together, Valtellina wines are regarded as one of the most original expressions of Nebbiolo outside Piedmont, tied to a difficult and costly form of mountain viticulture that survives thanks to the efforts of local growers.
