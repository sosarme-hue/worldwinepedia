Vino Nobile di Montepulciano

Vino Nobile di Montepulciano is a Tuscan red made from Sangiovese, known locally as Prugnolo Gentile, grown on the hills around the town of Montepulciano, in the province of Siena. It was one of Italy's first DOCGs.

Montepulciano and its wine

Montepulciano is a Renaissance town set on a hill in the south-east of the province of Siena, between the Val d'Orcia and the Val di Chiana. Its wines have been renowned for centuries, and the adjective nobile is traditionally linked to the esteem in which aristocratic families held them and to the praise of writers such as the poet Francesco Redi, who celebrated the wine in the seventeenth century in his poem Bacco in Toscana. This wine should not be confused with Montepulciano d'Abruzzo: in one case the name refers to the Tuscan town and in the other to a different grape variety, which is not grown here as the basis of the appellation.

History of the appellation

Vino Nobile became a DOC in 1966 and in 1980 was recognised as a DOCG, in the first group of appellations to receive this status, together with Brunello di Montalcino, Barolo and Barbaresco. The same area produces Rosso di Montepulciano, a DOC created for younger wines, with shorter ageing and a more immediate style. Vin Santo is also made here, under the Vin Santo di Montepulciano DOC.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean with continental features, with hot, dry summers and cool winters. The vineyards occupy hills of moderate altitude around the town, on very varied soils of marine and lake origin: sands, clays, silts and marls, with fossils in some places. The aspect and altitude of each slope, as well as proximity to the Val di Chiana, account for differences in style between the various parts of the appellation.

Grapes and rules

The wine is based on Sangiovese, known locally as Prugnolo Gentile, which must account for at least seventy per cent of the blend. The rest may be made up of other permitted varieties, including the traditional Canaiolo Nero and Mammolo, as well as international varieties. The production rules require a minimum ageing period of two years before release, and three for the Riserva, part of it in wood.

Style

Vino Nobile is a ruby-coloured red that develops garnet tones, with aromas of cherry, plum, violet and spice, and notes of leather and tobacco with time. On the palate it combines lively acidity and firm tannins, with a structure that usually sits between that of Chianti Classico and that of Brunello. The best examples improve for years in bottle.
