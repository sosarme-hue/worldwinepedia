Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG)

Roero

Roero is an area of sandy hills on the left bank of the Tanaro, facing the Langhe, in Piedmont. Its DOCG covers Nebbiolo reds and the white Roero Arneis.

Location and history

Roero lies in the province of Cuneo, north of Alba, separated from the Langhe by the Tanaro river. It takes its name from the Roero family, who ruled many of these villages in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance. For a long time it was regarded as secondary to the neighbouring Barolo and Barbaresco zones, and much of its fruit was sold to make wine outside the area. This changed in the last decades of the twentieth century, with the revival of Arneis and the work of producers who chose to bottle under the name of the area. The DOC was created in 1985 and was raised to DOCG status in 2004. Together with the Langhe and Monferrato, Roero is part of the vineyard landscape declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014.

Landscape and soils

The most distinctive feature of the landscape is the rocche, escarpments and ravines of sand and sandstone carved by erosion, which separate the higher part of the area from the Tanaro valley. The soils are sandier and looser than those of the Langhe, with marls and clays in some places. This texture favours drainage and is usually associated with wines whose aromas are more immediate and whose tannins are somewhat finer and more approachable when young. Alongside the vines are woods, orchards and hazelnut groves, which give the landscape a more varied look than that of the hills across the river.

Climate

The climate is continental, with cold winters and warm summers, and with the influence of the Alps, which shelter the area. Autumn fogs and the difference between day and night temperatures around harvest time help to preserve the acidity and aromas of the grapes.

Wines of the DOCG

The Roero DOCG covers two main types. Red Roero is made mainly from Nebbiolo and also exists as a Riserva, with a longer ageing period. It usually shows aromas of red fruit, flowers and spice, with a somewhat lighter structure than Barolo. Roero Arneis is a white made from the Arneis variety, a local grape that came close to disappearing and is now the most emblematic of the area; it gives wines with floral and white-fruit aromas and moderate acidity. There are also sparkling and Riserva versions of Arneis. Barbera and other varieties are grown here too and bottled under other Piedmontese appellations.
