Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC)

Port

The Porto DOC covers the fortified wine made from Douro valley grapes, whose fermentation is stopped with grape spirit. It is traditionally aged in the lodges of Vila Nova de Gaia, across the river from the city of Porto.

History

Port takes its name from the city from which it was exported. Its rise began in the seventeenth century, when English merchants, often deprived of French wines, took an interest in the wines of the Douro; the practice of adding spirit to stabilise them during the voyage eventually led to the present style. Many of the shipping houses were founded by British families, as well as Portuguese ones and families from other countries. In 1756 the production area was demarcated and regulated at the initiative of the Marquis of Pombal. For a long time the wine had to be aged in and shipped from Vila Nova de Gaia; since 1986 the rules have also allowed it to be exported directly from the Douro.

Winemaking

The grapes come from the same area as the Douro DOC, with its schist soils and very hot summers. The main varieties are Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz (Tempranillo), Tinta Barroca and Tinto Cão, and for white Port varieties such as Malvasia Fina and Gouveio. Extraction takes place in stone lagares, often with foot treading, or in mechanical systems that imitate it. While the must still retains much of its sugar, grape spirit is added, which stops fermentation and leaves a sweet wine with a high alcohol content. The amount of must each grower may turn into Port is set every year through the benefício system, and vineyards are classified into categories according to their quality potential.

Styles

Ports fall into two broad families. Ruby-style Ports spend a short time in large vats or casks to preserve their colour and fruit: they range from Ruby and Reserve to Late Bottled Vintage (LBV), from a single harvest and bottled between the fourth and sixth year, and Vintage, from an exceptional declared harvest, bottled in its second or third year and able to develop in bottle for decades. Tawny-style Ports age in smaller casks with slow oxidation that gives them an amber colour and nutty notes: Tawny, Reserve, age-dated tawnies (10, 20, 30 and 40 years old) and Colheitas, from a single harvest with long ageing in wood. There are also white Ports, dry or sweet, and rosé Ports.

The DOC is regulated by the Instituto dos Vinhos do Douro e do Porto, which approves Vintage declarations and certifies the various categories.

Country
Portugal
Climate
Continental, with very hot, dry summers in the Douro valley, where the grapes are grown.
Soils
Schist on terraced slopes, with some granite areas.
Styles
Sweet fortified wines in ruby styles (Ruby, Reserve, LBV, Vintage) and tawny styles (Tawny, age-dated, Colheita), plus white and rosé Ports.
Grapes
Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tempranillo, Malvasia, Godello
