DAC (Districtus Austriae Controllatus)

Carnuntum

A small region east of Vienna, between the Danube and the Slovak border, named after an ancient Roman city. It is known for warm, fruity reds from Zweigelt and Blaufränkisch.

History

Carnuntum was an important Roman city and military camp on the banks of the Danube, capital of the province of Pannonia Superior, whose remains can be visited today in a large archaeological park between Petronell and Bad Deutsch-Altenburg. The modern wine region adopted the name at the end of the twentieth century, when it became a separate area within Lower Austria. Since then a group of young, dynamic producers has built its reputation, above all with red wines, and more recently the region gained DAC status, which links the name of origin to a defined style and set of grape varieties.

Territory and climate

Carnuntum lies south-east of Vienna, between the capital’s airport and the Slovak border, close to Bratislava. It is bounded to the north by the Danube and extends over three small groups of hills: the Arbesthaler Hügelland, around Göttlesbrunn and Arbesthal; the Leithagebirge, at its southern end; and the Hainburg hills with the Spitzerberg, near Prellenkirchen. The climate is Pannonian, with hot, dry summers and cold winters, and the combined influence of the Danube and Lake Neusiedl moderates the extremes. Wind, frequent on this open plain, helps keep the grapes healthy.

Soils

Loess, gravel and sand deposited by the Danube predominate, often over clay, giving wines with fruit and roundness. On the Spitzerberg, an isolated hill, there are stony limestone soils that produce tauter, more mineral reds and are considered among the region’s best sites for Blaufränkisch.

Grapes and styles

Zweigelt is the main variety and gives soft, juicy reds with cherry fruit. Many producers sell a Zweigelt under the collective brand Rubin Carnuntum, which groups fruity, approachable wines made according to shared criteria. Blaufränkisch offers more structured reds with spice and fresh acidity, reaching its finest expression on the limestone soils of the Spitzerberg. Sankt Laurent, Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon are also grown and go into barrel-aged blends. The Carnuntum DAC also covers whites from Grüner Veltliner, Chardonnay and Weissburgunder (Pinot Blanc), broad in style thanks to the warm climate. As in other Austrian DACs, the wines may state the region, village or single vineyard on the label, following an origin pyramid.
