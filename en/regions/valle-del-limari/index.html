Limarí Valley

A semi-arid valley in Chile’s Norte Chico, around Ovalle, cooled by the coastal fog known as camanchaca. Its limestone soils, rare in the country, give highly regarded Chardonnay, as well as Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir and Syrah.

Territory

The Limarí Valley lies in the Coquimbo region, some four hundred kilometres north of Santiago, in the transition between the fertile valleys of central Chile and the Atacama Desert. It is one of the subregions of the Coquimbo wine region, together with the Elqui and Choapa valleys. Its axis is the Limarí river, which rises in the Andes and flows into the Pacific, and its main town is Ovalle. For a long time vines were grown here mainly to produce pisco, the grape brandy typical of the area, and table grapes. From the late twentieth century, however, several wineries discovered the valley’s potential for cool-climate still wines, particularly in the sectors closest to the coast.

Climate

The climate is semi-arid: rainfall is scarce and irregular and falls on a few winter days, so irrigation is essential; the water comes from the reservoirs of the Limarí system. What makes quality viticulture possible is the influence of the Pacific. Every morning a thick coastal fog, the camanchaca, moves up the valley and keeps the early hours of the day cool, while in the afternoon sea breezes moderate the heat. Light intensity is very high and ripening is long and even. Further inland the climate becomes warmer and drier.

Limestone soils

The most distinctive feature of Limarí is its soils. In several areas near the coast there are soils with a notable presence of calcium carbonate, something uncommon in Chile, along with clays and alluvial deposits. Many producers attribute the tension and mineral character of their white wines to these soils and have planted specific plots to make the most of them.

Grapes and styles

Chardonnay is the variety that has brought the valley prestige: its wines combine citrus and apple fruit with lively acidity and saline or chalky notes, often with moderate barrel ageing. Sauvignon Blanc gives fresh, aromatic whites, and Pinot Noir light-bodied, red-fruited reds. Syrah produces spicy, juicy reds with notes of pepper and black fruit, and Cabernet Sauvignon, Carmenère and some Mediterranean varieties are also grown. In the inland areas grapes for pisco, mainly from the Muscat family, remain important.
