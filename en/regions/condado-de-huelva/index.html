Condado de Huelva

Condado de Huelva is an Andalusian designation of origin in the south-east of the province of Huelva, near Doñana. Its native grape is Zalema, and the area produces young whites and fortified wines aged biologically and oxidatively, such as Condado Pálido and Condado Viejo.

History

Viticulture in Condado de Huelva has very ancient roots, and the area played a prominent role in the age of discovery: towns such as Moguer and Palos de la Frontera were closely linked to the voyages of Christopher Columbus, and the district's wines were among the first to be taken to the New World. For centuries the Condado produced fortified wines in a style similar to those of Jerez, and much of its output was sold in bulk to the Jerez bodegas. With the delimitation of the Jerez area at the beginning of the twentieth century, the Condado had to develop its own identity. The designation of origin is one of the oldest in Andalusia, and in recent decades it has promoted young Zalema whites alongside its traditional wines.

Territory and climate

The designation occupies the south-east of the province of Huelva, between the river Tinto and the border with Seville, and includes towns such as Bollullos Par del Condado, La Palma del Condado, Rociana, Almonte, Moguer and Palos de la Frontera. It is an area of plains and gentle hills at low altitude, close to the Atlantic Ocean and to the protected area of Doñana. The climate is Mediterranean with Atlantic influence: winters are mild, summers hot and dry, and the nearby sea brings humidity and breezes that moderate temperatures.

Soils

Neutral or slightly alkaline soils of loamy or sandy texture predominate, of medium fertility, with clayey areas and, in places, more chalky soils resembling the albarizas of Jerez.

Grape varieties

The native and majority variety is Zalema, a white grape adapted to heat that gives wines with soft, fresh aromas when picked early. Palomino, Listán de Huelva, Garrido Fino, Moscatel and Pedro Ximénez are also grown. In recent years some red varieties have been introduced, such as Tempranillo, Syrah, Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon.

Styles

The designation produces light, fruity young whites from Zalema and a range of fortified wines: Condado Pálido is aged under a veil of flor, in a style close to that of fino, while Condado Viejo undergoes oxidative ageing that gives it an amber colour and aromas of dried fruit and nuts. Sweet and liqueur wines are also made, as well as a local speciality, vino naranja, flavoured with bitter orange peel and given long ageing. Reds and rosés have also been added recently.
