Nemea

An appellation in the north-eastern Peloponnese, in the Corinth region, devoted to Agiorgitiko reds. Its vineyards rise from the valley floors to high slopes, giving wines that range from soft and fruity to age-worthy.

Nemea lies in the north-eastern Peloponnese, in the regional unit of Corinthia, and covers ancient Nemea and a series of nearby villages such as Koutsi, Asprokampos, Gymno, and Leontio. It is Greece’s largest red wine appellation and one of the best-known names in the country. In antiquity, the sanctuary of Zeus at Nemea hosted Panhellenic games, and mythology placed here the first of the labours of Heracles, the fight with the Nemean lion; for this reason the local wine has sometimes been called “the blood of Hercules”.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and mild winters, although altitude moderates it considerably. The vineyards are spread across a wide range of elevations, from the valley floors to slopes and plateaus well above 600 metres, and three zones are usually distinguished: the lower areas, warmer, which give generous, ripe wines often used for rosés or young reds; a middle zone considered the best balanced for aged reds; and the higher areas, cooler, where ripening is slower and wines with higher acidity are obtained. Soils are mainly clay-limestone, with areas of marl, gravel and red earth.

Grapes and styles

The Nemea PDO is reserved for red wines made entirely from Agiorgitiko, one of the most widely planted red grapes in Greece, whose name means “of Saint George”. It gives deeply coloured wines with aromas of red and black fruit, plum and sweet spice, and generally soft, rounded tannins. Its great versatility allows very different styles: fresh rosés, sold as geographical indication wines, young reds made by carbonic maceration or with short ageing, and structured barrel-aged reds able to age for several years. The terms Reserve and Grande Reserve indicate longer ageing.

Since the 1990s many wineries have set up in Nemea, alongside the local cooperative, and studies on differences between sub-zones and on Agiorgitiko clones have intensified. Producers have long discussed a possible classification of villages or altitudes, which would allow the origin of the wines to be stated more precisely. Outside the PDO, Agiorgitiko is often blended with international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon or Syrah, in Peloponnese geographical indication wines.
