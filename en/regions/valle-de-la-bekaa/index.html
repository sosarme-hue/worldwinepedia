Wine region

Bekaa Valley

A high plateau lying between the Mount Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges, the Bekaa is home to most of Lebanon’s vineyards. Its altitude and dry summers produce reds with ripe fruit and good structure, as well as whites from local varieties.

History

The Bekaa Valley has been farmland since Antiquity. At its northern end stand the ruins of Baalbek, the ancient Roman Heliopolis, whose temple dedicated to Bacchus is one of the best preserved in the Roman world and recalls the role of wine in the life of the region. For centuries the valley’s grapes were destined mainly for eating fresh and for making arak, the traditional aniseed spirit.

Modern viticulture in the Bekaa began in 1857 with the winery founded by the Jesuits at Ksara, near Zahlé, where French varieties such as Cinsault were planted. Other houses joined over the twentieth century, and the Bekaa became the main source of grapes even for wineries based on the coast or on Mount Lebanon, such as Château Musar. Since the 1990s numerous new projects have appeared in the valley.

Climate and soils

The valley is an elongated plateau running from north-east to south-west between two mountain ranges, with vineyards mostly at around a thousand metres above sea level or somewhat higher. Mount Lebanon blocks much of the moisture coming from the Mediterranean, so rain is concentrated in winter and summers are long, sunny and dry. Cool nights and the wide range between day and night temperatures help the grapes keep their acidity and aromatic freshness, and winters are cold, with snow on the nearby heights. The low summer humidity limits vine diseases, which makes it easier to farm with few treatments.

Soils combine limestone, clay, gravel and stones, with areas of reddish earth. Water availability and soil depth vary with position in the valley.

Areas

A distinction is usually drawn between the central part of the valley, around Zahlé and Chtaura, home to historic wineries such as Ksara, and the West Bekaa, at the foot of Mount Lebanon, with villages such as Kefraya, where slopes and altitude provide somewhat cooler conditions. Further north, around Baalbek and Hermel, the climate is drier and more extreme.

Grape varieties and styles

Red grapes dominate the vineyard: Cinsault, Carignan and Grenache, historically present, sit alongside Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Merlot and Mourvèdre. White varieties include Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Viognier and Muscat, and the native Obaideh and Merwah are increasingly valued. The most characteristic wines are blended reds, often barrel-aged, with ripe fruit, spicy notes and firm structure. Whites, rosés and sweet wines are also made. There are no official ageing rules comparable to those of European designations, and each winery sets its own practices.
