AVA

Finger Lakes

A region in western New York State, around several long lakes of glacial origin, known above all for its cool-climate Riesling.

History

Commercial viticulture in the Finger Lakes dates back to the nineteenth century. In 1860 the Pleasant Valley Wine Company was founded in Hammondsport, beside Keuka Lake, and became famous for its sparkling wines; for decades the region made mainly wines from American varieties such as Concord, Catawba and Niagara, and from French-American hybrids, which are more resistant to cold. The great change came in the 1950s, when Dr. Konstantin Frank, a scientist of Ukrainian origin, showed that Vitis vinifera could survive the area’s winters if grafted onto suitable rootstocks. In 1962 he founded his own winery, Vinifera Wine Cellars, known today as Dr. Konstantin Frank. From the 1970s and 1980s new producers committed themselves to Riesling and other European varieties. The region was recognised as an AVA in the early 1980s.

Climate and soils

The Finger Lakes are a series of narrow, elongated lakes running north to south, carved out by glaciers. The most important for wine are Seneca, Cayuga and Keuka, along with Canandaigua. Their great mass of water, especially in the deepest lakes such as Seneca, moderates the climate: it delays budbreak in spring, extends the autumn and softens the most extreme winter cold, which nonetheless remains a significant risk. The slopes running down to the lakes also help cold air to drain away. Soils are of glacial origin, with gravels and silts over a bedrock of shale.

Sub-regions

Within the Finger Lakes AVA there are two recognised sub-zones, Seneca Lake and Cayuga Lake. The shores of Seneca Lake have the largest number of wineries, while Keuka preserves the historic heritage of Hammondsport. The vineyards are concentrated on the gentle slopes closest to the water, where the moderating effect of the lakes is strongest.

Styles

Riesling is the emblematic variety and is made in styles ranging from dry to sweet, with lively acidity and citrus and floral aromas. Gewürztraminer, Chardonnay and Grüner Veltliner are also grown, and among the reds Cabernet Franc, Pinot Noir and Lemberger (Blaufränkisch). Traditional-method sparkling wines, ice wines and late-harvest wines complete the range, alongside the hybrids that remain present in the region. Many wineries are small, family-run businesses that sell a large share of their wine directly to visitors.
