Designation of Origin (DO)

Uclés

Uclés is a designation of origin in Castilla-La Mancha lying between the west of the province of Cuenca and the east of Toledo, at the foot of the Sierra de Altomira. It takes its name from the monastery of Uclés and mainly produces Tempranillo reds.

History

The designation takes its name from the town of Uclés, in the province of Cuenca, dominated by a large monastery that was for centuries the main seat of the Order of Santiago and which, because of its monumental scale, is popularly known as the «Escorial of La Mancha». Viticulture has a long tradition in the district, and as in much of Castilla-La Mancha production during the twentieth century was concentrated in the cooperatives and in bulk wine. The area's vineyards were included within the La Mancha designation, but a group of wineries and cooperatives considered that their conditions of altitude and soil differed from those of the great plain of La Mancha and promoted a designation of their own, recognised in the first decade of the twenty-first century.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises a number of municipalities in the west of Cuenca and the north-east of Toledo, lying around the Sierra de Altomira, a line of mountains separating the plain of La Mancha from the Alcarria. Unlike the flat lands of La Mancha, the landscape is undulating, with hills and slopes, and the vineyards are generally found between 600 and 1,000 metres above sea level. The climate is continental, with long, cold winters, hot, dry summers and low rainfall; the altitude and the influence of the mountains bring cool nights and a wide temperature range during ripening.

Soils

Light-coloured limestone soils predominate, poor in organic matter, with areas of clay and stonier ground on the slopes. Their good drainage and low fertility limit yields.

Grape varieties

The main variety is Tempranillo, known in the region as Cencibel, which occupies most of the vineyard. It is accompanied by Garnacha, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah. The whites include Verdejo, Macabeo, Moscatel, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc.

Styles

The designation mainly produces reds based on Tempranillo, on its own or blended, in young and barrel-aged versions; the cool nights favour wines with good colour, balanced acidity and well-defined fruit. Rosés and whites are also made, including some from Verdejo and Sauvignon Blanc. It is a small designation, made up of few wineries and cooperatives, which seeks to set itself apart from La Mancha as a whole through the altitude and limestone character of its soils.
