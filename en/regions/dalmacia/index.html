Wine region

Dalmatia

Dalmatia, the long coastal and island strip of southern Croatia, is the region of Plavac Mali reds and of a great variety of native grapes. Its vineyards, on karst slopes dropping to the Adriatic, include historic sites such as Dingač and Postup.

History

Dalmatian winegrowing is among the oldest in Europe. The Greeks founded colonies on islands such as Vis and Hvar from the fourth century BC and grew vines there; on Hvar, the Stari Grad Plain preserves the Greek land division and was declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO in 2008. Under Venetian rule wine was an important product of Adriatic trade. At the end of the nineteenth century the late arrival of phylloxera allowed Dalmatia to export a great deal of wine for some years, but the pest eventually ruined many growers and caused heavy emigration. In 1961 Dingač, on the Pelješac peninsula, became Croatia’s first protected wine area.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, with long, hot, dry summers, mild winters and very high sunshine, increased by light reflected from the sea. Winds such as the northerly bura and the southerly jugo affect vineyard health. Soils are karstic, of limestone and dolomite, poor and stony, often with pockets of terra rossa. Many vines are bush-trained without irrigation on steep slopes, and in some places the stones removed from the ground have been piled into walls that shape the landscape.

Subzones

The regulations divide Dalmatia into subregions such as northern Dalmatia, inland Dalmatia (Dalmatinska zagora) and central and southern Dalmatia. In the north, around Šibenik and Primošten, the red Babić and the white Debit stand out. The centre and south include the Pelješac peninsula, with the sites of Dingač and Postup, very steep slopes facing the sea, and the islands: Hvar, with vineyards on the south coast around Sveta Nedjelja and Ivan Dolac; Korčula, home of Pošip and of the Grk of Lumbarda; Brač; and Vis, where Vugava is grown. In Kaštela, near Split, the last vines of Crljenak Kaštelanski were found.

Grapes and styles

Plavac Mali is the main red variety and gives wines of deep colour, high alcohol, firm tannins and aromas of ripe black fruit, dried figs and Mediterranean herbs, especially on the sunny coastal slopes. It is the offspring of Crljenak Kaštelanski, genetically identified as the same variety as Zinfandel and Primitivo, and of Dobričić. Among the whites, Pošip, medium-bodied with aromas of ripe fruit and almond, stands out, along with Grk, Maraština, Debit, Vugava and Bogdanuša. As well as dry reds and whites, the region makes prošek, a traditional sweet wine from dried grapes.
