Vinho Verde

The Vinho Verde region occupies the green, rainy north-west of Portugal, in the Minho. It is known for its light, fresh, aromatic whites and for the Alvarinhos of Monção e Melgaço.

History

North-west Portugal has a long winegrowing tradition, and its wines were already being exported to England in the Middle Ages. The Vinho Verde region was officially demarcated in 1908 and is today one of the country’s largest appellations. The name does not refer to the colour of the wine but to its young, fresh character, intended for early drinking. For a long time vines were grown along the edges of fields, climbing up trees (vinha de enforcado) or on high pergolas (ramadas), to leave the ground free for other crops; today trellises and other modern training systems predominate.

Territory and climate

The region stretches between the Minho river, on the border with Galicia, and the Douro, and from the Atlantic coast to the mountains inland. The climate is Atlantic, with plentiful rainfall, mild temperatures and a very green landscape. The appellation is divided into nine subregions: Monção e Melgaço, Lima, Cávado, Ave, Basto, Sousa, Baião, Paiva and Amarante. Monção e Melgaço, sheltered by mountains, is somewhat warmer and drier, which helps Alvarinho to ripen.

Soils

Granite soils predominate: acidic, sandy and shallow, they contribute to the lively acidity and light character of the wines.

Grapes and styles

The region works with native varieties. The main whites are Alvarinho (Albariño), Loureiro, Arinto, also known here as Pedernã, Trajadura (Treixadura), Avesso and Azal. Among the reds, the most notable is Vinhão, with very deep colour and high acidity. The most widespread style is a young, light white with pronounced acidity and moderate alcohol, sometimes with a slight spritz. Alongside it there are more ambitious varietal wines, especially from Alvarinho and Loureiro, which can gain complexity with time, as well as reds, rosés and sparkling wines. The Comissão de Viticultura da Região dos Vinhos Verdes oversees the appellation, which also covers grape spirits.

Each subregion has its specialities: Monção e Melgaço is historically associated with Alvarinho, the Lima valley with Loureiro and Baião with Avesso, while Vinhão is grown across much of the region. The whites are usually served chilled and go well with fish, shellfish and the traditional cooking of the Minho.
