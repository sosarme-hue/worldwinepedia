Chablis

Chablis, in the Yonne département, is Burgundy's northernmost vineyard and is devoted exclusively to Chardonnay. Its whites, with lively acidity and a mineral character, are associated with Kimmeridgian marl soils.

History

Viticulture around Chablis developed in the Middle Ages, encouraged by the Cistercian monks of the nearby abbey of Pontigny. Thanks to the Yonne river, which allowed wine to be shipped to Paris, the area's vineyards became very extensive before the phylloxera crisis at the end of the nineteenth century. The pest, competition from southern wines carried by rail, and frosts drastically reduced the planted area, which did not begin a sustained recovery until the second half of the twentieth century, when frost-protection systems became widespread.

Climate

Chablis lies closer to Champagne than to the Côte d'Or and has a cool semi-continental climate, with cold winters and moderate summers. Spring frost is the main risk; to combat it, growers use heaters placed between the rows and water sprinklers, which form a protective layer of ice around the buds. Slow ripening favours wines with high acidity.

Soils

The best vineyards lie on marl and limestone of the Kimmeridgian stage, a formation of the Upper Jurassic rich in the fossils of small oysters, Exogyra virgula. The same formation appears in Sancerre and, across the English Channel, in southern England. The Petit Chablis areas lie mainly on younger Portlandian limestone.

Appellations

Chablis has four tiers: Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru and Chablis Grand Cru. The premiers crus are spread across both banks of the Serein river; among the best known are Montée de Tonnerre, Fourchaume, Mont de Milieu, Vaillons and Montmains. The grand cru occupies a single south-west-facing slope on the right bank of the Serein, opposite the town, divided into seven climats: Blanchot, Bougros, Les Clos, Grenouilles, Preuses, Valmur and Vaudésir. Within them, the plot known as La Moutonne, between Preuses and Vaudésir, enjoys traditional recognition but is not an official climat in its own right.

Grape and style

The only permitted variety is Chardonnay. Chablis wines stand apart from those of other parts of Burgundy for their tauter, more austere profile, with notes of citrus and green apple and often a character described as mineral or saline. Many producers ferment and age their wines in stainless steel tanks to preserve that freshness, while others use barrels, especially for the premiers and grands crus, which can age well for many years. Sparkling wines from the area are made under the Crémant de Bourgogne appellation.
