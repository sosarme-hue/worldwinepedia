Mâconnais

The Mâconnais, around the town of Mâcon, is Burgundy's southernmost subregion before Beaujolais. It is Chardonnay country, giving everything from fresh, fruity whites to the most ambitious Pouilly-Fuissé.

Location

The Mâconnais covers the south of the Saône-et-Loire department, west of the river Saône, between the Côte Chalonnaise to the north and Beaujolais to the south. It is a landscape of hills and valleys running north to south, where vines share the land with pasture, woodland and other crops. In its southern part two spectacular limestone escarpments stand out, the rocks of Solutré and Vergisson, which tower over the vineyards of Pouilly-Fuissé. The Mâconnais is also home to the village of Chardonnay, whose name is often linked to that of the grape.

Appellations

The base of the pyramid is the Mâcon AOC, which may be followed by the name of one of the authorised villages (Mâcon-Lugny, Mâcon-Azé and Mâcon-Pierreclos, among others). The Mâcon-Villages AOC is reserved for whites from a large group of communes. Above them are the village appellations, all for Chardonnay whites: Pouilly-Fuissé, the most prestigious, which since the 2020 vintage has had vineyards classified as premier cru; Pouilly-Loché and Pouilly-Vinzelles, its neighbours; Saint-Véran, which surrounds Pouilly-Fuissé and reaches the edge of Beaujolais; and Viré-Clessé, further north.

Climate and soils

The climate is moderately continental, somewhat warmer and sunnier than in the Côte d'Or, with southern influences that show in the ripeness of the grapes. Jurassic limestone and marl soils predominate, with clay on the lower slopes; in the south there are also granite and schist soils, already close to those of Beaujolais.

Grapes and styles

Chardonnay occupies the great majority of the vineyard. Mâcon and Mâcon-Villages are usually made in tank and offer fresh, fruity whites with notes of apple, citrus and white flowers. In Pouilly-Fuissé and the best plots of Saint-Véran and Viré-Clessé the wines gain breadth and complexity and are often aged in barrel. A smaller part of the vineyard is given over to Gamay and Pinot Noir, which yield light reds and some rosés under the Mâcon AOC. Crémant de Bourgogne is also produced.

Cooperatives and producers

Historically cooperatives, such as those of Lugny and Viré, have played a very important role in making and selling Mâconnais wines. In recent decades the number of family estates bottling their own single-plot wines has grown, and some Côte d'Or houses have also set up in the area.
