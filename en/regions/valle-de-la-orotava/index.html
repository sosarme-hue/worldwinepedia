Valle de La Orotava

Valle de La Orotava is a designation of origin in the north of Tenerife, in the broad valley that descends from Mount Teide to the sea. It is known for its traditional braided-cordon training system and for its whites and reds from Listán Blanco and Listán Negro.

History

The Orotava valley, one of Tenerife's best-known landscapes, has had vineyards since the first years after the Castilian conquest at the end of the fifteenth century. During the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, when Canary wines enjoyed great prestige in Europe, the valley and its port, today's Puerto de la Cruz, took part in the wine trade with England and other markets. As that trade declined, production turned to local consumption, and the vines came to share the land with other crops, such as bananas in the lower areas and potatoes on the mid-slopes. At the end of the twentieth century the valley's growers organised to protect their wine, and the designation of origin was recognised in the 1990s.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises the municipalities of La Orotava, Puerto de la Cruz and Los Realejos, which occupy a broad valley open to the north and dominated by the Teide volcano. The vineyards rise in terraces from areas close to the coast to levels above 700 metres. The climate is Atlantic subtropical, mild all year round and shaped by the trade winds, which bring humidity and cloud to the mid-slopes and make this side one of the greenest on the island. Altitude creates notable differences between the warmer vineyards of the lower areas and the cooler ones higher up.

Soils

The soils are volcanic, loamy in texture, brown or reddish in colour and fairly fertile, developed on material from eruptions around Teide. As throughout the archipelago, the vines are grown ungrafted, since phylloxera never reached the islands.

Viticulture

A distinctive feature of the valley is the braided cordon or multiple cordon, a traditional system in which the vine's canes are interwoven into long cordons that can reach several metres and rest on wooden forked props. This system keeps the foliage away from the damp ground and makes better use of the light.

Grape varieties and styles

The main varieties are Listán Blanco and Listán Negro, accompanied by Negramoll, Malvasía, Gual, Verdello, Vijariego and Tintilla, among other traditional Canary grapes. The designation mainly produces whites from Listán Blanco, fresh and with volcanic character, and reds from Listán Negro, young or aged, as well as rosés and some sweet wines. In recent years several producers have recovered old braided-cordon vineyards to make single-plot wines that have brought the area recognition beyond the island.
