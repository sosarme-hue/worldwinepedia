Wine-growing region (Weinbaugebiet) of Lower Austria

Thermenregion

A region south of Vienna, at the foot of the Vienna Woods, named after its thermal springs. It is known for two native whites, Zierfandler and Rotgipfler, and for its Sankt Laurent and Pinot Noir reds.

Territory and history

The Thermenregion extends immediately south of Vienna, along the eastern edge of the Vienna Woods, from the outskirts of the capital to the area around Wiener Neustadt. Its name refers to the thermal waters that made spa towns such as Baden bei Wien and Bad Vöslau famous. Winegrowing here is very old: the Romans already cultivated vines in the area, and in the Middle Ages Cistercian monks, particularly those of Heiligenkreuz Abbey, played a decisive role in developing and organising the vineyards.

Climate and soils

It is one of the warmest and driest parts of Austria. The Pannonian climate, with hot summers and long, sunny autumns, combines with the shelter of the Vienna Woods, which hold back cold currents from the west, and with breezes that flow down from the hills at night. The soils are very diverse: in the northern part, stony limestone soils predominate, with lime-rich marine deposits, while in the south there are gravels, sands and deeper alluvial soils.

The north: Zierfandler and Rotgipfler

Around Gumpoldskirchen, Pfaffstätten and Traiskirchen two white varieties are grown that are almost exclusive to this region. Zierfandler, also called Spätrot, ripens late and gives wines with high acidity, a broad body and aromas of quince, citrus and spice, with great ageing potential. Rotgipfler, aromatic and full-bodied, recalls tropical fruit and spices. Both are bottled on their own or blended, in the traditional Spätrot-Rotgipfler cuvée, and can produce anything from dry wines to sweet late-harvest wines. Weissburgunder (Pinot Blanc), Chardonnay, Grüner Veltliner and Neuburger are also grown.

The south: red-wine country

In the southern part, around Bad Vöslau, Sooss, Tattendorf and Teesdorf, red grapes predominate. Sankt Laurent, a variety related to the Pinot family, finds one of its best homes here, giving reds with dark fruit, notes of morello cherry and silky tannins. Pinot Noir makes elegant wines on the warm gravels of Tattendorf, and Zweigelt and Blaufränkisch complete the picture, along with some blends with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot. Being so close to Vienna, much of the wine is sold at the wineries themselves and in the region’s Heurigen.

Country
Austria
Climate
Warm, dry Pannonian climate, sheltered from westerly winds by the Vienna Woods.
Soils
Stony limestone in the north; gravel, sand and alluvium in the south.
Styles
Zierfandler and Rotgipfler whites, dry or sweet, and Sankt Laurent and Pinot Noir reds.
