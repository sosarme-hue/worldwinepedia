Wine region (AOC Bordeaux and Bordeaux Supérieur)

Bordeaux

Bordeaux, in the Gironde département, is one of the most influential wine regions in the world. It is famous for its blended reds based on Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, its dry whites and the sweet wines of Sauternes.

History

Vines were grown around Burdigala, Roman Bordeaux, in the first centuries AD. The great boost came in the Middle Ages: after the marriage of Eleanor of Aquitaine to Henry Plantagenet in 1152, the region spent three centuries under the English crown, and its light red wines, known in England as claret, were exported in large quantities. In the seventeenth century Dutch merchants encouraged the draining of the Médoc marshes, where the great estates now known as châteaux emerged. The 1855 classification enshrined the hierarchy of the wines of the Médoc and Sauternes, and the trading system based on brokers and négociants, with the en primeur sale of the leading wines, remains characteristic of the region.

Territory and climate

The vineyards lie along the Garonne, the Dordogne and the Gironde estuary, formed where the two rivers meet. The climate is temperate and oceanic: the Atlantic and the vast Landes forest moderate temperatures and shelter the vines, but humidity and rain mean that vintages vary considerably from year to year. For this reason, blending grape varieties that ripen at different times is a traditional way of spreading risk.

Left bank and right bank

A distinction is usually drawn between the left bank, west of the Garonne and the Gironde, which includes the Médoc, Graves, Pessac-Léognan and Sauternes, with gravel soils where Cabernet Sauvignon dominates; and the right bank, north of the Dordogne, with Saint-Émilion, Pomerol, Fronsac and the Côtes de Bordeaux, whose clay and limestone soils favour Merlot and Cabernet Franc. Between the two rivers lies Entre-Deux-Mers, known above all for its dry whites.

Grapes and styles

The permitted red varieties are Merlot, the most widely planted, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot, Malbec, known locally as Côt, and Carmenère. The leading white grapes are Sémillon, Sauvignon Blanc and Muscadelle. Most of the production is red, but dry and sweet whites, rosés, clairets and sparkling wines under the Crémant de Bordeaux appellation are also made.

Appellations

The regional appellations Bordeaux and Bordeaux Supérieur cover the entire département and account for a large share of the volume. Above them sit dozens of subregional and communal appellations, such as Médoc, Pauillac, Margaux, Saint-Émilion, Pomerol, Pessac-Léognan and Sauternes, each with its own rules.

Country
France
Climate
Temperate oceanic, moderated by the Atlantic and the Landes forest, with marked variation between vintages.
Soils
Gravel on the left bank; clay and limestone on the right bank; varied soils in Entre-Deux-Mers.
Styles
Mainly blended reds; also dry whites, sweet wines, rosés, clairets and Crémant de Bordeaux.
Grapes
Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot, Malbec, Semillon, Sauvignon Blanc
