Montsant

Montsant is a designation of origin in the province of Tarragona created in 2001, which almost completely surrounds Priorat. It mainly produces Grenache and Carignan reds on soils more varied than those of its neighbour.

History

The historic Priorat area, in the broad sense, comprises both the central core of the Priorat DOCa and the villages surrounding it. For decades the latter formed the Falset subzone of the Tarragona designation of origin. Viticulture here has a long tradition, partly linked to the influence of the Carthusian monastery of Scala Dei, and, like the rest of the area, it suffered the blow of phylloxera at the end of the nineteenth century. The drive of cooperatives and new wineries, and growing recognition of the quality of its wines, led in 2001 to the creation of a designation of its own, Montsant, named after the range that dominates the landscape.

Territory and climate

The designation forms a kind of ring around Priorat and includes villages such as Falset, Capçanes, Marçà, Cornudella de Montsant, Cabassers and La Morera de Montsant. The vineyards lie at varied altitudes, on slopes, terraces and in valleys. The climate is Mediterranean with continental influences: warm, dry summers, cool winters and little rain. The relative proximity of the sea in some areas and the shelter of the Montsant range in others create important local nuances.

Soils

Unlike Priorat, which is dominated by llicorella, Montsant has more diverse soils: calcareous clays, granitic sands known as sauló and, in some areas, slate similar to that of its neighbour. This variety is reflected in the style of the wines, which ranges from fruitier reds to more structured ones.

Grapes and styles

The main varieties are Grenache and Carignan, also known locally as Samsó, with many old vines. Tempranillo (Ull de Llebre), Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah are also grown, and among the whites White Grenache and Macabeo. Reds are the most characteristic style: ripe red and black fruit, notes of Mediterranean herbs, body and ripe tannins, with a profile often somewhat more approachable than that of Priorat reds. White Grenache whites, rosés and small quantities of sweet wines are also made.

Production

Montsant combines long-established cooperatives with small family or newly founded wineries. Reds are sold young or barrel-aged, and many wineries make single-plot wines from old vines that seek to express the differences in soil and altitude within the designation.
