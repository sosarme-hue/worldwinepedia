Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC)

Bolgheri

Bolgheri, on the Tuscan coast in the province of Livorno, is the birthplace of Sassicaia and the main centre for Italian reds made from Bordeaux varieties. Its DOC chiefly covers blends of Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc.

History

Bolgheri is a small village in the municipality of Castagneto Carducci, linked to the coast by a famous avenue of cypresses evoked by the poet Giosuè Carducci in his verse. Until the mid-twentieth century the area had no particular reputation for wine. In the 1940s, Mario Incisa della Rocchetta planted Cabernet Sauvignon at Tenuta San Guido to make a wine for family consumption; over the years that wine, Sassicaia, was released commercially from the 1968 vintage and won wide international recognition. In the 1980s and 1990s other estates followed, such as Ornellaia, and the area became a benchmark for the so-called Super Tuscans.

Evolution of the designation

The Bolgheri DOC was created in 1983 for white and rosé wines only, so the area's great reds were sold as table wines. In 1994 it was revised to include reds and the Bolgheri Sassicaia subzone was created, which in 2013 became a DOC in its own right, an unusual case in Italy as it corresponds to a single estate. The designation includes Bolgheri Rosso and the longer-aged Bolgheri Superiore, as well as whites and rosés.

Territory and climate

The vineyards extend over a strip of plain and gentle hills between the Tyrrhenian Sea and the Colline Metallifere, which shelter it from easterly winds. The climate is Mediterranean, with warm, dry summers, plenty of sunshine and sea breezes that temper the heat and help keep the grapes healthy. Most vineyards lie at low altitude, from near the coast up to the first slopes of the hills.

Soils

Soils vary: closer to the hills, clays and calcareous marls predominate, while towards the coast sands and alluvial gravels with rounded stones are abundant. The name Sassicaia refers precisely to these stony soils, deriving from sasso, stone.

Grapes and styles

The rules allow Bolgheri Rosso to be made from Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc, alone or blended, together with other varieties such as Syrah, Sangiovese and Petit Verdot in limited proportions. The reds typically show ripe black fruit, notes of Mediterranean herbs and spice, ripe tannins and barrel ageing. Whites are made mainly from Vermentino and Sauvignon Blanc.
