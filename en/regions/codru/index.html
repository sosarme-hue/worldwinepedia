Codru

Codru is the wine region of central Moldova, around the capital, Chișinău. Its wooded hills, with a slightly cooler climate, are known for their whites and sparkling wines and are home to the vast underground cellars of Cricova and Mileștii Mici.

History

The name Codru comes from codri, the Romanian word for the dense forests that cover the hills of central Moldova. Winegrowing in the area is very old and was linked for centuries to monasteries and to the estates of the nobility. In the nineteenth century, under the Russian Empire, European varieties were planted and commercial wineries developed. In the Soviet era the region, close to the capital, was home to large wine complexes, including those of Cricova and Mileștii Mici, set up in old limestone quarries dug underground. After independence, and above all since the country reorganised its system of geographical indications, Codru has become one of Moldova’s three major protected wine areas.

Climate and soils

Codru occupies the central part of the country, between the valleys of the Prut and the Dniester. The hills are higher and steeper than in the rest of Moldova; among them is Bălănești Hill, the country’s highest point. The forests and the altitude contribute to a continental climate that is slightly cooler and wetter than in the south, with warm summers, long autumns and cold winters. These conditions favour the acidity and aromatic freshness of white grapes and of those intended for sparkling wine. Soils are grey forest soils and chernozem, with limestone subsoils that in some places have been quarried for centuries for building stone.

The underground cellars

The galleries of Cricova and Mileștii Mici, a few kilometres from Chișinău, form genuine underground cities with streets named after wines. The stable temperature and humidity of these galleries are ideal for maturing sparkling wines and storing bottles, and both wineries hold large collections of old wines. They have become one of the country’s main tourist attractions.

Grapes and styles

White varieties predominate in Codru: Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Aligoté, Rkatsiteli, Fetească Albă and Fetească Regală, along with Pinot Gris and Riesling. Red varieties include Pinot Noir, widely used for sparkling wine too, Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Fetească Neagră. Styles include fresh dry whites, sparkling wines made by the traditional method and by other methods, medium-bodied reds and some dessert wines. Production conditions for the geographical indication are set by the area’s producer association.
