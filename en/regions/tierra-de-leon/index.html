Designation of Origin (DO)

Tierra de León

Tierra de León is the designation of origin of the southern part of the province of León, in Castilla y León, which in recent years has presented itself under the shorter name DO León. Its hallmark is Prieto Picudo, a native red grape used for reds and rosés of marked personality.

History

Vine growing in the lands of southern León has been documented since the Middle Ages, when monasteries and the passage of the Way of St James encouraged the spread of vineyards. For centuries wine was a product for local and district consumption, made in small family cellars. A very distinctive feature of the landscape is the underground cellars dug into the hillsides, with their vents or zarceras poking out of the ground, grouped into cellar quarters in villages such as Valdevimbre and Gordoncillo. After decades as a regional wine, the area was recognised as a designation of origin in the twenty-first century and, more recently, has adopted the shorter name DO León in its communication.

Territory and climate

The designation extends across the south of the province of León and a few neighbouring municipalities in Valladolid, in the basins of rivers such as the Esla, the Cea and the Órbigo. It is an area of high plateaus and river terraces lying at a considerable altitude, around 800 metres or somewhat more. The climate is continental, with long, cold winters, frequent late spring frosts, short, hot summers and low rainfall. The large difference between day and night temperatures during ripening helps the grapes keep their acidity and aromatic freshness.

Soils

Alluvial soils predominate, with abundant rounded pebbles over a base of reddish clays and, in places, sandier or limestone areas. These are well-drained soils which, in the best sites, warm up during the day and favour full ripening in such a cool climate.

Grape varieties

The leading variety is Prieto Picudo, a red grape with compact bunches and small berries grown almost exclusively in this area and in other nearby parts of Castilla y León. It gives deeply coloured wines with good acidity and aromas of red and black fruit with spicy notes. It is accompanied by Mencía, Tempranillo and Garnacha. Among the whites, Albarín Blanco stands out, another local variety revived in recent decades, together with Verdejo, Godello and Malvasía.

Styles

The most traditional style of the area is the Prieto Picudo rosé, light and fresh, which was sometimes made with a slight spritz using the technique known as madreo, which consists of adding whole bunches to the must during fermentation. Today the designation also produces young reds and barrel-aged reds, in which Prieto Picudo must make up the majority, as well as fresh whites from Albarín and Verdejo. Most of the wines are drunk within the region, although their reach beyond it has grown thanks to the interest in native grape varieties.
