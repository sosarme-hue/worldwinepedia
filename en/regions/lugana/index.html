Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC)

Lugana

Lugana is a white-wine DOC on the southern shore of Lake Garda, shared between Lombardy and Veneto, made from the local Turbiana variety on clay soils of glacial origin.

Location

The Lugana appellation extends across the plain and gentle undulations to the south of Lake Garda, Italy's largest lake. It includes municipalities in the province of Brescia, in Lombardy, such as Sirmione, Desenzano del Garda, Pozzolengo and Lonato, and that of Peschiera del Garda, in the province of Verona, in Veneto. The name comes from the ancient Lugana forest, a wooded and marshy area that covered this strip of the lakeshore before it was cleared. The DOC was recognised in 1967, which places it among Italy's earliest appellations.

Climate and soils

The lake acts as a great thermal regulator: it softens the winters, moderates the summer heat and creates a milder climate than that of the surrounding Po plain, to the point of allowing olive and lemon trees to grow on its shores. Breezes from the lake also help to keep the grapes healthy. The soils are Lugana's most distinctive feature: they are compact, calcareous, mineral-rich clays laid down by the glaciers that formed the lake basin. Further south there are morainic hills with more gravel and sand. The clays, which are hard to work, retain water and give wines of good structure.

The Turbiana grape

The main variety is Turbiana, long known as Trebbiano di Lugana. Genetic studies have shown that it does not belong to the large Trebbiano family but is closely related to Verdicchio from the Marche and to Trebbiano di Soave. The DOC requires a very large proportion of Turbiana, and many wines are made from it alone. It is a grape with good acidity and delicate aromas, able to give fresh wines for early drinking as well as wines of some longevity.

Styles

The production rules provide for several types. Basic Lugana is a dry white, straw-coloured with greenish glints, with aromas of white flowers, almond and stone fruit and lively acidity. Lugana Superiore requires longer ageing before release, and Riserva a longer period still, giving more complex and broader wines. There is also a late-harvest version, made from overripe grapes, and sparkling wines produced both by bottle fermentation and in tank. Lugana whites are traditionally paired with freshwater fish from the lake, and their ability to evolve in bottle has drawn growing attention in recent decades.
