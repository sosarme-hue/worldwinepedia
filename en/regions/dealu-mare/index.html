Dealu Mare

Dealu Mare, “the big hill”, is a strip of south-facing slopes in the foothills of the Carpathians, north of Bucharest. It is Romania’s most renowned red-wine area, with Fetească Neagră, Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Pinot Noir.

History

The hills stretching between the cities of Ploiești and Buzău, in Prahova and Buzău counties, have a long winegrowing tradition, linked for centuries to the monasteries and boyars of Wallachia; the name Valea Călugărească means precisely “valley of the monks”. In the nineteenth century French varieties were introduced, and after phylloxera the area was largely replanted with them. During the communist period large state farms and viticultural research stations were created. Since the 1990s, and above all since Romania joined the European Union, Dealu Mare has attracted investment and seen the birth of many of the country’s best-known private wineries, which have helped to revive Fetească Neagră.

Climate and soils

The appellation occupies a narrow strip of slopes, mostly facing south and south-east, on the edge of the Curvature Carpathians. The mountains shelter the vineyards from cold northerly winds, and the exposure ensures good sunshine. The climate is continental, with warm, dry summers, long sunny autumns and cold winters. Soils are varied: reddish-brown and clay soils, loess, marl and, in places, limestone or sandy ground that provides good drainage.

Subzones

Within the DOC several subzones or localities may be indicated, including Urlați, Valea Călugărească, Ceptura, Tohani, Merei, Breaza and Pietroasa. Pietroasa, in the eastern part, has long been known for its sweet Tămâioasă Românească wines. Producers usually work with plots spread across different hills, and some have begun to highlight individual sites on their labels. The proximity of Bucharest and Ploiești has also encouraged the growth of wine tourism, and several wineries in the area welcome visitors among their vineyards.

Grapes and styles

Dealu Mare is above all red-wine country. Fetească Neagră gives deeply coloured wines with aromas of plum, sour cherry and spice and soft tannins; it is bottled alone or blended with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, both widely planted in the area. Pinot Noir also has a long history here, and Syrah and other red varieties are grown. Whites include Fetească Albă, Fetească Regală, Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Tămâioasă Românească. Top reds are usually aged in barrels, often of Romanian or French oak. Depending on the ripeness of the grapes, wines may carry the terms DOC-CMD, DOC-CT or DOC-CIB.
