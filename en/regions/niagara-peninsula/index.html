Niagara Peninsula

The Niagara Peninsula, between Lakes Ontario and Erie, is Canada’s leading wine region. Thanks to the influence of the lake and the Niagara Escarpment, it produces Riesling, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Cabernet Franc, as well as much of the country’s icewine.

History

Viticulture on the Niagara Peninsula dates back to the nineteenth century, when American varieties such as Concord were planted for juice and for sweet and fortified wines. For much of the twentieth century the region produced mainly wines of this kind. The first serious trials with Vitis vinifera were carried out in the mid-twentieth century, and in 1975 the founding of Inniskillin, in Niagara-on-the-Lake, marked the start of the modern era. At the end of the 1980s, after the free trade agreement with the United States, much of the American-variety vineyard was uprooted and replanted with European varieties and quality hybrids such as Vidal. In 1988 the VQA system was created in Ontario, and it now regulates the peninsula’s appellations.

Climate

Niagara has a continental climate, with cold winters and warm, humid summers, but one strongly moderated by water. Lake Ontario, which never freezes over completely, stores heat in summer and releases it in autumn, extending the season; in spring, its cold waters delay budbreak until the risk of frost has passed. The Niagara Escarpment, a long limestone ridge running parallel to the shore, slows air masses coming off the lake and creates a circulation that reduces frost on the terraces, or benches, at its foot. In most years winters bring the temperatures needed for icewine.

Soils

Soils are of glacial and lacustrine origin: clays and clay loams predominate, with areas of silt, sand and gravel. On the escarpment benches the soils are stonier and lie over the limestone and dolomite of the escarpment, while deep clays are common on the plain near the lake.

Sub-appellations

The peninsula is divided into two regional appellations, Niagara Escarpment and Niagara-on-the-Lake, and several sub-appellations. The first includes Short Hills Bench, Twenty Mile Bench and Beamsville Bench, on the escarpment terraces; the second, Niagara River, Niagara Lakeshore, Four Mile Creek and St. David’s Bench. Other sub-appellations are Lincoln Lakeshore, Creek Shores and Vinemount Ridge.

Styles

The region is especially known for its Riesling, from dry to sweet, with lively acidity and citrus and mineral notes, and for its cool-climate Chardonnay. Among reds, Pinot Noir, Cabernet Franc and Gamay stand out, joined by Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon in blends in warmer years. Sparkling wines are also made, mainly by the traditional method. Icewine, from Vidal, Riesling or Cabernet Franc, is the most famous style: the grapes must be harvested naturally frozen on the vine, at minus eight degrees Celsius or below, and pressed while frozen.
