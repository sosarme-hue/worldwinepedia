Wine region

Valle de Uco

The Uco Valley, in west-central Mendoza, brings together some of the province’s highest and coolest vineyards. Since the 1990s it has become the benchmark area for high-altitude Malbec.

History

The Uco Valley has a long agricultural tradition, with fruit orchards and some old vineyards, especially around La Consulta in San Carlos. Its great viticultural transformation, however, began in the 1990s, when Argentine and foreign producers looked for cooler areas than the traditional ones of Luján de Cuyo and Maipú. Pioneering plantings at high altitudes, such as those of the Catena family in Gualtallary, showed the area’s potential, and within a few decades the valley filled with estates and wineries, some of striking architecture.

Geography and climate

The valley lies south of the city of Mendoza, along the Tunuyán River and many streams that flow down from the mountains, and covers three departments: Tupungato, Tunuyán and San Carlos. The vineyards lie roughly between 900 and more than 1,600 metres above sea level, at the foot of the Cordón del Plata and the Tupungato volcano. The climate is cooler than in the rest of Mendoza, with cold nights, wide diurnal ranges and intense solar radiation. Spring frosts and hail are significant hazards, so many estates use anti-hail netting.

Soils

The soils are alluvial and highly varied: sands and silts in some sectors, and in others abundant boulders and gravel, sometimes coated with calcium carbonate. This diversity, visible even within a single estate, has encouraged the production of wines by plot.

Subzones

Several Geographical Indications have been recognised within the valley, including Gualtallary in Tupungato, Los Chacayes and San Pablo in Tunuyán, and Paraje Altamira, La Consulta and Pampa El Cepillo in San Carlos. Each has its own features of altitude, exposure and soil: Gualtallary is known for its calcareous soils and taut wines, and Paraje Altamira for its boulders.

Grapes and styles

Malbec is the main variety and here gives deeply coloured wines with marked acidity, floral notes and firm, fine tannins. Cabernet Franc, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc are also grown successfully, taking advantage of the cool climate. Many producers use concrete fermentation, large oak casks and a more moderate use of new barrels to preserve the expression of place.

More and more labels state the name of the IG or locality of origin, a practice that reflects a growing interest in expressing the differences between the various sectors of the valley.
