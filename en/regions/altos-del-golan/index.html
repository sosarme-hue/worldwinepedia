Wine region

Golan Heights

A volcanic plateau at the foot of Mount Hermon, whose high-altitude vineyards were planted from the 1970s onwards. Its cool nights and basalt soils produce Bordeaux-style reds, whites and sparkling wines with good acidity.

History

The Golan plateau, north of the Sea of Galilee, preserves the remains of presses and other traces of ancient winemaking, but modern viticulture in the area is recent. The first experimental vineyards were planted in the 1970s, after agronomic studies indicated that the altitude and climate of the plateau were suitable for quality varieties. In 1983 several farming communities in the area founded Golan Heights Winery, in Katzrin, whose first vintage, 1984, marked a change in the image of Israeli wine. Over time other, smaller wineries have also been established on the plateau.

In the Israeli wine classification, the Golan Heights appear as a sub-region within the larger region of Galilee.

Climate and soils

The plateau rises from south to north, from around four hundred metres above sea level near the Sea of Galilee to more than a thousand metres on the slopes of Mount Hermon. This gradient allows very different conditions within a small area: the lower southern parts are warmer, while those in the north have cold winters, occasionally with snow, and summers with cool nights. The climate remains Mediterranean, with rain concentrated in winter and dry summers, but altitude moderates temperatures and delays ripening, which favours acidity and aromatic finesse.

The soils are volcanic in origin, made up mainly of basalt and tuff, often stony and well drained. Their composition and depth change from plot to plot, and wineries in the area usually work with many separate plots at different altitudes.

Areas

Broadly, a distinction is drawn between the southern Golan, lower and warmer, suited to fully ripened reds, and the northern Golan, higher and cooler, where white varieties and grapes for sparkling wine are planted as well as reds. Some of the highest vineyards lie near Mount Hermon.

Grape varieties and styles

Viticulture is based on international varieties. Among the reds, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Cabernet Franc stand out, giving reds with defined fruit and structure, often barrel-aged and in some cases suited to ageing. Among the whites, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and Gewürztraminer are prominent. The freshness of the higher areas has made it possible to produce traditional-method sparkling wines from Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. Some late-harvest sweet wines are also made. There are no specific ageing rules for the area: each winery sets its own categories and practices, and a significant share of production is certified kosher.
