Wine region

Campania

Campania, in southern Italy around Naples, was already one of the most celebrated wine areas in Roman times. Today it is known for Taurasi, a red from Aglianico, and for whites such as Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo.

History

In antiquity, Campania was one of the most prestigious wine regions of the Roman world, and Falernian wine, produced in the north of the region, was praised by writers such as Pliny the Elder and Horace. The excavations at Pompeii, destroyed by Vesuvius in AD 79, have shown the importance of wine in the life and trade of the area. After centuries of local production and the difficulties of the twentieth century, quality viticulture was reorganised in the post-war period, when some wineries, among them Mastroberardino, worked to preserve and promote the native varieties of Irpinia. In 1993 Taurasi became the first DOCG in southern Italy, followed in 2003 by Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo.

Territory and climate

The region combines a coastal strip with a Mediterranean climate, warm summers and mild winters, and a high, mountainous interior with a more continental climate. In Irpinia, in the province of Avellino, vineyards often lie several hundred metres above sea level, with cold winters, marked temperature swings and late harvests: Aglianico is frequently picked at the end of October or in November. Sannio, in the province of Benevento, has the largest vineyard area in the region.

Soils

Soils of volcanic origin are characteristic of the region, around Vesuvius, in the Campi Flegrei and on the extinct volcano of Roccamonfina. In Irpinia clay and limestone predominate, often with deposits of volcanic ash, and in Sannio clay and calcareous soils.

Grapes and styles

Aglianico is the region's great red grape, giving deeply coloured wines with high acidity, firm tannins and great ageing potential. Taurasi, with at least 85% Aglianico, must be aged for three years, one of them in wood, or four for the Riserva. Piedirosso is another local red, common on the coast and in the Campi Flegrei. Among the whites, Fiano gives wines with aromas of flowers, hazelnut and honey that can develop in bottle; Greco, whites with good acidity and structure; and Falanghina, fresh, fruity whites, especially in Sannio and the Campi Flegrei. Other varieties include Coda di Volpe and Asprinio.

Designations

In addition to Taurasi, Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo, the region has the Aglianico del Taburno DOCG and DOCs such as Vesuvio, with the well-known Lacryma Christi, Campi Flegrei, Falerno del Massico, Costa d'Amalfi, Irpinia and Sannio.

Age-worthy Aglianico reds, Fiano, Greco and Falanghina whites and wines from volcanic soils.
