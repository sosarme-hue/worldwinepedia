Designation of Origin (DO)

Rías Baixas

Rías Baixas, in south-western Galicia, is a designation of origin recognised in 1988 and devoted mainly to Albariño whites. Its Atlantic climate and granite soils produce fresh, aromatic wines.

History

Viticulture in southern Galicia has a long tradition, linked in the Middle Ages to monasteries and later to the pazos, the manor houses of the Galician rural gentry, which had their own vineyards. For centuries the area's wines were drunk mainly locally. In the second half of the twentieth century, growers began to focus on Albariño and on modern cool-fermentation winemaking. The Rías Baixas Designation of Origin was recognised in 1988, and since then its whites have become widely known in Spain and abroad.

Territory and subzones

The designation covers the province of Pontevedra and part of A Coruña, near the rías, the sea-flooded valleys that give the region its name. It has five subzones: Val do Salnés, the largest and closest to the sea, around Cambados; O Rosal, near the mouth of the Miño; Condado do Tea, further inland and warmer, along the Miño; Soutomaior, the smallest; and Ribeira do Ulla, in the valley of the Ulla river.

Climate and soils

The climate is Atlantic, with mild temperatures, abundant rainfall and high humidity, more pronounced near the coast. To air the bunches and protect them from disease, vines are traditionally trained on pergolas known as parra, supported by granite posts, although trellising is increasingly common. The soils are mostly granitic, sandy, acidic and well drained, with alluvial deposits in the river valleys.

Grapes and styles

Albariño is the dominant variety, giving whites with lively acidity, aromas of stone fruit, citrus and flowers, and often a saline undertone. Other white grapes are also permitted, such as Loureira, Treixadura, Caíño Blanco, Torrontés and Godello, as well as some reds, including Caíño Tinto, Espadeiro, Loureiro Tinto, Sousón, Mencía and Brancellao.

The regulations provide for several categories: Rías Baixas Albariño, made exclusively from this grape; Rías Baixas Condado do Tea and Rías Baixas Rosal, which require a minimum share of Albariño together with Treixadura or Loureira respectively; Rías Baixas Salnés and Rías Baixas Ribeira do Ulla; and wines aged on lees or in barrel, as well as sparkling wines and reds. Ageing on fine lees has spread as a way of giving the whites more texture and complexity.
