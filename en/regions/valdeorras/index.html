Designation of Origin (DO)

Valdeorras

Valdeorras, in the Sil valley in the east of the province of Ourense, is one of the oldest designations of origin in Galicia. It is the leading reference for the Godello grape, which was revived here from the 1970s onwards.

History

The name Valdeorras is traditionally linked to the gold that the Romans mined in the Sil valley, and local tradition traces the area's viticulture back to that period. During the Middle Ages monasteries kept vine growing alive. After phylloxera, much of the vineyard was replanted with productive varieties such as Palomino and Garnacha Tintorera, and Godello came close to disappearing. From the 1970s onwards, a project to recover native Galician varieties, driven by local growers and technicians, rescued Godello, which is now the hallmark of Valdeorras. The area is one of the oldest designations of origin in Galicia.

Territory and climate

The designation occupies the valley of the Sil river and its tributaries, such as the Xares and the Bibei, at the eastern end of the province of Ourense, around towns such as O Barco de Valdeorras, A Rúa and Vilamartín de Valdeorras. It is the gateway to Galicia from the Meseta. The climate is transitional between Atlantic and continental: the mountains surrounding the valley hold back part of the oceanic influence, so rainfall is lower than on the coast, summers are warm and dry and winters are cold. The difference between day and night temperatures helps preserve acidity.

Soils

The area is known for its slate quarries, and this rock dominates many slopes, alongside granitic soils and areas of clay and alluvial sediment on the valley floor. Vineyards are often planted on slopes and terraces at different altitudes.

Grapes and styles

Godello is the main white variety. It gives wines of medium to full structure, with white and stone fruit aromas, herbal notes and, with age, more complex nuances; it lends itself well to lees ageing and barrel fermentation. Other permitted white grapes are Doña Blanca, Treixadura, Albariño, Lado and Palomino. Among the reds, Mencía stands out, giving fruity, fresh red wines, accompanied by varieties such as Merenzao, Sousón, Brancellao, Garnacha Tintorera and Tempranillo. Producers make young and aged whites, young and barrel-aged reds, and some rosé and sparkling wines.

Landscape

The vineyards of Valdeorras coexist with slate quarries and chestnut woods, in a landscape of deep valleys where most wineries are concentrated along the Sil.
