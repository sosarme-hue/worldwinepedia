Fiano di Avellino

Fiano di Avellino is a white-wine DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), made from Fiano on cool hillsides. It gives aromatic, structured whites able to develop over many years.

Location

The appellation covers a group of municipalities in the province of Avellino, in the hills of Irpinia, around the city of Avellino itself and places such as Lapio, Montefredane, Summonte, Candida and Sorbo Serpico. It is one of Irpinia's three DOCGs, along with Taurasi and Greco di Tufo, and one of the most prestigious white-wine appellations in southern Italy. The vineyards often lie at considerable altitudes for the latitude, in a landscape of hills, chestnut woods and valleys.

History

Fiano is an ancient Campanian variety. It is often linked to the grape the Romans called Vitis apiana, a name supposedly connected with the bees attracted by the sweetness of its berries, and the Latin name Apianum is still permitted on the label; however, this lineage has not been proven with certainty. By the middle of the twentieth century the variety had become very scarce, and it was revived thanks to a few local producers, notably the Mastroberardino house. The DOC was recognised in 1978 and the DOCG in 2003.

Climate and soils

Irpinia has a cooler, more continental climate than its position in southern Italy might suggest, with cold winters, relatively abundant rainfall and a marked difference between day and night temperatures during ripening. This allows Fiano to keep its acidity and aromatic potential. The soils are varied: clays, limestones and sandstones, often with layers of volcanic origin from the eruptions of Campania's volcanoes.

The grape and the wines

The production rules require the wine to contain at least eighty-five per cent Fiano, and the rest may come from other local white grapes such as Greco, Coda di Volpe or Trebbiano Toscano, although many producers use Fiano alone. There is also a Riserva version, with longer ageing. When young, Fiano di Avellino shows aromas of white flowers, pear, citrus, aromatic herbs and often a note of toasted hazelnut, with lively acidity and a savoury finish. With age it develops notes of honey, nuts, spice and hints that sometimes recall smoke. Because of this capacity to age, unusual among the whites of southern Italy, it is regarded as one of the country's great white wines.
