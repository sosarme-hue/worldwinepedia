Eger

A region in northern Hungary, on the slopes of the Bükk hills, best known for Egri Bikavér, the red blend called “bull’s blood”. It also produces the white blend Egri Csillag and varietal wines.

The Eger region extends around the town of the same name in northern Hungary, on the southern slopes of the Bükk hills, one of the northernmost areas of the country devoted to red wines. The town is known for its castle and for its cellars dug into volcanic tuff; in the Szépasszony valley, on the outskirts, dozens of them stand side by side.

History

Viticulture in Eger is documented from the Middle Ages, encouraged by the bishopric. The name of its most famous wine, Bikavér (“bull’s blood”), is associated with a legend linked to the Ottoman siege of the castle in 1552: according to tradition, the defenders drank a dark red wine that stained their beards, and the besiegers believed they were drinking bull’s blood. For centuries Kadarka was the basis of local reds, but after phylloxera it was gradually replaced by other varieties, above all Kékfrankos. In the socialist period, Bikavér became a high-volume export wine, which damaged its image; since the 1990s producers have worked to restore its prestige with stricter rules.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental and relatively cool for reds, with cold winters, mild summers and slower ripening than in the south of the country. Soils are mainly rhyolitic volcanic tuff weathered into clays, with areas of limestone, loess and heavier clays. This combination gives reds with marked acidity and more finesse than power.

Wines and rules

Egri Bikavér must be a blend of several red varieties, in which Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch) is the main variety, accompanied by others such as Kadarka, Portugieser, Pinot Noir, Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or Syrah. The rules distinguish several quality levels, with increasingly strict requirements for yield, ageing and origin: Classicus, Superior and Grand Superior. Its white counterpart is Egri Csillag (“star of Eger”), a blend of white varieties that usually includes aromatic and local grapes such as Leányka, Királyleányka and Olaszrizling (Welschriesling), as well as Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc. Varietal wines are also made, especially from Pinot Noir, Kékfrankos and Cabernet Franc, which benefit from the region’s cool character.

In recent decades the number of small family wineries working hillside vineyards and bottling their own wine has grown, and many higher-tier Bikavér wines now come from specific vineyard sites, which has helped restore part of the region’s former reputation.
