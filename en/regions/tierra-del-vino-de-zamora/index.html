Tierra del Vino de Zamora

Tierra del Vino de Zamora is a designation of origin in Castilla y León located south of the city of Zamora, between Toro and Arribes. It preserves a remarkable heritage of old bush vines, above all Tempranillo, from which reds of continental character are made.

History

The district known as Tierra del Vino, the Land of Wine, owes its name to the importance the vine has had there for centuries. Lying south of the river Duero, opposite the city of Zamora, it historically supplied wine to the capital and the neighbouring districts, and many of its villages still have traditional cellars dug underground. As in the rest of Castile, phylloxera and later the abandonment of the countryside reduced the vineyard area, but the zone kept a considerable number of old vines. From the end of the twentieth century several wineries and a cooperative drove improvements in quality, and the territory was recognised as a designation of origin in the first decade of the twenty-first century.

Territory and climate

The designation covers municipalities in the south of the province of Zamora and a few in the north of Salamanca, on a gently rolling plain lying roughly between 650 and 800 metres above sea level. It borders Toro to the east and the Arribes del Duero to the west. The climate is continental, with long, cold winters, hot, dry summers, low rainfall and a marked difference between day and night temperatures. Spring frosts are a regular hazard.

Soils

The soils are generally poor, with a sandy-loam or clayey texture, often with abundant stones and gravel on the surface, and in some areas with limestone. Their good drainage and low fertility naturally limit the vigour of the vines. The presence of sandy soils helped some vineyards resist phylloxera, and very old vines can still be found today, in some cases planted ungrafted.

Grape varieties

The main variety is Tempranillo, known locally as Tinta del País, mostly grown as bush vines. It is accompanied by Garnacha, Cabernet Sauvignon and other red varieties in smaller proportions. The whites include Malvasía, traditional in the district, Moscatel de grano menudo (Muscat à petits grains), Verdejo, Albillo, Godello and Palomino. In the old vineyards it is common to find different varieties mixed in the same plot.

Styles

Production focuses on reds from Tempranillo, from young, fruity wines to barrel-aged reds with deep colour, firm structure and ripe fruit, often fresher than those of neighbouring Toro. Rosés and whites are also made, including some Malvasía wines with a personality of their own. It is a small designation, made up of few wineries, in which the value of its old vines is the main argument for quality.
