Designation of Origin (DO)

Rueda

Rueda, created in 1980, was the first designation of origin in Castilla y León. It is the leading reference for the Verdejo grape and for the white wines of the northern Meseta.

History

Viticulture in the Rueda area has been documented since the Middle Ages, and for centuries its wines supplied the towns of the Meseta, notably Valladolid and the fairs of Medina del Campo. The cellars dug underground in many villages bear witness to this tradition. For a long time the area was known for oxidatively aged wines, made largely from Palomino. A decisive change came in the 1970s: the arrival of wineries from other regions, such as Marqués de Riscal, encouraged the production of young, fresh whites based on Verdejo and the introduction of Sauvignon Blanc. In 1980 Rueda became the first designation of origin in Castilla y León.

Territory and climate

The designation lies south of the Duero river and covers municipalities in the provinces of Valladolid, which accounts for most of the vineyard area, Segovia and Ávila. The vineyards are on a flat plateau at around 700 to 800 metres above sea level. The climate is continental, with long cold winters, short springs with a risk of late frost, hot dry summers and little rain. The marked difference between day and night temperatures during ripening helps the grapes retain acidity and aroma. To protect those aromas, night harvesting is common.

Soils

The most characteristic soils are brown and stony, with abundant rounded river pebbles known locally as cascajo, which aid drainage and aeration. Elsewhere the soils are sandier or more calcareous.

Grapes and styles

Verdejo is the emblematic variety: it gives whites with good acidity, herbaceous and white-fruit aromas, and a characteristic slightly bitter finish. Sauvignon Blanc, Viura (Macabeo) and Palomino are also grown. Besides young whites, the designation covers barrel-fermented or barrel-aged whites, traditional-method sparkling wines and the fortified wines of local tradition: Dorado, aged oxidatively, and Pálido, aged biologically under a veil of flor. Since 2008 the regulations have also included red and rosé wines, made mainly from Tempranillo along with Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Grenache.

Labelling

Wines that name a variety on the label, such as Verdejo or Sauvignon Blanc, must be made predominantly from that grape, according to the percentages set by the designation's regulations.
