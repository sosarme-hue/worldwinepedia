Saint-Joseph

Saint-Joseph is a long strip of vineyards on the right bank of the Rhône, between Condrieu and Cornas. It produces mainly Syrah reds, lighter on their feet and fruitier than those of Hermitage or Côte-Rôtie, and Marsanne and Roussanne whites.

Location

Saint-Joseph is the largest appellation of the Northern Rhône after Crozes-Hermitage. It stretches for some fifty kilometres along the right bank of the river, in the Loire and Ardèche departments, from near Condrieu in the north to the border with Cornas in the south. It takes in a long series of communes, among them Mauves, Tournon-sur-Rhône, Saint-Jean-de-Muzols, Chavanay and Sarras. The historic heart of the appellation lies around Tournon and Mauves, opposite Tain-l'Hermitage.

History

The wines of this area, particularly those of Mauves and Tournon, have been appreciated for centuries. When the appellation was created in the mid-twentieth century, it covered a small core of communes around Tournon. It was later considerably enlarged to include flat land and plateaus less suited to the vine, which drew criticism about the uneven character of the wines. From the 1990s onwards the boundaries were revised with the aim of concentrating the vineyard on the slopes and excluding lower-quality areas.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental, with Mediterranean influences more noticeable in the south. The best plots lie on slopes facing south or south-east, often on terraces. The soils are mainly granite and other crystalline rocks such as gneiss and mica schist, with a sandy surface layer. In some sectors there are also loess deposits and deeper soils.

Grapes and styles

The reds, which account for the great majority of production, are made from Syrah, to which the rules allow a small proportion of Marsanne and Roussanne to be added. They are usually medium-bodied wines with red and black fruit, pepper and floral notes, more approachable in their youth than those of Hermitage or Côte-Rôtie, although those from the best slopes can age well. The whites are made from Marsanne and Roussanne; they are broad wines with notes of white flowers, stone fruit and almond, which can be drunk young or after a few years in bottle.

Producers

The size of the appellation means that many types of producer coexist: family growers, cooperatives and large Rhône houses such as Chapoutier, Guigal and Jaboulet. This diversity, together with that of the soils and aspects, explains the range of styles found under the Saint-Joseph name.
