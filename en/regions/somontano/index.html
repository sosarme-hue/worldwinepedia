Somontano

Somontano is a designation of origin in the province of Huesca, at the foot of the Pyrenees, centred on Barbastro. It combines native varieties such as Moristel and Parraleta with a wide range of international grapes.

History

The name Somontano means at the foot of the mountain and describes the area's position in the foothills of the pre-Pyrenean ranges. Viticulture here dates back at least to Roman times and was consolidated in the Middle Ages by the monasteries. In the nineteenth century, after phylloxera reached France, Somontano wines were sold on the French market, and links were forged with the Bordeaux region that left their mark on winemaking techniques. After a period of decline, the creation of the designation of origin in the 1980s and the founding of new wineries, alongside the local cooperative, thoroughly renewed production and gave the area a modern image.

Territory and climate

The designation lies in the centre of the province of Huesca, around the town of Barbastro, in the basins of rivers such as the Vero and the Cinca. The Pyrenees, visible to the north, shelter the area from cold winds. The climate is continental with a mountain influence: cold winters, warm summers, moderate rainfall and a marked difference between day and night temperatures, which helps preserve acidity and aroma, especially in the whites. The vineyards are spread across plains, slopes and terraces at different altitudes.

Soils

Brown calcareous soils predominate, with a loamy to clayey texture; they are generally of low fertility and well drained, with gravel and rounded stones on the river terraces.

Grapes and styles

Alongside native varieties such as the red Moristel and Parraleta and the white Alcañón, Somontano grows a wide range of grapes: Tempranillo, Grenache, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Pinot Noir among the reds, and Chardonnay, Macabeo, White Grenache, Gewürztraminer, Riesling and Sauvignon Blanc among the whites. This diversity translates into single-varietal wines and blends that combine local and international grapes. Gewürztraminer and Chardonnay have produced some of the area's best-known whites, while the reds range from young, fruity wines to barrel-aged ones.

Ageing and styles

The designation covers whites, rosés and reds, as well as wines bearing the traditional terms Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva. In recent years there has been growing interest in reviving old Moristel and Parraleta vineyards and in making wines that express the area's own character. Rosés, often from Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or Grenache, also account for a significant part of production.
