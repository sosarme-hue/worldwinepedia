Lodi

An AVA in the northern Central Valley of California, between Sacramento and Stockton, cooled by breezes from the Delta. It is one of the state’s most productive areas and a great reservoir of old Zinfandel vines.

Territory

Lodi lies at the northern end of San Joaquin County and the southern part of Sacramento County, in the northern section of California’s great Central Valley, east of the confluence of the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers. For a long time it was seen mainly as an area of abundant production, whose grapes were sold to large wineries across the state, and even today it is one of California’s most important sources of grapes. Since the late twentieth century, however, a growing number of wineries have bottled wines under the Lodi name and championed the quality of its old vines. The AVA includes seven sub-AVAs: Alta Mesa, Borden Ranch, Clements Hills, Cosumnes River, Jahant, Mokelumne River and Sloughhouse.

Climate

The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and mild winters. What sets Lodi apart from other parts of the Central Valley is the influence of the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta: in the afternoon, cool breezes from San Francisco Bay flow in through the Delta and lower night-time temperatures, helping the fruit keep its acidity and freshness. The western areas, closer to the Delta, are somewhat cooler than the eastern ones, near the Sierra Nevada foothills.

Soils and old vines

In the Mokelumne River area deep, fine, alluvial sandy soils washed down from the Sierra Nevada predominate. Phylloxera struggles to thrive in these sandy soils, which is why many old vines planted on their own roots survive in Lodi, some more than a century old. Among them is a Cinsault vineyard counted among the oldest known plantings of this variety. Further east the soils become more clay-rich and stony, with deposits of volcanic origin.

Grapes and styles

Zinfandel is the emblematic variety, and its old vines, often head-trained, give full-bodied reds with ripe fruit and spice. Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Petite Sirah and Chardonnay are also grown widely, along with a surprising diversity of Mediterranean and Iberian varieties such as Tempranillo, Grenache, Albariño, Vermentino and Touriga Nacional, which many small producers have adopted for their tolerance of heat. Lodi is also known for Lodi Rules, one of California’s first sustainable winegrowing certification programmes, which has influenced other regions.
