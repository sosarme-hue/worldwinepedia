Riverina

A large irrigated region in inland New South Wales, around Griffith and the Murrumbidgee river. It produces huge volumes of everyday wine and is famous for its sweet botrytised Semillon.

Territory and irrigation

The Riverina is a vast plain in south-western New South Wales, crossed by the Murrumbidgee river. Its viticulture depends entirely on irrigation: in the early twentieth century a system of dams and canals, the Murrumbidgee Irrigation Area, was built, transforming semi-arid land into one of Australia’s great farming areas, with rice fields, citrus orchards, vegetables and vineyards. The towns of Griffith and Leeton are its main centres. Together with the Murray river regions, the Riverina is one of the country’s great wine-producing areas and contributes a very significant share of the national harvest.

Italian immigration

Much of the Riverina’s winegrowing history is linked to Italian immigration, which arrived in large numbers during the twentieth century and gave Griffith a marked Italian character that can still be seen in its food and its family names. Several of Australia’s large wine companies started as family businesses of Italian origin in this area and have become leading exporters.

Climate and soils

The climate is hot and dry, with hot summers, cool winters and little rain, so irrigation is essential. The soils are mainly red and brown clays and loams, deep and fertile. These conditions allow high yields and consistent production, with little disease pressure in summer.

Volume wines

Most of the production goes into everyday varietal wines and blends, sold in bottle, in bag-in-box or in bulk in Australia and abroad, often under the broader South Eastern Australia indication. Among the reds, Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot dominate, and Durif (Petite Sirah) also has a notable presence; among the whites, Chardonnay, Semillon and other high-yielding white varieties. Some producers have also introduced Italian and Mediterranean varieties suited to the heat.

Botrytised Semillon

The region’s most prestigious wine is sweet Semillon made from grapes affected by noble rot (Botrytis cinerea). Autumn mists and humidity around the canals and rice fields encourage the fungus, which concentrates the grapes’ sugars and acidity. The best-known example is De Bortoli’s Noble One, regarded as a pioneer of this style in Australia. These wines show aromas of apricot, honey, candied citrus and spice, with sweetness balanced by lively acidity, and are among the country’s most recognised sweet wines.
