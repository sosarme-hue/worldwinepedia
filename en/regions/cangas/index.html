Protected Designation of Origin (PDO)

Cangas

Cangas is the protected designation of origin for the wines of south-western Asturias, around Cangas del Narcea. Its mountain vineyards, on very steep slate slopes, preserve native varieties such as Albarín Blanco, Albarín Negro, Carrasquín and Verdejo Negro.

History

Asturias is not usually associated with wine, but the south-west of the region has an ancient winemaking tradition. Its development is linked to the Benedictine monastery of Corias, in Cangas del Narcea, founded in the eleventh century, whose monks promoted vine growing in the Narcea valley. For centuries the area's wine supplied much of Asturias. The vine diseases of the nineteenth century, phylloxera, emigration and rural abandonment drastically reduced the vineyard during the twentieth century, leaving only a very small area. At the end of that century a group of growers and wineries set about its revival. The area was first recognised as a quality wine and later, in the 2010s, as a protected designation of origin.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises Cangas del Narcea and several neighbouring municipalities in south-western Asturias, in the valleys of the river Narcea and its tributaries. It is mountainous country where vines are grown on very steep slopes, often on terraces, roughly between 400 and 1,000 metres above sea level, which means almost all the work must be done by hand and makes this a form of heroic viticulture. The mountains shield the valleys from the direct influence of the sea, so that the climate, though Atlantic in essence, has continental traits: cold winters, mild, dry summers and rainfall concentrated in autumn and winter. The vines are preferably planted on the slopes with the best exposure to the sun.

Soils

Slate and quartzite soils predominate, poor, acidic, shallow and well drained, forcing the roots to seek water deep down.

Grape varieties

The designation preserves a valuable heritage of native varieties. Among the whites, Albarín Blanco stands out, together with Albillo. The leading reds are Albarín Negro, Carrasquín and Verdejo Negro, as well as Mencía, widespread in neighbouring Bierzo, which accounts for a significant part of the vineyard.

Styles

The designation produces whites from Albarín, fresh, with lively acidity and aromas of white fruit and herbs, and reds, often blends of local varieties, moderate in alcohol, with marked acidity and aromas of red fruit and spice, in young and aged versions. Rosés are also made. Production is very small and in the hands of a few wineries, which have committed to reviving the district's traditional varieties and its landscape of mountain vineyards.
