Casablanca Valley

The Casablanca Valley, between Santiago and Valparaíso, was the first major cool coastal area in Chilean viticulture. It is known for its Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.

History

Until the early 1980s Casablanca was a farming and cattle area with no significant winegrowing tradition. In 1982 the winemaker Pablo Morandé planted the first commercial vineyards there, convinced that the cool climate was ideal for white varieties. The success of this venture soon attracted many wineries, and in the 1990s Casablanca became the symbol of a new Chilean viticulture oriented towards the coast. Its example later encouraged plantings in other coastal valleys, such as San Antonio and Leyda.

Geography and climate

The valley lies in the Aconcagua Region, halfway between the capital and the port of Valparaíso, about 30 kilometres from the ocean at its nearest point. Its climate is shaped by the cold Humboldt Current: morning fogs and sea breezes keep summer temperatures moderate, and ripening is slow and prolonged. The western part, closest to the sea, is the coolest, while the eastern sector is somewhat warmer. Spring frosts are a major hazard, and the scarcity of irrigation water is one of the area’s main challenges.

Soils

The soils derive largely from the granite of the Coastal Range: granitic sands and clays predominate, well drained on the slopes and deeper on the valley floor.

Grapes and styles

Sauvignon Blanc is the best-known variety and gives wines with lively acidity and notes of citrus, fresh grass and tropical fruit. Chardonnay offers styles ranging from fresh and fruity to barrel-fermented and barrel-aged. Pinot Noir has gained importance, with light- to medium-bodied red-fruited wines, and in the somewhat warmer sectors cool-climate Syrah with spicy, peppery notes is also grown. The valley also produces base wines for sparkling wine.

Wine tourism

Its location on the road linking Santiago with the coast has encouraged intensive development of wine tourism, with many wineries offering visits, tastings and restaurants.

Regulations and labelling

Casablanca is a subregion of the Aconcagua Region within the Chilean system of designations of origin. As in the rest of the country, a label naming the valley requires the great majority of the grapes to come from it. Many of its wineries also make wines from grapes grown in other valleys, so it is worth checking the exact designation on the label. In recent years several producers have turned to organic viticulture and to plots on poorer slopes to obtain more concentrated wines.
