Designation of Origin (DO)

Méntrida

Méntrida is a designation of origin in the north of the province of Toledo, in Castilla-La Mancha, close to the border with the Community of Madrid. Traditionally Garnacha country, it has gained recognition for reds from this variety grown in the foothills of the Sierra de Gredos.

History

The Méntrida district, lying between the plain of Toledo and the first ranges of the Central System, has a long winemaking tradition. Thanks to their proximity to Madrid, its villages supplied the capital with wine for centuries, above all robust, high-alcohol Garnacha reds and claretes. For much of the twentieth century production, in the hands of cooperatives, went mostly to bulk wine, sometimes for blending with wines from other areas. The designation of origin was recognised in the second half of the twentieth century, but its real transformation came at the beginning of the twenty-first, when a group of wineries and growers began to recover the old Garnacha vineyards of the westernmost zone, bordering the Sierra de Gredos, and to make wines in a fresher style.

Territory and climate

The designation occupies the north of the province of Toledo, between the river Tagus and the borders with Madrid and Ávila, and includes towns such as Méntrida, Camarena, Fuensalida and Almorox, among others. To the east and south plains and gentle hills at about 400-600 metres predominate, while to the west, in the valley of the river Alberche and on the lower slopes of the Sierra de Gredos, the terrain becomes more rugged and the vineyards rise higher. The climate is continental, with cold winters, hot, dry summers and low rainfall; in the mountain area nights are cooler and rain somewhat more plentiful.

Soils

In the flat part sandy and clayey soils predominate, brown or reddish in colour. In the west there are soils of decomposed granite, sandy, acidic and poor, very well suited to Garnacha.

Grape varieties

The traditional and majority variety is Garnacha, often from old bush vines. It is accompanied by Tempranillo, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Petit Verdot. The whites, a minority, include Albillo, Macabeo, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc.

Styles

Méntrida mainly produces reds, from young, fruity wines to aged reds, often with Garnacha in the leading role or blended with Tempranillo and international varieties. The high-altitude Garnachas of the Gredos area are characterised by moderate colour, aromas of red fruit, flowers and wild herbs, and a lighter texture than the district's traditional reds. Garnacha rosés, with a long tradition in the area, and a few whites are also produced.
