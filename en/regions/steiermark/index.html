Styria (Steiermark)

A region in south-east Austria, on the Slovenian border, with very steep hills and a wetter climate than the rest of the country. It is famous for its Sauvignon Blanc, Gelber Muskateller and Schilcher rosé.

A landscape of hills

Styria is Austria’s southernmost wine region. Its vineyards are spread over very steep green hills, among forests, meadows and orchards, in a landscape sometimes compared to Tuscany. Part of the vineyard area lies literally on the border with Slovenia, along the so-called South Styrian Wine Road. The slopes, which in many places are far steeper than fifty per cent, require manual work and have given rise to winegrowing based on small family holdings. Since the late twentieth century a group of producers has given the region international prestige, above all for its dry whites.

Climate

The Styrian climate is often described as Illyrian: warm Mediterranean influence from the south combines with continental influence, and rainfall is higher than in eastern Austria. Warm days and cool nights favour aromatic wines with marked acidity. Hail and humidity are frequent risks.

The three DACs

The region is divided into three areas, each with its own DAC. Südsteiermark, the largest and best known, has very varied soils of sand, marl, limestone and slate, including opok, a very characteristic compact calcareous marl. Vulkanland Steiermark, to the east, is shaped by ancient volcanoes, with basalt and volcanic tuff soils in places such as Klöch, Kapfenstein and Straden; it is known for aromatic Traminer. Weststeiermark, the smallest, has gneiss and schist soils and is the home of Schilcher, a rosé with very lively acidity made from the Blauer Wildbacher variety. The Styrian DACs follow an origin-pyramid model, with regional, village and single-vineyard wines.

Grapes and styles

Sauvignon Blanc is the flagship variety: it gives intensely aromatic wines, from herbaceous and green pepper notes to ripe fruit, citrus and smoky hints, with texture and ageing potential that can be remarkable in the single-vineyard wines. Gelber Muskateller produces dry, light and very aromatic whites. Welschriesling gives fresh, simple whites that are very popular locally. Chardonnay, traditionally called Morillon here, Weissburgunder (Pinot Blanc) and Grauburgunder (Pinot Gris) give broader wines, sometimes aged in wood. Reds play a minor role, with some Zweigelt, and most of the region’s reputation rests firmly on its whites, which are increasingly sought after by restaurants and wine lovers far beyond Austria.

Country
Austria
Climate
Illyrian, with Mediterranean and continental influences, wetter than the rest of Austria and with cool nights.
Soils
Sand, marl (opok), limestone and slate in the south, volcanic soils in the east and gneiss and schist in the west.
Styles
Aromatic dry Sauvignon Blanc and Muskateller whites, broader Morillon whites and Schilcher rosé.
Grapes
Sauvignon Blanc, Muscat, Graševina (Welschriesling), Chardonnay, Pinot Blanc, Pinot Gris, Gewürztraminer, Zweigelt
