Wine region

Abruzzo

Abruzzo, between the Apennines and the Adriatic Sea, is the home of Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, one of Italy's most widespread reds. It also produces the rosé Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo and whites from Trebbiano, Pecorino and Passerina.

History

Vines have been grown in Abruzzo since antiquity, and for centuries the region maintained a peasant-based viticulture. In the twentieth century cooperative wineries spread, and they still make a large share of production today, as did the pergola abruzzese training system, a high horizontal trellis. The Montepulciano d'Abruzzo DOC was recognised in 1968, and in 2003 the area of the Teramo hills obtained the Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Colline Teramane DOCG. In 2010 the rosé Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo was given a DOC of its own. In recent decades, alongside the cooperatives, many family wineries have focused on more ambitious wines and on reviving local white varieties.

Territory and climate

Abruzzo is a very mountainous region, dominated by the Gran Sasso and Majella massifs. Most vineyards lie in the hills that descend from the mountains towards the Adriatic, in the provinces of Chieti, which has the largest area, Pescara and Teramo, while viticulture is more limited in the province of L'Aquila. The climate combines the Mediterranean influence of the sea, with warm summers, and that of the nearby mountains, which bring cool nights and temperature swings; inland the climate is more continental.

Soils

Clay and clay-limestone soils predominate in the hills, with sandier areas near the coast and alluvial soils in the river valleys.

Grapes and styles

Montepulciano is the main red grape and should not be confused with the Tuscan town of Montepulciano, where Vino Nobile is made from Sangiovese. It gives deeply coloured wines with black fruit and soft but plentiful tannins; Montepulciano d'Abruzzo must contain at least 85% of this variety, and there are young versions as well as longer-aged ones bearing the Riserva designation. The same grape is used for Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo, a deeply coloured rosé whose name refers to the cherry. Among the whites, Trebbiano d'Abruzzo is made from Trebbiano Abruzzese and other varieties of the Trebbiano family; in recent years Pecorino, Passerina and Cococciola have gained prominence.

Designations

The main ones are the Montepulciano d'Abruzzo DOC, with several subzones, the Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Colline Teramane DOCG, the Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo DOC, the Trebbiano d'Abruzzo DOC and the Abruzzo DOC, which covers wines from other varieties, including Pecorino and Passerina.
