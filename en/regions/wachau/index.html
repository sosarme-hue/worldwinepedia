Wachau

The Wachau is a narrow stretch of the Danube valley between Melk and Krems, with vineyards on dry-stone terraces over primary rock. It is Austria’s most famous region for dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling.

History

Winegrowing in the Wachau is very old, and its present landscape took shape mainly in the Middle Ages, when monasteries from Bavaria and Austria, such as Melk and Göttweig, owned vineyards in the area and encouraged the building of terraces supported by dry-stone walls. Besides the vine, the apricot is another traditional crop of the valley. In 2000 UNESCO inscribed the Wachau cultural landscape on the World Heritage List. In 1983 a group of growers founded the Vinea Wachau association, which laid down its own quality rules, such as exclusively hand harvesting and a ban on chaptalisation, and created three categories for its dry wines. Since the 2020 vintage the Wachau has also been a DAC, with regional, village and single-vineyard (Ried) levels.

Territory and climate

The region stretches for some thirty kilometres on both sides of the Danube, with villages such as Spitz, Wösendorf, Joching, Weissenkirchen, Dürnstein and Loiben on the left bank, and Mautern on the right. The climate results from the meeting of two influences: warm air from the Pannonian plain, arriving from the east, and cold air that flows down at night from the forests of the Waldviertel to the north. This alternation of warm days and cool nights favours wines with clear-cut aromas and good acidity. Rainfall is low, and some vineyards are irrigated in dry years.

Soils

The highest terraces sit on primary rock, above all gneiss, together with amphibolite and other metamorphic rocks, with poor, shallow soils. Lower down the slopes and near the river there is loess and sandy deposits. Vineyards such as Achleiten, Kellerberg, Loibenberg, Singerriedel, Steinertal and Klaus are among the best known.

Grapes and styles

Grüner Veltliner is the most widely planted variety, while Riesling occupies the stoniest and steepest sites. Vinea Wachau classifies its dry wines in three categories: Steinfeder, light wines with a maximum of 11.5% alcohol; Federspiel, between 11.5% and 12.5%; and Smaragd, with at least 12.5%, from the ripest grapes. The name Smaragd comes from an emerald-green lizard that lives on the terraces. The best Wachau wines combine concentration, freshness and a remarkable capacity to age.
