Friuli Colli Orientali

Friuli Colli Orientali, formerly Colli Orientali del Friuli, is a hillside DOC in the province of Udine, along the Slovenian border, known for its whites, native reds such as Refosco and Schioppettino, and sweet Picolit.

Location

The appellation covers the strip of hills that runs through the eastern part of the province of Udine, from the Tarcento area in the north to Corno di Rosazzo and Manzano in the south, by way of Cividale del Friuli, Faedis, Prepotto and Buttrio. To the east it borders Slovenia and to the south Collio, with which it shares many features. The DOC was recognised in 1970 under the name Colli Orientali del Friuli, which was later reversed to become Friuli Colli Orientali. The abbey of Rosazzo, of medieval origin, is one of the historic sites of winegrowing in the area.

Climate and soils

The Julian Alps and the Pre-Alps shelter the hills from cold northern winds, while the nearby Adriatic softens temperatures. Rainfall is abundant and the difference between day and night temperatures is marked, which favours freshness and aroma. As in Collio, the dominant soil is ponca, a flysch of marine marls and sandstones that breaks down easily and means that the steepest slopes must be farmed on terraces.

Grape varieties

This is one of the most varied appellations in Italy. Among the whites, Friulano, Ribolla Gialla, Verduzzo Friulano and Picolit stand out, along with international varieties such as Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio, Pinot Bianco and Chardonnay. Among the reds, Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso is the most widespread of the native grapes, and the area has revived other local varieties that had almost disappeared, such as Schioppettino, Pignolo and Tazzelenghe. Merlot and the Cabernets are grown as well.

Appellations and styles

Within the territory of the DOC there are three DOCGs. The Colli Orientali del Friuli Picolit DOCG covers a sweet wine made from Picolit, a variety with low yields owing to a natural flowering defect, whose grapes are usually left to dry. The Ramandolo DOCG, around Nimis, is for a sweet wine made from Verduzzo Friulano. The Rosazzo DOCG covers a blended white based on Friulano. Besides these wines, the DOC produces dry whites that are aromatic and well structured, varietal or blended, and reds that range from the fresh, spicy Schioppettino to the more tannic and long-lived wines made from Pignolo and Refosco. Taken together, they make this one of the most diverse corners of Italian winegrowing.

Country
Italy
Climate
Temperate and fairly rainy, sheltered by the Alps and softened by the Adriatic, with marked day-night swings.
Soils
Ponca: flysch of marine marls and sandstones, often terraced.
Styles
Aromatic dry whites, native reds from Refosco, Schioppettino and Pignolo, and sweet Picolit and Verduzzo.
Grapes
Friulano, Rebula (Ribolla Gialla), Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Gris, Pinot Blanc, Chardonnay, Merlot, Cabernet Franc
