Wine area with the Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG and the Montefalco DOC

Montefalco

Montefalco, in central Umbria, is the home of Sagrantino, a red grape with very marked tannins that gives long-lived dry reds and a traditional sweet wine from dried grapes.

Location

Montefalco is a small walled town set high on a hill in the province of Perugia, in central Umbria, overlooking the Topino and Clitunno valleys. Because of its panoramic position it is known as the balcony of Umbria. The production area covers the municipality of Montefalco and parts of some neighbouring municipalities, such as Bevagna, Gualdo Cattaneo, Castel Ritaldi and Giano dell'Umbria.

Sagrantino

Sagrantino is a red variety almost entirely confined to this area. Its origin has not been established, and its name is often linked to the sacramental use of wine, although this explanation has not been proven. For centuries it was used mainly to make a sweet wine from dried grapes, drunk at celebrations and religious feasts. By the middle of the twentieth century the variety had dwindled considerably, and it was revived by a few local producers who also began making it as a dry wine. It is a thick-skinned grape, very rich in polyphenols, which gives deeply coloured wines with especially powerful tannins.

Appellations

The Montefalco DOC was recognised in 1979, and Sagrantino gained DOCG status in 1992 under the name Montefalco Sagrantino. This DOCG includes two types: the dry wine, made entirely from Sagrantino and subject to long compulsory ageing before release, and Passito, the traditional sweet wine, made from grapes left to dry after the harvest. The Montefalco DOC covers Montefalco Rosso, a blend based on Sangiovese with a share of Sagrantino, more approachable when young, and Montefalco Bianco, based on local white grapes such as Grechetto and Trebbiano Spoletino. The latter is a local biotype of the Trebbiano family and is also made as a varietal wine under other Umbrian appellations.

Climate, soils and style

The climate is moderately continental, with warm summers and cool winters, and the hills enjoy good exposure to the sun. The soils are mainly clay and limestone, with sandier or stonier patches. Dry Montefalco Sagrantino is a red of great structure, with aromas of blackberry, plum, spice and earthy notes, and tannins that need time in bottle to soften. The Passito combines sweetness and tannin, an unusual pairing that sets it apart from other Italian sweet wines.
