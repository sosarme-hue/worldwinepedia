Bairrada

Bairrada, between Aveiro and Coimbra, is the home of the Baga grape, which gives tannic, long-lived reds, and one of Portugal’s main sparkling wine areas.

History

Winegrowing in Bairrada is old, and its wines were already traded in the Middle Ages. In the eighteenth century, under the government of the Marquis of Pombal, part of its vineyards were ordered to be uprooted, a measure associated with protecting Port from being blended with wines from other areas. In the late nineteenth century the region began making traditional-method sparkling wines, an activity that has become one of its hallmarks. Bairrada was recognised as a demarcated region in 1979. In recent decades producers such as Luís Pato helped to give Baga reds international standing.

Territory and climate

The region lies in central coastal Portugal, between the cities of Aveiro and Coimbra, in gently rolling country bounded to the east by the Buçaco and Caramulo ranges and open to the west towards the Atlantic, only a few kilometres away. The climate is maritime and mild, with rainy winters and moderate summers. Humidity and autumn rains can make it hard for late-ripening varieties to ripen fully, and vintage quality varies considerably from year to year. Anadia is one of its main wine centres.

Soils

The region’s name is linked to barro, clay. Clay-limestone soils predominate, and on them Baga produces its most structured wines, alongside areas of lighter sandy soils.

Grapes and styles

Baga is the flagship variety: thick-skinned, late-ripening, high in acidity and with firm tannins, it gives reds that are austere in their youth and can gain complexity for many years in bottle. Alongside it, Touriga Nacional and other red grapes are grown, as well as international varieties. The leading whites are Maria Gomes, the local name for Fernão Pires, Bical, Arinto and Cercial, widely used for sparkling wines. The region produces reds, whites, rosés and a significant amount of sparkling wine, some of them made from Baga itself. The term Bairrada Clássico is reserved for wines made from the traditional varieties. Its reds and sparkling wines are the traditional partners for leitão, the roast suckling pig typical of the area.

The region still has old Baga vineyards, and some producers have revived traditional methods, such as fermentation in lagares or ageing in large casks, to balance the grape’s tannins. The sparkling wines are made with a second fermentation in bottle and are mostly white and rosé, along with some reds.
