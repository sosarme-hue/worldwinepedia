Châteauneuf-du-Pape

Châteauneuf-du-Pape, near Avignon, is the most famous appellation of the Southern Rhône. Its reds, based mainly on Grenache, may blend up to thirteen varieties, and its rules of 1923 served as a basis for the French appellation system.

History

The appellation's name recalls the popes' residence in Avignon during the fourteenth century. John XXII had a castle built here as a summer residence; its ruins still overlook the village today, much of them destroyed during the Second World War. The presence of the papal court encouraged the development of vineyards in the area. After phylloxera, and amid widespread fraud, local growers led by Baron Pierre Le Roy de Boiseaumarié adopted in 1923 a set of rules that delimited the production area and fixed the permitted varieties, minimum alcohol and other practices. These rules served as a model for French appellation law, and Châteauneuf-du-Pape was recognised as an AOC in 1936, among the first in the country.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, warm and dry, with hot summers and abundant sunshine. The mistral, which frequently blows from the north, quickly dries the grapes after rain and limits disease, although it can also damage the vines' growth; for this reason many vines are bush-trained. The soils are varied. The best known are the galets roulés, large quartzite pebbles deposited by the ancient Rhône, which cover the La Crau plateau in particular and store the heat of the day. But there are also sandy soils, soils of sandstone and compacted sand known locally as safre, and limestone, especially in the west of the appellation; each influences the style of the wines.

Grapes

The rules permit thirteen varieties, or eighteen if their colour variants are counted separately, among them Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre, Cinsault, Counoise, Vaccarèse, Muscardin, Terret Noir, Picpoul, Clairette, Bourboulenc, Roussanne and Picardan. Grenache is by far the most widely planted and the basis of most reds, often accompanied by Syrah and Mourvèdre, which add colour, structure and freshness. Some producers make wines almost entirely from Grenache, while others use many of the permitted varieties.

Styles

The vast majority of production is red; the appellation also permits whites, made mainly from White Grenache, Clairette, Roussanne and Bourboulenc, but not rosés. The reds are warm, generous in alcohol and broad in texture, with aromas of ripe red and black fruit, spice and the herbs of the Mediterranean scrubland known as garrigue; the best age well for many years. Many bottles bear the embossed papal coat of arms, with the tiara and the keys of St Peter.

Surroundings

Nearby lie other Southern Rhône appellations, such as Gigondas and Vacqueyras on the left bank, and Lirac and Tavel, the latter devoted exclusively to rosé, on the right bank.

Country
France
Climate
Mediterranean, warm and dry, with abundant sunshine and frequent mistral winds.
Soils
Quartzite pebbles (galets roulés) over red clay, sand, safre and limestone.
Styles
Grenache-led red blends, warm and spicy; whites in smaller quantities; rosé is not permitted.
Grapes
Grenache, Syrah (Shiraz), Monastrell (Mourvèdre), Cinsault, Grenache Blanc
