Saint-Julien

Saint-Julien, between Margaux and Pauillac, is a compact Haut-Médoc appellation in which classified growths occupy much of the vineyard. Its reds are prized for their balance between power and elegance.

Location

The appellation covers the commune of Saint-Julien-Beychevelle, on the left bank of the Gironde, and a few plots in neighbouring villages. It lies between Pauillac to the north and Margaux to the south, although it does not border the latter directly. It is one of the smallest and most homogeneous communal appellations in the Médoc: a territory of gravel mounds overlooking the estuary, with few areas not covered by vines.

History and classification

Saint-Julien has no premier cru, but it does have a very high proportion of growths classified in 1855 relative to its size. The second growths include the three Léovilles, which arose from the division of a large estate in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries: Château Léoville Las Cases, Château Léoville Poyferré and Château Léoville Barton; to these can be added Château Ducru-Beaucaillou and Château Gruaud Larose. The third growths include Château Lagrange and Château Langoa Barton, and the fourth growths Château Beychevelle, Château Talbot, Château Branaire-Ducru and Château Saint-Pierre. The Barton family, of Irish origin, has owned Léoville Barton and Langoa Barton since the nineteenth century. The name Beychevelle is usually linked to the custom of ships lowering their sails as they passed the estate.

Climate and soils

The climate is temperate oceanic, moderated by the estuary. The soils are river-borne gravels mixed with sand and rounded pebbles, which in the best plots are very deep and lie over clay or marl. The vineyards nearest the river benefit from a slightly milder microclimate and excellent drainage.

Grapes and style

As in the other communal appellations of the Médoc, only red wines are made. Cabernet Sauvignon forms the backbone of the blends, completed by Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot and occasionally Malbec. Saint-Julien wines are often described as a midpoint between the power of Pauillac and the perfume of Margaux: wines of black fruit, cedar and spice, with firm but polished tannins and a very balanced structure. The uniformity of the soils and the weight of the great estates explain a consistency of quality that is considered typical of the appellation.

Ageing and cellaring

The wines are aged in French oak barrels for extended periods, and most estates make a second wine. The best Saint-Juliens have great ageing potential and usually need at least a decade to reach their peak.
