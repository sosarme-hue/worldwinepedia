Tokaj

A region in north-eastern Hungary, at the foot of the Zemplén hills, famous for centuries for Tokaji Aszú, a sweet wine made from grapes affected by noble rot. Today it also produces dry Furmint whites of great interest.

The Tokaj region, also called Tokaj-Hegyalja, covers the southern and eastern slopes of the Zemplén hills in north-eastern Hungary, down to the confluence of the Bodrog and Tisza rivers. It includes more than twenty villages, among them Tokaj, Tarcal, Mád, Tállya, Tolcsva, Erdőbénye, Sárospatak and Sátoraljaújhely. A small part of the historic zone now lies in Slovakia. In 2002 its cultural landscape was inscribed by UNESCO on the World Heritage List.

History

Aszú wines are documented at least from the sixteenth century and reached the height of their prestige in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, when they became a diplomatic gift and were served at courts across Europe. In 1737 a royal decree officially delimited the production zone, and during the eighteenth century the vineyards were classified by quality. After phylloxera, wars and the communist period, when production was concentrated in a large state company, the region opened up in the 1990s to Hungarian and foreign investors, and many wineries were reborn.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental, with warm summers and long, dry autumns. Humidity from the rivers and autumn morning mists, followed by sunny afternoons, encourage the development of Botrytis cinerea in its noble form, which concentrates sugars and acidity in the berries. Soils are of volcanic origin, with weathered andesites, rhyolites and tuffs, often covered with loess on lower ground. The traditional cellars, dug into the rock, are lined with a characteristic mould, Zasmidium cellare, which helps keep humidity stable.

Grapes and styles

Only a few white varieties are permitted: Furmint, the main one, Hárslevelű, Sárgamuskotály (Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains), Zéta, Kövérszőlő and Kabar. Tokaji Aszú is made by macerating botrytised berries, picked by hand, in must or base wine, and is then aged for a long time. Traditionally its sweetness was expressed in puttonyos, the number of baskets of aszú berries added to each cask; current regulations set a minimum equivalent to 5 puttonyos. Eszencia is the juice that runs from aszú berries under their own weight, extremely sweet and very slow to ferment. Szamorodni is made from whole bunches containing some botrytised grapes and may be sweet (édes) or dry (száraz). Late-harvest wines are also made and, increasingly, dry Furmint and Hárslevelű whites, often from specific vineyards.
