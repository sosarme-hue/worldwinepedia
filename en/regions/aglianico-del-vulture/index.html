Aglianico del Vulture

Aglianico del Vulture is the best-known wine of Basilicata. It is made from Aglianico on the slopes of Monte Vulture, an extinct volcano in the north of the region, and gives reds of great structure and acidity.

Monte Vulture

Vulture is an extinct volcano in northern Basilicata, in the province of Potenza, near the borders with Campania and Apulia. Its slopes, wooded in their upper reaches, descend towards an area of hills and plateaus where the vineyards are concentrated. Within it lie the Monticchio lakes, which fill ancient craters. The production area includes municipalities such as Rionero in Vulture, Barile, Rapolla, Ripacandida, Maschito, Melfi, Venosa and Acerenza, among others. Barile and Rionero are famous for their cellars dug into the volcanic tuff, which were used for centuries to make and store wine.

History

Winegrowing in the area has ancient roots; Venosa, birthplace of the Latin poet Horace, lies within the appellation. The variety's name has traditionally been linked to the word ellenico and to a Greek origin, although this etymology is disputed and has not been proven. The Aglianico del Vulture DOC was recognised in 1971, and in 2010 the Aglianico del Vulture Superiore DOCG was created, reserved for wines with stricter production and ageing rules.

Climate and soils

Although it lies in southern Italy, the area has a relatively cool climate thanks to the altitude of its vineyards, many of which are above four hundred metres. Winters are cold and the difference between day and night temperatures is marked, allowing slow ripening and a late harvest, often in October or November. The soils are of volcanic origin, with tuffs, ashes and lavas, mixed in some areas with clays and limestones. They are generally well drained and rich in minerals.

The grape and its styles

The wine is made exclusively from Aglianico, the great red grape of southern Italy, late-ripening, high in acidity and firm in tannin. The DOC provides for a red that can be released relatively early and for versions with longer ageing; the Superiore DOCG requires longer ageing, including a period in wood, and a Riserva version that is longer still. Aglianico del Vulture usually shows deep colour, aromas of black cherry, plum, violet, spice and earthy or smoky notes, with lively acidity and tannins that can be austere in youth. The best examples have remarkable ageing potential and develop notes of leather, tobacco and forest floor over the years, which has earned the wine comparisons with the great reds of northern Italy.
