Clare Valley

The Clare Valley, in the northern Mount Lofty Ranges of South Australia, is one of the world’s great references for dry Riesling. It also produces well-structured Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon.

History

The Clare Valley lies about 120 kilometres north of Adelaide. It was settled in the 1840s, partly by British, Irish and Silesian immigrants, and in 1851 the Jesuits founded a winery at Sevenhill to make sacramental wine; it is still operating and is the oldest in the region. For a long time Clare produced mainly robust reds and fortified wines, but Riesling, introduced by settlers of German origin, gradually took on an ever more important role and, in the second half of the twentieth century, became the valley’s hallmark.

In 2000 a group of Clare producers decided to bottle their Rieslings together under screwcap to avoid the problems of cork taint and to preserve the freshness of their wines. The initiative had a great impact and contributed to the widespread adoption of this closure in Australia and New Zealand.

Climate and soils

Despite its latitude, the valley has a relatively cool continental climate thanks to its altitude, generally between 300 and 500 metres, and a wide temperature range between day and night. Summers are warm and dry, but cool nights help preserve the grapes’ natural acidity. It is not a single valley but a series of small valleys and hills running north to south. Soils vary greatly: around Watervale, terra rossa and red loams over limestone predominate, while in Polish Hill River poor soils over slate and shale are common. Other recognised sub-areas are Auburn, Sevenhill and Clare.

Styles

Clare Riesling is dry, with vibrant acidity and aromas of lime, lemon and white flowers. With years in bottle it develops toasty and honeyed notes. It is traditionally made in stainless steel tanks, without oak or malolactic fermentation, to preserve the purity of the fruit. Watervale wines are often described as more floral and open, and those of Polish Hill River as tighter and more mineral. Among the reds, Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon give firm wines with dark fruit, minty notes and good ageing potential, and Malbec, Grenache and Semillon are also grown. Many producers make several Rieslings from different sub-areas to show these differences, and the best can develop favourably in bottle for a decade or more.
