Chianti Classico

Chianti Classico is the historic heart of Chianti, between Florence and Siena, and its wine is made from at least 80% Sangiovese. Its symbol is the black rooster, and it has been a separate DOCG since 1996.

History

In the Middle Ages the Chianti area, between Florence and Siena, was organised into the League of Chianti, whose emblem was a black rooster. In 1716 the edict of Grand Duke Cosimo III de' Medici delimited the production area of Chianti wine, which corresponds largely to today's classic zone. In the nineteenth century Bettino Ricasoli, at the castle of Brolio, proposed a formula based on Sangiovese with additions of Canaiolo and Malvasia. As the wine became successful, the Chianti name spread to a much larger territory, and in 1924 producers in the historic zone founded a consortium to protect it. Chianti Classico gained DOCG status in 1984, still as part of Chianti, and in 1996 it became a separate DOCG.

Territory and climate

The designation covers the whole of the municipalities of Castellina in Chianti, Gaiole in Chianti, Greve in Chianti and Radda in Chianti, and part of Barberino Tavarnelle, Castelnuovo Berardenga, Poggibonsi and San Casciano in Val di Pesa. It is a landscape of hills covered with woodland, olive groves and vineyards, with altitudes exceeding 600 metres in places. The climate is continental, with cool winters, warm summers and a marked difference between day and night temperatures, especially in the higher areas.

Soils

The most characteristic soils are galestro, a schistous clay marl that crumbles easily, and alberese, a compact limestone. Some areas also have sandstone, known as macigno, as well as clays and sands.

Rules and mentions

Chianti Classico must contain at least 80% Sangiovese; the remainder may come from other authorised red grapes, whether native, such as Canaiolo and Colorino, or international, such as Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot. White grapes have not been permitted since the 2006 vintage. There are three tiers: Annata; Riserva, with at least 24 months of ageing; and Gran Selezione, introduced in 2014, which requires grapes from the winery's own vineyards and at least 30 months of ageing. In 2021 eleven Unità Geografiche Aggiuntive (UGA) were approved, allowing areas such as Panzano, Radda and Gaiole to be named on Gran Selezione labels.

Style

The wines usually show a ruby colour, aromas of cherry, violet and herbs, lively acidity and fine-grained tannins. Longer-aged versions gain complexity, with spicy and earthy notes.
