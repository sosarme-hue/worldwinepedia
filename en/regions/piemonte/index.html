Piedmont

Piedmont, at the foot of the Alps in north-west Italy, is the home of Nebbiolo and of two of Italy's most prestigious reds, Barolo and Barbaresco. It also produces Barbera, Dolcetto, whites such as Gavi and Roero Arneis, and the aromatic Asti and Moscato d'Asti.

History

The name Piedmont means «at the foot of the mountains» and refers to the Alps that surround the region to the north and west. Viticulture has a long tradition here, and in the nineteenth century, under the House of Savoy, the great Nebbiolo reds of the Langhe gained prestige at the court in Turin. It was in this period that Barolo became established as a dry wine for ageing, and at the end of the century a school of oenology was founded in Alba, which helped to train the area's winemakers. In 2014 UNESCO added the vineyard landscapes of Langhe-Roero and Monferrato to the World Heritage List.

Climate and territory

The climate is continental, with cold winters, warm summers and autumns in which fog is common; the shelter of the Alps reduces rainfall. Most vineyards lie on hills, mainly in the provinces of Cuneo, Asti and Alessandria. The main areas are the Langhe, south of the Tanaro river, home to Barolo and Barbaresco; Roero, on the left bank of the Tanaro, with sandier soils; and Monferrato, a broad area of hills between Asti and Alessandria. Further north, in the hills of Alto Piemonte, Nebbiolo yields wines such as Gattinara and Ghemme.

Soils

Calcareous and clay marls of marine origin predominate, alternating with layers of sandstone, especially in the Langhe. Sands are abundant in Roero, while in the north of the region there are more acidic soils of glacial and volcanic origin.

Grapes and styles

Late-ripening Nebbiolo gives reds that are relatively pale in colour, with pronounced acidity and tannins and great ageing potential. Barbera, the region's most widely planted red, offers wines with good acidity and soft tannins, under designations such as Barbera d'Asti and Nizza. Dolcetto gives lighter reds intended for drinking young, such as Dogliani. Leading whites include Moscato Bianco, the basis of sparkling Asti and of lightly fizzy Moscato d'Asti; Cortese, for Gavi; and Arneis, from Roero. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are used for the traditional-method sparkling wine Alta Langa. Other local varieties include Brachetto, Grignolino, Freisa and Ruchè.

Designations

Piedmont has no IGT of its own, and all its wine with a geographical indication is covered by a DOC or DOCG. Its many DOCGs include Barolo, Barbaresco, Asti, Barbera d'Asti, Gavi, Roero, Gattinara, Ghemme and Dogliani, while broad DOCs such as Piemonte, Langhe and Monferrato cover wines from a range of varieties.

Italy
Climate

Calcareous and clay marls with sandstone in the Langhe, sands in Roero and glacial and volcanic soils in the north.

Age-worthy Nebbiolo reds, Barbera and Dolcetto reds, dry whites, sparkling wines and aromatic Moscato.
