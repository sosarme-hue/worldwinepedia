Designation of Origin (DO)

Yecla

Yecla is a designation of origin in the north of the Region of Murcia that is limited to a single municipality, that of the town of Yecla. Its dominant variety is Monastrell, which gives warm, fruity reds from a continental Mediterranean climate.

History

Remains found in the municipality of Yecla attest to vine growing since Antiquity, and viticulture has been a central activity of the town for centuries. At the end of the nineteenth century, when phylloxera devastated the French vineyards, demand for Spanish wines drove the expansion of Yecla's vineyards, and the arrival of the railway made trade easier. For much of the twentieth century production was geared to bulk wines, deep in colour and high in alcohol, and a large cooperative handled a significant share of the grapes. The designation of origin was created in the second half of the twentieth century. From the end of that century several family wineries committed to bottling and exporting, which today absorbs a very large share of their wines.

Territory and climate

Yecla is the only designation of origin in Spain made up of a single municipality. It lies on the high plateau of north-eastern Murcia, on the border with the provinces of Albacete and Alicante, and its vineyards are generally found between 400 and 800 metres above sea level. Two zones are distinguished: Campo Arriba, higher and rich in old vines, and Campo Abajo, at lower altitude. The climate is Mediterranean with marked continental traits: hot, dry summers, cold winters, very low rainfall and abundant sunshine, with cool nights that temper the summer heat.

Soils

The soils are mainly limestone, deep and permeable, poor in organic matter, with sandy and stony areas. The dry climate and the low fertility of the soil result in low yields.

Grape varieties

The leading role belongs to Monastrell, which occupies most of the vineyard and is often grown as unirrigated bush vines, including a significant number of old vines. It is accompanied by Garnacha Tintorera, Garnacha, Tempranillo, Syrah, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot. The whites, a minority, include Macabeo, Airén, Merseguera, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and Verdejo.

Styles

The designation mainly produces reds from Monastrell, on its own or blended, deep in colour, with ripe black fruit, body and rounded tannins. There are young versions and barrel-aged ones, as well as old-vine reds from Campo Arriba. Rosés, some whites and sweet Monastrell wines are also made. Most of the production is exported, and Yecla's wines have gained an international presence thanks to their good balance between character and price.
