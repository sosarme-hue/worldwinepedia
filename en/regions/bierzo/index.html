Designation of Origin (DO)

Bierzo

Bierzo is a designation of origin in the north-west of the province of León, recognised in 1989. It is the home of Mencía reds and Godello whites, often from old hillside vineyards.

History

Bierzo was an important area in Roman times, above all for gold mining, as shown by Las Médulas, now a World Heritage Site. Viticulture was consolidated in the Middle Ages by monasteries, among them the Cistercian monastery of Santa María de Carracedo, and by the Way of St James, which crosses the area through Ponferrada and Villafranca del Bierzo. The Designation of Origin was recognised in 1989. In the late 1990s the arrival of new producers interested in old hillside Mencía vineyards brought the region considerable international attention.

Territory and climate

Bierzo is a basin surrounded by mountains and watered by the Sil river and its tributaries, on the border between Castilla y León and Galicia. It is divided into Bierzo Bajo, the flat central part, and Bierzo Alto, which is more mountainous. The surrounding ranges partly shelter it from cold winds and Atlantic rain, creating a climate that is transitional between the Atlantic humidity of Galicia and the continental dryness of the Meseta, with relatively mild temperatures and moderate rainfall.

Soils

Slate and quartzite soils, poor and well drained, predominate on the slopes, while deeper clay and alluvial soils are common on the valley floor. Hillside vineyards tend to give more concentrated wines with greater tension.

Grapes and styles

Mencía is the dominant variety, giving reds with bright colour, red and black fruit aromas, floral and mineral notes and fresh acidity. Garnacha Tintorera is also grown. Among the whites, Godello has gained importance for its well-structured whites, alongside Doña Blanca and Palomino, which was very widespread in the past. Producers make young and oak-aged reds, rosés and whites, both unoaked and barrel-aged. Mencía, which is also grown in Galicia and in central Portugal, where it is known as Jaen, has one of its most traditional homes in Bierzo.

Origin-based classification

Recent regulations have introduced a classification based on geographical origin in Bierzo, inspired by the Burgundian model. In addition to the designation's generic wines, it recognises village wines, from a single municipality; paraje wines, from a specific named site; and the categories of classified vineyard and grand classified vineyard, reserved for plots with exceptional characteristics and age. This classification coexists with the traditional ageing terms.
