Designation of Origin (DO)

Getariako Txakolina

Getariako Txakolina is the designation of origin for the txakoli of Gipuzkoa, on the Basque coast, and was the first devoted to this wine. It mainly produces light, fresh whites with a slight spritz from the Hondarrabi Zuri grape.

History

Txakoli is the traditional wine of the Atlantic Basque Country. For centuries it was made in small quantities on family farms, known as caseríos, for home and local consumption, and its cultivation declined sharply during the twentieth century. On the coast of Gipuzkoa, around Getaria, Zarautz and Aia, a livelier tradition survived, and it was there that the first designation of origin devoted to txakoli was established; it was later extended to other municipalities in the province. The creation of the designation spurred the modernisation of wineries and improvements in quality. There are two other txakoli designations in the Basque Country: Bizkaiko Txakolina, in Bizkaia, and Arabako Txakolina, in Álava.

Territory and climate

The most characteristic vineyards lie on slopes facing the Bay of Biscay around Getaria and Zarautz, although the designation also covers areas further inland in Gipuzkoa. The climate is Atlantic, with mild temperatures, gentle winters, cool summers and abundant rainfall spread throughout the year. The high humidity calls for constant vigilance against vine diseases, and ripening is slow, so the grapes retain high acidity and moderate alcohol levels.

Viticulture and soils

To improve air circulation around the bunches, vines have traditionally been trained on high pergolas, known as parral, raised above the ground, although trellising now predominates in modern plantings. The vineyards occupy slopes close to the sea, with soils of varied texture and generally good water-holding capacity.

Grapes and styles

The main variety is the white Hondarrabi Zuri, which makes up the great majority of the vineyard area. The red Hondarrabi Beltza is used for rosés and reds, and to add a particular nuance to some whites. The classic style is a young white with a pale colour, citrus, herbal and green apple aromas, marked acidity, moderate alcohol and a slight spritz from fermentation. Rosés, a few reds, lees-aged wines, sparkling wines and, in small quantities, late-harvest wines are also made.

Serving

According to local custom, txakoli is poured from a height into a wide glass, which enhances its freshness and slight effervescence. It is usually served well chilled and in small measures, alongside fish, shellfish and the pintxos typical of Basque cuisine.
