Pla de Bages

Pla de Bages is a designation of origin in inland Catalonia, in the Bages district around Manresa. It is known for the native white grape Picapoll and for the stone vats built beside the vineyards, a testimony to its former importance as a wine area.

History

Vine growing in Bages has been documented since the Middle Ages, and the Benedictine monastery of Sant Benet de Bages was one of the centres that promoted it. Between the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the district went through a great expansion of viticulture: vineyards came to cover much of the territory and wine was traded towards Barcelona and other markets. From that period date the tines, stone vats built in the middle of the vineyards so that the grapes could be fermented without being carried away, and the dry-stone huts that sheltered the growers; many still survive and make up a unique heritage. The arrival of phylloxera at the end of the nineteenth century ruined the vineyards and led to their abandonment across much of the district. The revival began at the end of the twentieth century and culminated in the creation of the designation of origin in the 1990s.

Territory and climate

The designation lies in the centre of Catalonia, in a depression surrounded by high ground, including the mountain of Montserrat, and crossed by the rivers Llobregat and Cardener. The vineyards are found roughly between 200 and 500 metres above sea level. The climate is Mediterranean with a marked continental influence, as the mountains cut the area off from the sea: winters are cold, with frequent frost and fog, summers are hot and rainfall is moderate.

Soils

The soils are clay-loam or sandy-loam, generally calcareous and well drained, developed over marl, sandstone and conglomerate.

Grape varieties

The emblematic variety is white Picapoll, a local grape that gives fresh wines with notable acidity and aromas of white fruit, herbs and flowers. Other whites such as Macabeo and Chardonnay are also grown. The leading reds are Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Tempranillo, Garnacha and Syrah, along with recovered traditional varieties such as Sumoll and Mandó.

Styles

The most characteristic wines are Picapoll whites, young or with some lees or barrel ageing. Rosés and reds are also made, often from international varieties or blends, aged or unaged, as well as some wines from recovered local varieties. Several wineries have restored old tines and use them again to make wine, in an effort to link the district's heritage with its present-day production. Wine tourism, tied to this dry-stone landscape, has gained ground in recent years.
