Savennières

Savennières, on the right bank of the Loire south-west of Angers, is a small appellation for Chenin Blanc whites, mostly dry, known for their intensity, tension and long ageing.

Location

Savennières lies in Anjou, on the right bank of the Loire, a few kilometres south-west of Angers. The appellation covers the commune of Savennières and parts of neighbouring Bouchemaine and La Possonnière. It is a small area of hills and small valleys running perpendicular to the river, where the vineyards are spread over slopes facing mainly south and south-east, among woods and meadows. Opposite it, on the other bank of the Loire, begin the sweet-wine vineyards of the Layon.

Appellations

Within the area there are two separate appellations, each linked to a specific site. Savennières Roche aux Moines covers a group of plots on steep slopes, with several owners. Savennières Coulée de Serrant is a vineyard of a few hectares owned by a single family, the Joly family, who have farmed it since the mid-twentieth century. Nicolas Joly became one of the leading advocates of biodynamic farming in the wine world, which helped make the appellation known outside France.

Climate and soils

The climate is temperate, with an oceanic influence and moderate rainfall. The river moderates temperatures and favours a long ripening period. Unlike Touraine, where limestone predominates, Savennières belongs to the so-called Anjou noir, on the edge of the Armorican massif. Its soils are shallow and stony, made up of schist, sandstone and rocks of volcanic origin such as rhyolite, sometimes covered with wind-blown sand. These poor soils are considered to contribute to the concentration and tension of the wines.

Grape and style

The only variety is Chenin Blanc. Although the rules allow wines with some residual sugar, the great majority of Savennières are dry. They are whites of great intensity, with marked acidity, a firm texture and aromas of quince, apple, citrus, honey, flowers and notes often described as mineral or stony. In their youth they can be austere and closed, and producers often recommend waiting several years before drinking them.

Ageing

The best Savennières have great ageing capacity and can develop for decades, acquiring notes of wax, dried fruit and honey. Some producers age them in tanks or old large casks, while others use barrels to give the wine more breadth.
