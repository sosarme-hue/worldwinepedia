Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG)

Greco di Tufo

Greco di Tufo is a white-wine DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), made from the Greco variety around the village of Tufo. It gives whites of good structure and acidity, also in a sparkling version.

Location

The appellation includes a small group of municipalities in the north of the province of Avellino, in Irpinia: Tufo, which gives it its name, and neighbouring places such as Altavilla Irpina, Chianche, Montefusco, Petruro Irpino, Prata di Principato Ultra, Santa Paolina and Torrioni. It is the smallest of Irpinia's three DOCGs, alongside Taurasi and Fiano di Avellino. The vineyards occupy hills on both sides of the Sabato river valley, often at considerable altitudes.

History

The name of the variety, Greco, has traditionally been linked to a supposed Greek origin connected with the colonisation of Magna Graecia in southern Italy; however, this origin has not been proven, and the name Greco has historically been applied to various southern varieties. The name of the village of Tufo refers to tuff, a volcanic rock abundant in the area. The territory also had sulphur mines, worked until the twentieth century, whose memory forms part of local identity. The DOC was recognised in 1970 and the DOCG in 2003.

Climate and soils

Like the rest of Irpinia, the area has a cooler climate than its latitude might suggest, with cold winters, warm but not excessive summers and a marked difference between day and night temperatures. The soils are varied, with clays, limestones, tuffs of volcanic origin and, in some sectors, sulphur-rich seams. Part of the mineral character perceived in the wines is often attributed to these soils.

The grape and the wines

The wine must be made with at least eighty-five per cent Greco, which may be completed with Coda di Volpe. Greco is a fairly thick-skinned grape with compact bunches, which often have two lobes or wings. It gives whites of deep straw colour, with aromas of peach, apricot, pear, citrus, almond and notes described as mineral, with high acidity and notable structure, sometimes with a slight bitterness on the finish. Unlike the more aromatic Fiano, Greco usually stands out for its body and tension. The DOCG also provides for a Riserva version and a sparkling wine made by the traditional method of bottle fermentation. The best wines improve for a few years in bottle, developing notes of honey and nuts, and they rank among the most characterful whites of southern Italy.
