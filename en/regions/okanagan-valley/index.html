Okanagan Valley

The Okanagan Valley, in the interior of British Columbia, is western Canada’s leading wine region. It stretches along a chain of lakes, from a cool north to a hot, semi-desert south, and produces everything from Riesling and Pinot Noir to Merlot and Syrah.

History

The first vineyards in the Okanagan are attributed to Oblate missionaries who settled near present-day Kelowna in the mid-nineteenth century. For much of the twentieth century the region, better known for its apple and other fruit orchards, produced wines from American and hybrid varieties, often sweet or fortified. There were trials with European varieties, but the decisive change came at the end of the 1980s when, following the free trade agreement with the United States, much of the hybrid vineyard was uprooted and replanted with Vitis vinifera. Since then the number of wineries has grown considerably, from family estates to large companies such as Mission Hill, and the region has also become a wine tourism destination.

Climate

The valley, of glacial origin, runs north to south along Okanagan Lake and smaller lakes such as Skaha and Osoyoos, as far as the United States border. The coastal mountain ranges shelter it from Pacific moisture, so the climate is dry, with hot summers, many hours of sunshine and a marked diurnal temperature range that helps to preserve acidity. The high latitude brings very long summer days. The differences between the cooler north, around Kelowna, and the hot, almost desert-like south, around Oliver and Osoyoos, are considerable. The lakes moderate temperatures, but winters can be very cold and damage the vines, and irrigation is used because of the scarce rainfall.

Soils

The soils come from glacial, river and lake deposits. In the south deep, well-drained sands and gravels dominate, as on the Black Sage Bench terraces, while other areas have siltier and more clayey soils, gravel deposits and rock outcrops. In the north, more fertile, finer-textured soils are common.

Sub-regions

Within the Okanagan Valley geographical indication several sub-regions have been recognised, among them Golden Mile Bench, near Oliver, the first to be defined; Naramata Bench, on the eastern shore of Okanagan Lake; Okanagan Falls and Skaha Bench. Other well-known areas without official status are Black Sage Road and the surroundings of Osoyoos, at the southern tip.

Styles

The range of climates allows a wide spectrum of styles. In the north and in cooler sites, aromatic whites from Riesling, Gewürztraminer and Pinot Gris stand out, along with Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, as well as sparkling wines. In the warmer south, reds are made from Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Syrah, often in Bordeaux-style blends. Icewine is also produced, though in smaller quantities than in Ontario.
