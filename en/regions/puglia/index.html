Wine region

Puglia

Puglia, the «heel» of the Italian boot, is one of Italy's largest wine-producing regions. It is known for reds from Primitivo, Negroamaro and Nero di Troia and for a long tradition of rosé.

History

Viticulture in Puglia dates back to antiquity, encouraged by contacts with the Greek world across the Adriatic and Ionian seas. For much of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the region was above all a major producer of deeply coloured, high-alcohol bulk wine, shipped to northern Italy and to other countries to strengthen lighter wines. From the 1990s many wineries turned to bottled wine and to promoting their native varieties. Puglia also has a long tradition of rosé, made mainly from Negroamaro in Salento.

Territory and climate

The region is long and narrow and mostly flat or gently hilly. In the north lies the Tavoliere plain; in the centre, the limestone plateau of the Murge, home to Castel del Monte and Gioia del Colle; and in the south, the Salento peninsula, between the Adriatic and the Ionian. The climate is Mediterranean, hot and dry, with very warm summers and abundant sunshine; breezes from the two seas temper temperatures somewhat. The traditional training system is the low bush vine, the alberello pugliese, although trellising is now also widely used.

Soils

Limestone-based soils predominate, often covered with iron-rich red clays, known as terra rossa, especially in Salento and the Murge. The plains have deeper clay and alluvial soils.

Grapes and styles

Early-ripening Primitivo gives full-bodied, high-alcohol reds with ripe fruit, especially in Manduria and Gioia del Colle. DNA analysis has shown it to be the same variety as California's Zinfandel, both originating in Croatia, where it is known as Crljenak Kaštelanski or Tribidrag. Negroamaro, typical of Salento, gives reds and rosés with spicy, slightly bitter notes, often blended with Malvasia Nera. In the north, Nero di Troia stands out as the basis of the reds of Castel del Monte. Whites include Bombino Bianco, Verdeca and Fiano Minutolo, which is distinct from the Fiano of Campania.

Designations

The main designations include the Primitivo di Manduria DOC and the Primitivo di Manduria Dolce Naturale DOCG, the Gioia del Colle DOC, the Salice Salentino DOC, the Castel del Monte DOC and the Castel del Monte Rosso Riserva, Castel del Monte Nero di Troia Riserva and Castel del Monte Bombino Nero DOCGs. A large share of production falls under the Puglia IGT and the Salento IGT.
