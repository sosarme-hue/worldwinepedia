Designation of Origin (DO)

Monterrei

Monterrei is the southernmost designation of origin in Galicia and the one with the most continental climate, located in the Támega valley around Verín, next to the Portuguese border. It produces mainly Godello and Treixadura whites and Mencía reds.

History

The Monterrei area has a long winemaking tradition, linked to the castle and fortress of Monterrei, which overlooks the valley from a hill near Verín, and to the religious orders that held land in the area. Its position on a historic route between Galicia, Castile and Portugal encouraged trade in its wines. As elsewhere in Galicia, the vineyards suffered from phylloxera and emigration during the twentieth century, and in the last decades of the century the designation of origin was established, encouraging the modernisation of wineries and the recovery of native varieties.

Territory and subzones

The designation extends across the valley of the Támega river and its slopes, in the south-east of the province of Ourense, in municipalities such as Verín, Monterrei, Oímbra and Castrelo do Val. There are two subzones: Val de Monterrei, on the valley floor, with deeper soils, and Ladeira de Monterrei, on the surrounding slopes.

Climate

Monterrei has the most continental climate of the Galician designations. The surrounding mountains largely isolate it from Atlantic influence, so summers are warm and dry, winters cold and rainfall lower than in the rest of Galicia. Spring frosts are a risk and the difference between day and night temperatures is marked, which helps preserve acidity and aroma.

Soils

Sedimentary, clayey and more fertile soils predominate on the valley floor, while poorer slate and granite soils with better drainage are found on the slopes.

Grapes and styles

Among the whites, Godello and Treixadura stand out, together with Doña Blanca, Albariño, Loureira and other permitted varieties. Mencía is the main red, accompanied by Merenzao, Arauxa, the local name for Tempranillo, Sousón and Caíño. Whites, especially those from Godello, are the most widespread style and combine ripe fruit with freshness. Mencía reds are usually fruity and medium-bodied. Producers make young wines as well as others aged on lees or in barrel.

Landscape

The Támega valley, which opens towards Portugal, has a landscape of hillside vineyards interspersed with crops and woodland, dominated by the silhouette of the Monterrei fortress. The Verín area is also known for its mineral water springs. The nearby town of Chaves, just across the border in Portugal, is a reminder of the close historical ties between the two sides of the frontier, which share the same valley and a very similar farming culture.
