Conca de Barberà

Conca de Barberà is a Catalan designation of origin in the interior of the province of Tarragona, around Montblanc and the monastery of Poblet. It is known for its native Trepat grape, used for rosés and light reds, and for its white grapes, a large share of which go into cava.

History

Conca de Barberà is a district with a long winemaking tradition, closely linked to the Cistercian monastery of Poblet, founded in the twelfth century, whose monks grew vines for centuries. The walled town of Montblanc is the district capital. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, after the phylloxera crisis, growers organised themselves into cooperatives, and several of these built modernista-style buildings, popularly known as wine cathedrals, some of them designed by the architect Cèsar Martinell. For much of the twentieth century production went to bulk wine and, above all, to supplying white grapes and base wine to the large cava houses of Penedès. The designation of origin was created in the 1980s.

Territory and climate

The district is a basin, as its name indicates (conca in Catalan), surrounded by ranges such as the Prades and Miramar mountains and crossed by the river Francolí and its tributaries. The vineyards lie roughly between 350 and 600 metres above sea level. The climate is Mediterranean with a continental influence: the mountains soften the effect of the sea, so that winters are colder and nights cooler than on the coast, which favours acidity in the grapes.

Soils

Calcareous brown soils predominate, with varying proportions of clay and sand and, in some areas, plenty of stones on the surface. They are low in organic matter and well drained.

Grape varieties

The most emblematic variety is Trepat, a thin-skinned, lightly coloured native red grape grown almost only in Conca de Barberà and neighbouring areas. Among the whites, which account for a significant part of the vineyard, Macabeo and Parellada stand out, the latter finding particularly favourable conditions here thanks to the cool climate, together with Chardonnay. The reds grown also include Tempranillo (Ull de Llebre), Garnacha, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.

Styles

The most characteristic wines are the Trepat rosés, fresh and aromatic, and light reds from the same grape, with red fruit, spicy notes and soft tannins. Whites from Parellada and Macabeo are also made, as well as reds from local and international varieties, aged or unaged, and part of the district is included in the cava production area. At the monastery of Poblet and in its surroundings lie some of the district's best-known vineyards, which helped to bring prestige to its reds.
