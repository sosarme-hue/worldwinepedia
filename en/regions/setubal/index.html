Setúbal DOC, Palmela DOC and Península de Setúbal GI

Setúbal

The Setúbal peninsula, south of Lisbon, is the home of Moscatel de Setúbal, a sweet fortified wine with long skin maceration, and also produces Castelão reds under the Palmela DOC.

History

Winegrowing on the Setúbal peninsula is old, and the area’s Muscat wine was already known and exported in past centuries. In the nineteenth century the firm of José Maria da Fonseca, based in Azeitão, played an important part in developing and spreading both Moscatel and Castelão reds, notably with the Periquita brand. Setúbal was among the first areas in Portugal to be demarcated for its fortified wine, and today it includes two DOCs, Setúbal and Palmela, as well as the Península de Setúbal geographical indication, under which a large part of its output is sold.

Territory and climate

The peninsula lies south of the Tagus estuary, across from Lisbon. Its relief is dominated by the Arrábida range, a chain of limestone hills rising beside the sea that shelters the vineyards at its foot. To the east lie broad sandy plains around Palmela and Pegões, where much of the vineyard is concentrated. The climate is Mediterranean with Atlantic influence, with warm, dry summers and mild winters; the nearness of the ocean and the estuary moderates temperatures.

Soils

Clay-limestone soils predominate on the slopes of the Arrábida, while the inland plains have plenty of sandy, poor, well-drained soils that are especially suited to Castelão.

Grapes and styles

Moscatel de Setúbal is made mainly from Muscat of Alexandria. Fermentation is stopped with grape spirit, and the wine then stays in contact with the skins for several months, which gives it intense aromas of grape, orange blossom and candied fruit. It is then aged in cask, and over the years it develops notes of dried fruit, honey and caramel; it is sold at different ages. Moscatel Roxo, made from a very scarce pink-skinned variety, gives a rarer version of this style. The Palmela DOC covers mainly reds from Castelão, with red fruit and soft tannins, as well as whites. Under the Península de Setúbal GI, reds and whites are made from local and international varieties such as Syrah, Touriga Nacional, Arinto and Fernão Pires.

Moscatel de Setúbal is traditionally served chilled as a dessert wine or after a meal, and goes well with egg-based sweets, nuts and cheeses. The oldest versions, dark amber in colour, are often enjoyed on their own, in small measures, at the end of a meal.
