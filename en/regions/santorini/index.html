Santorini

A volcanic island in the Cyclades whose very old vineyards, largely ungrafted, produce dry Assyrtiko whites of high acidity and saltiness, as well as Vinsanto, a sweet wine made from sun-dried grapes.

Santorini, in the south of the Cyclades archipelago, is what remains of an ancient volcano whose great Bronze Age eruption caused the centre of the island to collapse, forming the present caldera. That eruption covered the land with ash, pumice and fragments of lava, the soil on which the vines grow today. The island has a long wine tradition: for centuries it exported sweet wines across the eastern Mediterranean and as far as Russia, where Santorini wine was used as altar wine.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean and very dry, with little rain, hot summers and strong, persistent winds. Moisture arriving from the sea as night-time mist, absorbed by the porous soils, helps the vines withstand drought. The volcanic soils, poor in organic matter and almost free of clay, did not allow phylloxera to take hold, so many vines are still planted on their own roots, and some have very old root systems that are renewed by layering.

Viticulture

To protect the grapes from wind and sand, vines are traditionally trained into a basket shape called kouloura: the canes are woven into a spiral close to the ground and the bunches ripen inside. Yields are very low. Pressure from building and tourism has reduced the vineyard area in recent decades.

Grapes and styles

The dominant variety is Assyrtiko, which in this warm environment keeps very high acidity. The Santorini PDO covers dry white wines made from a minimum of 75% Assyrtiko, which may be completed with the local varieties Athiri and Aidani, as well as sweet wines. The dry whites are marked by tension, citrus and mineral notes and a salty sensation; some are aged in barrel or on lees and age well in bottle. Nykteri is a traditional style of dry wine with oak ageing, whose name recalls that the grapes were harvested and pressed at night to avoid the heat. Vinsanto is made from grapes, mostly Assyrtiko, left to dry in the sun after harvest; it gives a sweet, concentrated, acidic wine that is aged for a long time in barrel. Red varieties such as Mandilaria and Mavrotragano are also grown on the island and bottled outside the PDO as geographical indication wines.
