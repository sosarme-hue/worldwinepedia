Sauternes

Sauternes, south of Graves, is the appellation for the great sweet wines of Bordeaux, made from grapes affected by noble rot. Its best-known wine, Château d'Yquem, was the only one classified as premier cru supérieur in 1855.

Territory

The Sauternes appellation covers five municipalities on the left bank of the Garonne, some forty kilometres south-east of the city of Bordeaux: Sauternes, Bommes, Fargues, Preignac and Barsac. Wines from Barsac may be sold under that commune's own appellation or as Sauternes. On the opposite bank of the Garonne lie other sweet-wine appellations, such as Loupiac, Cadillac and Sainte-Croix-du-Mont, and next to Barsac is Cérons.

Climate and noble rot

What makes Sauternes unique is its microclimate. The small Ciron river, whose waters are cool because it flows through the Landes forest, joins the warmer Garonne here. In autumn this contrast encourages morning mists which, followed by sunny, dry afternoons, create ideal conditions for Botrytis cinerea to develop in its beneficial form, known as noble rot. The fungus pierces the grape skin, so that the berry loses water and concentrates its sugars, acids and aromas, while also acquiring characteristic notes.

The process does not occur evenly, nor with the same intensity every year. The harvest is therefore carried out by hand in several successive passes through the vineyard, known as tries, picking only the bunches or berries in the right condition. Yields are very low, and in some poor vintages certain estates decide not to make their main wine at all.

Soils

The soils alternate gravel, sand and clay over limestone or clay subsoils; in Barsac the land is flatter and limestone lies close to the surface beneath a thin layer of reddish sand and clay.

The 1855 classification

As well as the reds, the 1855 classification included the sweet white wines of Sauternes and Barsac. Château d'Yquem was given the unique rank of premier cru supérieur, and the other estates were divided between premiers crus and deuxièmes crus. The first growths include, for example, Château Climens and Château Coutet in Barsac, and Château Suduiraut and Château Rieussec.

Grapes and styles

Sémillon is the main variety, thanks to its thin skin and its susceptibility to botrytis; it is joined by Sauvignon Blanc, which adds freshness, and a small proportion of Muscadelle. The wines, usually fermented and aged in barrel, are sweet and unctuous, balanced by acidity, with aromas of apricot, candied citrus, honey and spice. They can age for decades. Dry whites made in the area are sold under the Bordeaux appellation.
