Santa Barbara

Santa Barbara County, at the southern end of California’s Central Coast, owes its coolness to east-west valleys that funnel in Pacific fog. It is a benchmark for Californian Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

History

Spanish missionaries grew vines in the Santa Barbara area from the late eighteenth century, but the county’s modern viticulture is recent. In the late 1960s and early 1970s the first large commercial vineyards were planted, among them Sanford & Benedict, in 1971, west of Buellton, and shortly afterwards Bien Nacido, in the Santa Maria Valley, which supplies numerous wineries. In the 1980s producers such as Au Bon Climat demonstrated the area’s potential for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. The film Sideways, shot in the region and released in 2004, greatly raised its profile with the general public.

Climate and soils

Santa Barbara’s distinguishing feature is geographical: unlike most of California’s Coast Ranges, which run north to south, here the mountains and valleys run east to west. This layout opens corridors through which Pacific fog and wind penetrate deep inland. As a result, the western areas, closest to the sea, are among the coolest in California, while the climate becomes progressively warmer towards the east. Soils are varied: sandy and silty in the Santa Maria Valley, with diatomaceous earth and calcareous soils in the Sta. Rita Hills, and more clay-rich or stony inland.

Sub-regions

The county includes several AVAs. Santa Maria Valley, to the north, is one of the oldest in California and stands out for cool-climate Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Syrah. Santa Ynez Valley encompasses several sub-zones: Sta. Rita Hills, at the western end and very cool, whose name was abbreviated after an agreement with the Chilean winery Viña Santa Rita; Ballard Canyon, associated above all with Syrah; Los Olivos District; and Happy Canyon of Santa Barbara, the warmest, known for its Bordeaux varieties.

Styles

In the cool western areas Pinot Noir and Chardonnay dominate, with lively acidity and styles ranging from the lightest to the most concentrated. Syrah and other Rhône varieties hold a prominent place in the centre of the county, and Cabernet Sauvignon and Sauvignon Blanc are grown mainly in the warmer eastern areas. Because the county is so varied, labels often name a specific AVA or even an individual vineyard rather than the county alone.
