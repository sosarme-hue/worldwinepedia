Eden Valley

The Eden Valley occupies the eastern hills of the Barossa, at a higher altitude than the valley floor. Its cooler climate gives Riesling of great finesse and elegant Shiraz, including some of Australia’s most famous reds.

History

The Eden Valley lies in the Barossa Ranges, east of the Barossa Valley, with which it forms the Barossa wine zone. In 1847 Joseph Gilbert, an English settler, planted vines at Pewsey Vale, one of the first vineyards in the area. Soon afterwards families of Silesian and Prussian origin arrived and settled around places such as Eden Valley, Springton, Keyneton and Flaxman Valley. Among them was the Henschke family, whose winery at Keyneton has become one of the most prestigious in the country thanks to wines such as Hill of Grace and Mount Edelstone, from very old Shiraz vineyards.

For much of the twentieth century the region was known mainly as a source of Riesling for the large Barossa companies. In the 1960s emblematic vineyards were planted in the higher areas, such as the Steingarten vineyard, on a stony slope near the boundary with the Barossa, which showed the potential of Riesling at the highest elevations.

Climate and soils

The vineyards generally lie between 400 and 600 metres above sea level, in a landscape of rolling hills with pasture, eucalyptus woodland and rocky outcrops. The climate is clearly cooler than on the floor of the Barossa Valley, with cold nights, more rain and a longer ripening cycle, which favours acidity and aromatic finesse. The High Eden subregion includes some of the highest vineyards. Soils are poor and stony, formed from weathered metamorphic and granitic rocks, with sands, gravels, quartz and clays at depth, and tend to limit the vigour of the vine.

Styles

Riesling is the emblematic white: dry, delicate, with aromas of lime, orange blossom and white flowers and firm acidity that allows it to age for many years and develop toasty notes. It is often compared with Clare Riesling, though it is frequently described as somewhat more floral and fine. Shiraz gives medium-bodied reds, spicier and fresher than those of the Barossa, with red and black fruit and fine tannins. Chardonnay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Viognier are also grown, the last of these having been revived in Australia precisely in this area. Many wines carry the Eden Valley name, but the region’s grapes are also used in blends labelled Barossa.
