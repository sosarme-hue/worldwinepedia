Designation of Origin (DO)

Almansa

Almansa is a designation of origin in the south-east of the province of Albacete, in Castilla-La Mancha, on the border with the Valencian Community and the Region of Murcia. Its most characteristic grape is Garnacha Tintorera, one of the few red varieties with coloured flesh, together with Monastrell.

History

Almansa is a historic town, known for its castle and for the battle fought nearby in 1707, during the War of the Spanish Succession. Its district, lying in a natural corridor between the central plateau and the Levante, has grown vines for centuries. During the twentieth century production was largely geared towards deeply coloured bulk wines, much prized for blending with wines from other areas, and made mainly by cooperatives. The designation of origin is one of the oldest in Castilla-La Mancha, although its development as an area for quality bottled wines is relatively recent and linked to a small number of wineries.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises the town of Almansa and several nearby municipalities in the south-east of the province of Albacete. It is a transitional area between the Castilian plateau and the Mediterranean lands, with plains surrounded by mountain ranges and vineyards generally lying at around 700 metres or higher. The climate is continental with Mediterranean influence: winters are cold, summers hot and dry, and rainfall scant. The altitude brings cool nights during ripening, which helps the grapes retain acidity and colour.

Soils

Light-coloured limestone soils, poor in organic matter, predominate, with areas of clay and sand and, in some places, plenty of stones on the surface. These well-drained soils limit the vigour of the vines.

Grape varieties

The most emblematic variety is Garnacha Tintorera (Alicante Bouschet), a teinturier grape, that is, one with red flesh and juice, which gives very deeply coloured, full-bodied wines with black fruit. Almansa is one of the areas of Spain where it is most widely planted. It is accompanied by Monastrell, Tempranillo (Cencibel), Syrah, Garnacha, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Petit Verdot. The whites, a minority, include Verdejo, Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Macabeo and Moscatel.

Styles

The designation mainly produces reds, young and aged, often blends in which Garnacha Tintorera contributes colour and structure and Monastrell ripe fruit and warmth. Some wineries also make varietal Garnacha Tintorera reds from old vines. Rosés and a few whites are also produced. Although most of the district's wine is still sold in bulk, the wineries that bottle their wine have revealed the potential of this variety, which is unusual elsewhere in Spain.
