Regions & appellations

Travel the world of wine country by country: history, climate, soils, grapes, appellations and wineries of every producing country.

Abruzzo

Wine region · Abruzzo, between the Apennines and the Adriatic Sea, is the home of Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, one of Italy's most widespread reds. It also produces the rosé Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo and whites from Trebbiano, Pecorino and Passerina.

Aconcagua Valley

Designation of Origin (region and subregion) · The Aconcagua Valley, north of Santiago, takes its name from the Aconcagua River, which flows down from the Andes not far from the highest mountain in the Americas. It combines a warm inland sector with a red wine tradition and recent cool-climate coastal vineyards.

Adelaide Hills

Geographical Indication (GI) · A high-altitude region in the Mount Lofty Ranges, just east of Adelaide, in South Australia. Its cool climate has made it an Australian benchmark for Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and sparkling wine.

Aglianico del Vulture

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC); Aglianico del Vulture Superiore is a DOCG · Aglianico del Vulture is the best-known wine of Basilicata. It is made from Aglianico on the slopes of Monte Vulture, an extinct volcano in the north of the region, and gives reds of great structure and acidity.

Ahr

Anbaugebiet (specified wine region) · A small valley south of Bonn, one of Germany’s northernmost wine regions and, paradoxically, one of its great red-wine areas. Its Spätburgunder grows on very steep slate slopes.

Alella

Designation of Origin (DO) · Alella is a small Catalan designation of origin in the Maresme district, very close to Barcelona. Its vineyards, planted on granitic sands known as sauló, mainly produce whites from Pansa Blanca, the local name for Xarel·lo.

Alentejo

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) and Alentejano GI · The Alentejo, the great warm plain of southern Portugal, produces mainly ripe, fruity reds and broad whites, and keeps alive the ancient tradition of fermenting wine in clay amphorae.

Algarve

Vinho Regional Algarve (GI) and four DOCs: Lagos, Portimão, Lagoa and Tavira · The southernmost wine region of mainland Portugal, on the country’s south coast. After decades of decline, its small wineries now make ripe reds, rosés and fresh whites shaped by a sunny climate tempered by the sea.

Alicante

Designation of Origin (DO) · Alicante is a designation of origin in the Valencian Community divided into two subzones, Vinalopó and La Marina. It is known for its Monastrell reds, the Moscatels of the Marina Alta and Fondillón, a historic long-aged wine.

Almansa

Designation of Origin (DO) · Almansa is a designation of origin in the south-east of the province of Albacete, in Castilla-La Mancha, on the border with the Valencian Community and the Region of Murcia. Its most characteristic grape is Garnacha Tintorera, one of the few red varieties with coloured flesh, together with Monastrell.

Alsace

Wine region (AOC Alsace, Alsace Grand Cru and Crémant d'Alsace) · Alsace, between the Vosges and the Rhine, is a region of aromatic white wines which, unlike most of France, are identified by grape variety. Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat are its most prestigious grapes.

Anjou

Loire wine area and AOC (Anjou, Anjou Villages, Rosé d'Anjou, Cabernet d'Anjou and others) · Anjou, around the city of Angers, is one of the most varied areas of the Loire: it produces dry and sweet Chenin Blanc whites, Cabernet Franc reds and a large quantity of rosé, including Rosé d'Anjou and Cabernet d'Anjou.

Arabako Txakolina

Designation of Origin (DO) · Arabako Txakolina is the designation of origin for the txakoli of Álava, concentrated in the Ayala valley, in the north-west of the province. It is the smallest and most recent of the three txakoli designations and mainly produces fresh whites from Hondarrabi Zuri.

Arlanza

Designation of Origin (DO) · Arlanza is a designation of origin in Castilla y León that stretches along the valley of the river Arlanza, between the provinces of Burgos and Palencia. It is one of the coldest and highest wine areas of the northern plateau and mainly produces Tempranillo reds, along with traditional rosés and claretes.

Arribes

Designation of Origin (DO) · Arribes is a designation of origin recognised in 2007 in the Duero canyon, on the border between Spain and Portugal, in the provinces of Zamora and Salamanca. It is notable for the native Juan García grape and its terraced vineyards.

Asti

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (Asti / Moscato d'Asti DOCG) · The Asti DOCG, in south-eastern Piedmont, covers the sweet, aromatic wines made from Moscato Bianco: sparkling Asti and the delicate, gently fizzy, low-alcohol Moscato d'Asti.

Baden

Anbaugebiet (specified wine region) · Baden is Germany’s southernmost wine region, a long strip stretching from the Tauber valley to Lake Constance, largely facing Alsace. It is the German home of the Pinot varieties, with Spätburgunder as its main grape.

Bairrada

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · Bairrada, between Aveiro and Coimbra, is the home of the Baga grape, which gives tannic, long-lived reds, and one of Portugal’s main sparkling wine areas.

Bandol

AOC (Provence) · Bandol, on the Provençal coast west of Toulon, is the benchmark appellation for Mourvèdre. Its structured, long-lived reds are joined by well-structured rosés and some whites.

Banyuls

AOC (Banyuls and Banyuls Grand Cru, vin doux naturel), Roussillon · Banyuls, on the Côte Vermeille of Roussillon near the Spanish border, produces Grenache-based vins doux naturels, fortified during fermentation. Its vineyards on schist terraces run down to the Mediterranean.

Barbaresco

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Barbaresco is a Nebbiolo red from the Langhe, made in a small area north-east of Alba, beside the Tanaro river. It shares its grape and its prestige with Barolo, although its ageing requirements are somewhat shorter.

Bardolino

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC); Bardolino Superiore is a DOCG · Bardolino is an appellation on the eastern shore of Lake Garda, in the province of Verona, known for its light reds and its Chiaretto rosés made mainly from Corvina and Rondinella.

Barolo

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Barolo, made exclusively from Nebbiolo in the Langhe hills of Piedmont, is one of Italy's great age-worthy reds. It was one of the country's first DOCGs, recognised in 1980.

Barossa Valley

Geographical Indication (GI) · The Barossa Valley, north-east of Adelaide, is Australia’s most iconic region for Shiraz. It preserves very old own-rooted vines and a strong cultural heritage from settlers of German origin.

Beaujolais

Wine region (AOC Beaujolais, Beaujolais-Villages and ten crus) · Beaujolais, between the Mâconnais and Lyon, is the home of Gamay. Its wines range from Beaujolais Nouveau, released just weeks after the harvest, to the ten crus of the north, grown on granite soils.

Beira Interior

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · A mountain region in Portugal’s eastern interior, along the Spanish border and around the Serra da Estrela. Its high-altitude vineyards give fresh whites and lively reds that are still little known outside the country.

Bekaa Valley

Wine region · A high plateau lying between the Mount Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges, the Bekaa is home to most of Lebanon’s vineyards. Its altitude and dry summers produce reds with ripe fruit and good structure, as well as whites from local varieties.

Bierzo

Designation of Origin (DO) · Bierzo is a designation of origin in the north-west of the province of León, recognised in 1989. It is the home of Mencía reds and Godello whites, often from old hillside vineyards.

Binissalem

Designation of Origin (DO) · Binissalem, officially Binissalem-Mallorca, was the first designation of origin in the Balearic Islands. Located in the centre of Mallorca, at the foot of the Serra de Tramuntana, its emblem is the native red grape Manto Negro.

Bizkaiko Txakolina

Designation of Origin (DO) · Bizkaiko Txakolina is the designation of origin for the txakoli of Bizkaia, in the Basque Country. Its vineyards, spread across much of the province, mainly yield fresh, crisp whites from Hondarrabi Zuri, along with some reds and rosés.

Bolgheri

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · Bolgheri, on the Tuscan coast in the province of Livorno, is the birthplace of Sassicaia and the main centre for Italian reds made from Bordeaux varieties. Its DOC chiefly covers blends of Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc.

Bordeaux

Wine region (AOC Bordeaux and Bordeaux Supérieur) · Bordeaux, in the Gironde département, is one of the most influential wine regions in the world. It is famous for its blended reds based on Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, its dry whites and the sweet wines of Sauternes.

Brunello di Montalcino

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Brunello di Montalcino is a 100% Sangiovese red made in the municipality of Montalcino, in the province of Siena. It is known for its long mandatory ageing and its capacity to develop in bottle.

Bucelas

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · A small white-wine appellation just north of Lisbon, devoted above all to the Arinto grape. Its crisp dry whites enjoyed a reputation in England in the nineteenth century.

Bullas

Designation of Origin (DO) · Bullas is a designation of origin in the north-west of the Region of Murcia, in mountainous country of medium to high altitude. Its main variety is Monastrell, used for reds and rosés of Mediterranean character.

Burgenland

Wine-growing region (Weinbaugebiet), with several DACs · Burgenland, Austria’s easternmost state, borders Hungary and is shaped by the Pannonian climate and by Lake Neusiedl. It is Austria’s great region for red wines, above all from Blaufränkisch and Zweigelt, and for nobly sweet wines.

Burgundy

Wine region · Burgundy, in east-central France, is the home of Pinot Noir and Chardonnay expressed through precisely defined vineyard plots, the climats. Its hierarchy of appellations culminates in the grands crus of the Côte d'Or and Chablis.

Bío-Bío Valley

Designation of Origin (subregion of the Southern region) · A subregion in southern Chile, around the Biobío river, cooler and wetter than the central valleys. With a long tradition of País and Muscat, it now also stands out for aromatic whites and cool-climate Pinot Noir.

Cahors

AOC (South West France) · Cahors, in the Lot valley, is the historic home of Malbec, known here as Côt or Auxerrois. Its dark, tannic reds were known for centuries as “black wine”.

Calatayud

Designation of Origin (DO) · Calatayud is a designation of origin in the south-west of the province of Zaragoza, in the Iberian System, with some of the highest vineyards in Aragón. It is known for old-vine Grenache grown on poor slate and limestone soils.

Campania

Wine region · Campania, in southern Italy around Naples, was already one of the most celebrated wine areas in Roman times. Today it is known for Taurasi, a red from Aglianico, and for whites such as Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo.

Campo de Borja

Designation of Origin (DO) · Campo de Borja is a designation of origin in the province of Zaragoza, between the Ebro valley and the slopes of the Moncayo. It specialises in Grenache, with many old vineyards, and promotes itself as the Empire of Grenache.

Canelones

Wine department · The department of Canelones, to the north and east of Montevideo, holds most of Uruguay’s vineyards. On clay soils and under the influence of the Río de la Plata and the Atlantic, it is the heartland of Uruguayan Tannat and home to many historic family wineries.

Cangas

Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) · Cangas is the protected designation of origin for the wines of south-western Asturias, around Cangas del Narcea. Its mountain vineyards, on very steep slate slopes, preserve native varieties such as Albarín Blanco, Albarín Negro, Carrasquín and Verdejo Negro.

Cappadocia

Wine region · A region on the high plateau of Central Anatolia, with landscapes of volcanic tuff and an ancient wine tradition. It is the home of the white Emir grape and one of Turkey’s best-known wine areas.

Cariñena

Designation of Origin (DO) · Cariñena, south of Zaragoza, is one of Spain's oldest designations of origin and gives its name to the Cariñena grape, known in France as Carignan. Today its main variety is Grenache.

Carnuntum

DAC (Districtus Austriae Controllatus) · A small region east of Vienna, between the Danube and the Slovak border, named after an ancient Roman city. It is known for warm, fruity reds from Zweigelt and Blaufränkisch.

Casablanca Valley

Designation of Origin (subregion) · The Casablanca Valley, between Santiago and Valparaíso, was the first major cool coastal area in Chilean viticulture. It is known for its Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.

Catalunya

Designation of Origin (DO) · Catalunya is a region-wide designation of origin that covers most of the Catalan vineyard and allows wines to be made from grapes sourced in different districts. It is the designation of many high-volume wines from Catalonia's leading wineries, alongside more distinctive ones.

Cava

Designation of Origin (DO) · Cava is the designation of origin for Spanish sparkling wines made by the traditional method, with a second fermentation in bottle. Although production is concentrated in Penedès, around Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, its production area covers municipalities in several autonomous communities.

Central Otago

Geographical Indication (GI) · Central Otago, in the mountainous interior of the southern South Island, is one of the world’s most southerly wine regions and the only one in New Zealand with a continental climate. It is famous for its Pinot Noir.

Central Valley

Designation of Origin (wine region grouping Maipo, Rapel, Curicó and Maule) · Chile’s great wine region, a depression between the Andes and the Coastal Range stretching south of Santiago. It groups the Maipo, Rapel, Curicó and Maule valleys and holds most of the country’s vineyards.

Chablis

AOC (Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru and Chablis Grand Cru) · Chablis, in the Yonne département, is Burgundy's northernmost vineyard and is devoted exclusively to Chardonnay. Its whites, with lively acidity and a mineral character, are associated with Kimmeridgian marl soils.

Champagne

AOC (sparkling wine) · Champagne, north-east of Paris, is the region that made sparkling wines with a second fermentation in bottle famous around the world. Its wines are based on Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Meunier, grown over a chalk subsoil.

Chianti Classico

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Chianti Classico is the historic heart of Chianti, between Florence and Siena, and its wine is made from at least 80% Sangiovese. Its symbol is the black rooster, and it has been a separate DOCG since 1996.

Chinon

AOC (Touraine, Loire valley) · Chinon, in Touraine beside the river Vienne, is one of the great red-wine appellations of the Loire. Its Cabernet Franc wines range from light, fruity reds to structured, age-worthy versions, complemented by some rosés and Chenin whites.

Châteauneuf-du-Pape

AOC · Châteauneuf-du-Pape, near Avignon, is the most famous appellation of the Southern Rhône. Its reds, based mainly on Grenache, may blend up to thirteen varieties, and its rules of 1923 served as a basis for the French appellation system.

Cigales

Designation of Origin (DO) · Cigales is a designation of origin north of Valladolid, in the Pisuerga valley, recognised in 1991. Traditionally associated with rosés and claretes, it also produces Tempranillo reds.

Cirò

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · Cirò is the best-known appellation in Calabria. It lies on the Ionian coast of the province of Crotone and is famous above all for its reds and rosés from Gaglioppo, along with whites from Greco Bianco.

Clare Valley

Geographical Indication (GI) · The Clare Valley, in the northern Mount Lofty Ranges of South Australia, is one of the world’s great references for dry Riesling. It also produces well-structured Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon.

Codru

Protected geographical indication (PGI) · Codru is the wine region of central Moldova, around the capital, Chișinău. Its wooded hills, with a slightly cooler climate, are known for their whites and sparkling wines and are home to the vast underground cellars of Cricova and Mileștii Mici.

Colares

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · A tiny Atlantic appellation near Sintra, west of Lisbon, famous for its ungrafted vines planted in sand, which withstood phylloxera. Its Ramisco reds and Malvasia de Colares whites are a historic rarity.

Colchagua Valley

Designation of Origin (zone within the Rapel Valley) · The Colchagua Valley, in the southern part of the Rapel Valley, is one of Chile’s best-known red wine areas. Apalta, with its horseshoe-shaped slopes, is its most famous sector.

Collio

Controlled Designation of Origin (Collio Goriziano or Collio DOC) · Collio is a hilly area in the province of Gorizia, in Friuli-Venezia Giulia, along the Slovenian border, famous for white wines grown on marl and sandstone soils known as ponca.

Columbia Valley

AVA · A large AVA in Washington State, with a small part in Oregon, lying east of the Cascades in a semi-arid climate with sunny summers. It contains most of Washington’s vineyards.

Conca de Barberà

Designation of Origin (DO) · Conca de Barberà is a Catalan designation of origin in the interior of the province of Tarragona, around Montblanc and the monastery of Poblet. It is known for its native Trepat grape, used for rosés and light reds, and for its white grapes, a large share of which go into cava.

Condado de Huelva

Designation of Origin (DO) · Condado de Huelva is an Andalusian designation of origin in the south-east of the province of Huelva, near Doñana. Its native grape is Zalema, and the area produces young whites and fortified wines aged biologically and oxidatively, such as Condado Pálido and Condado Viejo.

Condrieu

AOC (Northern Rhône cru) · Condrieu, south of Côte-Rôtie, is the benchmark appellation for Viognier. Its very aromatic, unctuous whites come from terraced granite slopes above the Rhône.

Constantia

Wine of Origin (WO) · Constantia, on the Cape Peninsula south of Cape Town, is South Africa’s oldest wine area. It was famous in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries for its sweet wines and today also stands out for its cool-climate whites.

Coonawarra

Geographical Indication (GI) · Coonawarra, in the south-east of South Australia, is famous for a narrow strip of terra rossa over limestone. It is the Australian region most closely associated with Cabernet Sauvignon.

Cornas

AOC (Northern Rhône cru) · Cornas, opposite the city of Valence, is a small appellation on the right bank of the Rhône devoted exclusively to Syrah reds. Its dark, tannic wines come from very sunny granite slopes.

Corsica

Wine region (Patrimonio, Ajaccio, Vin de Corse / Corse with geographical names, and Muscat du Cap Corse AOCs) · Corsica, a mountainous Mediterranean island, grows its own varieties such as Niellucciu, Sciaccarellu and Vermentinu. Its best-known appellations are Patrimonio, in the north, and Ajaccio, in the west.

Costers del Segre

Designation of Origin (DO) · Costers del Segre is a designation of origin in inland Catalonia, mainly in the province of Lleida, made up of several scattered subzones along the Segre river and its tributaries. Its dry continental climate gives rise to a wide variety of reds, whites and rosés.

Cotnari

Controlled designation of origin (DOC) · Cotnari, in the hills of Iași county in Romanian Moldavia, is Romania’s historic appellation for sweet white wines. It is based on four local varieties: Grasă de Cotnari, Tămâioasă Românească, Fetească Albă and Frâncușă.

Curicó Valley

Designation of Origin (subregion of the Central Valley) · A subregion of Chile’s Central Valley, between Colchagua and Maule, watered by the Teno and Lontué rivers. It is one of the country’s most productive areas, with a wide diversity of white and red varieties.

Côte Chalonnaise

Burgundy subregion (regional AOC Bourgogne Côte Chalonnaise and village AOCs) · The Côte Chalonnaise extends the Côte de Beaune southwards, west of Chalon-sur-Saône. Its five village appellations, Bouzeron, Rully, Mercurey, Givry and Montagny, offer Burgundian-style Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Aligoté at more moderate prices.

Côte de Beaune

Burgundy subregion (Côte d'Or) · The Côte de Beaune is the southern half of the Côte d'Or, from Ladoix to Maranges. It produces some of the world's most highly regarded Chardonnay whites, such as Montrachet, Meursault and Corton-Charlemagne, as well as Pinot Noir reds such as Pommard and Volnay.

Côte de Nuits

Burgundy subregion (Côte d'Or) · The Côte de Nuits is the northern half of the Côte d'Or, between Dijon and Corgoloin. Devoted almost entirely to Pinot Noir, it is home to the great majority of Burgundy's red grands crus.

Côte-Rôtie

AOC (Northern Rhône cru) · Côte-Rôtie is the northernmost appellation of the Rhône valley, on dizzying slopes above the village of Ampuis. Its Syrah reds, sometimes co-fermented with a little Viognier, combine perfume, finesse and ageing potential.

Côtes de Bordeaux

AOC (with additional geographical names: Blaye, Cadillac, Castillon, Francs, Sainte-Foy) · Côtes de Bordeaux is an appellation that brings together several hilly areas on the right banks of the Garonne, the Dordogne and the Gironde. It produces mainly Merlot-dominated reds and, to a lesser extent, whites.

Côtes du Rhône

Regional AOC of the Rhône valley (Côtes du Rhône and Côtes du Rhône Villages) · Côtes du Rhône is the regional appellation of the Rhône valley and one of the largest in France. Most of its output comes from the south of the valley and consists of red blends based on Grenache, Syrah and Mourvèdre.

Dalmatia

Wine region · Dalmatia, the long coastal and island strip of southern Croatia, is the region of Plavac Mali reds and of a great variety of native grapes. Its vineyards, on karst slopes dropping to the Adriatic, include historic sites such as Dingač and Postup.

Dealu Mare

Controlled designation of origin (DOC) · Dealu Mare, “the big hill”, is a strip of south-facing slopes in the foothills of the Carpathians, north of Bucharest. It is Romania’s most renowned red-wine area, with Fetească Neagră, Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Pinot Noir.

Douro

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · The Douro valley, in northern Portugal, is the birthplace of Port and one of the oldest demarcated wine regions in the world. Under the Douro DOC it also produces still reds and whites of growing prestige.

Dão

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · The Dão is a granite plateau in north-central Portugal, ringed by mountains, producing fresh, elegant Touriga Nacional reds and Encruzado whites with ageing potential.

Eden Valley

Geographical Indication (GI) · The Eden Valley occupies the eastern hills of the Barossa, at a higher altitude than the valley floor. Its cooler climate gives Riesling of great finesse and elegant Shiraz, including some of Australia’s most famous reds.

Eger

Protected designation of origin (OEM) · A region in northern Hungary, on the slopes of the Bükk hills, best known for Egri Bikavér, the red blend called “bull’s blood”. It also produces the white blend Egri Csillag and varietal wines.

Elgin

Wine of Origin (WO), district of the Cape South Coast region · An elevated plateau south-east of Cape Town, traditionally devoted to apple orchards and now one of South Africa’s coolest wine areas. It stands out for cool-climate Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Syrah.

Empordà

Designation of Origin (DO) · Empordà is the designation of origin of north-eastern Catalonia, in the province of Girona, between the Pyrenees and the Mediterranean. Shaped by the tramontana wind, it produces Carignan and Grenache reds, whites and the traditional sweet wine Garnatxa de l'Empordà.

Entre-Deux-Mers

Bordeaux area and AOC (dry whites) · Entre-Deux-Mers is the broad area of hills lying between the Garonne and the Dordogne. Its own appellation is reserved for dry whites, while the area's reds are sold as Bordeaux or Bordeaux Supérieur.

Etna

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · The Etna DOC, on the slopes of the Sicilian volcano, produces Nerello Mascalese reds and Carricante whites from high-altitude vineyards on volcanic soils. Recognised in 1968, it was Sicily's first DOC.

Fiano di Avellino

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Fiano di Avellino is a white-wine DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), made from Fiano on cool hillsides. It gives aromatic, structured whites able to develop over many years.

Finger Lakes

AVA · A region in western New York State, around several long lakes of glacial origin, known above all for its cool-climate Riesling.

Franciacorta

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Franciacorta, in the Lombard province of Brescia beside Lake Iseo, produces traditional-method sparkling wines based on Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. In 1995 it became the first Italian bottle-fermented sparkling wine to obtain DOCG status.

Franken

Anbaugebiet (specified wine region) · Franken (Franconia), in northern Bavaria, follows the course of the river Main around Würzburg. It is the region of Silvaner par excellence, with traditionally dry wines bottled in the distinctive flattened bottle known as the Bocksbeutel.

Franschhoek

Wine of Origin (WO) · Franschhoek, the “French corner”, is a mountain-ringed valley east of Stellenbosch where the Huguenots settled in the late seventeenth century. It is known for its old Semillon vines, its sparkling wines and its food scene.

Friuli Colli Orientali

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC), with the Picolit, Ramandolo and Rosazzo DOCGs within its territory · Friuli Colli Orientali, formerly Colli Orientali del Friuli, is a hillside DOC in the province of Udine, along the Slovenian border, known for its whites, native reds such as Refosco and Schioppettino, and sweet Picolit.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Wine region · Friuli-Venezia Giulia, in north-eastern Italy, between the Alps, the Adriatic, Slovenia and Austria, is one of Italy's great white-wine regions, with hillside appellations such as Collio and Friuli Colli Orientali and broad lowland DOCs.

Galilee

Wine region (Galil), with the Upper Galilee, Lower Galilee and Golan Heights subregions · Israel’s northernmost wine region and its most highly regarded for quality wine. Its high-altitude vineyards, on limestone and volcanic soils, give structured reds and fresh whites.

Gavi

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Gavi, also called Cortese di Gavi, is a white-wine DOCG in south-eastern Piedmont, in the province of Alessandria, made exclusively from the Cortese variety.

Getariako Txakolina

Designation of Origin (DO) · Getariako Txakolina is the designation of origin for the txakoli of Gipuzkoa, on the Basque coast, and was the first devoted to this wine. It mainly produces light, fresh whites with a slight spritz from the Hondarrabi Zuri grape.

Gigondas

AOC (Southern Rhône cru) · Gigondas, at the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail in the Vaucluse, is one of the most highly regarded crus of the Southern Rhône. Its Grenache-based reds, with Syrah and Mourvèdre, are powerful yet fresh thanks to the altitude of part of the vineyard.

Golan Heights

Wine region · A volcanic plateau at the foot of Mount Hermon, whose high-altitude vineyards were planted from the 1970s onwards. Its cool nights and basalt soils produce Bordeaux-style reds, whites and sparkling wines with good acidity.

Goriška Brda

Wine district with protected designation of origin (ZGP) · Goriška Brda, at the western tip of Slovenia, is a hilly area adjoining the Italian Collio, from which it is separated only by the border drawn after the Second World War. It is the land of Rebula and one of the cradles of long-macerated white wines.

Gran Canaria

Designation of Origin (DO) · Gran Canaria is the designation of origin covering the wines of the whole island of that name, which includes as a subzone the former Monte de Lentiscal designation. Its vineyards, spread across areas of very different altitudes, yield Listán Negro reds, whites and sweet Malvasía and Moscatel wines.

Graves

AOC (Graves and Graves Supérieures), Bordeaux subregion · Graves, south of the city of Bordeaux on the left bank of the Garonne, takes its name from its gravel soils. It is one of the few Bordeaux areas where reds and dry whites carry comparable weight.

Greco di Tufo

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Greco di Tufo is a white-wine DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), made from the Greco variety around the village of Tufo. It gives whites of good structure and acidity, also in a sparkling version.

Hawke's Bay

AOC (Anjou, Loire valley) · Savennières, on the right bank of the Loire south-west of Angers, is a small appellation for Chenin Blanc whites, mostly dry, known for their intensity, tension and long ageing.

Savoie

Wine region (Vin de Savoie / Savoie AOC with crus, Roussette de Savoie, Seyssel, Crémant de Savoie) · Savoie, in the French Alps, brings together scattered mountain vineyards between Lake Geneva and the Isère valley. It produces mainly fresh whites from Jacquère, Altesse and Roussanne, and Mondeuse reds.

Serra Gaúcha

Wine region (with IP and DO geographical indications) · The Serra Gaúcha, in the north-east of the state of Rio Grande do Sul, is Brazil’s leading wine region. Settled by Italian immigrants from 1875, it is known above all for its sparkling wines and is home to the Vale dos Vinhedos, the country’s first designation of origin.

Setúbal

Setúbal DOC, Palmela DOC and Península de Setúbal GI · The Setúbal peninsula, south of Lisbon, is the home of Moscatel de Setúbal, a sweet fortified wine with long skin maceration, and also produces Castelão reds under the Palmela DOC.

Shandong

Wine-producing province (with the coastal areas of Yantai and Penglai) · A coastal province in eastern China, the cradle of the country’s modern wine industry since the founding of Changyu in Yantai. Its maritime monsoon climate, humid in summer, shapes wines of a fresher profile than those of the north-west.

Sicily

Wine region · Sicily, the largest island in the Mediterranean, has one of the largest vineyard areas in Italy. Its production ranges from Nero d'Avola reds and the volcanic wines of Etna to fortified Marsala and the sweet wines of Pantelleria and the Aeolian Islands.

Sierras de Málaga

Designation of Origin (DO) · Sierras de Málaga is an Andalusian designation of origin devoted to the unfortified still wines of the province of Málaga, complementing the Málaga DO, with which it shares a regulatory council. It includes the Serranía de Ronda subzone, known for its mountain reds.

Soave

DOC and DOCG · Soave is one of Italy's best-known white wines, made mainly from Garganega in the hills east of Verona. The area also produces Soave Superiore and the sweet Recioto di Soave, both DOCG.

Somontano

Designation of Origin (DO) · Somontano is a designation of origin in the province of Huesca, at the foot of the Pyrenees, centred on Barbastro. It combines native varieties such as Moristel and Parraleta with a wide range of international grapes.

Sonoma

County with several AVAs · Sonoma County, between the Pacific and Napa, brings together a wide range of climates and AVAs, from the cool Russian River Valley and Sonoma Coast to the warm Dry Creek and Alexander valleys.

Sta. Rita Hills

AVA (within Santa Ynez Valley, Santa Barbara County) · An AVA in the western Santa Ynez Valley, in Santa Barbara County, open to Pacific winds and fog. It is one of California’s coolest areas and a benchmark for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

Stellenbosch

Wine of Origin (WO) · Stellenbosch, east of Cape Town, is South Africa’s best-known wine district and the centre of its wine education and research. It stands out for Cabernet Sauvignon, Bordeaux blends, Pinotage and Chenin Blanc.

Styria (Steiermark)

Wine-growing region (Weinbauregion) with three DACs: Südsteiermark, Vulkanland Steiermark and Weststeiermark · A region in south-east Austria, on the Slovenian border, with very steep hills and a wetter climate than the rest of the country. It is famous for its Sauvignon Blanc, Gelber Muskateller and Schilcher rosé.

Sussex

Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) · Sussex, on the south coast of England, is one of the birthplaces of English traditional-method sparkling wine. Its vineyards are spread between the chalk hills of the South Downs and the clay and sandstone soils of the Weald.

Swartland

Wine of Origin (WO) · Swartland, north of Cape Town, went from being an area of wheat and bulk wine to becoming the focus of South Africa’s wine renewal, with old, dry-farmed bush vines of Chenin Blanc, Syrah and Mediterranean varieties.

Tacoronte-Acentejo

Designation of Origin (DO) · Tacoronte-Acentejo is a designation of origin in the north of Tenerife and was the first in the Canary Islands. Its vineyards, on volcanic slopes exposed to the trade winds, mainly yield reds from Listán Negro and Negramoll, along with whites and rosés.

Tarragona

Designation of Origin (DO) · Tarragona is one of the oldest designations of origin in Catalonia, with vineyards in the Camp de Tarragona and the Ribera d'Ebre. Historically famous for its fortified and rancio wines, it now mainly produces Mediterranean whites, rosés and reds.

Tasmania

Geographical Indication (GI) · Tasmania, the island south of the Australian mainland, is the country’s coolest wine region. It has made its name with traditional-method sparkling wines, Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

Taurasi

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Taurasi is a DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), devoted to structured, long-aged reds from Aglianico. It was the first DOCG in southern Italy.

Tavel

AOC (Southern Rhône cru, rosé only) · Tavel, on the right bank of the Rhône near Avignon, is an appellation devoted exclusively to rosé. Its wines, deep in colour and well structured, are food rosés made to accompany a meal.

Tejo

DOC do Tejo and Vinho Regional Tejo (GI) · A region of central Portugal that follows the course of the Tagus river north-east of Lisbon. Formerly known as Ribatejo, it has moved from bulk production towards fresh, fruity wines offering good value.

Terra Alta

Designation of Origin (DO) · Terra Alta is a designation of origin in the far west of the province of Tarragona, around Gandesa. It is particularly known for its White Grenache whites, although it also makes Grenache and Carignan reds and traditional rancio and sweet wines.

Thermenregion

Wine-growing region (Weinbaugebiet) of Lower Austria · A region south of Vienna, at the foot of the Vienna Woods, named after its thermal springs. It is known for two native whites, Zierfandler and Rotgipfler, and for its Sankt Laurent and Pinot Noir reds.

Thracian Valley

Wine region (Thracian Lowlands PGI) · The great plain of southern Bulgaria, between the Balkans and the Rhodopes, is the country’s warmest wine region and the home of Mavrud. It produces mainly red wines, from both native and international varieties.

Tierra de León

Designation of Origin (DO) · Tierra de León is the designation of origin of the southern part of the province of León, in Castilla y León, which in recent years has presented itself under the shorter name DO León. Its hallmark is Prieto Picudo, a native red grape used for reds and rosés of marked personality.

Tierra del Vino de Zamora

Designation of Origin (DO) · Tierra del Vino de Zamora is a designation of origin in Castilla y León located south of the city of Zamora, between Toro and Arribes. It preserves a remarkable heritage of old bush vines, above all Tempranillo, from which reds of continental character are made.

Tokaj

Protected designation of origin (OEM) · A region in north-eastern Hungary, at the foot of the Zemplén hills, famous for centuries for Tokaji Aszú, a sweet wine made from grapes affected by noble rot. Today it also produces dry Furmint whites of great interest.

Toro

Designation of Origin (DO) · Toro is a designation of origin in Zamora and Valladolid, on the banks of the Duero, recognised in 1987. Its reds from Tinta de Toro, the local form of Tempranillo, are noted for their deep colour, structure and ripeness.

Trentino-Alto Adige

Wine region · Trentino-Alto Adige, Italy's northernmost Alpine region, brings together two autonomous provinces with their own identities: Trento and Bolzano. It is known for its mountain whites, its Lagrein, Schiava and Teroldego reds and the traditional-method sparkling Trento DOC.

Tuscany

Wine region · Tuscany, in central Italy, is the great home of Sangiovese, the basis of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. Its coast also gave rise to the Bordeaux-variety wines known as Super Tuscans.

Uclés

Designation of Origin (DO) · Uclés is a designation of origin in Castilla-La Mancha lying between the west of the province of Cuenca and the east of Toledo, at the foot of the Sierra de Altomira. It takes its name from the monastery of Uclés and mainly produces Tempranillo reds.

Utiel-Requena

Designation of Origin (DO) · Utiel-Requena is a designation of origin in the interior of the province of Valencia, located on a high plateau with a continental climate. It is the heartland of the Bobal grape, used to make reds and rosés.

Vacqueyras

AOC (Southern Rhône cru) · Vacqueyras, Gigondas's neighbour in the Vaucluse, has been a Southern Rhône cru since 1990. It produces mainly generous, spicy Grenache and Syrah reds, along with smaller amounts of rosé and white.

Valais

AOC Valais · The Valais, in the upper Rhône valley, is Switzerland’s largest wine canton and the one with the greatest diversity of varieties. Its dry, sunny terraced slopes give Fendant, Petite Arvine, Pinot Noir and a long list of native grapes.

Valdeorras

Designation of Origin (DO) · Valdeorras, in the Sil valley in the east of the province of Ourense, is one of the oldest designations of origin in Galicia. It is the leading reference for the Godello grape, which was revived here from the 1970s onwards.

Valdepeñas

Designation of Origin (DO) · Valdepeñas is a designation of origin in the south of the province of Ciudad Real, surrounded by the La Mancha DO. Historically a supplier of wine to Madrid, it is known for its reds from Tempranillo, called Cencibel here, and its tradition of making wine in tinajas.

Valencia

Designation of Origin (DO) · Valencia is a designation of origin in the Valencian Community that covers various districts of the province of Valencia, organised into subzones. It is known for its Moscatel de Valencia, a sweet wine with a centuries-old tradition, and also produces Mediterranean-style whites, rosés and reds.

Valle de Guadalupe

Wine valley · The Valle de Guadalupe, in the municipality of Ensenada (Baja California), is Mexico’s best-known wine region. With a semi-arid Mediterranean climate and granitic soils, it produces mainly ripe blended reds and has become a leading destination for wine tourism and gastronomy.

Valle de La Orotava

Designation of Origin (DO) · Valle de La Orotava is a designation of origin in the north of Tenerife, in the broad valley that descends from Mount Teide to the sea. It is known for its traditional braided-cordon training system and for its whites and reds from Listán Blanco and Listán Negro.

Valle de Uco

Wine region · The Uco Valley, in west-central Mendoza, brings together some of the province’s highest and coolest vineyards. Since the 1990s it has become the benchmark area for high-altitude Malbec.

Valpolicella

DOC and DOCG (Amarone and Recioto) · Valpolicella, in the hills north of Verona, produces Corvina-based reds ranging from young Valpolicella to Ripasso and wines from dried grapes: the dry Amarone and the sweet Recioto, both DOCG.

Valtellina

Wine area with the Valtellina Superiore and Sforzato di Valtellina DOCGs and the Rosso di Valtellina DOC · Valtellina is an Alpine valley in Lombardy, in the province of Sondrio, where Nebbiolo, known here as Chiavennasca, is grown on terraces on the sunny slope. It produces fine reds and Sforzato, made from dried grapes.

Vaud

AOC Vaud · The canton of Vaud, on the shores of Lake Geneva, is the great territory of Chasselas. Its Lavaux terraces, a World Heritage Site, and areas such as La Côte and the Chablais give delicate whites that clearly express their place of origin.

Veneto

Wine region · Veneto, in north-east Italy, is one of the country's largest wine-producing regions. It is the source of Prosecco, Soave, Bardolino and the reds of Valpolicella, including Amarone.

Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC); the Riserva is a DOCG (Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva) · Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is the best-known white-wine appellation of the Marche. It is made from Verdicchio on the hills of the province of Ancona, around the town of Jesi, and its Riserva version holds DOCG status.

Vienna (Wien)

Wine-growing region (Weinbaugebiet) with the Wiener Gemischter Satz DAC · The Austrian capital is one of the few major cities in the world with significant winegrowing within its limits. Its vineyards, on the hills around the city, are the home of Gemischter Satz and of the Heuriger tradition.

Villány

Protected designation of origin (OEM) · Hungary’s southernmost wine region, near the Croatian border, is the warmest in the country and is devoted mainly to full-bodied reds from Bordeaux varieties, especially Cabernet Franc.

Vinho Verde

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · The Vinho Verde region occupies the green, rainy north-west of Portugal, in the Minho. It is known for its light, fresh, aromatic whites and for the Alvarinhos of Monção e Melgaço.

Vino Nobile di Montepulciano

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Vino Nobile di Montepulciano is a Tuscan red made from Sangiovese, known locally as Prugnolo Gentile, grown on the hills around the town of Montepulciano, in the province of Siena. It was one of Italy's first DOCGs.

Vinos de Madrid

Designation of Origin (DO) · Vinos de Madrid is the designation of origin of the Community of Madrid, with vineyards spread across several subzones in the south, east and west of the region. Notable wines include the Garnachas of the San Martín de Valdeiglesias subzone, in the foothills of the Sierra de Gredos, and whites from the local Malvar grape.

Vouvray

AOC (Touraine, Loire valley) · Vouvray, east of Tours on the right bank of the Loire, is one of the benchmark appellations for Chenin Blanc. It produces dry, off-dry, sweet and sparkling whites, often of great longevity.

Wachau

DAC (Districtus Austriae Controllatus) · The Wachau is a narrow stretch of the Danube valley between Melk and Krems, with vineyards on dry-stone terraces over primary rock. It is Austria’s most famous region for dry Grüner Veltliner and Riesling.

Wairarapa

Geographical Indication (GI), which includes Martinborough · A region in the south-east of New Zealand’s North Island, across the mountains from Wellington. Dry and windy, it groups the Martinborough, Gladstone and Masterton areas and is known above all for its Pinot Noir.

Walker Bay

Wine of Origin (WO), district of the Cape South Coast region · A coastal district around Hermanus and Walker Bay, south-east of Cape Town. It groups several wards, including the three of Hemel-en-Aarde, and is known for cool-climate Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc.

Walla Walla Valley

AVA (shared by Washington and Oregon, within Columbia Valley) · An AVA in south-eastern Washington State that also extends into north-eastern Oregon. With a dry continental climate, it is known for Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah reds and for the cobblestone soils of The Rocks District.

Weinviertel

DAC (Districtus Austriae Controllatus) · The Weinviertel (“wine quarter”) is Austria’s largest wine region and stretches north of Vienna to the Czech and Slovak borders. It was the country’s first DAC and is known for its fresh, peppery Grüner Veltliner.

Willamette Valley

AVA · A valley in north-west Oregon, between the Coast Range and the Cascades, which has become the leading American reference for cool-climate Pinot Noir.

Württemberg

Anbaugebiet (specified wine region) · A region in south-west Germany, around Stuttgart and the Neckar valley, where red grapes predominate. It is the home of Trollinger and Lemberger, with a strong cooperative tradition and largely local consumption.

Yarra Valley

Geographical Indication (GI) · The Yarra Valley, east of Melbourne, was the cradle of winegrowing in Victoria. Today it is one of Australia’s most highly regarded cool-climate regions for Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and sparkling wine.

Ycoden-Daute-Isora

Designation of Origin (DO) · Ycoden-Daute-Isora is a designation of origin in the north-west and west of Tenerife, centred on Icod de los Vinos, Garachico and Guía de Isora. It mainly produces Listán Blanco whites, along with reds from Listán Negro and other Canary varieties and sweet Malvasía wines.

Yecla

Designation of Origin (DO) · Yecla is a designation of origin in the north of the Region of Murcia that is limited to a single municipality, that of the town of Yecla. Its dominant variety is Monastrell, which gives warm, fruity reds from a continental Mediterranean climate.
