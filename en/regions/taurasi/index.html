Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG)

Taurasi

Taurasi is a DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), devoted to structured, long-aged reds from Aglianico. It was the first DOCG in southern Italy.

Irpinia

The Taurasi area lies in Irpinia, the mountainous territory of inland Campania that largely corresponds to the province of Avellino. It takes its name from the village of Taurasi, in the valley of the Calore river, and covers this municipality and others nearby. Irpinia is one of the most important winegrowing areas in southern Italy and is home, besides Taurasi, to the white-wine DOCGs Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo. Despite its southern latitude, it is a land of hills and mountains, far from the sea and with a much cooler climate than one might expect.

History

Vines have been grown in Irpinia since antiquity. At the end of the nineteenth century, when phylloxera was affecting many of Europe's vineyards, the area exported wine, and the railway line that crossed the valley became known as the wine train. For much of the twentieth century, the Mastroberardino house played a decisive role in preserving and promoting Taurasi and the region's native varieties. The DOC was recognised in 1970 and the DOCG in 1993, making Taurasi the first DOCG in southern Italy.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental by the standards of the region, with cold winters, sometimes with snow, warm summers and a marked difference between day and night temperatures. The harvest of Aglianico, a late-ripening variety, often runs to the end of October or even into November. The soils are varied: clays, limestones and marls, often with deposits of volcanic ash from eruptions of Vesuvius and other Campanian volcanoes.

The grape and the rules

Taurasi is made from Aglianico, which must account for at least eighty-five per cent of the wine; the remainder may be made up of other red varieties permitted in the region. The production rules require minimum ageing of three years, at least one of them in wood, and four years for the Riserva, with a longer period in wood.

Style

Taurasi is a deeply coloured red, with aromas of black cherry, plum, violet, spice, tobacco and earthy or smoky notes. On the palate it stands out for high acidity and firm tannins that can seem austere in youth. For this reason it is regarded as a wine for long ageing, gaining complexity and harmony over the years. It has often been compared with the great reds of northern Italy for its structure and capacity to age.
