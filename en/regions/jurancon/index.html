AOC (Jurançon and Jurançon sec), South West France

Jurançon

Jurançon, in the Béarn south of Pau, produces whites from Petit Manseng and Gros Manseng on slopes at the foot of the Pyrenees. It is best known for its sweet wines from grapes shrivelled on the vine, although today most of the output is Jurançon sec.

Location

The Jurançon appellation lies in the Béarn, in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department, south and west of the city of Pau. Its vineyards are spread across numerous communes in a landscape of steep hills and green valleys, often in small plots separated by woods and meadows, with the Pyrenees as a backdrop. It is an area of mid-mountain viticulture, with vineyards at a higher altitude than in many other parts of the South West.

History

Jurançon wines have a long tradition. The most often repeated story tells that, at the baptism of the future Henri IV, born in Pau in 1553, his lips were rubbed with a clove of garlic and a few drops of Jurançon wine, according to a Béarn custom; it is a traditional tale whose details cannot be verified. For centuries the sweet wines of Jurançon were exported to northern Europe. After phylloxera, the vineyard was rebuilt with local varieties, and Jurançon was one of the appellations recognised in the 1930s.

Climate and soils

The climate has an Atlantic influence, damp, with abundant rainfall, but the autumns are usually dry and sunny thanks to the foehn, a warm south wind that blows down from the Pyrenees. These conditions make it possible to leave the grapes on the vine until late autumn or even winter. To protect the vines from frost and humidity, they are traditionally trained high, on tall trellises, and many slopes are terraced. The soils consist of clay, marl, sandstone and pebble conglomerates known as poudingues.

Grapes

The two main varieties are Petit Manseng, with small, thick-skinned berries, very well suited to drying on the vine, and Gros Manseng, more productive and used mainly for dry wines. Other local varieties such as Courbu, Camaralet and Lauzet are also grown.

Styles

The Jurançon AOC is reserved for sweet wines, obtained mainly by passerillage, that is, the concentration of the grapes' sugars as they dehydrate on the vine, rather than by noble rot. Some producers also make late-harvest wines and selections from several passes through the vineyard. They have aromas of tropical fruit, pineapple, mango, quince, honey and spice, with very lively acidity that balances the sweetness and gives them great ageing potential. The Jurançon sec AOC, which today accounts for most of the volume, produces fresh, aromatic dry whites with marked acidity and notes of citrus and exotic fruit.

Drinking

The sweet wines of Jurançon are often served as an aperitif in the region, as well as with foie gras, Pyrenean cheeses or fruit desserts. The dry wines go with fish and local cooking.
