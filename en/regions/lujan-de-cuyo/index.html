Luján de Cuyo

Luján de Cuyo, south of the city of Mendoza, is one of the historic areas of Argentine Malbec and one of the country’s few Controlled Designations of Origin. It retains many old vineyards and numerous emblematic wineries.

History

Luján de Cuyo is part of what is known as Mendoza’s Primera Zona, the upper Mendoza River area, where many of the wineries that built the reputation of Mendoza wine from the late nineteenth century were concentrated. European immigrants planted Malbec and other French and Italian varieties here, and several of those old vineyards are still in production. Luján de Cuyo was one of the first Argentine areas to obtain Controlled Designation of Origin status, whose rules are linked above all to Malbec.

Geography and climate

The department lies south of the provincial capital, on both sides of the Mendoza River, with vineyards roughly between 850 and 1,100 metres above sea level. The climate is continental and dry, warmer than that of the Uco Valley but tempered by altitude and by cool air descending from the mountains at night. Rainfall is scarce and irrigation with water from the Mendoza River is essential.

Soils

The soils are alluvial and of variable texture, with deep silt and clay areas near the river and stonier sectors, with gravel and boulders, on the higher terraces. Good drainage and low organic matter limit the vigour of the vines.

Districts

Its best-known districts include Agrelo, to the south, with more clayey soils and many modern wineries; Perdriel, by the river, with many old vineyards; Vistalba and Las Compuertas, in the highest and coolest part, near the foothills; and others such as Mayor Drummond, Carrodilla and Ugarteche. Each has given its name to estate and single-plot wines.

Grapes and styles

Malbec dominates and here tends to give wines with ripe black fruit, notes of violet and spice, ample body and soft tannins, able to age well when they come from old vines. Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Syrah and Chardonnay complete the picture. Many wineries in the area combine their own Luján de Cuyo grapes with others from the Uco Valley to make blends from different altitudes.

Landscape and wine tourism

Its proximity to the city of Mendoza has made Luján de Cuyo one of the most visited areas for wine tourism in Argentina, with many wineries open to the public. At the same time, urban growth poses a challenge to the preservation of the old vineyards closest to the capital.
