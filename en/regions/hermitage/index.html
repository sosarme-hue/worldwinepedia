Hermitage

Hermitage is a hill on the left bank of the Rhône, above Tain-l'Hermitage, that produces some of France's longest-lived Syrah reds and broad whites from Marsanne and Roussanne.

Location

The hill of Hermitage rises above the small town of Tain-l'Hermitage, on the left bank of the Rhône, opposite Tournon. It is one of the few areas of the Northern Rhône located on that bank. The hill faces south, which guarantees excellent sunshine and good protection from northern winds. The appellation is small and divided into numerous traditional sites, such as Les Bessards, Le Méal, L'Hermite, Les Greffieux and Les Rocoules. At the summit stands a small chapel dedicated to Saint Christopher, which has become a symbol of the place.

History

According to tradition, the name comes from a knight, Gaspard de Stérimberg, who is said to have retired to live as a hermit on the hill in the thirteenth century. Since at least the seventeenth century, Hermitage wines have enjoyed a high reputation in France and abroad. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries they were also used to strengthen some Bordeaux wines, a practice that gave rise to the term hermitagé. Next to Hermitage lies the much larger Crozes-Hermitage appellation, on flatter land surrounding the hill.

Soils

The hill's geology is varied. In the western part, around Les Bessards, granite soils predominate, an extension of the massif that forms the right bank, and they give wines of great structure. Towards the east there are deposits of loess, gravel and pebbles of river and glacial origin, as well as more calcareous soils, in sectors such as Le Méal and Les Rocoules. Producers usually blend grapes from different sites to achieve a complete wine, although some also make single-site wines.

Grapes and styles

The reds are made from Syrah, and the rules allow a small proportion of the white varieties Marsanne and Roussanne to be added, although this is rarely done today. They are dark wines with firm tannins and aromas of black fruit, violet, pepper and earthy notes, which need many years to reach their peak. The whites are based on Marsanne and Roussanne; they are broad wines with moderate acidity and great ageing capacity, with notes of almond, honey and stone fruit. There is also a small production of vin de paille, a sweet wine made from dried grapes.

Producers

Among the houses most closely associated with the hill are Paul Jaboulet Aîné, with its wine La Chapelle, M. Chapoutier, which owns many plots, and the Jean-Louis Chave estate, a long-established family of growers. The Tain cooperative also carries considerable weight.

Country
France
Climate
Continental with Mediterranean influences, on a very sunny south-facing hill.
Soils
Granite on the western part of the hill; loess, gravel, pebbles and calcareous soils towards the east.
Styles
Powerful, very long-lived Syrah reds, broad Marsanne and Roussanne whites, and vin de paille.
Grapes
Syrah (Shiraz), Marsanne, Roussanne
