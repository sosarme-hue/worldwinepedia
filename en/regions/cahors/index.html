Cahors

Cahors, in the Lot valley, is the historic home of Malbec, known here as Côt or Auxerrois. Its dark, tannic reds were known for centuries as “black wine”.

Location

The Cahors appellation lies in the Lot department, in South West France, along the valley of the river Lot, which winds in great meanders upstream and downstream of the town of Cahors. The vineyards occupy both the valley terraces and the limestone plateaus that overlook it, known as causses. It is an area far from the sea, several hundred kilometres upriver from Bordeaux, with which it maintained a sometimes conflictual trading relationship for centuries.

History

Viticulture in Cahors is very old. In the Middle Ages its very dark wines were exported to England and northern Europe via the river Lot and the port of Bordeaux, where Bordeaux merchants imposed restrictions to favour their own wines. Cahors wines became known as vin noir, “black wine”, because of their depth of colour. Phylloxera at the end of the nineteenth century and the great frost of 1956 destroyed much of the vineyard. Replanting was done mainly with Malbec, and Cahors obtained AOC status in 1971. Later, the success of Malbec in Argentina sparked new international interest in the appellation, regarded as the variety's historic home in France.

Climate and soils

The climate combines Atlantic and Mediterranean influences with continental features: warm summers, often sunny autumns and a risk of spring frost. Two main types of terrain are distinguished. In the valley, the alluvial terraces of the Lot, with gravel, sand and clay, give fruitier, more supple wines. On the limestone plateaus and their slopes, at higher altitude, the poor, stony soils produce more structured, taut and long-lived wines.

Grapes

The rules require Malbec, known locally as Côt or Auxerrois, to make up at least seventy per cent of the blend. It may be completed with Merlot and Tannat, although many producers make single-varietal Malbec wines. The appellation is reserved for reds; whites and rosés from the area are sold under other designations.

Style

Cahors wines are very deeply coloured reds, with aromas of plum, black cherry, violet, liquorice and earthy notes, fresh acidity and firm tannins. Styles range from young, fruity wines aged in tank to plateau reds aged in barrel that need several years in bottle and can age for a long time. In recent decades producers have tended to seek more finesse and less extraction, with wines that keep their dark character but are better balanced.
