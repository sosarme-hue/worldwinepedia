Arabako Txakolina

Arabako Txakolina is the designation of origin for the txakoli of Álava, concentrated in the Ayala valley, in the north-west of the province. It is the smallest and most recent of the three txakoli designations and mainly produces fresh whites from Hondarrabi Zuri.

History

Although Álava is known in the wine world mainly for Rioja Alavesa, the north of the province has a different tradition, that of txakoli, linked to the Atlantic side of the territory. In the Ayala valley (Aiara in Basque) vine growing has been documented for centuries, and it once covered a considerable area before the vine diseases of the nineteenth century, phylloxera and the industrial development of the district during the twentieth century brought it close to disappearing. At the end of the twentieth century a group of growers drove the revival of Álava txakoli, culminating in the creation of the Arabako Txakolina Designation of Origin, the most recent of the three devoted to this wine, after those of Getaria and Bizkaia.

Territory and climate

The designation is concentrated in the Ayala district, in the north-west of Álava, which includes municipalities such as Amurrio, Llodio, Artziniega, Okondo and Ayala. It is a territory of green valleys and slopes on the Cantabrian side, open to the influence of the sea, draining towards the river Nervión and the Bilbao estuary. The climate is Atlantic, temperate and humid, with plentiful rain spread throughout the year, although somewhat cooler and slightly more continental than on the coast, owing to its inland position and higher altitude. The vines are preferably planted on well-exposed slopes, where they make the most of the sun and ventilation.

Soils

The soils are clay-loam in texture, generally deep and with good water retention, developed over marl, sandstone and limestone.

Grape varieties

The main variety is Hondarrabi Zuri, which accounts for most of the vineyard and brings acidity and citrus and floral aromas. Other whites such as Petit Manseng, Gros Manseng and Petit Courbu are also grown, together with small amounts of other authorised varieties. The traditional red grape of txakoli, Hondarrabi Beltza, has only a limited presence.

Styles

Production centres on young white txakoli, moderate in alcohol, with lively acidity and fresh aromas of white fruit, citrus and herbs, which may show a slight spritz. Some producers have introduced wines aged on their lees and other more ambitious styles, as well as small quantities of rosé. It is a very small designation, with few wineries, mostly family-run, whose wines are drunk mainly in the Basque Country and go well with the local cuisine of fish and vegetables.
