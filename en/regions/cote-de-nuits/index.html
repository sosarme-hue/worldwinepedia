Burgundy subregion (Côte d'Or)

Côte de Nuits

The Côte de Nuits is the northern half of the Côte d'Or, between Dijon and Corgoloin. Devoted almost entirely to Pinot Noir, it is home to the great majority of Burgundy's red grands crus.

Territory

The Côte de Nuits is a narrow strip of vineyards, often only a few hundred metres wide, following the fault that separates the limestone plateau from the Saône plain. It takes its name from Nuits-Saint-Georges, its main wine town, and runs from Marsannay, on the edge of Dijon, to Corgoloin. The vines cover east- and south-east-facing slopes at roughly 250 to 350 metres; the grands crus are generally found in the middle of the slope, where the soils are best drained and balanced.

History

Monastic influence was decisive. The Cistercians of Cîteaux assembled the Clos de Vougeot and enclosed it with a wall; its château is now home to the Chevaliers du Tastevin, a brotherhood founded in 1934. The abbey of Bèze gave its name to the Clos de Bèze in Gevrey. In the nineteenth century several villages added the name of their most famous vineyard to their own, as in Gevrey-Chambertin, Chambolle-Musigny and Vosne-Romanée, a custom repeated elsewhere in Burgundy.

Climate and soils

The climate is semi-continental, with a risk of frost and hail. The soils combine Middle Jurassic limestone and marl in proportions that vary from one plot to the next; the harder limestone and stony soils of the upper slopes contrast with deeper, more clay-rich land at the foot of the slope, where the regional appellations are found.

Villages and grands crus

From north to south, the main villages are Marsannay, the only communal appellation in the Côte d'Or that allows rosé; Fixin; Gevrey-Chambertin, with nine grands crus, including Chambertin and Chambertin-Clos de Bèze; Morey-Saint-Denis, with Clos de la Roche, Clos Saint-Denis, Clos des Lambrays, Clos de Tart and part of Bonnes-Mares; Chambolle-Musigny, with Musigny and most of Bonnes-Mares; Vougeot; Vosne-Romanée, with Romanée-Conti, La Tâche, La Romanée, Richebourg, Romanée-Saint-Vivant and La Grande Rue, as well as Échézeaux and Grands-Échézeaux in neighbouring Flagey-Échézeaux; and Nuits-Saint-Georges, which has no grands crus but many premiers crus. The southernmost villages share the Côte de Nuits-Villages appellation, and the hills behind them form the Hautes-Côtes de Nuits.

Grapes and styles

Pinot Noir is almost entirely dominant, producing reds of great aromatic finesse which, depending on the village, may be more structured, as in Gevrey-Chambertin, or more delicate, as in Chambolle-Musigny. Small quantities of Chardonnay whites are made, such as Musigny blanc, one of the few white grands crus of the Côte de Nuits.
