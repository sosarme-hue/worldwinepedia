Bordeaux area and AOC (dry whites)

Entre-Deux-Mers

Entre-Deux-Mers is the broad area of hills lying between the Garonne and the Dordogne. Its own appellation is reserved for dry whites, while the area's reds are sold as Bordeaux or Bordeaux Supérieur.

Location

The name, meaning “between two seas”, refers to the two great rivers that bound the area and whose waters are affected by the Atlantic tides: the Garonne to the south-west and the Dordogne to the north, which meet downstream to form the Gironde estuary. It is an extensive territory of gentle hills, woods, meadows and villages to the east of the city of Bordeaux, and it is the largest production area of the Bordeaux region. It is also home to medieval remains such as the abbey of La Sauve-Majeure, on the old pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela.

An area and an appellation

It is important to distinguish between the geographical area and the appellation. The Entre-Deux-Mers AOC applies only to dry white wines. There is also a more restricted appellation, Entre-Deux-Mers Haut-Benauge, in the central sector. The reds, which today account for a large part of what is grown in the area, are sold under the regional Bordeaux and Bordeaux Supérieur AOCs. Other appellations lie on the edges of the territory: on the slopes overlooking the Garonne, Cadillac, Loupiac and Sainte-Croix-du-Mont, known for their sweet whites, and Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux for reds; to the north, Graves de Vayres; and to the east, Sainte-Foy.

Climate and soils

The climate is temperate oceanic, with rainfall spread throughout the year and a somewhat higher risk of spring frost in the valley bottoms. The soils are very varied: clay and limestone on the plateaus and slopes, gravel in some sectors near the rivers, and silty, sandy soils known locally as boulbènes.

Grapes and style

Entre-Deux-Mers whites are made mainly from Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon, with Muscadelle and, in smaller proportions, other authorised varieties. They are fresh, aromatic wines with notes of citrus, white flowers and grass, generally intended to be drunk young, although some producers ferment or age them partly in barrel. The reds of the area, sold as Bordeaux, are based mainly on Merlot, with Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc, and are usually fruity, approachable wines. Rosés and clairets are also made.

Economic importance

Entre-Deux-Mers supplies a considerable share of the volume of Bordeaux wine and is home to many family holdings and cooperatives. It is, to a large extent, the foundation of everyday Bordeaux wines.
