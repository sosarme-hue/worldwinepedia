Calatayud

Calatayud is a designation of origin in the south-west of the province of Zaragoza, in the Iberian System, with some of the highest vineyards in Aragón. It is known for old-vine Grenache grown on poor slate and limestone soils.

History

The Calatayud area was an important site in Roman times, when Bilbilis, birthplace of the Latin poet Martial, stood next to the present-day town. Viticulture continued through the medieval period and developed significantly in the early modern era. As in other parts of Aragón, for much of the twentieth century production was geared towards bulk wine, largely through cooperatives. With the recognition of the designation of origin and growing interest in old-vine Grenache, Calatayud has gained a foothold in the market for bottled wines, often offering good value for money.

Territory and climate

The designation occupies the south-west of the province of Zaragoza, in the valleys of the Jalón river and its tributaries, such as the Jiloca, Manubles, Ribota, Perejiles and Piedra, in the heart of the Iberian System. Many vineyards lie at high altitudes, on slopes and terraces that are among the highest in Aragón. The climate is extreme continental: long cold winters, hot dry summers, little rainfall and a wide difference between day and night temperatures, which favours freshness and aromatic intensity in the wines. Late frosts are a common risk.

Soils

Soils are very varied and generally poor: slate on the slopes in some areas, stony calcareous soils, reddish clays and, in certain places, gypsum. Old bush vines make use of these low-fertility soils, which naturally limit yields. Many of these vineyards occupy small, scattered plots on slopes that are hard to mechanise, so much of the work is done by hand.

Grapes and styles

Grenache is the main variety and the one that best defines the designation, with a large proportion of old vines. It gives reds with intense red fruit, spicy and floral notes and, in high plots on slate, remarkable acidity and tension. Tempranillo, Mazuelo (Carignan), Monastrell, Syrah, Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon are also grown, and among the whites Macabeo, Malvasía, White Grenache, Moscatel and Chardonnay. Producers make young and oak-aged reds, Grenache rosés and whites.

Old-vine Grenache

The designation has placed particular emphasis on Grenache from old, low-yielding, unirrigated vineyards, for which it has created a specific, more demanding category. These wines tend to combine ripeness and freshness, reflecting the influence of the altitude and poor soils of the area.
