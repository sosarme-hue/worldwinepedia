Muscadet

Muscadet, in the Pays Nantais near the mouth of the Loire, produces light, saline dry whites from Melon de Bourgogne. Many are bottled after ageing on their lees, and the crus communaux offer long-lived versions.

Location

Muscadet is the westernmost wine area of the Loire valley. It extends around the city of Nantes, in the Loire-Atlantique department and part of Maine-et-Loire, a short distance from the ocean. The landscape is one of gentle hills cut by rivers, among them the Sèvre and the Maine, which give their name to the most important sub-area. The name Muscadet refers both to the region and to the wine; the variety from which it is made is called Melon de Bourgogne, also known locally as Melon or Muscadet.

History

As its name suggests, Melon comes from Burgundy. Its wide spread in the Pays Nantais is usually explained by the extremely severe frost of the winter of 1709, which destroyed much of the region's vineyards; the cold-resistant variety is said to have been planted on a massive scale afterwards. For centuries the area's wines were sold largely to Dutch merchants, who were also interested in producing brandy.

Appellations

There are four regional appellations: Muscadet, the broadest; Muscadet Sèvre et Maine, which accounts for most of the production; Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire, to the east along the river; and Muscadet Côtes de Grandlieu, to the south-west, around the lake of Grand-Lieu. To these are added the crus communaux, such as Clisson, Gorges, Le Pallet, Château-Thébaud, Goulaine and Monnières-Saint-Fiacre, which require much longer lees ageing and come from delimited soils.

Climate and soils

The climate is oceanic, damp and mild, with gentle winters and cool summers; spring frost is a significant risk. The soils belong to the Armorican massif and are very varied: granite, gneiss, schist, orthogneiss and gabbro, a dark igneous rock that characterises the Gorges cru. Producers attribute a different nuance in the wine to each type of rock.

Winemaking and style

The term sur lie indicates that the wine has spent the winter on its fine lees and been bottled without racking, which gives it texture, freshness and sometimes a slight prickle. Muscadets are dry whites of moderate alcohol and lively acidity, with aromas of citrus, green apple and white flowers and saline, iodine notes. They are a classic match for oysters, shellfish and fish. Most are drunk young, but the crus communaux and some single-plot cuvées can develop for many years.
