Wine of Origin (WO), district of the Cape South Coast region

Elgin

An elevated plateau south-east of Cape Town, traditionally devoted to apple orchards and now one of South Africa’s coolest wine areas. It stands out for cool-climate Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Syrah.

Territory

Elgin lies about seventy kilometres south-east of Cape Town, beyond Sir Lowry’s Pass, in an elevated basin surrounded by mountains, between the Hottentots Holland, Groenland and Kogelberg ranges. For much of the twentieth century it was mainly a fruit-growing area, South Africa’s leading apple producer, and orchards still dominate the landscape. From the late twentieth century some farmers and new producers began replacing part of their orchards with vineyards, attracted by the cool climate. In the Wine of Origin system, Elgin is a district of the Cape South Coast region.

Climate

Elgin is one of the country’s coolest wine areas. Altitude and the proximity of the ocean, a few kilometres to the south, keep summer temperatures moderate, and south-easterly winds bring sea air and cloud that reduce the hours of intense heat. Rainfall is higher than in most parts of the Cape, especially in winter. Ripening is slow and the harvest usually later than in Stellenbosch or Paarl. This coolness allows wines with marked acidity and clear-cut aromas, although it requires careful management of humidity and disease.

Soils

The soils derive mostly from Bokkeveld Group shales, which form clays with good water-holding capacity, and from Table Mountain sandstone, which gives lighter, sandier soils. Many vineyards are dry-farmed or very lightly irrigated, thanks to the combination of rainfall and retentive soils.

Grapes and styles

Sauvignon Blanc was one of the first varieties to give Elgin its reputation, with aromatic whites showing citrus and herbaceous notes and lively acidity. Chardonnay produces some of South Africa’s finest, tautest and most elegant whites, often barrel-aged. Pinot Noir gives light, aromatic reds, and cool-climate Syrah shows notes of pepper, flowers and black fruit. Riesling, which gives dry or off-dry whites, Chenin Blanc and some Bordeaux varieties in the warmer sectors are also grown. Elgin also produces base wines for traditional-method sparkling wine, known in South Africa as Méthode Cap Classique. The region is characterised by small and medium-sized wineries, many of them family-owned, and by relatively small production. Because many growers still farm apples and pears alongside their vines, the landscape keeps its agricultural character, and the region remains less visited than the famous wine valleys closer to Cape Town.
