Montilla-Moriles

Montilla-Moriles is a designation of origin in the south of the province of Córdoba, devoted mainly to fortified-style wines made from Pedro Ximénez. Its finos often reach their strength naturally, without fortification, and its sweet Pedro Ximénez wines come from sun-dried grapes.

History

Viticulture in the Córdoba countryside has ancient roots and developed considerably in the early modern period around towns such as Montilla and Moriles. Ties with Jerez have been close: for a long time some of the area's wines were sent to the Jerez bodegas, and the very term amontillado comes from Montilla, referring to wines made in the style of Montilla. The designation of origin, with its own regulatory council, has defended the identity of these wines, which share techniques with sherry but have their own features, due above all to the predominance of Pedro Ximénez.

Territory and climate

The designation extends across the south of the province of Córdoba, in a countryside of gentle hills around Montilla, Moriles, Aguilar de la Frontera, Puente Genil, Lucena and other municipalities. Within it, two superior quality zones are recognised: Sierra de Montilla and Moriles Altos. The climate is Mediterranean with a marked continental influence: very hot, dry summers, with high temperatures for many weeks, mild or cool winters and little rain, concentrated in autumn and winter.

Soils

In the superior zones, white calcareous soils known locally as albero or albariza predominate; they retain moisture and release it slowly to the vine. Elsewhere in the area the soils are sandier and more fertile, and are known as ruedos.

Grapes and winemaking

Pedro Ximénez is the dominant variety. Thanks to the heat, the grapes reach a sugar content high enough for the wines to achieve their strength naturally, so many finos do not need fortifying with spirit. Fermentation was traditionally carried out in large earthenware jars called tinajas, which are still found in many wineries. Other white varieties are also grown, such as Moscatel and Airén.

Types of wine

The wines are aged in oak casks using the criaderas and solera system. Fino is aged under a veil of flor; amontillado combines biological and oxidative ageing; oloroso and palo cortado are oxidatively aged wines. Sweet Pedro Ximénez is made from grapes dried in the sun after harvest, in a process called asoleo, until their sugars are concentrated; it gives very dense, dark wines with aromas of raisins, figs and nuts. The area also makes young, unaged white wines.
