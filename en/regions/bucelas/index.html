Bucelas

A small white-wine appellation just north of Lisbon, devoted above all to the Arinto grape. Its crisp dry whites enjoyed a reputation in England in the nineteenth century.

History

Bucelas is one of the historic appellations around Lisbon, together with Colares and Carcavelos, and was demarcated in the early twentieth century. Its reputation, however, is older: in the nineteenth century its whites were appreciated in England, where they were sometimes called Lisbon Hock, by comparison with the Rhine whites the British knew as hock. This popularity is usually linked to the British presence in Portugal during the Peninsular War. Over time, the city’s urban growth and changing tastes caused the region to lose prominence, and the vineyard area shrank considerably. Today it is a small appellation with few producers that has kept a very recognisable style.

Territory and climate

The area extends along the valley of the river Trancão and the surrounding hills, in the municipality of Loures, a very short distance north of Lisbon. Although it is not right on the coast, it is influenced by the Atlantic, which softens the summers and brings cool nights. The hills partly shelter the vineyards from the strongest winds. The result is a temperate climate with a long ripening period that helps preserve acidity, an essential feature of Bucelas wines.

Soils

The soils are mainly clay-limestone, with marl and some areas of sandier origin. Their ability to hold water helps the vines get through dry summers without excessive stress.

Grapes and styles

The Bucelas DOC is reserved for white wines only, both still and sparkling. The undisputed star is Arinto, which must make up the majority of the appellation’s wines. This variety, highly valued throughout Portugal, keeps high acidity even in warm climates and gives wines with aromas of citrus, green apple and white flowers, a firm texture and an often mineral finish. It is complemented by Sercial, known here as Esgana Cão, a popular name alluding to its biting acidity, and by Rabo de Ovelha. Bucelas whites are enjoyable young, but the best develop well in bottle for several years, gaining notes of honey, nuts and spice without losing freshness. Some producers also make oak-aged versions and traditional-method sparkling wines, which show how well the variety’s natural acidity suits this kind of winemaking.
