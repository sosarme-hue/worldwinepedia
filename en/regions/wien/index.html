Vienna (Wien)

The Austrian capital is one of the few major cities in the world with significant winegrowing within its limits. Its vineyards, on the hills around the city, are the home of Gemischter Satz and of the Heuriger tradition.

Vineyards in the city

Vienna’s relationship with wine goes back to Roman times and has survived the growth of the city. Today its vineyards lie mainly on the slopes of the Vienna Woods to the north-west, in districts such as Grinzing, Sievering, Nussdorf and Neustift am Walde, with the Nussberg and Kahlenberg hills as emblematic sites; on the left bank of the Danube, around the Bisamberg, Stammersdorf and Strebersdorf; and in the south of the city, in Mauer, Rodaun and Oberlaa. Viennese winegrowing is protected by urban planning, which reserves these areas for vines, and is part of the city’s identity.

The Heurigen

Inseparable from Viennese wine is the Heuriger, the grower’s tavern where the wine of the year is served with simple cold dishes. Its tradition goes back to a decree of Emperor Joseph II in the late eighteenth century allowing growers to sell their own produce. A bunch of pine twigs hung above the door shows that the tavern is open. The Heurigen remain one of the city’s great attractions and the main sales channel for many producers.

Gemischter Satz

Vienna’s most characteristic wine is Wiener Gemischter Satz, which has its own DAC. It is a white made from a vineyard planted with several interplanted varieties that are picked and fermented together, rather than by blending finished wines. The rules require at least three white varieties to grow together in the vineyard, with none of them dominating excessively. The usual grapes include Grüner Veltliner, Riesling, Weissburgunder (Pinot Blanc), Welschriesling, Neuburger and Traminer. The result is complex whites with varied aromas and good freshness, ranging from light versions for the Heuriger to more ambitious single-vineyard wines.

Climate, soils and other wines

The climate combines warm, dry Pannonian continental influence with coolness coming from the Vienna Woods, while the Danube moderates temperatures. Soils range from limestone and soils rich in fossil shells in the north-west to loess and gravel on the left bank of the river and more clay-rich soils in the south. Besides Gemischter Satz, Vienna produces varietal Grüner Veltliner and Riesling, Chardonnay and Pinot Blanc, and some reds from Zweigelt, Pinot Noir and Sankt Laurent, mainly in the southern area.
