Tarragona

Tarragona is one of the oldest designations of origin in Catalonia, with vineyards in the Camp de Tarragona and the Ribera d'Ebre. Historically famous for its fortified and rancio wines, it now mainly produces Mediterranean whites, rosés and reds.

History

Ancient Tarraco, capital of one of the provinces of Roman Hispania, was an important centre of wine production and trade, and Latin authors such as Pliny the Elder mentioned the wines of the area. In the early modern period and above all in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, the port of Tarragona became one of Spain's great wine-exporting centres, and the city was home to many trading houses. Its most famous product was so-called Tarragona wine, a sweet, high-alcohol fortified wine exported in large quantities, including for religious use. The designation of origin, one of the first in Catalonia, covered a much larger area for decades; in 2001 the former Falset subzone broke away to form the Montsant designation.

Territory and climate

Today the designation is divided into two zones. Camp de Tarragona occupies the plain and hills around the city, open to the sea and sheltered to the north by coastal ranges. Ribera d'Ebre lies further inland, on the banks of the river Ebro, in a more mountainous setting of more continental character. The climate is Mediterranean, with mild winters, hot, dry summers and scant, irregular rainfall, milder on the coast and with greater temperature contrasts inland.

Soils

In Camp de Tarragona light, well-drained brown calcareous soils predominate, while Ribera d'Ebre also has alluvial soils on the river terraces and stonier soils on the slopes.

Grape varieties

The leading whites are Macabeo, Xarel·lo, Parellada, Garnacha Blanca and Moscatel. Among the reds are Garnacha and Cariñena, traditional varieties of southern Catalonia, together with Tempranillo (Ull de Llebre), Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah. Part of the Camp de Tarragona vineyard is included in the cava production area, and much of its white fruit goes into this sparkling wine.

Styles

The designation produces fresh whites, rosés and young or aged reds of Mediterranean character. It also preserves a distinctive tradition of fortified wines and rancios: Tarragona Clásico is a sweet fortified wine given long ageing, and the rancios, aged oxidatively, develop aromas of dried fruit and nuts and toasty notes. Mistelas and sweet Moscatel wines are also made. Although these traditional styles carry little commercial weight today, they are an essential part of the area's wine identity and of its long history as an exporter.

Climate

Soils
Light brown calcareous soils in the Camp, and alluvial or stony soils in Ribera d'Ebre.

Mediterranean whites, rosés and reds, plus fortified wines, rancios and Tarragona Clásico.
