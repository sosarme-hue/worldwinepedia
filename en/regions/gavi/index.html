Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG)

Gavi

Gavi, also called Cortese di Gavi, is a white-wine DOCG in south-eastern Piedmont, in the province of Alessandria, made exclusively from the Cortese variety.

Location

The Gavi area lies at the far south-eastern edge of Piedmont, in the province of Alessandria, around the town of Gavi and a small group of neighbouring municipalities, including Novi Ligure. It is very close to the border with Liguria, and for centuries the area was linked politically and commercially to the Republic of Genoa, whose port lay a short distance away. That relationship partly explains the area's focus on white wine in a region dominated by reds: the white wine of these hills traditionally accompanied the fish dishes of the Ligurian coast.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental, but with some Mediterranean influence thanks to the nearby sea, from which it is separated by the Apennine ridges. Summers are warm and winters cool, and the breezes that sweep the hills help to keep the grapes healthy. The soils alternate between whitish calcareous marls and red clays that are richer in iron. It is often said that the former give finer, more taut wines and the latter wines with a little more body, although the aspect of each plot also plays an important part.

The Cortese grape

Cortese is a white variety from eastern Piedmont that has been known for centuries. It is productive, ripens in mid-season and keeps its acidity well, which makes it suited to fresh wines. In Gavi it is grown on its own: the DOCG requires the wine to be made entirely from Cortese. The variety is also found in other parts of Piedmont and around Lake Garda, but Gavi is its most prestigious appellation.

The appellation and its wines

Gavi became a DOC in 1974 and a DOCG in 1998. The wine may be labelled Gavi or Cortese di Gavi; when the grapes come from the municipality of Gavi itself it may carry the words del Comune di Gavi, popularly known as Gavi di Gavi. Most of the production consists of still, dry, young whites, pale in colour, with aromas of white flowers, apple, pear and citrus and a lively acidity that makes them refreshing. There are also Riserva versions, with longer ageing, and sparkling wines made both by bottle fermentation and in tank. Some producers ferment or age in wood, although the prevailing style seeks to preserve the freshness and aromatic delicacy of the grape, which pairs naturally with seafood and light dishes.
