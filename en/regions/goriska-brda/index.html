Goriška Brda

Goriška Brda, at the western tip of Slovenia, is a hilly area adjoining the Italian Collio, from which it is separated only by the border drawn after the Second World War. It is the land of Rebula and one of the cradles of long-macerated white wines.

History

The hills of Brda, whose name simply means “hills” in Slovenian, have a long agricultural tradition based on vines and fruit trees, especially cherries. For centuries, together with the Collio, they formed a single territory around the city of Gorizia, ruled by the Counts of Gorizia and later by the Habsburgs. The border established after the Second World War divided the area between Italy and Yugoslavia, and many families were left with vineyards on both sides of the line, a situation that still applies to some wineries today. In the Yugoslav period the local cooperative handled much of the production; since the 1990s many family wineries have emerged.

Climate and soils

Brda enjoys a transitional climate between Mediterranean and Alpine. The nearby Adriatic brings relatively mild winters and warm summers, while the Julian Alps to the north shelter the area from cold winds and encourage cool nights. Rainfall is fairly abundant. The characteristic soil is opoka or ponca, a flysch made of alternating layers of marl and sandstone that crumbles easily and holds water well. The hills, of modest height, are often cultivated in terraces.

Villages and vineyards

The district is part of the Primorska wine region. Its villages include Dobrovo, home to a castle and the local cooperative, Medana, Kojsko, Šmartno, Vipolže, Ceglo and Plešivo. Holdings are usually small and family-run, with plots spread across different slopes and exposures.

Grapes and styles

The emblematic variety is Rebula, the Ribolla Gialla of the Italian side, which gives everything from young, light, citrusy whites to age-worthy wines fermented in wood or macerated on their skins for weeks or months. This maceration practice, revived along the border from the 1990s, gives amber-coloured wines with a tannic texture and aromas of dried fruit, orange peel and herbs. Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Gris, Pinot Blanc, Sauvignonasse (the former Tocai or Friulano), Malvazija and the sweet Picolit are also grown, as well as reds from Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc, which give structured wines, often barrel-aged. White blends of several varieties are common, and sparkling wines are made, some of them based on Rebula.
