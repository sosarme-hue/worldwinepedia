San Juan

A province in western Argentina, north of Mendoza, and the country’s second-largest wine producer. Hot and very dry, it is known for its Syrah and for a viticulture ranging from the high volumes of the Tulum Valley to the high-altitude vineyards of Calingasta and Pedernal.

Territory and history

San Juan lies in the Cuyo region, at the foot of the Andes, between Mendoza to the south and La Rioja to the north. Vines reached the area in colonial times, and for centuries the province was, together with Mendoza, one of the country’s great winegrowing centres. After Mendoza it is the Argentine province with the largest vineyard area and the largest wine production. A significant share of its grapes does not go into bottled wine but into concentrated grape must, table grapes and raisins, activities in which San Juan plays a very prominent role. In recent decades, however, a growing number of wineries have committed to quality wines and to exploring new high-altitude valleys.

Climate

The climate is desert-like: rainfall is very scarce, the sun shines almost every day of the year and summers are hot, with a marked difference between day and night temperatures. The zonda, a hot, dry wind, sometimes descends from the Andes with great force. The dry air greatly limits vine diseases but makes it necessary to irrigate with snowmelt brought by the San Juan and Jáchal rivers, distributed through canals and ditches.

Valleys

The Tulum Valley, around the provincial capital, holds most of the vineyards; it is a relatively low, warm area with alluvial soils, where large volumes are produced. The Zonda and Ullum valleys, west of the city, are somewhat cooler and associated with more structured reds. Further west, in the heart of the mountains, the Calingasta and Pedernal valleys are home to high-altitude vineyards with cold nights and stony soils, which give fresher, tauter wines and have attracted the interest of producers from all over the country.

Grapes and styles

Syrah is the red variety most associated with San Juan, where it has adapted very well to the heat and gives wines with ripe black fruit, spice and soft tannins. Malbec, Bonarda, Cabernet Sauvignon and Tannat are also grown. Among the whites, the Muscats, such as Muscat of Alexandria, are important and are also used for must and raisins, as is Torrontés, along with Chardonnay and other varieties in the high valleys. Overall, the province offers everything from everyday wines to increasingly recognised high-altitude reds and whites.

Country
Argentina
Climate
Desert climate, very sunny and dry, with hot summers, a wide diurnal range and the zonda wind.
Soils
Alluvial in the Tulum Valley and poor and stony in the high valleys.
Styles
Ripe, spicy Syrah, Malbec and Bonarda reds, Muscat and Torrontés whites and high-altitude wines.
