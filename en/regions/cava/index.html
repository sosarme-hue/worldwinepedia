Designation of Origin (DO)

Cava

Cava is the designation of origin for Spanish sparkling wines made by the traditional method, with a second fermentation in bottle. Although production is concentrated in Penedès, around Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, its production area covers municipalities in several autonomous communities.

History

The production of sparkling wines by the traditional method began in Catalonia in the second half of the nineteenth century. In 1872 Josep Raventós, of the house of Codorníu, made the first bottles in Sant Sadurní d'Anoia following the method used in Champagne. After phylloxera, much of the Penedès vineyard was replanted with white varieties intended for sparkling wine. For decades these wines were known as champán or xampany, until the name cava was adopted, a reference to the underground cellars where the bottles are aged. The Cava Designation of Origin was regulated in 1986 and, unlike other designations, it is defined by the production method and by a list of authorised municipalities located in different regions. In 2019 a group of Penedès producers left the designation to create the collective brand Corpinnat.

Zones

The great majority of production is concentrated in Catalonia, above all in Penedès, but the protected area also includes municipalities in Aragón, La Rioja, Navarra, the Basque Country, the Valencian Community and Extremadura. A zoning system approved in 2020 distinguishes four main zones: Comtats de Barcelona, which accounts for most production; Valle del Ebro; Viñedos de Almendralejo, in Extremadura; and Zona de Levante, around Requena.

Production

Cava is made from a base wine to which the liqueur de tirage, a mixture of sugar and yeast, is added. The second fermentation takes place in the bottle itself, where the wine stays in contact with the lees throughout the ageing period. The bottles are then riddled, to gather the sediment in the neck, and disgorged to expel it. Depending on the amount of dosage added, Cava can be Brut Nature, Extra Brut, Brut, Extra Seco, Seco, Semiseco or Dulce.

Classification by ageing

Cava de Guarda requires a minimum of nine months' ageing in bottle. Cava de Guarda Superior groups the categories Reserva, with at least 18 months; Gran Reserva, with at least 30 months; and Cava de Paraje Calificado, with at least 36 months and from a specific named site. Cavas de Guarda Superior must state the vintage and meet additional requirements regarding vine age and yields, as well as the requirement for organic viticulture.

Grapes

The traditional white varieties are Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada, joined by Chardonnay and Malvasía (Subirat Parent). Grenache, Monastrell, Pinot Noir and Trepat, a native Catalan red variety, are used for rosés.

Country
Spain
Climate
Mainly Mediterranean in Penedès, with varied conditions in the other authorised areas.
Soils
In Penedès, mainly calcareous with loamy to clayey textures; varied in the other zones.
Styles
Traditional-method white and rosé sparkling wines, from Brut Nature to Dulce, in various ageing categories.
Grapes
Macabeo (Viura), Xarel·lo, Parellada, Chardonnay, Malvasia, Grenache, Monastrell (Mourvèdre), Pinot Noir
