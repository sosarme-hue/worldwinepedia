Rhône valley subregion

Northern Rhône

The Northern Rhône is the narrow strip of terraced vineyards that follows the river between Vienne and Valence. It is the home of Syrah in its most classic form, in appellations such as Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage and Cornas, and of the Viognier of Condrieu.

Territory and history

South of Lyon, the Rhône forces its way between the Massif Central to the west and the Alps to the east. Along this stretch, between Vienne and Valence, the vineyards cover slopes that are often very steep and have required the building of terraces held up by dry-stone walls. Viticulture here is ancient: the city of Vienne was an important Gallo-Roman centre and Latin authors mentioned its wines. The Northern Rhône accounts for a small share of the Rhône valley's production, far less than the Southern Rhône, but it is home to several of its most prestigious appellations.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental, with cold winters and warm summers, and a Mediterranean influence that increases towards the south. The mistral, a cold, dry northerly wind, keeps the vineyards healthy and reduces the risk of disease. The soils of the slopes are mainly granite and metamorphic rocks such as gneiss and schist, often weathered into a stony sand; in some areas there are deposits of loess and, on the terraces near the river, pebbles and alluvium.

Appellations

From north to south, on the right bank, Côte-Rôtie, around Ampuis, produces Syrah reds to which up to 20% Viognier may be added, traditionally co-fermented with the red grapes; its best-known slopes are the Côte Brune and the Côte Blonde. Condrieu is devoted to aromatic, unctuous Viognier whites, and within its area lies Château-Grillet, an appellation corresponding to a single estate. Saint-Joseph stretches along much of the right bank and produces Syrah reds and Marsanne and Roussanne whites. Opposite, above Tain-l'Hermitage on the left bank, rises the hill of Hermitage, whose powerful, long-lived Syrah reds may include up to 15% white grapes; its Marsanne and Roussanne whites are also highly regarded, and a rare straw wine is made as well. Around it, Crozes-Hermitage is the largest appellation of the Northern Rhône, on flatter land. To the south, Cornas produces reds solely from Syrah and Saint-Péray makes still and sparkling whites from Marsanne and Roussanne.

Grapes and styles

Syrah is the only red variety in the Northern Rhône appellations. It gives deeply coloured wines with aromas of black fruit, violet and pepper and, with age, notes of leather and black olive; on the best slopes they have a firm structure and great ageing potential. Among the whites, Viognier dominates in Condrieu and Château-Grillet, while Marsanne and Roussanne are used in Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph and Saint-Péray.
