Designation of Origin (DO)

Gran Canaria

Gran Canaria is the designation of origin covering the wines of the whole island of that name, which includes as a subzone the former Monte de Lentiscal designation. Its vineyards, spread across areas of very different altitudes, yield Listán Negro reds, whites and sweet Malvasía and Moscatel wines.

History

The vine reached Gran Canaria with the Castilian conquest at the end of the fifteenth century, and during the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries the island took part, alongside Tenerife and La Palma, in the trade in Canary wines to Europe and the Americas. One of the island's historic wine enclaves is Monte Lentiscal, around the Bandama caldera, a few kilometres from Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, where vines have been grown on volcanic soils for centuries. For much of the twentieth century viticulture on the island was reduced to a family activity geared to local consumption. At the end of that century the Monte de Lentiscal designation was recognised, and later the Gran Canaria designation was created, covering the whole island and incorporating the former as a subzone.

Territory and climate

The designation covers the entire island, although the vineyards are concentrated in certain areas: the north-east, around Santa Brígida, the Vega de San Mateo and Monte Lentiscal; the north, with municipalities such as Gáldar; and various mid-slope and upland areas of the interior and the south. Gran Canaria is an island of very rugged relief, which makes it possible to grow vines from levels close to the sea up to mountain areas above 1,000 metres. The climate is Atlantic subtropical, with mild temperatures all year round and a clear contrast between the north, wetter owing to the trade winds, and the drier, sunnier south.

Soils

The soils are volcanic in origin and highly diverse: in Bandama and Monte Lentiscal layers of lapilli or picón predominate, while elsewhere there are more developed, clayey volcanic soils. As in the rest of the Canaries, phylloxera never reached the island and the vines are grown ungrafted.

Grape varieties

The leading reds are Listán Negro, Negramoll, Tintilla, Vijariego Negro and Baboso Negro. The whites include Listán Blanco, Malvasía, Moscatel, Albillo, Vijariego Blanco and Verdello.

Styles

The designation produces young and aged reds, mainly from Listán Negro, fresh and fruity, which have traditionally been the predominant style of Monte Lentiscal. Dry, fruity whites, rosés and sweet wines from Malvasía and Moscatel are also made. Most of the production, which is small and made by small wineries, is drunk on the island itself.
