Pouilly-Fuissé

Pouilly-Fuissé is the most prestigious appellation of the Mâconnais, at the foot of the rocks of Solutré and Vergisson. It produces only Chardonnay whites, broad and mineral, and since the 2020 vintage has had vineyards classified as premier cru.

Location

Pouilly-Fuissé lies in the south of the Mâconnais, west of the town of Mâcon, and covers four communes: Fuissé, Solutré-Pouilly, Vergisson and Chaintré. The landscape is dominated by the rocks of Solutré and Vergisson, two great limestone escarpments rising above an amphitheatre of vine-covered hills. At the foot of the rock of Solutré lies an important prehistoric site, which gave its name to an Upper Palaeolithic culture, the Solutrean. Around it extends the Saint-Véran appellation, and to the east lie the small appellations of Pouilly-Loché and Pouilly-Vinzelles.

History and regulations

Pouilly-Fuissé was recognised as an AOC in the 1930s and for a long time was the best-known Mâconnais wine abroad, especially in the United States. Its producers worked for years to obtain a hierarchy of plots comparable to that of the Côte d'Or, and finally, from the 2020 vintage, some twenty climats were recognised as premier cru, the first in the whole of the Mâconnais. Wines from these plots may state their name on the label.

Climate and soils

The climate is moderately continental, with southern influences that favour good ripeness. The geology is complex: limestones of different Jurassic ages, marl, clay and, in some sectors, older rocks such as schist. The diversity of aspects, from sunny slopes to cooler inclines at the foot of the rocks, allows each village and each plot to show its own nuances. In general terms, wines from the lower, warmer sectors such as Chaintré are usually considered rounder, and those from the higher slopes of Vergisson tauter and more mineral.

Grape and style

The only authorised variety is Chardonnay. Pouilly-Fuissé wines are dry whites with more body and concentration than Mâcon-Villages, with aromas of white and yellow fruit, citrus, flowers, hazelnut and often a mineral note associated with the limestone. Many are fermented and aged in barrel, with varying proportions of new oak, while other producers prefer tanks or large casks to preserve freshness. The best, especially those from classified plots, can improve in bottle for several years.

Producers

The appellation brings together many independent growers, a few négociant houses and cooperative wineries. In recent decades, plot-by-plot work and estate bottling have helped to strengthen the area's image for quality.
