Paarl

Paarl, north of Stellenbosch, is one of the Cape’s historic districts, dominated by the great granite domes that give it its name. It produces a wide range of wines, mainly warm-climate reds from Syrah, Pinotage and Cabernet Sauvignon, and Chenin Blanc.

History

The name Paarl, “pearl” in Dutch, comes from the enormous granite rocks that rise above the town and which, according to tradition, glistened like pearls when the sun shone on them after rain. European settlers took up land in the Berg river valley in the late seventeenth century, and the town grew up around a long main street lined with Cape Dutch houses. Paarl also played an important part in the history of the Afrikaans language, which is commemorated by a large monument on the slopes of the mountain.

In the twentieth century the town became a key centre of the South African wine industry, since it became the headquarters of the KWV cooperative, founded in 1918, which for decades regulated the country’s wine production and prices. Large companies and historic wineries were also based in Paarl, and for a long time the Cape’s main wine auction was held here. Since the 1990s, with the liberalisation of the industry, numerous independent wineries and family estates bottling their own wines have emerged.

Climate and soils

Paarl lies further from the sea than Stellenbosch or Constantia, so its Mediterranean climate is warmer and drier in summer. Differences in altitude and aspect, from the floor of the Berg valley up to the slopes of Paarl Mountain and the Simonsberg, make it possible to grow very different varieties. The soils on the slopes are mainly decomposed granite, while on the valley floor and in other areas Table Mountain sandstone and Malmesbury shale predominate. The district includes wards such as Simonsberg-Paarl, Voor Paardeberg and Agter Paarl.

Styles

The warmth favours full-bodied reds: Syrah, often labelled Shiraz, Pinotage and Cabernet Sauvignon give ripe, spicy wines with soft tannins, and Mediterranean varieties such as Grenache, Mourvèdre and Cinsault are also grown. Chenin Blanc is the most important white, and Chardonnay and Viognier are also made. The region also has a long tradition of port-style fortified wines and brandy, a legacy of the era when the KWV steered production towards distillation. Many wineries in the district offer tastings and visits. In recent years several producers have revived old Chenin Blanc and Cinsault vineyards in the drier parts of the district, following the same trend as in neighbouring Swartland.
