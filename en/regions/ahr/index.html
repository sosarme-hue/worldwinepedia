Ahr

A small valley south of Bonn, one of Germany’s northernmost wine regions and, paradoxically, one of its great red-wine areas. Its Spätburgunder grows on very steep slate slopes.

A red-wine valley in the north

The Ahr is a tributary of the Rhine that joins it shortly before Bonn, and its narrow, winding valley is home to one of Germany’s thirteen wine regions. It is also one of the smallest and northernmost, which makes it all the more striking that most of its vineyard is planted with red grapes. Spätburgunder, the German name for Pinot Noir, is the dominant variety, followed by Frühburgunder, an early-ripening mutation, and Portugieser. Among the whites, Riesling and Weissburgunder (Pinot Blanc) stand out.

Climate and soils

The secret of this paradox lies in the topography. The valley is deep and sheltered from cold winds, and many vineyards sit on very steep south-facing slopes, often on terraces held up by stone walls. The soils, dark slate and greywacke, absorb the sun’s heat during the day and release it at night, creating a microclimate noticeably warmer than that of the surrounding areas. In the lower part of the valley, towards Bad Neuenahr-Ahrweiler, there are also loess and alluvial soils, which give somewhat rounder wines.

Villages and structure

The vineyards stretch along the lower course of the river, through villages such as Altenahr, Mayschoss, Rech, Dernau, Marienthal, Walporzheim and Ahrweiler. The valley has a strong cooperative tradition, and Mayschoss is home to one of the oldest wine cooperatives in Germany. Alongside the cooperatives are family estates which, since the late twentieth century, have brought Ahr reds international recognition, with single-vineyard wines classified by the VDP growers’ association.

The 2021 flood

In July 2021 torrential rain caused a catastrophic flood of the Ahr that claimed many lives and destroyed houses, bridges, wineries and wine stocks throughout the valley. The hillside vineyards suffered less, but many wineries lost their buildings and equipment. Reconstruction has been long and has been supported by a great wave of solidarity from across Germany.

Styles

Ahr Spätburgunder is typically a medium-bodied red with red and black fruit, spicy and sometimes smoky notes, fresh acidity and, in the best cases, barrel ageing. Frühburgunder is softer and fruitier, and the slate-grown Rieslings, though scarce, show tension and mineral character.

Country
Germany
Climate
Cool continental, softened by the shelter of the valley and the heat stored by the slate slopes.
Soils
Slate and greywacke on steep slopes, with loess and alluvium in the lower valley.
Styles
Mainly medium-bodied, fresh Spätburgunder and Frühburgunder reds, plus some Riesling.
Grapes
Pinot Noir, Riesling, Pinot Blanc
