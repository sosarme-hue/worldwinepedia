Saint-Émilion

Saint-Émilion, on the right bank of the Dordogne, is one of the great appellations of Bordeaux, known for its reds dominated by Merlot and Cabernet Franc. Its medieval town and vineyard landscape are on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

Viticulture around Saint-Émilion dates back to Roman times. The town, named after the monk Émilion, who according to tradition settled there in the eighth century, grew in the Middle Ages around its convents and its monolithic church, carved out of the limestone rock. The quarries from which stone was cut to build the town and part of Bordeaux left a network of underground galleries that now serve as cellars for many estates. In 1999 the former jurisdiction of Saint-Émilion was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as a cultural wine landscape. The Jurade, a brotherhood linked to the old municipal institution, was revived in 1948.

Climate and soils

The climate is oceanic, slightly more continental than in the Médoc because of the greater distance from the sea. Saint-Émilion's strength lies in the variety of its soils: a limestone plateau around the town; the côtes, clay-limestone slopes running down to the valley; areas of gravel and sand to the north-west, on the border with Pomerol; and sandy and alluvial land on the plain near the Dordogne. These cool, water-retentive soils suit Merlot, which ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon.

Appellations

There are two appellations covering the same area: Saint-Émilion and Saint-Émilion Grand Cru, the latter with stricter yield and ageing requirements. Both are for red wine only. North of the small Barbanne river lie four so-called satellite appellations: Montagne-Saint-Émilion, Lussac-Saint-Émilion, Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion and Saint-Georges-Saint-Émilion.

The classification

Unlike the 1855 classification in the Médoc, the Saint-Émilion classification, created in 1955, is designed to be revised roughly every ten years, so estates can be promoted or demoted. It distinguishes between Premiers Grands Crus Classés, divided into categories A and B, and Grands Crus Classés. Only wines from the Saint-Émilion Grand Cru appellation are eligible. The revisions have given rise to legal challenges, and at the time of the 2022 revision some very well-known estates, such as Château Cheval Blanc, Château Ausone and Château Angélus, chose not to apply.

Grapes and styles

Merlot is the dominant variety, accompanied by Cabernet Franc, known locally as Bouchet, and to a lesser extent by Cabernet Sauvignon. The wines tend to be rounder and fleshier than those of the Médoc, although those from the limestone plateau can show remarkable freshness and firmness.
