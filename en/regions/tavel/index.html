Tavel

Tavel, on the right bank of the Rhône near Avignon, is an appellation devoted exclusively to rosé. Its wines, deep in colour and well structured, are food rosés made to accompany a meal.

Location

Tavel is a village in the Gard department, on the right bank of the Rhône, a few kilometres north-west of Avignon and opposite Châteauneuf-du-Pape. The appellation covers the commune of Tavel and a small part of neighbouring Roquemaure. To the north it borders Lirac, another Southern Rhône cru that makes reds, rosés and whites. Tavel is the only Rhône appellation devoted solely to rosé and one of the few in France with that distinction.

History

Tavel wines have enjoyed a reputation for centuries. The area was part of the old right-bank “Côte du Rhône”, whose wines were traded via the river, and it is often recalled that the popes of Avignon and later the French court appreciated this wine, although such references come largely from tradition. Tavel was one of the first appellations recognised in the Rhône valley, in the 1930s, and has kept its exclusive dedication to rosé ever since.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, hot and dry, with the mistral as the prevailing wind. Three main soil types are distinguished: terraces of rounded pebbles, similar to those of Châteauneuf-du-Pape; areas of sand; and limestone ground covered with flat stone slabs called lauzes. Many producers blend grapes from the different soils to balance structure, fruit and freshness.

Grapes and winemaking

The main variety is Grenache, which may not exceed a maximum share of the vineyard, accompanied by Cinsault, Clairette, Bourboulenc, Picpoul, Syrah, Mourvèdre and Carignan, among others. Winemaking traditionally combines maceration of the grapes on their skins for a longer period than for most rosés with bleeding the must, that is, drawing off the coloured juice by gravity. The result is a deeply coloured rosé, between raspberry and pale ruby, much more intense than the pale rosés of Provence.

Style and drinking

Tavel wines are dry rosés with aromas of red berries, stone fruit, spice and herbs, medium body and some tannic structure. Although most are drunk in their first few years, some develop well for a time and acquire spicier, candied-fruit notes. Because of their body they are considered very versatile rosés at the table, suited to Mediterranean cooking, spiced dishes and white meats.
