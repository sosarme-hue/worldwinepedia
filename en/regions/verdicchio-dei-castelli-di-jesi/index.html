Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi

Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is the best-known white-wine appellation of the Marche. It is made from Verdicchio on the hills of the province of Ancona, around the town of Jesi, and its Riserva version holds DOCG status.

Location

The appellation extends over the inland hills of the province of Ancona, in the central Marche, along the valley of the Esino river and its tributaries. Its name refers to the castelli, the fortified villages that in the Middle Ages depended on the town of Jesi and still crown many of the hills, such as Cupramontana, Staffolo, Montecarotto, Serra de' Conti and San Paolo di Jesi. The oldest, most traditional zone may use the term Classico. Further inland, in an enclosed Apennine valley, lies another appellation for the same grape, Verdicchio di Matelica, which is separate.

History

Verdicchio has been grown in the Marche for centuries. The Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC was recognised in 1968. In the mid-twentieth century the wine became known outside the region, partly thanks to an amphora-shaped bottle that served as its emblem for decades. Over time many producers switched to conventional bottles and began to emphasise the grape's ability to give quality wines with some ageing potential. In 2010 the Riserva version was granted DOCG status under the name Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean with continental influences, with the Adriatic nearby on one side and the Apennines on the other. Summers are warm and nights relatively cool in the hills. The soils are mainly clay and limestone, with patches of sand and marl. The right bank of the Esino, higher and better exposed, has traditionally been prized.

The grape and its styles

Verdicchio is a white variety with good acidity and structure, whose name is linked to the greenish tints of its grapes and wines. It must make up the great majority of the blend, and many wines are made from it alone. The most widespread style is a dry white, straw-coloured with green glints, with aromas of white flowers, almond, aniseed and white-fleshed fruit, and a characteristic slightly bitter finish. The Superiore version requires a higher alcohol level, and the Riserva DOCG longer ageing. The appellation also provides for sparkling wines and a sweet Passito. The best Verdicchio wines develop well in bottle, acquiring notes of honey and nuts over the years, which has helped the grape gain recognition as one of the finest native white varieties of central Italy.
