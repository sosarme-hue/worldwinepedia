Mittelrhein

The Middle Rhine region, along the spectacular gorge between Bingen and the outskirts of Bonn. Its slate vineyards on dizzying slopes produce mainly crisp Riesling, in a landscape inscribed as a World Heritage Site.

A historic landscape

The Mittelrhein, or Middle Rhine, follows the stretch where the great river cuts through the Rhenish Slate Mountains, from Bingen and Rüdesheim in the south to the outskirts of Bonn and the Siebengebirge in the north. It is the Rhine of the postcards: a deep, narrow valley dotted with medieval castles on the crags, villages of half-timbered houses and vineyards clinging to slopes that in places are far steeper than fifty per cent. The stretch between Bingen and Koblenz, known as the Upper Middle Rhine Valley, was inscribed by UNESCO on the World Heritage List as a cultural landscape in 2002, and the Loreley rock, linked to legends and Romantic poems, is its best-known symbol.

A small and vulnerable region

Despite its long winegrowing history, the Mittelrhein is today one of the smallest regions in Germany. Farming such steep slopes requires almost all work to be done by hand, and during the twentieth century many plots were abandoned as younger generations found more profitable alternatives; in some stretches forest has reclaimed old terraces. Several associations and producers work to keep the most emblematic vineyards in cultivation, aware that they are an inseparable part of the landscape.

Climate and soils

The climate is cool, but the river itself acts as a heat regulator and reflects light, and south-facing slopes capture warmth well. The soils are mainly slate and greywacke, sometimes with loess and, in the north, volcanic material. Slate warms the soil and drains well, ideal conditions for Riesling in a marginal climate.

Villages and vineyards

Leading wine villages include Bacharach, with vineyards such as Hahn and Posten; Oberwesel; and Boppard, whose great amphitheatre of vines, the Bopparder Hamm, lies on a broad bend of the river. On the right bank are places such as Kaub and Sankt Goarshausen, and in the north the region continues towards Leutesdorf and Königswinter.

Grapes and styles

Riesling covers most of the vineyard area and gives light- to medium-bodied wines with very lively acidity, notes of stone fruit and citrus and a marked mineral character; they are made both dry and with residual sweetness, along with some sweet late-harvest wines. Spätburgunder (Pinot Noir), Müller-Thurgau and Weissburgunder (Pinot Blanc) complete the range, together with a tradition of quality sparkling wine, or Sekt.
