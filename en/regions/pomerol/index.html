Pomerol

Pomerol is a small appellation on the right bank of Bordeaux, next to Libourne, devoted to Merlot-based reds with some Cabernet Franc. It has never had an official classification, yet it is home to some of the region's most sought-after wines.

History

Vines were already grown in Pomerol in Roman times, and in the Middle Ages the Knights Hospitaller of St John of Jerusalem had a commandery there that sheltered pilgrims on the Way of St James. For a long time, however, its wines were sold in the shadow of those of Saint-Émilion, and Pomerol does not appear in the 1855 classification, which was limited to the Médoc, Graves and Sauternes. The appellation d'origine contrôlée was recognised in 1936. Its international fame is relatively recent: it became established mainly in the second half of the twentieth century, driven by the négociants of Libourne, a port town at the confluence of the Dordogne and the Isle.

Territory, climate and soils

Pomerol is a small area almost entirely covered by vineyards, with no real town centre apart from its church and a few scattered hamlets. It borders Saint-Émilion to the east and, across the Barbanne stream to the north, Lalande-de-Pomerol. The climate is oceanic, as in the rest of the right bank. The soils form a gentle plateau on which the best plots combine gravel and clay; towards the west and south, in the direction of Libourne, they become sandier. The subsoil often contains a layer rich in iron oxides, known locally as crasse de fer. On the highest part of the plateau a very dense bluish clay comes to the surface, and it is on this clay that Pétrus stands.

Estates

The pattern of ownership is unusual: small estates predominate, many of them family-run, and there are none of the monumental châteaux found in the Médoc. Among the best-known properties are Pétrus, Le Pin, Vieux Château Certan, Château Lafleur, Château Trotanoy, Château L'Évangile and Château La Conseillante. Several of them produce very small volumes, which adds to their scarcity on the market.

No classification

Unlike its neighbours, Pomerol has never established an official classification of its estates. The reputation of each wine therefore depends on its track record and on recognition by the market, not on an administrative hierarchy.

Grapes and styles

Merlot is the dominant variety, favoured by the water-retentive clay soils and by its early ripening. It is complemented by Cabernet Franc and, in small proportions, Cabernet Sauvignon. The appellation is for red wine only. Pomerol wines are noted for their velvety texture, ripe fruit and supple tannins, although the finest retain a structure that allows them to develop over many years.
