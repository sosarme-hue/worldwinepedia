Designation of Origin (DO)

Valencia

Valencia is a designation of origin in the Valencian Community that covers various districts of the province of Valencia, organised into subzones. It is known for its Moscatel de Valencia, a sweet wine with a centuries-old tradition, and also produces Mediterranean-style whites, rosés and reds.

History

Valencian viticulture has very ancient roots, and vine growing continued during the period of Islamic rule and after the Christian conquest of the thirteenth century. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the port of Valencia became a major centre for wine exports, especially when phylloxera devastated the French vineyards and demand for Spanish wine rose. Many trading houses set up in the Grao, the harbour district, and exported bulk wine to numerous countries. For much of the twentieth century production was dominated by bulk wines and cooperatives, and in recent decades a growing number of wineries have committed to quality bottled wines.

Subzones and territory

The designation is organised into several subzones with very different conditions. Alto Turia, in the north-west, is the highest and coolest and specialises in white wines. Valentino, in the centre of the province, occupies hills of medium altitude. Moscatel de Valencia comprises municipalities around towns such as Cheste, Godelleta and Turís, devoted to sweet Moscatel wine. Clariano, in the south of the province, in districts such as the Vall d'Albaida and La Costera, has a warmer climate and a strong tradition of red wines. The overall climate is Mediterranean, with hot summers and mild winters, becoming more continental as altitude increases inland.

Soils

Brown or reddish soils rich in limestone predominate, with variable proportions of clay and sand, generally low in organic matter.

Grape varieties

The most characteristic variety is Muscat of Alexandria, the basis of Moscatel de Valencia. The leading whites also include Merseguera, a traditional variety of the area, Malvasía, Macabeo, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc. Among the reds, Monastrell, Garnacha Tintorera, Tempranillo, Garnacha, Bobal, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah stand out.

Styles

The best-known wine is Moscatel de Valencia, a sweet fortified wine traditionally made from Muscat must whose fermentation is stopped by adding grape spirit, which preserves the sugar and the variety's intense floral and grapey aromas. The designation also produces young, aromatic whites, rosés and young and aged reds, as well as semi-sparkling and sparkling wines. Its wines, in many cases produced in large volumes, have always had a strong export focus.
