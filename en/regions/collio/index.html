Controlled Designation of Origin (Collio Goriziano or Collio DOC)

Collio

Collio is a hilly area in the province of Gorizia, in Friuli-Venezia Giulia, along the Slovenian border, famous for white wines grown on marl and sandstone soils known as ponca.

Location and history

Collio occupies an arc of hills in the province of Gorizia, between the Isonzo and Judrio rivers, in Italy's far north-east. Across the border it continues into Slovenia under the name Goriška Brda, and the two areas actually form a single geographical unit, divided by the border drawn after the Second World War. For centuries the area belonged to the Habsburg domains, and local viticulture developed in contact with Italian, Slovene and Germanic cultures. The DOC, officially known as Collio Goriziano or simply Collio, was recognised in 1968 and is one of the oldest in the region.

Climate

Collio enjoys a privileged position: the Julian Alps shelter it from cold northern winds, while the Adriatic Sea, a few dozen kilometres away, brings milder influences. The result is a climate with relatively mild winters, warm summers and a marked difference between day and night temperatures, which helps white grapes to retain their aromas. Rainfall is relatively abundant.

Soils

The characteristic soil is ponca, the local name for a flysch made of alternating layers of marl and sandstone of marine origin. It breaks up easily when exposed to air and rain and gives poor soils that can nonetheless retain some moisture. Terraces were built to cultivate its often steep slopes. The structure and savoury quality of the area's wines are attributed to this soil.

Grapes and styles

Collio is above all white-wine country. The most important native varieties are Friulano, formerly called Tocai Friulano, Ribolla Gialla and Malvasia Istriana. Alongside them, international varieties such as Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio, Pinot Bianco and Chardonnay have been grown since the nineteenth century. A type particular to the area is Collio Bianco, which may be made as a blend of local and international varieties. Reds, mainly from Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Cabernet Sauvignon, play a secondary role. In the Oslavia area, some producers revived the practice of long skin maceration for Ribolla, sometimes in amphorae, and helped to spread so-called orange wines internationally. Collio whites usually combine intense aromas, good structure and balanced acidity, and the best of them are known for ageing well in bottle.

Country
Italy
Climate
Temperate, sheltered by the Julian Alps and softened by the Adriatic, with good day-night swings.
Soils
Ponca: flysch of alternating marine marls and sandstones, often terraced.
Styles
Aromatic, structured whites, varietal or blended (Collio Bianco), skin-contact whites and some reds.
