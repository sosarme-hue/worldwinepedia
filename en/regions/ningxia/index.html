Wine region (geographical indication of the Helan Mountains’ eastern foothills)

Ningxia

A region in north-west China lying between the Yellow River and the Helan Mountains, which since the early twenty-first century has become one of the country’s most dynamic wine areas. Its dry, sunny, continental climate produces above all reds from Cabernet Sauvignon and other Bordeaux varieties.

History

The Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, in north-west China, has a long farming tradition thanks to irrigation with water from the Yellow River. Viticulture for wine began to develop in the second half of the twentieth century, with state farms planting vines, but its great expansion came from the early years of the twenty-first century, with strong support from the regional authorities. In a short time thousands of hectares were planted along the eastern foothills of the Helan Mountains and numerous wineries were created, from small family projects such as Silver Heights and Helan Qingxue to investments by large national and international groups, among them Changyu, Pernod Ricard and Moët Hennessy, which makes sparkling wine here under the Chandon brand.

The area of the eastern foothills of the Helan Mountains is recognised as a geographical indication, and the regional authorities have launched their own winery classification system to promote quality.

Climate and soils

The vineyards stretch along a strip between the Yellow River to the east and the Helan range to the west, at altitudes of around one thousand to twelve hundred metres. The mountains shield the area from cold winds and sand blowing from the deserts to the west. The climate is continental and semi-arid: rainfall is low and concentrated in summer, sunshine is very abundant, and the difference between day and night temperatures is large, which helps the grapes build up colour and aroma. Viticulture depends on irrigation with water from the Yellow River.

The main challenge is winter, very cold and dry, when temperatures often fall far below freezing. To protect the vines, in most vineyards they are bent over and buried under soil every autumn and uncovered in spring, a costly task that shapes the way the vines are trained. Spring frosts and summer storms are further risks. The soils are poor in organic matter and are usually sandy, stony or made of alluvial gravel, with good drainage.

Areas

The wineries are grouped in several sub-zones along the Helan foothills, such as those near the regional capital, Yinchuan, and those of Helan, Yongning, Qingtongxia and Hongsipu, with differences in altitude, soil and exposure.

Grape varieties and styles

Cabernet Sauvignon is the most widely planted variety and the basis of most prestige reds, often blended with Merlot, Cabernet Franc or Carménère, known locally as Cabernet Gernischt. Marselan has gained ground in recent years, and Syrah and Pinot Noir are also grown. Among the whites, Chardonnay, Riesling and Welschriesling stand out. The most common style is the Bordeaux-style red, with ripe fruit and firm tannins, aged in oak barrels, alongside whites, rosés and traditional-method sparkling wines. There are no compulsory ageing rules comparable to European ones; each winery defines its own categories.

Climate

Barrel-aged Bordeaux-style reds, as well as whites, rosés and traditional-method sparkling wines.
