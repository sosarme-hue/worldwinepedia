Maremma

The Maremma is the coastal strip of southern Tuscany, mainly in the province of Grosseto. Once a land of marshes, it has become one of the region's most dynamic wine areas, with reds from Sangiovese and international varieties and whites from Vermentino.

Territory and history

The name Maremma refers to the broad coastal area that stretches across southern Tuscany and northern Lazio. In wine terms it usually means above all the Maremma of Grosseto, although there is traditionally also a Maremma of Livorno, home to Bolgheri. For centuries it was a land of marshes, sparsely populated and afflicted by malaria, largely given over to extensive livestock farming. Major land-reclamation works in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries transformed the landscape and made agricultural development possible. In wine, the area rose to prominence from the last decades of the twentieth century, when many producers, including some from other parts of Tuscany, bought land and planted vineyards.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, warmer and drier than that of the hills of inland Tuscany, with the moderating influence of the Tyrrhenian Sea and breezes that ventilate the vineyards. Further inland, the hills and the slopes of Monte Amiata bring greater altitude and freshness. The soils are very diverse: sands and clays near the coast, stony and calcareous soils in the hills, and soils of volcanic origin around Pitigliano and Sovana.

Appellations

The Maremma Toscana DOC, which grew out of an earlier geographical indication and gained DOC status in 2011, covers the province of Grosseto and embraces a wide range of wines. The best-known appellation of the area is the Morellino di Scansano DOCG, a red based on Sangiovese, known locally as Morellino. Other appellations include the Montecucco Sangiovese DOCG and the Montecucco, Monteregio di Massa Marittima, Sovana, Bianco di Pitigliano, Ansonica Costa dell'Argentario and Capalbio DOCs.

Grapes and styles

Sangiovese is the traditional red variety, accompanied by Ciliegiolo, Alicante, the local name for Grenache, and other local grapes. Since the late twentieth century, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Cabernet Franc have spread, alone or blended with Sangiovese. Among the whites, Vermentino stands out, giving aromatic, savoury whites, alongside Trebbiano and Ansonica on the coast. Maremma reds usually show riper fruit and softer tannins than those of Chianti, reflecting a warmer climate. The variety of soils, altitudes and appellations means that the area produces everything from simple, fruity wines for early drinking to ambitious reds intended for long ageing.
