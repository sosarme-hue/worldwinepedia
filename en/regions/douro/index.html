Douro

The Douro valley, in northern Portugal, is the birthplace of Port and one of the oldest demarcated wine regions in the world. Under the Douro DOC it also produces still reds and whites of growing prestige.

History

Winegrowing in the Douro valley is very old, but its great expansion came with the Port trade with England from the seventeenth century onwards. In 1756 the Marquis of Pombal created the Companhia Geral da Agricultura das Vinhas do Alto Douro and had the region marked out with stone posts, the so-called marcos pombalinos, making it one of the first wine areas in the world with boundaries laid down by law. For centuries the Douro produced mainly wine for Port, and still wines played a secondary role. This began to change in the mid-twentieth century, with pioneering wines such as Barca Velha, and accelerated from the late 1980s, when many quintas began making and bottling their own reds and whites. In 2001 UNESCO inscribed the Alto Douro Wine Region on the World Heritage List as a cultural landscape.

Territory and climate

The region follows the course of the Douro river and its tributaries from near Mesão Frio to the Spanish border, and is divided into three subregions. The Baixo Corgo, in the west, is the coolest and wettest; the Cima Corgo, centred around Pinhão, is home to many of the best-known quintas; and the Douro Superior, the easternmost, is the driest and hottest. The Marão and Montemuro ranges shelter the valley from Atlantic winds, resulting in very hot, dry summers and cold winters.

Soils and landscape

Schist predominates, a rock that splits into vertical layers through which roots push down in search of water, with some granite intrusions. The steep slopes made terracing necessary: the old socalcos, held up by dry-stone walls; the patamares, earth terraces cut by machine since the 1970s; and vertical planting, or vinha ao alto, on less steep hillsides.

Grapes and styles

Many native varieties are grown, often interplanted in the old vineyards. The leading reds are Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz (Tempranillo), Tinta Barroca, Tinto Cão and Sousão. Among the whites are Malvasia Fina, Viosinho, Gouveio (Godello), Rabigato and Códega do Larinho. Douro DOC reds tend to be concentrated and structured, with black fruit and floral notes, and many are aged in barrel. The whites, often from high-altitude vineyards, have gained freshness and recognition. The region is regulated by the Instituto dos Vinhos do Douro e do Porto, which manages both the Douro DOC and the Porto DOC.

Country
Portugal
Climate
Continental, with very hot, dry summers and cold winters, cooler and wetter in the Baixo Corgo and more arid in the Douro Superior.
Soils
Schist on terraced slopes, with some granite areas.
Styles
Structured reds, often barrel-aged, and fresh high-altitude whites; the same area produces Port.
Grapes
Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tempranillo, Godello, Malvasia
