Condrieu

Condrieu, south of Côte-Rôtie, is the benchmark appellation for Viognier. Its very aromatic, unctuous whites come from terraced granite slopes above the Rhône.

Location

Condrieu lies on the right bank of the Rhône, immediately south of Côte-Rôtie, and spreads over several communes in the Rhône, Loire and Ardèche departments, around the village that gives it its name. The vineyards occupy steep slopes, often on dry-stone terraces, facing mostly south and south-east. Within the area lies Château-Grillet, a small estate that forms a separate appellation in its own right, also devoted to Viognier.

History

Viognier has been grown in this part of the valley for a very long time, although its exact origin is not known with certainty. In the mid-twentieth century the variety came close to disappearing: only a few hectares of vineyard remained in Condrieu, because terrace farming was very hard and unprofitable, and many plots were abandoned. From the 1980s and 1990s onwards, international interest in Viognier made it possible to replant the slopes and restore the appellation's prestige. At the same time the variety spread across southern France and to other countries, but Condrieu is still regarded as its benchmark expression.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental with a Mediterranean influence that becomes noticeable further south. The slopes are sheltered from cold winds and receive plenty of light. The soils are granite and related rocks, with a surface layer of decomposed granite, sandy and very poor, known locally as arzelle. These soils drain quickly and force the vine to send its roots deep.

Grape and style

The only authorised variety is Viognier, a grape with irregular yields and susceptible to disease. Condrieu wines are dry whites of great aromatic intensity, with notes of apricot, peach, white flowers, violet and spice; on the palate they are broad and unctuous, with moderate acidity. Some producers ferment or age them partly in barrel, while others prefer tanks to preserve the fruit. Most are drunk in their first few years, although the best can develop somewhat longer. The rules also allow wines with residual sugar and late-harvest wines, an old tradition of the area that is now a minority practice.

Producers

Among the producers who contributed to the appellation's revival was Georges Vernay, whose estate is still active. Today both local growers and large Rhône houses, such as Guigal and Chapoutier, work in Condrieu.
