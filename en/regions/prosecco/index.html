DOC and DOCG

Prosecco

Prosecco is a wine, usually sparkling, made mainly from the Glera grape in Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia. Its historic heart lies in the hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, which hold DOCG status.

History

The name comes from the village of Prosecco, near Trieste, and was long used for the grape as well. Cultivation became established in the hills of the province of Treviso, between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, where a school of oenology was founded in 1876 that contributed to modernising the area. During the twentieth century the second fermentation in large tanks was perfected, making it possible to produce a fresh, fruity sparkling wine. The system was reorganised in 2009: the name Prosecco became exclusively a geographical designation, the grape took back the name Glera, the broad Prosecco DOC was created, and the historic areas of Conegliano Valdobbiadene and Asolo were raised to DOCG. In 2019 UNESCO added the Prosecco Hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene to the World Heritage List.

Designations

The Prosecco DOC covers nine provinces: Treviso, Venice, Vicenza, Padua and Belluno in Veneto, and Trieste, Udine, Gorizia and Pordenone in Friuli Venezia Giulia; most of its vineyards are on the plain. The Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco Superiore DOCG comprises fifteen hill municipalities, including the Cartizze cru in Valdobbiadene and the Rive, which designate wines from a specific municipality or hamlet. The Asolo Prosecco DOCG lies in the hills around Asolo.

Climate and soils

The climate is moderately continental, with cool winters and warm summers; in the DOCG area the hills, sheltered by the Prealps, enjoy good ventilation and temperature swings that favour aromatic freshness. Hill soils are of marine and glacial origin, with marl, sandstone and conglomerate, on slopes that are often very steep; on the plain, alluvial soils predominate.

Grape and winemaking

Glera, an aromatic and productive white variety, must make up at least 85% of the wine, and may be complemented by varieties such as Verdiso, Bianchetta Trevigiana, Perera or Chardonnay. Most Prosecco is made by the Martinotti or Charmat method, with the second fermentation in pressurised tanks, which preserves the grape's primary aromas. There are fully sparkling, semi-sparkling (frizzante) and still versions, and in the DOCG also the sui lieviti version, refermented in bottle. Since 2020 the DOC has allowed Prosecco Rosé, made with Pinot Noir.

Style

It is a light, fruity sparkling wine with aromas of apple, pear, citrus and white flowers. Depending on its sugar content it may be Brut, Extra Dry or Dry, among other categories.

Country
Italy
Climate
Moderately continental, with hills sheltered by the Prealps and marked temperature swings in the DOCG area.
Soils
Marl, sandstone and conglomerate of marine and glacial origin in the hills; alluvial soils on the plain.
Styles
Charmat-method sparkling wines (Brut, Extra Dry, Dry), frizzante, still, sui lieviti and rosé.

Glera, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir
