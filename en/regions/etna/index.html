Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC)

Etna

The Etna DOC, on the slopes of the Sicilian volcano, produces Nerello Mascalese reds and Carricante whites from high-altitude vineyards on volcanic soils. Recognised in 1968, it was Sicily's first DOC.

History

Etna, in north-eastern Sicily, is the highest active volcano in Europe, and its slopes have been cultivated since antiquity. In the nineteenth century viticulture on the volcano went through a period of great expansion, and the area still has many palmenti, old stone wineries where grapes were trodden and fermented. After phylloxera, the crises of the twentieth century and the abandonment of many terraces, the vineyard area shrank considerably. The Etna DOC was recognised in 1968, the first in Sicily. Since the late twentieth century, and especially from the 2000s onwards, local producers and others from different regions and countries have driven a remarkable revival of the area and its old vineyards.

Territory and climate

The designation forms an arc around the volcano, on its northern, eastern and southern to south-western slopes. Vineyards lie at altitudes of roughly 400 to more than 1,000 metres, giving the area a mountain climate within a Mediterranean setting: warm summers with cool nights, marked temperature swings and a harvest that often extends into October. The northern slope, with municipalities such as Randazzo, Castiglione di Sicilia and Linguaglossa, is known for its reds; the eastern slope, closer to the sea and wetter, for its whites, particularly in Milo. Rainfall is higher than across most of the island.

Soils

The soils derive from lava flows and volcanic ash of different ages: they are sandy, loose, mineral-rich and well drained. Their composition changes from one flow to another, which has led to the official delimitation of more than a hundred contrade, sites whose names may appear on the label. The sandy nature of some soils has allowed old ungrafted vines to survive.

Grapes

Nerello Mascalese is the main red grape, and Etna Rosso must contain at least 80%, which may be completed with Nerello Cappuccio. Among the whites, Carricante is the basis of Etna Bianco, with a minimum of 60%, and of Etna Bianco Superiore, reserved for the municipality of Milo, with at least 80%; it is joined mainly by Catarratto and other local varieties. Vines are traditionally trained as bush vines, the alberello, supported by chestnut stakes, on terraces of volcanic stone.

Styles

Etna Rosso is usually a pale- to medium-coloured red with red fruit, floral, spicy and ashy notes, lively acidity and fine but firm tannins. Etna Bianco is a dry white with marked acidity, aromas of citrus and apple and a profile often described as saline. The DOC also includes rosé and sparkling wines.
