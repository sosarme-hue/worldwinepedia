Pessac-Léognan

Pessac-Léognan is the appellation created in 1987 in the northern part of Graves, on the outskirts of the city of Bordeaux. It produces reds and dry whites and includes all the estates in the Graves classification, among them Château Haut-Brion.

History

The Graves area, named after its gravel soils, is one of the historic cradles of Bordeaux wine: its vineyards, very close to the city, supplied much of the trade with England in the Middle Ages. Some estates have very old origins, such as Château Pape Clément, linked to Bertrand de Got, archbishop of Bordeaux and later pope as Clement V. In the seventeenth century the Pontac family, owners of Haut-Brion, were among the first to sell their wine under the name of the estate, and the English diarist Samuel Pepys recorded in 1663 that he had drunk in London a wine called Ho Bryan. In 1855 Haut-Brion was the only red wine from outside the Médoc to be included among the first growths.

Creation of the appellation

During the twentieth century the urban growth of Bordeaux considerably reduced the vineyard area in the municipalities closest to the city. To set apart the northern part of Graves, regarded as its finest land, the Pessac-Léognan appellation was created in 1987, covering ten or so municipalities, including Pessac, Talence, Léognan, Martillac and Villenave-d'Ornon. The Graves appellation still exists for the southern part of the area, which stretches towards Langon.

Climate and soils

The climate is temperate and oceanic, with a slight urban influence on the vineyards nearest the city and shelter from the Landes forest to the west. The soils combine gravel, sometimes forming well-drained mounds, with sand and clay over varied subsoils.

The Graves classification

The classification of Graves wines was established in 1953 for reds and completed in 1959 with the addition of whites. Unlike that of 1855, it has no tiers: all estates bear the designation cru classé de Graves, for their red, their white or both. All the classified estates now lie within the Pessac-Léognan appellation; among them are Château Haut-Brion, Château La Mission Haut-Brion, Château Pape Clément, Château Haut-Bailly, Château Smith Haut Lafitte, Domaine de Chevalier and Château Carbonnieux.

Grapes and styles

The reds, which make up most of the production, are based on Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, with Cabernet Franc and, to a lesser extent, Petit Verdot and Malbec. The dry whites, made from Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon, are often fermented and aged in barrel, and rank among the most highly regarded whites of Bordeaux for their ability to age.
