Leyda Valley

A coastal zone of the San Antonio Valley, in the Valparaíso region, very close to the Pacific Ocean. Its cool climate, shaped by the Humboldt Current, has made it a Chilean benchmark for Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

A recent viticulture

The Leyda Valley is a young wine area. Located in the Coastal Range, south of the Casablanca Valley and a few kilometres from the port of San Antonio, it was until the late twentieth century a land of dry farming and livestock, where the lack of water made planting vineyards unthinkable. In the late 1990s a group of producers undertook the construction of a pipeline to bring water from the Maipo river, which made it possible to irrigate new plantings. Since then the valley has grown rapidly and earned a solid reputation among Chile’s cool-climate wines. In the hierarchy of Chilean appellations, Leyda is a zone within the San Antonio Valley, which in turn belongs to the Aconcagua region.

Climate

The proximity of the Pacific is the decisive factor. The Humboldt Current, flowing up from Antarctica, keeps coastal waters very cold, and sea breezes and morning fog cool the vineyards throughout the season. Summers are mild, with sunny days and moderate temperatures, and rainfall is concentrated in winter. Ripening is slow and long, allowing acidity and aromas to be preserved. Spring frost is one of the area’s main challenges.

Soils

Soils of granitic origin predominate, often with a topsoil of clay and silt over weathered granite, poor and well drained. On some slopes and in areas close to the coast there are also soils with marine influence and more recent deposits. The combination of poor soils and frequent wind limits vine vigour and yields.

Grapes and styles

Sauvignon Blanc is the variety that made Leyda famous: its wines are intense, with aromas of citrus, herbs and grapefruit, saline notes and vibrant acidity. Pinot Noir gives light- to medium-bodied reds with fresh red fruit and a delicate character, and Chardonnay produces taut, mineral whites. Cool-climate Syrah, with notes of pepper and black fruit, has also attracted the interest of several producers, and to a lesser extent other aromatic white varieties such as Riesling are grown. Some of Chile’s best-known wineries buy grapes in the area or own vineyards there, and the wines are sold under the Leyda Valley or San Antonio Valley appellations.
