Trentino-Alto Adige

Trentino-Alto Adige, Italy's northernmost Alpine region, brings together two autonomous provinces with their own identities: Trento and Bolzano. It is known for its mountain whites, its Lagrein, Schiava and Teroldego reds and the traditional-method sparkling Trento DOC.

History and territory

The region is made up of the autonomous province of Trento, which is Italian-speaking, and that of Bolzano, Alto Adige or South Tyrol, which belonged to Austria until the end of the First World War and where most of the population speaks German; labels are therefore often bilingual, with names such as Südtirol. Viticulture is concentrated in the valleys: the Adige valley, which crosses the region from north to south, the Isarco valley, Val Venosta and, in Trentino, the Piana Rotaliana and the Valle dei Laghi. Cooperative wineries play a very important role in both provinces. In 1902 Giulio Ferrari founded the winery in Trento that pioneered traditional-method sparkling wine there, and in 1993 Trento became the first Italian DOC reserved for this type of wine.

Climate

The Alps shelter the region from cold northerly winds, and summers on the valley floors can be very warm, especially in the Bolzano basin. Cool nights and the altitude of many slopes, in some cases above 1,000 metres, help preserve acidity and aromas. Sunshine is abundant and rainfall moderate.

Soils

Soils vary greatly: volcanic porphyry around Bolzano, limestone and dolomite, schist and metamorphic rock in the Isarco valley, and glacial and alluvial deposits on the valley floors.

Grapes and styles

In Alto Adige the leading whites include Pinot Grigio, Pinot Bianco, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling, Sylvaner, Kerner and Gewürztraminer, whose name is usually associated with the village of Termeno (Tramin). Among the reds, the most important are Schiava (Vernatsch), the basis of Santa Maddalena and Lago di Caldaro; deeply coloured Lagrein; and Pinot Noir. Sweet wines are also made from Moscato Giallo and Moscato Rosa. Characteristic of Trentino are Teroldego, which gives the red Teroldego Rotaliano, Marzemino and the white Nosiola, used to make Vino Santo Trentino from dried grapes. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are the basis of the sparkling Trento DOC. Merlot and Cabernet are grown in the warmer areas.

Designations

The main ones are the Alto Adige (Südtirol) DOC, with subzones such as Valle Isarco, Val Venosta, Santa Maddalena and Terlano; the Trentino DOC; the Trento DOC for sparkling wines; the Teroldego Rotaliano DOC; and the Lago di Caldaro DOC, shared by both provinces.
