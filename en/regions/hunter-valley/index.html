Hunter Valley

The Hunter Valley, in New South Wales, is one of Australia’s oldest wine regions. It is famous for its light, long-lived Semillon and for medium-bodied Shiraz.

History

The Hunter Valley lies about 150 kilometres north of Sydney, along the river of the same name. It was one of the first areas of the country where vines were grown successfully, in the early decades of the nineteenth century. James Busby, who assembled a large collection of grape varieties in Europe in the 1830s, owned land in the area, and some of the plants he brought were grown there. In the mid-nineteenth century family wineries were founded that still exist, such as Tyrrell’s, established in 1858, and in the following decades other historic houses contributed to the valley’s reputation.

The proximity of Sydney, the country’s largest city, has long encouraged wine tourism, and today the valley has numerous wineries, restaurants and places to stay. During the twentieth century the Hunter developed two highly individual styles that are regarded as Australian classics: low-alcohol Semillon, which gains complexity with time, and medium-weight, soft-textured Shiraz.

Climate and soils

The Hunter has a warm, humid climate, very different from that of the South Australian regions. Rain often falls in summer and can coincide with harvest, while afternoon cloud cover and easterly breezes moderate the heat somewhat. As a result, grapes are often picked early, at moderate sugar levels. The region is divided into the Lower Hunter, around Pokolbin, where the historic wineries are concentrated, and the Upper Hunter, further north. Soils are varied: sands and alluvium on the flat land near the creeks, where Semillon thrives, and clays and reddish loams, partly of volcanic origin, on the slopes, preferred for Shiraz.

Styles

Hunter Semillon is picked early and made in stainless steel, without oak. When young it is light, at about 10-11% alcohol, very high in acidity and with aromas of lemon and grass; with years in bottle it develops toasty, honeyed and buttered-toast notes without ever having seen a barrel. Shiraz is medium-bodied, with red fruit, earthy and spicy notes and soft tannins, and ages well. Chardonnay is also made, and enjoyed one of its first commercial successes in Australia here, along with some Verdelho. Many of these wines are released young, but some wineries hold back part of their Semillon and sell it after several years of bottle ageing.
