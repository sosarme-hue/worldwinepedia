Gigondas

Gigondas, at the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail in the Vaucluse, is one of the most highly regarded crus of the Southern Rhône. Its Grenache-based reds, with Syrah and Mourvèdre, are powerful yet fresh thanks to the altitude of part of the vineyard.

Location

Gigondas is a village in the Vaucluse department, north-east of Avignon and Châteauneuf-du-Pape, lying at the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail, a jagged limestone ridge that dominates the landscape. The appellation covers only the commune of Gigondas. Its vineyards range from the terraces of the plain, near the river Ouvèze, to slopes and small valleys at higher altitude, among pine and oak woods, making it one of the highest appellations in the Southern Rhône.

History

Viticulture in the area is ancient and is often linked to the Roman presence; the village's name has been connected with a Latin word evoking joy or merriment, although this etymology is uncertain. For a long time its wines were sold as Côtes du Rhône and later as Côtes du Rhône Villages. In 1971 Gigondas obtained its own appellation for its reds, and it was the first of the Côtes du Rhône Villages to achieve that promotion. It borders Vacqueyras, another cru, to the south and Sablet to the north.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers, and is marked by the mistral, the north wind that dries the vegetation and limits disease. The altitude of the slopes and the shade of the mountains bring cooler nights and somewhat slower ripening than on the plain. The soils are very varied: clay and rounded pebbles on the lower terraces, and limestone, marl and sand on the slopes near the Dentelles.

Grapes and styles

The main variety is Grenache, which must make up most of the blend, completed by Syrah and Mourvèdre and, to a lesser extent, other varieties authorised in the Rhône, such as Cinsault. Gigondas reds are deeply coloured wines with aromas of ripe red and black fruit, garrigue, spice and liquorice, and firm tannins. They are often said to have a more rustic structure and greater freshness than many Châteauneuf-du-Papes. The appellation also covers a small production of rosé. The reds are traditionally aged in large oak casks and, at some estates, in barrels.

Ageing

Gigondas can be enjoyed a few years after the vintage, and the best develop well for a decade or more, gaining notes of leather, undergrowth and candied fruit.
