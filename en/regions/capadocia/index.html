Cappadocia

A region on the high plateau of Central Anatolia, with landscapes of volcanic tuff and an ancient wine tradition. It is the home of the white Emir grape and one of Turkey’s best-known wine areas.

Cappadocia is a historic region of Central Anatolia that extends mainly across the province of Nevşehir and neighbouring parts of Kayseri, Niğde and Aksaray. Its landscape of eroded valleys, fairy chimneys and churches and dwellings carved into the rock formed from the volcanic ash deposits of ancient volcanoes such as Erciyes and Hasan, which over time compacted into soft tuff. Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia have been on UNESCO’s World Heritage List since 1985. The best-known wine towns are Ürgüp, Uçhisar, Avanos, Göreme and Mustafapaşa.

History

The vine has been grown in the region since antiquity, and the Christian communities of the Byzantine and Ottoman periods kept winemaking alive for centuries. The tuff, easy to dig and stable in temperature, has traditionally been used to build cellars and presses in the rock, and the many dovecotes in the valleys supplied a highly prized manure for fertilising vineyards and gardens. After the population exchange between Greece and Turkey in 1923, which affected the area’s Orthodox communities, wine production declined, although the vineyards were kept for table grapes, raisins and pekmez. During the twentieth century, and above all since its final decades, several wineries in Ürgüp, Uçhisar and Avanos have revived winemaking, partly thanks to tourism.

Climate and soils

The vineyards lie on a high plateau, more than a thousand metres above sea level. The climate is continental and dry, with cold, often snowy winters, warm summers and a large difference between day and night temperatures, which helps the grapes keep their acidity. Rainfall is scarce and many vines are dry-farmed, trained as low bush vines. Soils derive from tuff and volcanic ash; they are light, sandy and well drained, and in some valleys mixed with alluvium.

Grapes and styles

The emblematic variety is Emir, a local white grape that gives light, fresh wines with marked acidity and notes of citrus and green apple; it is made dry and also as a sparkling wine. Among red grapes, local varieties such as Dimrit are grown and, increasingly, grapes from other Turkish regions, such as Kalecik Karası and Öküzgözü, along with international varieties. Wineries in the area also work with grapes bought from other parts of Anatolia, so not all wines made in Cappadocia come from local vineyards. Turkey does not have a system of designations of origin comparable to the European one, so labels usually state the region or town and the grape variety.
