Médoc

The Médoc is the peninsula on the left bank of the Gironde, north of the city of Bordeaux. Its gravel soils and Cabernet Sauvignon-dominated reds account for most of the wines in the 1855 classification.

History

For centuries much of the Médoc was marshland of little use for farming. In the seventeenth century, with the help of Dutch engineers and merchants, large areas were drained, and over the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries the Bordeaux nobility and bourgeoisie created large wine estates on the gravel rises close to the estuary. Many of the best-known châteaux of Bordeaux were born there. In 1855, for the Universal Exhibition in Paris, the city's brokers drew up a classification of red wines based on their reputation and the prices they fetched: of the 61 wines classified, all except Château Haut-Brion, from Graves, came from the Médoc.

Territory, climate and soils

The Médoc stretches for some eighty kilometres between the Gironde estuary and the Atlantic Ocean, from which it is separated by the pine forest of the Landes. The estuary moderates temperatures and reduces the risk of frost in the vineyards closest to the water. The best sites are the croupes, gentle mounds of gravel and pebbles deposited by the rivers, which drain very well and retain heat, conditions that favour the ripening of Cabernet Sauvignon, a late-ripening variety.

Appellations

The area is divided into two subregional appellations: Médoc, covering the northern part traditionally known as the Bas-Médoc, and Haut-Médoc, to the south and closer to the city of Bordeaux. Within the Haut-Médoc lie six communal appellations: Saint-Estèphe, Pauillac, Saint-Julien, Margaux, Listrac-Médoc and Moulis-en-Médoc. Pauillac is home to three of the five first growths, Château Lafite Rothschild, Château Latour and Château Mouton Rothschild, the last promoted to that rank in 1973; Margaux to its namesake, Château Margaux.

Classifications

The 1855 classification ranks the wines in five tiers, from premiers to cinquièmes crus. In addition, many estates not included in it belong to the Crus Bourgeois du Médoc classification, which is revised periodically.

Grapes and styles

The Médoc appellations are for red wines only; the whites produced in the area are sold as Bordeaux. Blends are usually based on Cabernet Sauvignon, accompanied by Merlot and, in smaller proportions, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot and Malbec. The share of Merlot tends to be higher on cooler, more clay-rich soils, where Cabernet Sauvignon ripens less easily. These are firmly structured wines with pronounced tannins, usually aged in barrel and capable of long cellaring.

Country
France
Climate
Temperate oceanic, softened by the Gironde estuary and sheltered from the Atlantic by the Landes forest.
Soils
Mounds of gravel and pebbles (croupes), with clay and limestone in some areas such as Saint-Estèphe and Listrac.
Styles
Cabernet Sauvignon-led red blends, structured and suited to long ageing.
Grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot, Malbec
