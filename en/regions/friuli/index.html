Wine region

Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Friuli-Venezia Giulia, in north-eastern Italy, between the Alps, the Adriatic, Slovenia and Austria, is one of Italy's great white-wine regions, with hillside appellations such as Collio and Friuli Colli Orientali and broad lowland DOCs.

A border region

Friuli-Venezia Giulia occupies Italy's far north-eastern corner. It borders Austria to the north, Slovenia to the east, Veneto to the west and the Adriatic Sea to the south. Its history has been shaped by this frontier position: for centuries the territory was divided between the Republic of Venice and the Habsburg domains, and Italian, Friulian, Slovene and Germanic traditions live side by side. This mixture is reflected in its viticulture, which combines native varieties with others of French and Central European origin introduced in the nineteenth century. In the second half of the twentieth century the region became a benchmark for modern Italian whites, thanks to careful, temperature-controlled winemaking.

Climate and soils

The Alpine arc shelters the region from cold northern winds, while the Adriatic brings milder influences. Rainfall is abundant, and around Trieste the bora wind blows hard. The soils fall into two broad types: in the eastern hills ponca, a flysch of marls and sandstones, predominates; on the plain, rivers have laid down gravel and pebbles, as in the Grave area, and near the coast the soils become more clayey and sandy. In the Carso, above the Trieste coast, vines grow in shallow, reddish limestone soils.

Appellations

The most prestigious hill areas are Collio and Friuli Colli Orientali, within whose territory lie the Picolit, Ramandolo and Rosazzo DOCGs. On the plain, the leading DOCs are Friuli Grave, the largest, Friuli Isonzo, Friuli Aquileia, Friuli Latisana and Friuli Annia, joined by Carso and the regional Friuli DOC. The region shares the Lison DOCG and the Prosecco DOC with Veneto, and part of its territory grows Glera for that sparkling wine.

Grapes and styles

Among the native white grapes, Friulano, Ribolla Gialla, Malvasia Istriana, Verduzzo and Picolit stand out; Vitovska is grown in the Carso. Alongside them, Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Bianco carry great weight. Among the reds, Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso, Schioppettino, Pignolo and Terrano share the vineyards with Merlot and the Cabernets. The region is known for aromatic dry whites with good acidity, and also for whites given long skin maceration, in the tradition revived by some producers in Oslavia and the Carso.

Country
Italy
Climate
Temperate and rainy, sheltered by the Alps and softened by the Adriatic, with the bora on the Trieste coast.
Soils
Marl and sandstone ponca in the hills, alluvial gravels on the plain and reddish limestone in the Carso.
Styles
Aromatic dry whites, skin-contact whites, native and international reds, and sweet Picolit and Verduzzo.
Grapes
Friulano, Rebula (Ribolla Gialla), Malvasia, Pinot Gris, Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Blanc, Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso, Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Glera
