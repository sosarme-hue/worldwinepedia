Pla i Llevant

Pla i Llevant is a designation of origin in Mallorca covering the central plain and the eastern district of the island, around towns such as Manacor, Felanitx and Petra. It produces whites, rosés and reds that combine native varieties, such as Callet, with non-native grapes.

History

Eastern and central Mallorca have a long winemaking tradition. In the nineteenth century towns such as Felanitx and Manacor became important production centres, and for a number of years, while phylloxera was devastating French vineyards, Mallorcan wine was exported in quantity to the mainland. The arrival of the plague on the island at the end of that century ruined much of the vineyard, which was replaced by other crops such as almond trees. During the twentieth century viticulture was reduced to a secondary activity, until in recent decades a group of wineries committed to quality. The designation of origin, the second in Mallorca after Binissalem, was recognised at the end of the 1990s.

Territory and climate

The designation comprises a number of municipalities in the Pla, Mallorca's central plain, and the Llevant, the eastern district, including towns such as Manacor, Felanitx, Petra, Porreres, Sant Joan, Vilafranca de Bonany and Algaida, among others. It is a landscape of plains and gentle hills, with small ranges in the east, where vines alternate with almond trees, olives and cereals. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers, mild winters and moderate rainfall concentrated in autumn; the nearby sea and its breezes moderate temperatures in the areas closest to the coast.

Soils

Calcareous soils predominate, together with the characteristic red soils known as call vermell, rich in clay and iron oxides, often over a limestone base, with a good capacity to retain water during the dry months.

Grape varieties

Among the reds, Callet stands out, a native Mallorcan variety that gives light-coloured, fresh and spicy wines, together with Manto Negro and Fogoneu, other local grapes. Tempranillo, Monastrell, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah are also grown. The whites include native Prensal Blanc, Parellada, Macabeo, Moscatel and Chardonnay.

Styles

The designation produces a varied range of wines: young and aged reds, often blends of local and international varieties, fresh and aromatic whites, rosés, sparkling wines and some sweet wines. In recent years several wineries have chosen to give greater prominence to Callet and other native varieties, which lend the island's wines an identity of their own. As in the rest of Mallorca, a large part of production is sold on the local market, closely linked to tourism.
