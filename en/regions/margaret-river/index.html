Margaret River

Margaret River, in the far south-west of Western Australia, is a young region that has established itself as one of the country’s best for Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay.

History

Margaret River occupies a coastal strip about a hundred kilometres from north to south between Cape Naturaliste and Cape Leeuwin, nearly 300 kilometres south of Perth. For a long time it was an area of timber, dairy farming and smallholdings, and winegrowing had hardly any presence. In the 1960s several studies, in particular those of the agronomist John Gladstones, pointed out that its climate was comparable in some respects to that of Bordeaux and especially suited to the vine. In 1967 the doctor Tom Cullity planted the Vasse Felix vineyard, regarded as the first of the modern era in the region, and shortly afterwards other pioneering estates were founded, such as Moss Wood, Cullen and Cape Mentelle, often also by doctors from Perth.

In the 1970s came Leeuwin Estate, which in its early days was advised by the Californian producer Robert Mondavi. Within a few decades the region went from a few experimental hectares to a significant vineyard area, with numerous wineries, restaurants and busy tourism linked also to surfing and nature.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean and strongly maritime, with the Indian Ocean to the west and the Southern Ocean to the south. Winters are mild and rainy and summers dry and temperate, with little difference in temperature between the seasons and between day and night. The main risks are wind in spring, which can affect flowering, and birds that attack the ripening grapes. The most prized soils are sandy and gravelly loams of lateritic origin, rich in iron, over a very old granite and gneiss basement; they are poor and well drained.

Styles

Cabernet Sauvignon and Bordeaux-style blends with Merlot are the benchmark reds: wines with black fruit and cassis, herbal and cedar notes, firm but fine tannins and good ageing potential. Chardonnay, often from a local clonal selection known as Gin Gin, gives whites that are both rich and taut, with stone fruit and citrus and barrel ageing. Also highly characteristic is the blend of Semillon and Sauvignon Blanc, fresh and aromatic, which has become a style of its own in the region. Shiraz and some Chenin Blanc are also made.
