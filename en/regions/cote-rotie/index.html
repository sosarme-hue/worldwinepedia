Côte-Rôtie

Côte-Rôtie is the northernmost appellation of the Rhône valley, on dizzying slopes above the village of Ampuis. Its Syrah reds, sometimes co-fermented with a little Viognier, combine perfume, finesse and ageing potential.

Location

Côte-Rôtie lies on the right bank of the Rhône, a few kilometres south of the city of Vienne, and mainly covers the communes of Ampuis, Saint-Cyr-sur-le-Rhône and Tupin-et-Semons. Its name, meaning “roasted slope”, refers to the strong sun exposure of its slopes, which face largely south-east. The vineyard is very old in origin; the Vienne area was already a wine-growing centre in Roman times, and wines from the area were known in Antiquity.

Landscape and viticulture

The slopes of Côte-Rôtie are among the steepest in France. To cultivate them, terraces supported by dry-stone walls, known locally as chaillées, were built, and the vines are traditionally trained on wooden stakes, sometimes tied together in a tent-like shape. Almost all the work is done by hand, which demands great effort and limits the area under vine. After a long period of decline in the mid-twentieth century, the appellation regained its prestige in the following decades and the vineyard expanded again onto the upper plateaus, a matter that has given rise to debate.

Soils: Côte Brune and Côte Blonde

The soils are made of metamorphic rocks, chiefly mica schist and gneiss, very poor and easily eroded. Traditionally two sectors are distinguished. To the north, the Côte Brune, with darker soils rich in iron and clay, gives more structured wines. To the south, the Côte Blonde, with paler, sandier soils linked to gneiss, is associated with finer, more aromatic wines. Many traditional cuvées blend grapes from both sectors.

Grapes and style

Côte-Rôtie produces only reds. The main variety is Syrah, but the rules allow a small proportion of Viognier, which must be co-fermented with it. This practice, whose use varies widely from producer to producer, adds perfume and can help to stabilise colour. The wines are characterised by aromas of violet, raspberry, blackberry, black olive, pepper and smoked bacon, a silky texture and lively acidity. They are aged in barrels or large casks and can develop for many years.

Producers

The house of Guigal, based in Ampuis, played a decisive part in the appellation's international fame with its single-vineyard wines La Mouline, La Landonne and La Turque. Alongside it work many family growers and Rhône houses.
