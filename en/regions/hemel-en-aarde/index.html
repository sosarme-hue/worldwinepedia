Hemel-en-Aarde

A group of valleys near Hermanus, on South Africa’s south coast, divided into three wards. Cooled by the Atlantic Ocean, it is South Africa’s benchmark area for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

Territory

The name Hemel-en-Aarde means “heaven and earth” in Afrikaans and refers to a valley set between mountains that runs down to the sea beside the coastal town of Hermanus, on Walker Bay, about a hundred kilometres south-east of Cape Town. In the South African Wine of Origin system, the area is divided into three wards within the Walker Bay district: Hemel-en-Aarde Valley, closest to the sea; Upper Hemel-en-Aarde Valley, further up the valley; and Hemel-en-Aarde Ridge, the furthest from the coast and the highest. Together they form a corridor of a little over fifteen kilometres lined with small family wineries.

History

Modern winegrowing began here in the mid-1970s, when Tim Hamilton Russell bought land in the valley, convinced that its cool climate suited Burgundian varieties. Hamilton Russell Vineyards became the area’s pioneering winery and, together with Bouchard Finlayson and others that followed, demonstrated the potential of Pinot Noir and Chardonnay in South Africa. Since then the number of producers has grown, but the valley retains a small scale and a very clearly defined wine profile.

Climate

The proximity of the ocean is the decisive factor. South-easterly winds, often strong in summer, bring cool air from the sea and moderate temperatures, and cloud cover is frequent. The result is a cool maritime climate with slow ripening that preserves acidity and aromas. The ward nearest the coast is the most influenced by the sea, while the Ridge, further inland and higher, shows greater contrasts.

Soils

The soils combine two main types: rich clays derived from Bokkeveld Group shales, which hold water and give wines of structure and depth, and lighter, sandy soils derived from Table Mountain sandstone, which give finer, more aromatic wines. Granite-derived soils also occur in some areas. Producers usually choose the soil type according to the variety and the style they are seeking.

Grapes and styles

Pinot Noir gives reds with red and black fruit, earthy and spicy notes, fine tannins and good ageing potential. Chardonnay produces taut, mineral whites, often aged in French oak barrels. Sauvignon Blanc, Pinotage in a fresher style than usual, Syrah and some white varieties such as Chenin Blanc are also grown.
