Designation of Origin (DO)

Cigales

Cigales is a designation of origin north of Valladolid, in the Pisuerga valley, recognised in 1991. Traditionally associated with rosés and claretes, it also produces Tempranillo reds.

History

Viticulture in the Cigales area has been documented since the Middle Ages, when monasteries and nobles owned vineyards there, and its proximity to Valladolid, for a time the seat of the royal court, guaranteed a steady market for its wines. One of its hallmarks is the underground cellars dug into the hillsides, often grouped into cellar quarters on the edge of villages, with their characteristic ventilation chimneys known as zarceras. The area became famous for its claretes, light-coloured wines traditionally made from a blend of red and white grapes. The Designation of Origin was recognised in 1991.

Territory and climate

The designation extends on both sides of the Pisuerga river, north of the city of Valladolid, and includes municipalities in that province and in the province of Palencia, such as Dueñas. The vineyards lie at around 700 to 800 metres above sea level. The climate is continental with some Atlantic influence: long cold winters, hot dry summers, little rainfall and a significant difference between day and night temperatures. Late spring frosts are a frequent risk.

Soils

The soils are generally poor and varied in texture: sandy and calcareous, with clay at depth and, in some areas, abundant rounded stones on the surface that retain heat during the day.

Grapes and styles

The main variety is Tempranillo, called Tinta del País here, followed by Grenache. Among the whites, Verdejo stands out, along with other traditionally grown varieties such as Albillo. The style historically most associated with the area is rosé, traditionally made with a proportion of white grapes, which gives it freshness and a bright colour. In recent decades reds have gained ground, both young and barrel-aged, and they now account for a significant share of production. The designation also covers whites and some sparkling and sweet wines.

Ageing

Reds can be sold young or with the traditional terms Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva, in accordance with the minimum ageing periods set by the regulations. Some rosés are also made with a short spell in barrel.

Landscape and heritage

The quarters of underground cellars, the bush-trained vines of many old plots and the small villages of the valley form a landscape typical of the northern Meseta, tied for centuries to winemaking.
