Bizkaiko Txakolina

Bizkaiko Txakolina is the designation of origin for the txakoli of Bizkaia, in the Basque Country. Its vineyards, spread across much of the province, mainly yield fresh, crisp whites from Hondarrabi Zuri, along with some reds and rosés.

History

Txakoli is the traditional wine of the Basque Country, documented for centuries on the farmsteads, or caseríos, of the coast and the inland valleys. In Bizkaia it was made mainly for household consumption and for sale in taverns and on the farms themselves, and its cultivation was once widespread throughout the province. The nineteenth-century plagues, powdery mildew and phylloxera, and the industrialisation of the territory during the twentieth century drastically reduced the vineyard area. From the 1980s a group of producers set about reviving it, and in 1994 the Bizkaiko Txakolina Designation of Origin was created, the second devoted to this wine after that of Getaria.

Territory and climate

Unlike other designations, Bizkaiko Txakolina does not occupy a single district: its vineyards are spread across many municipalities in the province, with notable clusters on the coast around Bakio, in the Urdaibai area and in the Encartaciones valley to the west, near Balmaseda. The vines are usually planted on sunny, well-ventilated slopes. The climate is Atlantic, temperate and very rainy, with mild winters, cool summers and high humidity, which calls for great care to prevent vine diseases. For this reason, many vines are trained on wires or on high pergolas, which help air circulate around the bunches.

Soils

The soils are varied, generally clay-loam in texture, derived from sandstone, marl and limestone, with good water retention and some acidity.

Grape varieties

The main variety is Hondarrabi Zuri, which brings freshness and citrus and herbaceous aromas. Another traditional grape in Bizkaia is Folle Blanche, known locally as Mune Mahatsa. Other authorised white varieties are also grown, such as Petit Manseng and Gros Manseng, known locally as Izkiriota, as well as small amounts of Riesling, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc. The most characteristic red is Hondarrabi Beltza.

Styles

The best-known style is white txakoli: young, moderate in alcohol, with lively acidity and often a slight spritz, traditionally poured from a height to enhance its freshness. In recent years there have also appeared txakolis aged on their lees, barrel-fermented versions, sparkling wines and late-harvest wines, alongside rosés and reds from Hondarrabi Beltza. It is a wine closely bound to Basque cuisine, especially fish, seafood and pintxos.
