Designation of Origin (DO)

Valdepeñas

Valdepeñas is a designation of origin in the south of the province of Ciudad Real, surrounded by the La Mancha DO. Historically a supplier of wine to Madrid, it is known for its reds from Tempranillo, called Cencibel here, and its tradition of making wine in tinajas.

History

Valdepeñas has a long winemaking tradition, and for centuries its wines were widely drunk in Madrid and other cities in the centre of the peninsula. Before the arrival of the railway they were transported in wineskins and carts; in the nineteenth century the railway line linking Madrid with Andalusia eased trade and gave a great boost to the town's wineries. Many of them preserve underground caves and large earthenware jars, or tinajas, in which wine was traditionally fermented and stored. For a long time aloque was typical, a light-coloured wine made from a blend of red and white grapes. In recent decades the designation has focused on Tempranillo reds aged in barrel.

Territory and climate

The designation occupies an enclave in the south of the province of Ciudad Real, around the town of Valdepeñas and neighbouring municipalities such as Santa Cruz de Mudela, Moral de Calatrava and Torrenueva, and is surrounded by the territory of the La Mancha DO. The vineyards lie on a plain and gentle undulations at about 700 metres above sea level, in and around the valley of the Jabalón river. The climate is continental, with cold winters, very hot dry summers, little rainfall and abundant sunshine.

Soils

Reddish-brown soils over limestone subsoils predominate, with a calcareous crust that in some areas lies close to the surface. These soils retain moisture at depth and help the vines withstand summer drought.

Grapes and styles

The main red variety is Tempranillo, known locally as Cencibel, accompanied by Grenache, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Petit Verdot. Among the whites, Airén predominates, together with Macabeo, Verdejo, Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Moscatel. Producers make young whites, rosés, and young and oak-aged reds.

Ageing

Valdepeñas reds are sold young or with the traditional terms Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva, in accordance with the minimum periods of barrel and bottle ageing set by the regulations. Oak-aged Cencibel reds are the style most associated with the designation today: wines with ripe fruit, oak notes and soft tannins, often offering good value for money.

Heritage

The town's historic wineries, with their tinajas, caves and courtyards, form part of a wine heritage that has also become an attraction for wine tourism.
