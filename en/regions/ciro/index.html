Cirò

Cirò is the best-known appellation in Calabria. It lies on the Ionian coast of the province of Crotone and is famous above all for its reds and rosés from Gaglioppo, along with whites from Greco Bianco.

Location

The Cirò area extends over the hills and the coastal strip of Calabria's Ionian shore, in the province of Crotone. It includes the municipalities of Cirò and Cirò Marina, which form the oldest zone, entitled to the term Classico, and parts of Crucoli and Melissa. The vineyards lie between the sea and the first foothills of the Sila plateau, in a landscape of gentle hills with olive groves and other Mediterranean crops.

History

Calabria's Ionian coast was part of Magna Graecia, and the ancient city of Krimisa is traditionally placed in the Cirò area; in local tradition its wine is associated with offerings made to the athletes of the ancient Olympic Games. These traditions, though widely repeated, cannot be verified precisely. What is documented is the long presence of the vine in the area and the importance of Cirò wine to the local economy. The DOC was recognised in 1969, and for decades much of the production was in the hands of a few houses and cooperatives. Since the beginning of the twenty-first century a new generation of small producers has revived interest in the area's traditional wines.

Climate and soils

The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers, mild winters and little rain, concentrated in the colder months. The nearby Ionian Sea and the breezes that rise from the coast moderate the heat and help keep the grapes healthy. The soils are mainly clay and limestone, with areas of sand and marl, and are generally of low fertility.

Grapes and styles

The emblematic red variety is Gaglioppo, a native grape of southern Italy, relatively pale in colour but with firm tannins and good structure. It is used for Cirò Rosso, with Classico, Superiore and Riserva versions, and for Cirò Rosato, one of the traditional rosés of southern Italy. Cirò Rosso usually shows aromas of cherry, red berries, spice and Mediterranean herbs, with marked tannins and moderate acidity; aged examples develop notes of leather and tobacco. Cirò Bianco is made mainly from Greco Bianco, a Calabrian variety distinct from the Campanian Greco despite the name, and gives fresh, aromatic whites. The area also has some plots of old bush vines, prized by producers seeking to express the character of Gaglioppo.
