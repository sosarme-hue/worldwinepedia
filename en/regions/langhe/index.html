Wine area and Controlled Designation of Origin (Langhe DOC)

Langhe

The Langhe are a hilly area of southern Piedmont, around Alba, and the home of Barolo and Barbaresco. Besides those DOCGs, the area has its own DOC, Langhe, which covers reds and whites from many varieties.

A landscape of hills

The Langhe cover a group of hills in the province of Cuneo, south of the Tanaro river, with the town of Alba as their historic and commercial centre. Across the river lies the Roero, and to the north-east the Monferrato. The hills form a succession of ridges and narrow valleys where vines occupy the best-exposed slopes, while the higher, cooler areas, known as Alta Langa, are largely given over to hazelnut groves, woodland and, increasingly, to base wines for sparkling production. In 2014 the vineyard landscapes of the Langhe, Roero and Monferrato were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

Climate and soils

The climate is continental, with cold winters, warm summers and autumns in which fog is frequent; indeed, the name Nebbiolo is usually linked to fog (nebbia). The Alps shelter the area from northern and western winds. The soils are of marine origin and consist mainly of calcareous and clayey marls, with a greater or lesser share of sand depending on the hill. These differences go a long way towards explaining the variety of styles between neighbouring villages.

Appellations

The Langhe are home to some of Italy's most famous appellations. The Barolo and Barbaresco DOCGs are reserved for Nebbiolo and have their own ageing rules. Alongside them are the Dogliani DOCG, centred on Dolcetto, and several varietal and area DOCs such as Dolcetto d'Alba, Barbera d'Alba and Nebbiolo d'Alba. The broader Langhe DOC allows wines to be made from local and international varieties, including Langhe Nebbiolo, Langhe Dolcetto, Langhe Freisa, Langhe Arneis, Langhe Chardonnay and blended reds and whites. Many producers use it for younger wines or for vineyards outside the Barolo and Barbaresco zones, and also for wines that do not follow the rules of those DOCGs.

Grapes and styles

Nebbiolo is the noble grape of the area and occupies the best slopes. Barbera gives reds with lively acidity and soft tannins, and Dolcetto fruity reds to be drunk relatively young. Among the whites, Arneis and Chardonnay stand out, along with smaller amounts of Riesling and Moscato. The result is a range that runs from long-lived reds to simple wines for everyday drinking, all shaped by the same hills, the same fogs and a long tradition of family estates.
