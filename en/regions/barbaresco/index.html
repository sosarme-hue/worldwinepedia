Barbaresco

Barbaresco is a Nebbiolo red from the Langhe, made in a small area north-east of Alba, beside the Tanaro river. It shares its grape and its prestige with Barolo, although its ageing requirements are somewhat shorter.

History

For a long time, Nebbiolo from Barbaresco was often sold to other areas or marketed without a name of its own. At the end of the nineteenth century, Domizio Cavazza, director of the school of oenology in Alba, promoted the making of a wine bearing the village's name and the creation of a cooperative cellar in Barbaresco in 1894. The Produttori del Barbaresco cooperative was founded in 1958, and in the following decades the work of producers such as Gaja helped to raise its international profile. The designation was recognised as a DOC in 1966 and obtained DOCG status in 1980.

Territory and climate

The zone, much smaller than that of Barolo, covers the municipalities of Barbaresco, Neive and Treiso, together with the hamlet of San Rocco Seno d'Elvio, which belongs to Alba. It lies at a lower altitude than much of Barolo and close to the Tanaro river, which moderates temperatures; the result is a slightly warmer mesoclimate in which Nebbiolo usually ripens a few days earlier. The general climate is continental, with cold winters, warm summers and autumn fogs.

Soils

Bluish-grey calcareous marls of marine origin predominate, with varying proportions of clay and sand. These soils, together with the exposure of each slope, account for the differences between individual vineyards.

Geographical mentions

Barbaresco was the first Piedmontese designation to officially delimit its historic vineyards, with the Menzioni Geografiche Aggiuntive (MGA) applied from the 2007 vintage. Among the best known are Asili, Rabajà, Montestefano, Pajè, Martinenga, Montefico and Ovello in Barbaresco, and Gallina, Albesani and Serraboella in Neive.

Ageing rules and style

Barbaresco is made from 100% Nebbiolo. It must be aged for at least 26 months, nine of them in wood, and the Riserva designation requires a minimum of 50 months. It is often described as somewhat more approachable in its youth than Barolo, while sharing the same framework of acidity and tannin and great ageing potential. It shows a garnet colour of medium intensity and aromas of rose, violet, cherry and spice, developing notes of leather, tobacco and truffle over time. Many producers also use the area's Nebbiolo for wines under the Langhe DOC, with shorter ageing.
