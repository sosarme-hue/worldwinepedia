Sta. Rita Hills

An AVA in the western Santa Ynez Valley, in Santa Barbara County, open to Pacific winds and fog. It is one of California’s coolest areas and a benchmark for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

A valley open to the ocean

The Sta. Rita Hills AVA occupies the western end of the Santa Ynez Valley, between the towns of Buellton and Lompoc, in Santa Barbara County. What makes it exceptional is the orientation of its mountains. Unlike most ranges along the Californian coast, which run north to south, the ranges in this part of the county run east to west, forming an open corridor through which cold Pacific air and fog flow directly inland along the course of the Santa Ynez River. The AVA lies within the broader Santa Ynez Valley AVA and the Santa Barbara wine region.

History and name

Modern winegrowing began here in the early 1970s, when the Sanford & Benedict vineyard was planted, one of the first to demonstrate the area’s potential for Pinot Noir. In the following decades numerous vineyards and wineries joined it, many of them small. The AVA was created under the name Santa Rita Hills, but after an objection from the Chilean winery Viña Santa Rita, which feared confusion with its brand, the official name came to be written in abbreviated form: Sta. Rita Hills.

Climate and soils

The climate is cool and markedly maritime, with morning fog, strong and steady afternoon winds and a very long ripening season. The grapes retain high acidity and develop thick skins, giving deeply coloured wines with great freshness. The soils are varied: sands and sandy loams, clays and, in some areas, diatomaceous earth, a light white rock formed from the fossil remains of marine algae, which contributes to the character of some hillside vineyards.

Grapes and styles

Pinot Noir is the dominant variety. Its wines combine intense red and black fruit with spicy, sometimes herbal or saline notes and vibrant acidity that gives them tension and ageing potential. Chardonnay gives very lively whites with notes of citrus and apple and an often mineral profile, which can gain richness from barrel ageing without losing freshness. To a lesser extent Syrah is grown, taking on a fresh, spicy cool-climate profile here, along with some aromatic white varieties. Many producers sell single-vineyard wines, whose names frequently appear on the label.
