AOC (Touraine, Loire valley)

Vouvray

Vouvray, east of Tours on the right bank of the Loire, is one of the benchmark appellations for Chenin Blanc. It produces dry, off-dry, sweet and sparkling whites, often of great longevity.

Location

Vouvray lies in Touraine, on the right bank of the Loire, just east of the city of Tours. The appellation covers the commune of Vouvray and several neighbouring villages, such as Rochecorbon, Vernou-sur-Brenne, Chançay, Noizay and Reugny. The vineyards occupy the plateau and the slopes overlooking the river and the small valleys of its tributaries, such as the Brenne and the Cisse. On the opposite bank, south of the Loire, lies the Montlouis-sur-Loire appellation, which is also devoted to Chenin Blanc.

Landscape and cellars

The most characteristic feature of the area is tuffeau, a soft, pale limestone from which many of the Loire châteaux were built. Over the centuries numerous galleries and cellars were dug into the cliffs lining the river, some of them still inhabited as troglodyte dwellings. These caves keep a cool, stable temperature all year round, ideal for ageing and storing bottles.

Climate and soils

The climate is transitional between oceanic and continental influences, with cool winters, mild summers and great variation from one vintage to the next. Over the tuffeau lie clay soils with flint, known locally as perruches, and clay-limestone soils called aubuis, as well as areas of sand and gravel. This diversity influences the style of each plot.

Grape and styles

The main variety is Chenin Blanc, also known here as Pineau de la Loire; the rules allow a small proportion of Orbois, a very minor local variety. Chenin's natural acidity makes it possible to produce very diverse styles, and the type of wine often depends on the vintage. In cool years more dry and sparkling wines are made; in warm years, when the grapes ripen further or are affected by noble rot or shrivel on the vine, off-dry and sweet wines, called moelleux, appear. The sparkling wines, made by the traditional method, may be mousseux or the lower-pressure pétillant, and account for a significant share of production.

Profile and ageing

Vouvray wines show aromas of quince, apple, pear, honey and acacia blossom and, with time, notes of wax and dried fruit. Their acidity gives them remarkable ageing potential, especially the sweet wines, which can develop for decades. Among the best-known producers are Domaine Huet, Domaine du Clos Naudin and Domaine Champalou.
