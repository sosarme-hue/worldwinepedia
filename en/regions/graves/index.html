AOC (Graves and Graves Supérieures), Bordeaux subregion

Graves

Graves, south of the city of Bordeaux on the left bank of the Garonne, takes its name from its gravel soils. It is one of the few Bordeaux areas where reds and dry whites carry comparable weight.

Location

The Graves region stretches along the left bank of the Garonne, from the southern outskirts of the city of Bordeaux to beyond Langon, in a strip some fifty kilometres long. To the west it is bounded by the great pine forest of the Landes. Within its territory lie the sweet-wine areas of Sauternes and Barsac, as enclaves with their own appellations, and in its northern part the Pessac-Léognan appellation was created in 1987, grouping the communes closest to the city.

History

Graves is the historic cradle of Bordeaux wine. During the Middle Ages, when Aquitaine belonged to the English crown, the vineyards near the city supplied the trade in so-called claret with England. In the seventeenth century Château Haut-Brion, now in Pessac-Léognan, was one of the first wines known by the name of its estate, and in 1855 it was the only red outside the Médoc included in the classification. In the mid-twentieth century a separate classification of Graves wines, red and white, was established; all the properties listed in it now lie within Pessac-Léognan.

Climate and soils

The climate is temperate oceanic. The Landes forest shelters the vineyards from Atlantic wind and moisture, and the Garonne moderates temperatures. The soils combine gravel and pebbles deposited by the river with sand, clay and, in some places, limestone. The proportion of gravel is higher in the north, while further south sandier and more clay-rich soils appear.

Appellations and grapes

The Graves AOC covers reds and dry whites, while the Graves Supérieures AOC is reserved for whites with residual sugar, from off-dry to sweet. The reds are made from Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, completed by Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot and Malbec; Merlot often plays a significant part. The whites are based on Sémillon and Sauvignon Blanc, with a little Muscadelle.

Styles

Graves reds usually show red and black fruit, earthy and smoky notes and tannins somewhat softer than those of the Médoc. The whites range from fresh, fruity wines made in tank to whites fermented and aged in barrel, with a broader texture and the capacity to develop in bottle. Alongside long-established châteaux, the area has many family estates offering moderately priced wines.
