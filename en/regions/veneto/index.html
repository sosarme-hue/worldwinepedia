Wine region

Veneto

Veneto, in north-east Italy, is one of the country's largest wine-producing regions. It is the source of Prosecco, Soave, Bardolino and the reds of Valpolicella, including Amarone.

History

Viticulture in Veneto dates back to antiquity, and in Roman times the wines of the Verona area were already known. For centuries the Republic of Venice was a major centre of the Mediterranean wine trade, which encouraged both local production and the arrival of wines from other regions. In 1876 a school of viticulture and oenology was founded in Conegliano, which played an important role in the region's technical training. In the second half of the twentieth century Veneto became one of Italy's most productive regions, and the international success of Prosecco and Pinot Grigio has strengthened that position.

Climate and territory

The region stretches from the Dolomites and the Prealps to the Po plain and the Venetian lagoon, and in the west reaches the eastern shore of Lake Garda. The climate is generally moderately continental, with cold winters and warm summers; the mountains provide shelter from northerly winds, while Lake Garda and the Adriatic soften temperatures in the areas they influence. The best vineyards lie mainly in the hills: the Lessini mountains north of Verona, the hills between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, the Colli Berici and the Colli Euganei.

Soils

In the hills around Verona, limestone, marl and soils of volcanic origin alternate, particularly in the Soave area. Glacial moraine deposits dominate around Lake Garda, and more fertile alluvial soils on the plain.

Main designations

The province of Verona is home to Valpolicella, with Amarone and Recioto, Soave and Bardolino, a light red and Chiaretto rosé from the shores of Lake Garda, as well as Custoza and the Veneto part of Lugana. To the north-east, the Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco Superiore DOCG and the Asolo Prosecco DOCG form the historic core of Prosecco, whose DOC also extends into Friuli Venezia Giulia. Other designations include Gambellara, Breganze, Piave and Colli Euganei. The Delle Venezie DOC, shared with Friuli Venezia Giulia and Trentino, covers much of the area's Pinot Grigio.

Grapes and styles

Leading red varieties include Corvina, the basis of Valpolicella and Bardolino, together with Corvinone, Rondinella and Molinara, as well as the local Raboso and Merlot and the Cabernets. Among the whites, the most important are Glera for Prosecco, Garganega for Soave and Gambellara, and Pinot Grigio. A characteristic technique of the region is appassimento, the drying of grapes after harvest, used to make Amarone and the sweet Recioto wines.

Country
Italy
Climate
Moderately continental, sheltered by the Alps and softened by Lake Garda and the Adriatic.
Soils
Limestone, marl and volcanic soils in the hills, glacial moraines by Lake Garda and alluvium on the plain.
Styles
Prosecco sparkling wines, Garganega and Pinot Grigio whites, Corvina reds, Amarone and sweet Recioto from dried grapes.
Grapes
Corvina, Glera, Garganega, Pinot Gris, Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Chardonnay
