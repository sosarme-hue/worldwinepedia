Burgundy subregion (Côte d'Or)

Côte de Beaune

The Côte de Beaune is the southern half of the Côte d'Or, from Ladoix to Maranges. It produces some of the world's most highly regarded Chardonnay whites, such as Montrachet, Meursault and Corton-Charlemagne, as well as Pinot Noir reds such as Pommard and Volnay.

Territory

The Côte de Beaune continues the Côte de Nuits southwards along the same fault, from Ladoix-Serrigny to Les Maranges, which lies in the Saône-et-Loire département. The vineyard strip is somewhat wider and the slopes, generally gentler, face east and south-east. Its centre is the town of Beaune, the historic hub of the Burgundy wine trade and home to many négociants, and the site of the Hospices de Beaune, founded in 1443 by Nicolas Rolin, chancellor to Duke Philip the Good. The charity auction of the wines from its vineyards is held every year in November.

Climate and soils

The climate is semi-continental, as in the rest of the Côte d'Or. The soils combine Jurassic limestone and marl; marl, more abundant here than in the Côte de Nuits, is considered especially well suited to Chardonnay in areas such as Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet and Chassagne-Montrachet, while the clay- and iron-rich soils of Pommard suit Pinot Noir.

Villages

In the north, the hill of Corton, shared by Ladoix-Serrigny, Aloxe-Corton and Pernand-Vergelesses, is home to the red grand cru Corton, the only red grand cru of the Côte de Beaune, and to the white Corton-Charlemagne, whose name recalls the tradition that Charlemagne owned vines on the hill. Next come Savigny-lès-Beaune, Chorey-lès-Beaune and Beaune, with numerous premiers crus. South of Beaune, Pommard is known for firm reds and Volnay for more delicate, perfumed ones. Beyond Monthélie, Auxey-Duresses and Saint-Romain lies Meursault, which has no grands crus but some highly regarded premiers crus, such as Perrières, Charmes and Genevrières.

Puligny-Montrachet and Chassagne-Montrachet share the grand cru Montrachet and its neighbour Bâtard-Montrachet; Chevalier-Montrachet and Bienvenues-Bâtard-Montrachet lie in Puligny, and Criots-Bâtard-Montrachet in Chassagne. Saint-Aubin, Santenay and Maranges complete the area. Several villages may sell their reds under the Côte de Beaune-Villages appellation, and the hills to the west form the Hautes-Côtes de Beaune.

Grapes and styles

Unlike the Côte de Nuits, the Côte de Beaune produces significant quantities of white wine. Its Chardonnay whites, usually fermented and aged in barrel, combine breadth, fruit and acidity, and those from the best vineyards can age for many years. The Pinot Noir reds tend to be somewhat softer and earlier-maturing than those of the Côte de Nuits, although Corton and the best premiers crus of Pommard and Volnay have considerable ageing potential. Aligoté is grown for the regional Bourgogne Aligoté appellation.

Country
France
Climate
Semi-continental, with cold winters, warm summers and a risk of frost and hail.
Soils
Jurassic limestone and marl, with more marl than in the Côte de Nuits and clay- and iron-rich soils in Pommard.
Styles
Great barrel-aged Chardonnay whites and Pinot Noir reds from village, premier cru and grand cru sites.
Grapes
Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, Aligoté
