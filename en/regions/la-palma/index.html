Designation of Origin (DO)

La Palma

La Palma is the designation of origin of the Canary island of the same name, divided into three subzones: Fuencaliente, Hoyo de Mazo and Norte de La Palma. It is known for the sweet Malvasía of Fuencaliente, grown on volcanic ash, and for vino de tea, aged in Canary pine wood.

History

The vine reached La Palma after the Castilian conquest at the end of the fifteenth century, and during the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries the island took part in the prosperous trade in Canary wines, especially Malvasía, which was exported to Europe and the Americas from the port of Santa Cruz de La Palma. As that trade declined, production turned to local consumption, although the island kept a rich heritage of varieties and traditional winemaking methods. The designation of origin was recognised in the 1990s. The history of the island's vineyards is bound up with volcanic activity: the eruption of Teneguía in 1971 took place in the south of the island, in Fuencaliente, and that of the Cumbre Vieja volcano in 2021 affected farming areas on the western side.

Subzones and territory

The designation is divided into three subzones. Fuencaliente, at the southern tip, has vineyards planted on broad mantles of volcanic ash, in an almost lunar landscape. Hoyo de Mazo, in the east, occupies mid-altitude slopes. Norte de La Palma, the wettest and greenest subzone, comprises municipalities in the north of the island with high-altitude vineyards. The vineyards extend from areas close to the coast up to levels above 1,000 metres. The climate is Atlantic subtropical, influenced by the trade winds, wetter in the north and east and drier and sunnier in the south.

Soils

The soils are volcanic. In Fuencaliente and other southern areas the vines are planted in layers of picón or lapilli, which retain soil moisture and protect the roots from the heat. In the north and in Hoyo de Mazo there are more developed volcanic soils. Phylloxera never reached the Canaries, and the vines are grown ungrafted.

Grape varieties

The leading whites are Malvasía, Listán Blanco, Albillo, Gual, Sabro and Bujariego. Among the reds are Negramoll and Listán Negro, together with other minor local varieties. The island preserves a remarkable diversity of old varieties.

Styles

The island's most famous wine is the sweet Malvasía of Fuencaliente, made from overripe grapes, with intense aromas of ripe fruit, honey and flowers. Another speciality is vino de tea, typical of the north, which is aged in casks made of tea, the resinous heartwood of the Canary pine, and thus acquires characteristic resinous notes. The designation also produces dry whites, rosés and young reds from Negramoll and Listán Negro.
