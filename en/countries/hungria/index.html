Hungary

Hungary is one of the historic wine countries of Central Europe, famous above all for the sweet wines of Tokaj made from botrytised grapes. It also produces dry Furmint whites and characterful reds in regions such as Eger and Villány.

History

The vine was grown in the Carpathian Basin before the arrival of the Magyars, and the Romans had already spread it across the province of Pannonia, west of the Danube. After the founding of the Hungarian kingdom around the year 1000, monasteries and the nobility maintained and extended the vineyards. From the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, the Tokaj region gained an international reputation that would last for centuries: its sweet wines made from grapes affected by noble rot, the aszú, became a customary diplomatic gift and were served at many European courts. In 1737 a royal decree delimited the Tokaj production zone, one of the earliest official delimitations of a wine region.

At the end of the nineteenth century phylloxera destroyed much of the vineyard, which was replanted on American rootstocks. After the Second World War, the communist regime grouped production into state farms and cooperatives geared to volume and to exports to the Eastern Bloc, which damaged the reputation of many areas. After 1989 came privatisation, foreign investment, above all in Tokaj, and the return of many family wineries. Since joining the European Union in 2004, the country has followed a European-style system of designations of origin.

Climate and soils

Hungary lies at the heart of the Pannonian Plain, sheltered by the arc of the Carpathians. The climate is continental, with warm summers and cold winters, though there are important nuances: Lake Balaton moderates temperatures around it, the south, around Villány and Szekszárd, receives warmer influences and the mountainous north is cooler. In autumn, the mists that form where the Bodrog and Tisza rivers meet encourage the development of Botrytis cinerea in Tokaj.

Soils are very diverse. Volcanic soils stand out in Tokaj, in Eger, on Somló hill and on the northern shore of Balaton, at Badacsony. Elsewhere loess, clays and limestones are common, while on the Great Plain, between the Danube and the Tisza, sandy soils predominate, and much of the country’s volume is produced there.

Grapes and styles

Among white grapes, the leading variety is Furmint, the basis of the wines of Tokaj together with Hárslevelű and Sárgamuskotály (Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains). Olaszrizling (Welschriesling) is the most widely planted white grape in the country and very common around Balaton; Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and local varieties such as Juhfark, Kéknyelű, Leányka and Ezerjó are also grown. Among red grapes, the most widespread is Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch), alongside the historic Kadarka, Kékoportó (Portugieser) and the Bordeaux varieties, especially Cabernet Franc, which has found very favourable ground in Villány.

Styles are varied: the sweet wines of Tokaj (aszú, eszencia, szamorodni), mineral dry whites from volcanic soils, the red blend Bikavér (“bull’s blood”) of Eger and Szekszárd, structured reds from Villány, and traditional-method and tank-method sparkling wines.

Regulations

The country is divided into 22 wine regions (borvidék), grouped into several larger zones. Quality wines are covered by protected designations of origin (OEM in Hungarian) and protected geographical indications (OFJ). Some appellations, such as Tokaj, Eger and Villány, have developed their own rules governing varieties, yields and categories, with quality tiers that distinguish wines from more specific origins.

Regions & appellations

Tokaj

Protected designation of origin (OEM) · A region in north-eastern Hungary, at the foot of the Zemplén hills, famous for centuries for Tokaji Aszú, a sweet wine made from grapes affected by noble rot. Today it also produces dry Furmint whites of great interest.

Eger

Protected designation of origin (OEM) · A region in northern Hungary, on the slopes of the Bükk hills, best known for Egri Bikavér, the red blend called “bull’s blood”. It also produces the white blend Egri Csillag and varietal wines.

Villány

Protected designation of origin (OEM) · Hungary’s southernmost wine region, near the Croatian border, is the warmest in the country and is devoted mainly to full-bodied reds from Bordeaux varieties, especially Cabernet Franc.
