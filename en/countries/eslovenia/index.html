Slovenia

Slovenia, lying between the Alps, the Adriatic and the Pannonian plain, is a small wine country with a long tradition, known above all for its whites. Its hills of Goriška Brda, adjoining the Italian Collio, are a benchmark for Rebula and for skin-macerated white wines.

History

Winegrowing in what is now Slovenia predates the arrival of the Romans, who consolidated and extended it. In the Middle Ages monasteries and religious orders maintained the vineyards, and for centuries the region was part of the Habsburg lands, which tied it to the central European wine tradition. In the city of Maribor grows a Žametovka vine planted several centuries ago, considered one of the oldest in the world, and the historic cellar of Ptuj is among the oldest in the country. As in the rest of Europe, phylloxera destroyed much of the vineyard area at the end of the nineteenth century.

After the First World War the west of the country was for a time under Italian sovereignty, while the rest became part of Yugoslavia. In the Yugoslav period large cooperatives and bulk production dominated. Independence in 1991 and accession to the European Union in 2004 spurred the growth of family wineries bottling their own wine. Some of them, above all along the Italian border, have become reference points for natural winemaking and for long-macerated whites, often called orange or amber wines.

Climate and soils

Slovenia brings together very different influences in a small space. The west is influenced by the Adriatic, with mild winters, warm summers and winds such as the bora, while the east has a continental climate with cold winters and hot summers. In the west the marls and sandstones of flysch, known locally as opoka or ponca, predominate, and in the Karst there is terra rossa over limestone. The east has plenty of marl, clay and sand on gentle hills.

Regions

The country is divided into three main wine regions. Primorska, in the west, includes Goriška Brda, the Vipava valley, the Karst and Slovenian Istria. In Goriška Brda, the natural continuation of the Italian Collio, the leading grapes are Rebula, Sauvignonasse, Chardonnay, Pinot Gris and Merlot; the Karst is the home of Teran, a red made from the Refošk variety. Podravje, in the north-east, which includes Slovenian Styria and Prekmurje, is above all an area of fresh, aromatic whites from Laški Rizling, Šipon (Furmint), Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling, Traminer and Pinot Gris, as well as late-harvest sweet wines. Posavje, in the south-east, is known for Cviček, a light, low-alcohol red made from a blend of red and white varieties, and for the reds of Bela krajina, where Modra frankinja (Blaufränkisch) is grown.

Grapes and styles

Although interesting reds are made, above all in Primorska, most of the production is white. Styles range from young, fresh, aromatic whites to age-worthy wines fermented and matured in wood, whites macerated on their skins for days or months, sparkling wines and sweet wines made from overripe, botrytised or frozen grapes. Rebula is the emblematic variety of the west; Laški Rizling, the Slovenian name for Welschriesling, is the most widespread white in the east.

Regulations

Slovenian law distinguishes table wine, regional wine with a geographical indication and quality wines with a protected designation of origin, which may carry the term kakovostno (quality) or vrhunsko (premium quality). Some traditional wines, such as Cviček and Teran, have a specific recognition tied to their area. For late-harvest wines there are designations such as pozna trgatev, izbor, jagodni izbor, suhi jagodni izbor and ledeno vino (ice wine), which recall those of Germany and Austria.

Key grapes
Rebula (Ribolla Gialla), Graševina (Welschriesling), Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Merlot, Furmint, Riesling, Blaufränkisch

Regions & appellations

Goriška Brda

Wine district with protected designation of origin (ZGP) · Goriška Brda, at the western tip of Slovenia, is a hilly area adjoining the Italian Collio, from which it is separated only by the border drawn after the Second World War. It is the land of Rebula and one of the cradles of long-macerated white wines.
