China

China has one of the largest vineyard areas in the world, although much of it is devoted to table grapes and raisins. Its modern wine industry, born in the late nineteenth century, has grown rapidly since the 1980s, with Ningxia, Shandong, Xinjiang and Yunnan among its most prominent areas.

History

China has its own wild vine species, and archaeologists have found in the north of the country remains of very ancient fermented drinks that included fruit, possibly wild grapes, together with rice and honey. The European vine, Vitis vinifera, however, arrived from Central Asia along the routes later known as the Silk Road. Tradition attributes its introduction to the time of the Han dynasty, in the second century BC, following the journeys westwards of the diplomat Zhang Qian. In the centuries that followed grape wine was made in some regions, especially in the western oases such as Turpan, and it appears in the poetry of the Tang dynasty, though it never displaced fermented and distilled grain drinks, which continued to dominate consumption.

The modern wine industry was born in 1892, when the businessman Zhang Bishi founded the Changyu winery in Yantai, in Shandong province, and imported European vines. From the 1980s, with the country’s economic opening, joint ventures with foreign partners were set up and large national groups grew. Since the early twenty-first century the number of wineries has multiplied, plantings of wine grape varieties have spread to new provinces, and international wine and luxury groups have invested.

Climate and regions

The country’s enormous size explains its diversity of conditions. In the north and north-west, in regions such as Ningxia, Xinjiang, Gansu, Hebei and Shanxi, the climate is continental, dry and very sunny, with warm summers and winters so cold that in many areas the vines must be buried every autumn to protect them from frost and uncovered again in spring. On the Shandong peninsula, on the east coast, the climate is milder and maritime, but the summer monsoon rains coincide with ripening and increase the risk of disease. In the south-west, in Yunnan, vineyards have been planted at high altitude in mountain valleys, where elevation offsets the low latitude.

Soils vary accordingly: sandy, stony and alluvial soils in the arid areas of the north-west, often poor in organic matter, and deeper soils in the eastern areas. In the dry regions viticulture depends heavily on irrigation.

Grape varieties and styles

Cabernet Sauvignon is the dominant variety for red wine and the basis of many of the most ambitious wines, often blended with Merlot and Cabernet Franc. A variety long known as Cabernet Gernischt has been largely identified as Carménère. In recent years Marselan, a French crossing of Cabernet Sauvignon and Grenache that adapts well to several Chinese regions, has also spread, and Syrah and Pinot Noir are grown. Among the whites, Chardonnay, Riesling and Welschriesling, known locally as “Italian Riesling”, stand out, along with some more recently planted varieties.

Production focuses on red wines, from simple, mass-market wines to barrel-aged Bordeaux-style reds made by medium-sized and small wineries. Whites, traditional-method sparkling wines and ice wines from the coldest areas of the north-east are also produced.

Regulations

China regulates wine production through national standards on labelling and quality, and has recognised geographical indications for some wine areas, such as the eastern foothills of the Helan Mountains in Ningxia, or Yantai in Shandong. Some regional authorities have also developed their own systems for promoting and classifying wineries. The sector combines large industrial groups with a growing number of family-owned or private wineries, where much of the pursuit of quality is concentrated.

Key grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Carmenère, Syrah (Shiraz), Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Riesling, Graševina (Welschriesling)

Regions & appellations

Ningxia

Wine region (geographical indication of the Helan Mountains’ eastern foothills) · A region in north-west China lying between the Yellow River and the Helan Mountains, which since the early twenty-first century has become one of the country’s most dynamic wine areas. Its dry, sunny, continental climate produces above all reds from Cabernet Sauvignon and other Bordeaux varieties.
