South Africa

South Africa combines more than three and a half centuries of winegrowing history with a highly dynamic modern scene. The Western Cape produces everything from Cabernet Sauvignon and Pinotage to old-vine Chenin Blanc, its most widely planted variety.

History

South African winegrowing began with the settlement established at the Cape in 1652 by the Dutch East India Company under the command of Jan van Riebeeck, as a stopover on the route to Asia. Van Riebeeck recorded the first Cape winemaking in his diary in February 1659. In 1685 Governor Simon van der Stel founded the Constantia estate, south of present-day Cape Town, and from 1688 French Huguenots fleeing religious persecution arrived and settled in the Franschhoek valley, bringing their agricultural knowledge. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the sweet wines of Constantia became highly famous at European courts and tables.

Powdery mildew and, from 1886, phylloxera ruined much of the vineyard. The subsequent replanting led to chronic overproduction, and in 1918 the KWV cooperative was founded; for decades it regulated the market, set prices and quotas and steered production towards volume, brandy and fortified wines. In 1925 Professor Abraham Izak Perold of Stellenbosch University created Pinotage by crossing Pinot Noir with Cinsault, a variety then known locally as Hermitage. The Wine of Origin system was created in 1973. After the end of apartheid and the democratic elections of 1994, the country opened up to international markets, the industry was liberalised and a new generation of independent producers emerged, reviving old vineyards, exploring new areas and giving a strong boost to quality.

Climate and soils

Almost all the vineyards are in the Western Cape province, around latitudes 33 and 34 degrees south. The climate is Mediterranean, with rainy winters and dry, warm summers, moderated by the influence of two oceans: the cold Benguela current, flowing up the Atlantic coast, cools the coastal areas, and the south-easterly wind known as the Cape Doctor ventilates the vineyards in summer. The mountains create a great variety of slopes and aspects. The soils are among the oldest in the wine world: decomposed granite, sandstones of the Table Mountain formation, and shales and slates of the Malmesbury formation.

Grapes and styles

Chenin Blanc, traditionally called Steen, is the most widely planted variety. It is made in styles ranging from fresh, fruity whites to complex, barrel-fermented old-vine wines that have brought the country great prestige. Among the reds, Cabernet Sauvignon and Bordeaux-style blends stand out, along with Syrah, labelled here as Shiraz or Syrah depending on the style, and Pinotage itself. Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Merlot and Cinsault are also grown, the last of these revalued in old vineyards. Traditional-method sparkling wines are sold under the term Méthode Cap Classique, and Constantia keeps up the tradition of sweet Muscat wines.

Regulations

The Wine of Origin system divides the territory into geographical units, regions, districts and smaller areas called wards, and certifies the origin, variety and vintage stated on the label. Many farms produce “Estate Wine” from grapes grown in their own vineyards. The Old Vine Project has promoted the Certified Heritage Vineyards seal to identify wines from vineyards at least 35 years old. The industry has also promoted programmes for sustainability, the protection of native flora and social transformation.

Key grapes
Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah (Shiraz), Pinotage, Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Merlot, Cinsault

Regions & appellations

Stellenbosch

Wine of Origin (WO) · Stellenbosch, east of Cape Town, is South Africa’s best-known wine district and the centre of its wine education and research. It stands out for Cabernet Sauvignon, Bordeaux blends, Pinotage and Chenin Blanc.

Swartland

Wine of Origin (WO) · Swartland, north of Cape Town, went from being an area of wheat and bulk wine to becoming the focus of South Africa’s wine renewal, with old, dry-farmed bush vines of Chenin Blanc, Syrah and Mediterranean varieties.

Constantia

Wine of Origin (WO) · Constantia, on the Cape Peninsula south of Cape Town, is South Africa’s oldest wine area. It was famous in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries for its sweet wines and today also stands out for its cool-climate whites.

Franschhoek

Wine of Origin (WO) · Franschhoek, the “French corner”, is a mountain-ringed valley east of Stellenbosch where the Huguenots settled in the late seventeenth century. It is known for its old Semillon vines, its sparkling wines and its food scene.

Paarl

Wine of Origin (WO) · Paarl, north of Stellenbosch, is one of the Cape’s historic districts, dominated by the great granite domes that give it its name. It produces a wide range of wines, mainly warm-climate reds from Syrah, Pinotage and Cabernet Sauvignon, and Chenin Blanc.

Hemel-en-Aarde

Three wards (WO) in the Walker Bay district · A group of valleys near Hermanus, on South Africa’s south coast, divided into three wards. Cooled by the Atlantic Ocean, it is South Africa’s benchmark area for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

Elgin

Wine of Origin (WO), district of the Cape South Coast region · An elevated plateau south-east of Cape Town, traditionally devoted to apple orchards and now one of South Africa’s coolest wine areas. It stands out for cool-climate Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Syrah.

Walker Bay

Wine of Origin (WO), district of the Cape South Coast region · A coastal district around Hermanus and Walker Bay, south-east of Cape Town. It groups several wards, including the three of Hemel-en-Aarde, and is known for cool-climate Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc.
