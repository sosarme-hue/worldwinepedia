Europe

Italy

Italy is one of the world's largest wine producers and grows vines in all twenty of its regions, from the Alps to Sicily. It stands out for an extraordinary wealth of native grape varieties and for wines as different as Barolo, Chianti Classico, Amarone, Prosecco and the volcanic reds of Etna.

History

Vines have been grown on the Italian peninsula for thousands of years. The Greek colonists who settled in the south from the eighth century BC spread vine-growing and winemaking techniques, and called part of these lands Oenotria, a name usually associated with wine. Under Rome, viticulture spread across the whole peninsula and much of the empire, and writers such as Cato, Varro, Columella and Pliny the Elder left treatises and descriptions of vineyards and of celebrated wines, such as Falernian from Campania. After the fall of the Roman Empire, monasteries and towns kept vine-growing alive. In 1716 Grand Duke Cosimo III de' Medici issued an edict delimiting the production areas of Chianti, Pomino, Carmignano and Valdarno di Sopra, one of the earliest legal delimitations of wine areas in Europe.

In the nineteenth century, alongside the unification of the country, viticulture and winemaking were modernised in regions such as Piedmont and Tuscany. Phylloxera, vine diseases and the economic crises and wars of the first half of the twentieth century slowed this progress, and for decades a large share of production was geared towards bulk wine. The great transformation in quality began in the 1960s and 1970s, with new legislation on designations, the renewal of wineries and vineyards and growing attention to local varieties and terroirs.

Geography, climate and soils

Italy stretches for more than a thousand kilometres from north to south, between the Alps and the centre of the Mediterranean, and includes the large islands of Sicily and Sardinia. The Apennines run down the peninsula like a backbone, so much of the vineyard area lies on hills and slopes. The climate ranges from the continental and alpine conditions of the north, with cold winters and marked temperature swings, to the hot, dry Mediterranean climate of the south and the islands, where altitude and the nearby sea help to temper the heat. Soils are highly varied: calcareous marls in the Langhe, galestro and alberese in Tuscany, volcanic soils on Etna, around Vesuvius and in the hills of Soave, and glacial moraine deposits around the northern lakes, as in Franciacorta.

A wealth of native varieties

Few countries have preserved so many grape varieties of their own: the national register of vine varieties lists several hundred. Sangiovese is the most widely planted red and the basis of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. Nebbiolo gives the great reds of Piedmont and of Valtellina in Lombardy, while Barbera and Dolcetto are Piedmont's everyday varieties. Corvina dominates in Veneto; Montepulciano in the central Adriatic area; Aglianico, Primitivo and Negroamaro in the south; and Nero d'Avola and Nerello Mascalese in Sicily. Leading whites include Glera for Prosecco, Garganega for Soave, Vermentino, Fiano, Greco, Verdicchio, Arneis, Cortese and the Trebbiano family, a name shared by several distinct varieties. Alongside them grow international varieties such as Pinot Grigio, widespread in the north-east, Chardonnay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.

The DOC, DOCG and IGT system

A decree of 1963 created the Denominazioni di Origine Controllata (DOC); the first were recognised in 1966, starting with Vernaccia di San Gimignano. The same law provided for a higher category, Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita (DOCG), first granted in 1980 to Brunello di Montalcino, Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, Barolo and Barbaresco. Law 164 of 1992 reformed the system and introduced the Indicazione Geografica Tipica (IGT), a category covering broader areas with more flexible rules. DOCG wines are subject to stricter controls and carry a numbered guarantee strip on the neck of the bottle. Today there are more than seventy DOCGs and more than three hundred DOCs. Since the European reform of 2009, DOC and DOCG belong to the EU category PDO and IGT to PGI, although the traditional terms remain in use. Each designation has a disciplinare setting out the area, varieties, yields and ageing, as well as terms such as Classico (the historic zone), Superiore and Riserva.

The Super Tuscans

In the late 1960s and the 1970s, some Tuscan producers began making wines outside the rules of the time, which in Chianti required white grapes in the blend and made no provision for Bordeaux varieties. Sassicaia from Tenuta San Guido in Bolgheri, made from Cabernet Sauvignon planted by Mario Incisa della Rocchetta, reached the market with the 1968 vintage; Antinori's Tignanello, first made in 1971, combined Sangiovese with Cabernet and ageing in small barrels. Sold as mere table wines but at high prices, they were dubbed Super Tuscans by the English-language press. Their success influenced the creation of the IGT category, the extension of the Bolgheri DOC to red wines and the reform of the Chianti Classico rules.

Styles

The range of styles is vast: age-worthy reds, fresh and aromatic whites, sparkling wines made by the Charmat method, such as Prosecco and Asti, or by the traditional method, such as Franciacorta and Trento, wines from dried grapes such as Amarone, Recioto, Vin Santo and Passito di Pantelleria, and fortified wines such as Marsala.

Regions & appellations

Piedmont

Wine region · Piedmont, at the foot of the Alps in north-west Italy, is the home of Nebbiolo and of two of Italy's most prestigious reds, Barolo and Barbaresco. It also produces Barbera, Dolcetto, whites such as Gavi and Roero Arneis, and the aromatic Asti and Moscato d'Asti.

Barolo

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Barolo, made exclusively from Nebbiolo in the Langhe hills of Piedmont, is one of Italy's great age-worthy reds. It was one of the country's first DOCGs, recognised in 1980.

Barbaresco

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Barbaresco is a Nebbiolo red from the Langhe, made in a small area north-east of Alba, beside the Tanaro river. It shares its grape and its prestige with Barolo, although its ageing requirements are somewhat shorter.

Tuscany

Wine region · Tuscany, in central Italy, is the great home of Sangiovese, the basis of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. Its coast also gave rise to the Bordeaux-variety wines known as Super Tuscans.

Chianti Classico

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Chianti Classico is the historic heart of Chianti, between Florence and Siena, and its wine is made from at least 80% Sangiovese. Its symbol is the black rooster, and it has been a separate DOCG since 1996.

Brunello di Montalcino

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Brunello di Montalcino is a 100% Sangiovese red made in the municipality of Montalcino, in the province of Siena. It is known for its long mandatory ageing and its capacity to develop in bottle.

Bolgheri

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · Bolgheri, on the Tuscan coast in the province of Livorno, is the birthplace of Sassicaia and the main centre for Italian reds made from Bordeaux varieties. Its DOC chiefly covers blends of Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc.

Veneto

Wine region · Veneto, in north-east Italy, is one of the country's largest wine-producing regions. It is the source of Prosecco, Soave, Bardolino and the reds of Valpolicella, including Amarone.

Valpolicella

DOC and DOCG (Amarone and Recioto) · Valpolicella, in the hills north of Verona, produces Corvina-based reds ranging from young Valpolicella to Ripasso and wines from dried grapes: the dry Amarone and the sweet Recioto, both DOCG.

Prosecco

DOC and DOCG · Prosecco is a wine, usually sparkling, made mainly from the Glera grape in Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia. Its historic heart lies in the hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, which hold DOCG status.

Soave

DOC and DOCG · Soave is one of Italy's best-known white wines, made mainly from Garganega in the hills east of Verona. The area also produces Soave Superiore and the sweet Recioto di Soave, both DOCG.

Trentino-Alto Adige

Wine region · Trentino-Alto Adige, Italy's northernmost Alpine region, brings together two autonomous provinces with their own identities: Trento and Bolzano. It is known for its mountain whites, its Lagrein, Schiava and Teroldego reds and the traditional-method sparkling Trento DOC.

Franciacorta

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Franciacorta, in the Lombard province of Brescia beside Lake Iseo, produces traditional-method sparkling wines based on Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. In 1995 it became the first Italian bottle-fermented sparkling wine to obtain DOCG status.

Sicily

Wine region · Sicily, the largest island in the Mediterranean, has one of the largest vineyard areas in Italy. Its production ranges from Nero d'Avola reds and the volcanic wines of Etna to fortified Marsala and the sweet wines of Pantelleria and the Aeolian Islands.

Etna

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · The Etna DOC, on the slopes of the Sicilian volcano, produces Nerello Mascalese reds and Carricante whites from high-altitude vineyards on volcanic soils. Recognised in 1968, it was Sicily's first DOC.

Campania

Wine region · Campania, in southern Italy around Naples, was already one of the most celebrated wine areas in Roman times. Today it is known for Taurasi, a red from Aglianico, and for whites such as Fiano di Avellino and Greco di Tufo.

Puglia

Wine region · Puglia, the «heel» of the Italian boot, is one of Italy's largest wine-producing regions. It is known for reds from Primitivo, Negroamaro and Nero di Troia and for a long tradition of rosé.

Abruzzo

Wine region · Abruzzo, between the Apennines and the Adriatic Sea, is the home of Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, one of Italy's most widespread reds. It also produces the rosé Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo and whites from Trebbiano, Pecorino and Passerina.

Langhe

Wine area and Controlled Designation of Origin (Langhe DOC) · The Langhe are a hilly area of southern Piedmont, around Alba, and the home of Barolo and Barbaresco. Besides those DOCGs, the area has its own DOC, Langhe, which covers reds and whites from many varieties.

Roero

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Roero is an area of sandy hills on the left bank of the Tanaro, facing the Langhe, in Piedmont. Its DOCG covers Nebbiolo reds and the white Roero Arneis.

Asti

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (Asti / Moscato d'Asti DOCG) · The Asti DOCG, in south-eastern Piedmont, covers the sweet, aromatic wines made from Moscato Bianco: sparkling Asti and the delicate, gently fizzy, low-alcohol Moscato d'Asti.

Gavi

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Gavi, also called Cortese di Gavi, is a white-wine DOCG in south-eastern Piedmont, in the province of Alessandria, made exclusively from the Cortese variety.

Lugana

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · Lugana is a white-wine DOC on the southern shore of Lake Garda, shared between Lombardy and Veneto, made from the local Turbiana variety on clay soils of glacial origin.

Bardolino

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC); Bardolino Superiore is a DOCG · Bardolino is an appellation on the eastern shore of Lake Garda, in the province of Verona, known for its light reds and its Chiaretto rosés made mainly from Corvina and Rondinella.

Collio

Controlled Designation of Origin (Collio Goriziano or Collio DOC) · Collio is a hilly area in the province of Gorizia, in Friuli-Venezia Giulia, along the Slovenian border, famous for white wines grown on marl and sandstone soils known as ponca.

Friuli Colli Orientali

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC), with the Picolit, Ramandolo and Rosazzo DOCGs within its territory · Friuli Colli Orientali, formerly Colli Orientali del Friuli, is a hillside DOC in the province of Udine, along the Slovenian border, known for its whites, native reds such as Refosco and Schioppettino, and sweet Picolit.

Vino Nobile di Montepulciano

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Vino Nobile di Montepulciano is a Tuscan red made from Sangiovese, known locally as Prugnolo Gentile, grown on the hills around the town of Montepulciano, in the province of Siena. It was one of Italy's first DOCGs.

Montefalco

Wine area with the Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG and the Montefalco DOC · Montefalco, in central Umbria, is the home of Sagrantino, a red grape with very marked tannins that gives long-lived dry reds and a traditional sweet wine from dried grapes.

Maremma

Wine area with the Maremma Toscana DOC and several DOCs and DOCGs, including Morellino di Scansano · The Maremma is the coastal strip of southern Tuscany, mainly in the province of Grosseto. Once a land of marshes, it has become one of the region's most dynamic wine areas, with reds from Sangiovese and international varieties and whites from Vermentino.

Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC); the Riserva is a DOCG (Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva) · Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is the best-known white-wine appellation of the Marche. It is made from Verdicchio on the hills of the province of Ancona, around the town of Jesi, and its Riserva version holds DOCG status.

Taurasi

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Taurasi is a DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), devoted to structured, long-aged reds from Aglianico. It was the first DOCG in southern Italy.

Fiano di Avellino

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Fiano di Avellino is a white-wine DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), made from Fiano on cool hillsides. It gives aromatic, structured whites able to develop over many years.

Greco di Tufo

Controlled and Guaranteed Designation of Origin (DOCG) · Greco di Tufo is a white-wine DOCG in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino (Campania), made from the Greco variety around the village of Tufo. It gives whites of good structure and acidity, also in a sparkling version.

Aglianico del Vulture

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC); Aglianico del Vulture Superiore is a DOCG · Aglianico del Vulture is the best-known wine of Basilicata. It is made from Aglianico on the slopes of Monte Vulture, an extinct volcano in the north of the region, and gives reds of great structure and acidity.

Cirò

Controlled Designation of Origin (DOC) · Cirò is the best-known appellation in Calabria. It lies on the Ionian coast of the province of Crotone and is famous above all for its reds and rosés from Gaglioppo, along with whites from Greco Bianco.

Sardinia

Wine region · Sardinia, the second-largest island in the Mediterranean, has a winegrowing tradition of its own, shaped by varieties such as Cannonau, Vermentino and Carignano. Its only DOCG is Vermentino di Gallura.

Valtellina

Wine area with the Valtellina Superiore and Sforzato di Valtellina DOCGs and the Rosso di Valtellina DOC · Valtellina is an Alpine valley in Lombardy, in the province of Sondrio, where Nebbiolo, known here as Chiavennasca, is grown on terraces on the sunny slope. It produces fine reds and Sforzato, made from dried grapes.

Lambrusco of Emilia

Group of Controlled Designations of Origin (DOCs) for Lambrusco in Emilia-Romagna · Lambrusco is the sparkling or semi-sparkling red wine typical of Emilia, above all of the provinces of Modena and Reggio Emilia, where several DOCs cover the different types of Lambrusco, dry or with some sweetness.

Oltrepò Pavese

Wine area with the Oltrepò Pavese DOC, other DOCs and the Oltrepò Pavese Metodo Classico DOCG · Oltrepò Pavese is the hilly area of the province of Pavia lying south of the Po, in Lombardy. It is one of Italy's main Pinot Noir areas, the basis of its traditional-method sparkling wines, and also produces reds from Croatina and Barbera.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Wine region · Friuli-Venezia Giulia, in north-eastern Italy, between the Alps, the Adriatic, Slovenia and Austria, is one of Italy's great white-wine regions, with hillside appellations such as Collio and Friuli Colli Orientali and broad lowland DOCs.
