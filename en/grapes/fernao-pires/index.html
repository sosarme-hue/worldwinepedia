Fernão Pires

One of Portugal’s most widely planted white grapes, aromatic, versatile and early-ripening, called Maria Gomes in Bairrada, which makes everything from young floral whites to sparkling and sweet wines.

Origin

Fernão Pires is a traditional white variety of Portugal and one of the most widespread in the country. It goes by this name in most regions, while in Bairrada it is known as Maria Gomes. Its precise origin is not known for certain, although it has long been associated with the central regions, such as the Tejo, Lisboa and Bairrada, where it was for a long time the dominant white grape.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous and productive vine that adapts to a wide range of soils and climates. It buds early, which exposes it to spring frosts, and also ripens early, often before most Portuguese white varieties. It is prone to rot in wet years and loses acidity quickly if picked late, so the harvest date is decisive for the style of wine. In warm climates it is usually picked early to preserve freshness.

In the glass

It is an aromatic grape: its wines show floral notes (orange blossom, rose), citrus such as lemon and lime, stone fruit and often a spicy or herbal touch that some compare to the Muscats. On the palate they are usually light- to medium-bodied with moderate acidity. Its versatility is remarkable: it makes fresh, perfumed young whites, barrel-aged whites, semi-sparkling and sparkling wines, especially in Bairrada, where it is the basis of many of them, and even some sweet wines from late-harvested grapes. It is also often blended with Arinto, which adds acidity.

Where it is grown

It is grown in almost every Portuguese wine region, being especially important in the Tejo, where it is the main white variety, in Lisboa, the Setúbal Peninsula and Bairrada. It is also found in the Alentejo and other parts of the country. Outside Portugal it is grown only on a very small scale.

At the table

Its young whites go with fish, shellfish, salads and lightly spiced dishes; the sparkling wines of Bairrada are the traditional match for roast suckling pig. Serve at 8–10 °C.
