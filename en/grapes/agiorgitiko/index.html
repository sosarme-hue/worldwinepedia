Agiorgitiko

The emblematic red grape of the Peloponnese and the sole variety of the Nemea PDO. It gives deeply coloured wines with generous fruit and soft tannins, from rosés and young reds to oak-aged reds.

Origin

Agiorgitiko comes from the north-eastern Peloponnese, around Nemea, in the Corinth region, where it has been grown for centuries. Its name means “of Saint George” (Agios Georgios), and it is usually linked to a chapel or village dedicated to the saint in the area, although the exact origin of the name is unclear. Locally it has also been known as Mavro Nemeas, “black of Nemea”. It is one of the most widely planted red grapes in Greece.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine that adapts to very different altitudes and soils. Its behaviour changes greatly with elevation: in low, warm areas it ripens easily and gives wines rich in sugar with moderate acidity; on higher, cooler slopes it ripens more slowly and retains more acidity. It needs careful yield control to produce concentrated wines. It is rich in colouring matter and lends itself to very different winemaking approaches.

In the glass

It gives wines of intense, bright red colour, with aromas of ripe red and black fruit, such as cherry, raspberry, plum and blackberry, often with notes of sweet spices such as nutmeg or cinnamon and, with barrel ageing, hints of vanilla, cocoa and tobacco. On the palate it is usually juicy and approachable, with soft, rounded tannins and medium acidity. This combination makes it very versatile: it gives fresh, fruity rosés, young reds for early drinking, sometimes made by carbonic maceration, and structured oak-aged reds that can improve for several years in bottle.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark area is Nemea, where the PDO requires 100% Agiorgitiko, but it is grown throughout the Peloponnese and in other Greek regions, such as Attica and Macedonia. It is often blended with international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or Syrah in geographical indication wines, or with other Greek grapes. Outside Greece it is hardly grown. Its versatility has made it one of the Greek red grapes most present on international markets.
