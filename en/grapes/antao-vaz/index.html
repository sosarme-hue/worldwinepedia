Antão Vaz

The great white grape of the Alentejo, tolerant of heat and drought, which makes medium- to full-bodied whites with tropical and citrus fruit, often blended with Arinto for freshness.

Origin

Antão Vaz is a white variety native to southern Portugal, closely associated with the Vidigueira area in the Baixo Alentejo, where it has traditionally been grown. For a long time it was a local grape, little known outside its district, but with the modernisation of Alentejo viticulture at the end of the twentieth century it spread throughout the region and became one of its hallmarks.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, reliable and productive vine with large bunches and thick-skinned berries. Its main virtue is its adaptation to the Alentejo climate: it copes well with intense heat, strong sunshine and summer drought, and is fairly resistant to disease. It ripens easily and accumulates sugar quickly, while its acidity falls if the harvest is delayed. Growers therefore choose the picking date according to the style they want: an early harvest preserves freshness and a later one gives broader, more alcoholic wines.

In the glass

It makes straw-yellow whites of medium to full body, with aromas of ripe tropical fruit, peach, citrus and tangerine peel, and sometimes mineral notes. Its acidity is moderate, so it is often blended with Arinto, which adds tension, and with Roupeiro, which adds perfume. It responds well to barrel fermentation and ageing, which give it texture and toasty nuances, and it is also made in talha, the large traditional clay jars of the Alentejo, especially around Vidigueira. Varietal wines are common and some develop well in bottle for a few years.

Where it is grown

Together with Arinto and Roupeiro it is one of the main white grapes of the Alentejo and is found in all its subregions, being especially important in Vidigueira. Its good performance in warm climates has encouraged planting outside the Alentejo, in other warm regions of the country.

At the table

Its whites go with grilled fish, salt cod, shellfish, white meat and sheep’s cheeses. Serve young wines at 8–10 °C and oak-aged ones at 10–12 °C.
