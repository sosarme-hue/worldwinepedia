Sousón

A red-fleshed grape with very high acidity, the same variety as Portugal’s Vinhão: the backbone of the traditional reds of the Minho and a revived variety in Galicia, where it brings colour, freshness and structure.

Origin

Sousón is a red variety of north-western Iberia. It bears this name in Galicia, while in northern Portugal it is known as Vinhão; genetic studies have shown that they are a single variety. In the Douro it is called Sousão, a spelling that has also spread to other regions. Most specialists place its origin in the Minho, on one bank of the river or the other, where it has long been documented.

Viticulture

It is a teinturier grape: not only its skin but also its flesh is coloured, which allows it to give extremely deep-coloured musts. Its bunches are small to medium and its thick-skinned berries ripen with very high acidity and moderate sugar. It buds and ripens mid-season and adapts to the cool, humid climate of the Atlantic coast, although it is susceptible to powdery mildew. In Galicia, where it almost disappeared after phylloxera, it was revived from the last decades of the twentieth century.

In the glass

It makes almost opaque, purple wines with lively acidity, firm tannins and aromas of black and tart red fruit (blackberry, blueberry, blackcurrant), with herbal, floral and sometimes earthy notes. On its own it is austere and taut, so in Galicia it is often blended with Mencía, Caíño, Brancellao or Espadeiro, to which it brings colour, freshness and ageing potential. In the Douro it is used in small proportions in Port, precisely for its colour and acidity.

Where it is grown

In Portugal, as Vinhão, it is the main red grape of the Vinho Verde region, where it makes the traditional reds of the Minho: very dark, with piercing acidity and a purple froth, they were traditionally drunk from white bowls. It is also found in the Douro. In Spain it is grown in Galicia, mainly in Ribeiro, in Rías Baixas, particularly in the O Rosal and Condado do Tea subzones, and in Ribeira Sacra, always on modest areas.

At the table

Its acidity and fruit suit fatty meats, charcuterie, cured pork shoulder, hearty stews and fish casseroles. Serve it at 14–16 °C.
