Verdelho

A white grape from Madeira and the Azores that gives the medium-dry style of Madeira as well as fresh, saline still whites; it is also grown in Australia.

Origin

Verdelho is a Portuguese white grape associated for centuries with the Atlantic islands of Madeira and the Azores. Before the arrival of powdery mildew and phylloxera in the nineteenth century it was the most widely planted variety on Madeira. It should not be confused with Spain’s Verdejo or Italy’s Verdello, and in the Douro the name Verdelho has sometimes been applied to Gouveio, which is in fact Godello: these are distinct varieties.

Viticulture

It is a vine of medium vigour, with small bunches and moderate yields, that ripens relatively early and keeps good acidity. On Madeira it is grown mainly on the cooler north side of the island, on terraces of volcanic soil. In the Azores, particularly on the island of Pico, it grows in small plots protected from wind and salt spray by walls of volcanic stone, the currais; this vineyard landscape was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004.

In the glass

On Madeira it gives the medium-dry style of varietal wine: amber to deep golden, with aromas of nuts, caramel, citrus peel, spice and smoky notes, and lively acidity that balances the sweetness. Like other Madeiras it undergoes oxidative ageing with heat and can last for many decades. As a dry still wine, in the Azores or Australia, it makes medium-bodied whites with aromas of citrus, peach and tropical fruit and a pronounced saline character in the island wines.

Where it is grown

In Portugal it is found mainly in the Madeira DOP and in the Azores, where the Pico DOP produces both fortified and still wines; it is also grown in small quantities on the mainland. Outside Portugal it has had a notable presence in Australia, especially in the Hunter Valley and Western Australia, where fruity dry whites are made. There are also plantings in South Africa and elsewhere.

At the table

Verdelho Madeira goes with soups, pâté, mushrooms and semi-hard cheeses; dry whites with fish, shellfish and light Asian dishes. Serve Madeira at 12–14 °C and dry whites at 8–10 °C.
