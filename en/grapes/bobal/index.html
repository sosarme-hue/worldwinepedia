Bobal

A native red grape from the Utiel-Requena plateau and La Manchuela, deeply coloured and fresh, which has moved from bulk wine to quality wines.

Origin

Bobal is native to eastern Spain, closely tied to the Requena-Utiel plateau in inland Valencia and to the neighbouring lands of Cuenca and Albacete. Its name is often linked to the shape of its bunch, said to resemble a bull’s head (from the Latin bovale), though this is a traditional etymology.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive, late-ripening vine, well suited to the continental climate with Mediterranean influence of these plateaus, with cold winters, hot summers and little rain. Its bunches are compact and its thick-skinned berries are very rich in colouring matter. Old, dry-farmed bush vines are common.

In the glass

Its most striking feature is the combination of very deep colour and relatively high acidity with moderate alcohol. The wines show red and black fruit (sour cherry, blackberry, blueberry), floral and sometimes herbal notes; the tannins can be pronounced. It is excellent for fresh, fruity rosés, long a speciality of the area, and for young reds. Old vines give deeper reds that can take oak ageing.

Where it is grown

It is one of Spain’s most widely planted red varieties, though almost entirely within its home area. It dominates the Utiel-Requena D.O. and is important in Manchuela and Ribera del Júcar, with a presence in Valencia and Almansa too. For decades it went mostly into bulk and blending wine thanks to its colour, but since the late twentieth century many producers have championed it as a quality variety. Outside Spain it is grown only on a very small scale.

Its richness in colouring matter explains why it was traditionally used in the doble pasta (double paste) technique, in which the must ferments with a double proportion of skins to obtain very dark, concentrated wines, mainly intended to add colour to blends from other areas.

At the table

Bobal rosés go well with rice dishes, salads and tapas, and young reds with cured meats and grilled meat. The more structured reds from old vines are paired with local hearty dishes, such as gazpacho manchego, and with game.
