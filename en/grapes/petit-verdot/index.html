Petit Verdot

A very late-ripening Bordeaux red used in small proportions to add colour, tannin and spice to Médoc blends; in warm climates it also makes varietal wines.

Origin

Petit Verdot is an old variety from south-west France, present in Bordeaux before Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot became the region’s leading grapes. Its name, “little green one”, refers to how hard it is for it to ripen fully in Bordeaux’s Atlantic climate: in cool years its small berries can stay green.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous vine that buds early and ripens very late, later even than Cabernet Sauvignon. Its bunches are small and its berries tiny and thick-skinned, which explains the intensity of its colour and tannins. In Bordeaux its irregularity led to many plantings being uprooted during the twentieth century, but climate warming and better vineyard management have revived interest in it. In warm, sunny climates it ripens without difficulty and keeps good acidity.

In the glass

It makes very dark, almost black wines, full-bodied, with firm tannins and marked acidity. Its aromas recall violet, blackberry, blueberry and black plum, with notes of spice, liquorice, dried herbs and sometimes pencil lead or graphite. In blends, small amounts are enough to add colour, structure and a characteristic floral perfume.

Where it is grown

In Bordeaux it is used mainly in Médoc blends, usually in small percentages. Spain has a significant area, especially in Castilla-La Mancha, Extremadura and Andalusia, where single-varietal wines are also made. In Australia it is one of the most widely planted reds outside France, particularly in the Riverland. It is also grown in the United States (California, Virginia), Chile, Argentina, South Africa, Portugal (Alentejo) and Italy, mainly on the Tuscan coast.

At the table

Its structure calls for chargrilled red meat, game stews, lamb and aged cheeses. Serve at 16–18 °C; young wines benefit from some aeration before serving. Older bottles, by contrast, should be opened shortly before drinking and decanted carefully to separate any sediment.
