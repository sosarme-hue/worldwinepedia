Lagrein

A native red grape of Alto Adige, grown mainly around Bolzano, which gives very dark, fruity reds with firm tannins, as well as rosés known as Kretzer.

Origin

Lagrein is a native red grape of Alto Adige (Südtirol), the bilingual province of northern Italy, and has been grown for centuries around Bolzano. Its name is often linked to the Lagarina valley in neighbouring Trentino, although this origin has not been proven. DNA analysis indicates that it is an offspring of Teroldego, Trentino’s great red grape, and that it is related to Marzemino, confirming its deep Alpine roots. For a long time it was valued mainly for rosés and for adding colour to other wines, but since the late 20th century it has established itself as one of the region’s benchmark red grapes.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, late-ripening vine that needs warm sites to ripen properly in an Alpine setting. Its classic area is the Bolzano basin, and in particular the district of Gries at the foot of the mountains, where summers are very hot and the soils are alluvial, deep and rich in gravel and sand, with good drainage. It was traditionally trained on pergolas, although vertical trellising is also used today. It is susceptible to some diseases and can suffer from poor fruit set, which calls for careful vineyard work.

In the glass

As a red, traditionally labelled Dunkel (“dark”), it gives almost opaque, purple wines with good acidity, firm tannins and a medium to full body. Its aromas recall blackberry, black cherry, plum and violet, with notes of cocoa, coffee, liquorice and herbs. It can be made in tank to preserve the fruit or aged in barriques and large casks, and the best examples age well. The rosé, known as Kretzer, is fresh, fruity and deeply coloured.

Where it is grown

It is essentially an Alto Adige variety, made under the Alto Adige / Südtirol DOC, sometimes with the mention of Gries. It is also grown, to a lesser extent, in Trentino. Outside Italy there are small plantings of no great commercial weight.

At the table

It goes well with red meat, game, speck and cured meats, mountain stews, mushrooms and aged cheeses. Serve the red at 16–18 °C and the rosé at 10–12 °C.
