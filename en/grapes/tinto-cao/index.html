Tinto Cão

An old red grape of the Douro, with very low yields and late ripening, which gives fine, fresh, long-lived wines; it is one of the five recommended varieties for Port.

Origin

Tinto Cão is one of the oldest red varieties of the Douro, where it has been grown for centuries; it also has a traditional presence in the Dão. Its name, which in Portuguese literally means “red dog”, has no reliable explanation. Despite its quality, its low yields led it almost to disappear during the twentieth century, until research into Douro varieties placed it among the five grapes recommended for Port, alongside Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz and Tinta Barroca. As a result it was replanted, although it remains a minor variety.

Viticulture

It is a very low-yielding vine with small bunches and small, thick-skinned berries. It ripens late and slowly, so it keeps notable acidity even in the hot Douro summers. It is fairly resistant to disease and adapts well to the schist soils of the valley. Its performance in warm climates, where it retains freshness and balance, has aroused the interest of growers looking for varieties able to cope with rising temperatures.

In the glass

It makes wines of medium colour, less dark than those of Touriga Nacional, with firm but fine tannins, lively acidity and moderate alcohol. Its aromas are delicate: red and black fruit (raspberry, cherry, blackberry), floral and spicy notes and, with age, hints of leather and dried herbs. Its chief virtue is its ageing potential, which makes it valuable in long-lived Ports and in dry reds meant to develop in bottle. It is used mainly as a blending component, bringing freshness, elegance and longevity, although some producers make small quantities of varietal wine.

Where it is grown

It is found mainly in the Douro and, to a lesser extent, in the Dão, always on small areas. Outside Portugal there are experimental or very small plantings in a few New World countries, linked to the production of Port-style wines.

At the table

Its reds go with red meat, game, lamb and aged cheeses; long-aged Ports with nuts and blue cheeses. Serve the reds at 16–18 °C.
