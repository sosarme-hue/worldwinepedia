Counoise

A minor red grape of the southern Rhône, permitted in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, which brings freshness, peppery notes, red fruit and moderate colour to blends.

Origin

Counoise is a red variety from southern France, traditionally grown in the southern Rhône Valley and Provence. Its exact origin is not well documented, and various unreliable local traditions circulate about it, so it is best simply to note its long presence in the region. For a long time it was one of the many secondary varieties planted together in the mixed vineyards of the southern Rhône, alongside Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre and Cinsault.

Viticulture

It is a late-ripening and fairly productive vine, with large bunches and berries with relatively thin skins. It needs the long, warm summers of the south to ripen fully, and yields must be controlled if concentration is the aim, because heavy crops produce dilute wines. Its limited spread is partly explained by these demands and by growers’ preference for more reliable varieties.

In the glass

It makes wines of moderate colour, medium body, soft tannins and fresh acidity, with aromas of red fruit such as raspberry and strawberry and marked notes of pepper, spice and Mediterranean scrubland herbs. It is rarely bottled on its own; its usual role is as a complement, because it brings freshness, spice and a lively aromatic profile to Grenache-dominated blends, giving them balance and a lighter feel. When made as a single variety, it gives light, aromatic reds to drink fairly young.

Where it is grown

Its main territory is the southern Rhône: it is one of the varieties permitted in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, where some producers particularly value it in their blends, and it is also among the varieties of Côtes du Rhône and other appellations of the area. It is likewise found in Provence and the Languedoc, always on small areas. Outside France it has been planted by some Californian producers, especially those interested in Rhône varieties, who use it in blends and, more rarely, in single-varietal wines.

At the table

The blends it goes into suit lamb, Provençal stews, charcuterie, roast vegetables and herb-scented cooking. Serve at 15–17 °C.
