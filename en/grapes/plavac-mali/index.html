Plavac Mali

The main red grape of the Dalmatian coast, an offspring of Crljenak Kaštelanski (Zinfandel). It gives powerful wines, high in alcohol and with firm tannins, on sunny slopes above the Adriatic.

Origin

Plavac Mali is a native variety of the Dalmatian coast of Croatia. Its name comes from plavo, “blue”, for the colour of its berries, and mali, “small”, for their size. For a time it was thought to be the same as Californian Zinfandel, but genetic analyses carried out in the early twenty-first century showed that it is a natural cross between Crljenak Kaštelanski, which is indeed identical to Zinfandel and Primitivo, and Dobričić, an old variety from the island of Šolta. It is therefore an offspring of Zinfandel and not the same grape.

Viticulture

It is a late-ripening vine, well adapted to the dry, hot Mediterranean climate and to the poor, stony soils of the karst. It is traditionally bush-trained without irrigation, often on very steep slopes facing the sea, such as those of Dingač and Postup on the Pelješac peninsula, where direct sunlight and light reflected off the water lead to high sugar accumulation. It tends to ripen unevenly, so that raisined berries and less ripe ones can coexist in the same bunch. On more fertile soils and in inland areas it gives lighter wines.

In the glass

The best Plavac Mali wines have deep colour, high alcohol, full body and plentiful, sometimes slightly rustic tannins, with moderate acidity. Their aromas recall blackberry, black cherry, ripe plum, dried figs and raisins, with notes of carob, pepper, Mediterranean herbs and, with age, leather and tobacco. Many are aged in barrels or large casks. As well as dry reds, the variety is used for traditional rosés and for prošek, Dalmatia’s sweet wine from dried grapes.

Where it is grown

It is grown almost exclusively in Dalmatia: on the Pelješac peninsula, with the protected sites of Dingač and Postup; on the islands of Hvar, Brač, Korčula and Vis; and on the mainland coast, in areas such as the Makarska coast. It is the most widely planted red in coastal Croatia. Outside Croatia it is very rare.

At the table

It pairs with grilled meats, lamb, Dalmatian stews such as pašticada, game and aged sheep’s cheeses. Serve at 16-18 °C, preferably decanted.
