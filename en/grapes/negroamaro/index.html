Negroamaro

The great red grape of Salento, in southern Apulia, giving dark, warm reds with a slightly bitter finish; it is also the basis of the area’s traditional rosés.

Origin

Negroamaro is the signature red grape of Salento, the peninsula that forms the “heel” of the Italian boot in southern Apulia. It has been grown there for centuries, although its exact origin is unknown. Its name is usually explained in one of two ways: as the combination of two words meaning “black”, the Latin niger and the Greek mavros, or as a reference to the dark colour and slight bitterness of its wines. For a long time much of its production was sold in bulk to add colour and body to wines from other regions; over time, bottling in the area itself, under protected designations, became more important.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine, well suited to the hot, dry climate of Salento, where the sun is intense but winds from the Adriatic and Ionian seas temper the heat somewhat. It was traditionally trained as low bush vines (alberello), a system that limits yields and shields the bunches from the sun, although trellised vineyards are now common too. It ripens fairly late, at the end of September or in October, and does well in the clay and limestone soils, often reddish in colour, of the Salento plain.

In the glass

It gives deeply coloured reds with a medium to full body, firm but rounded tannins and a slightly bitter finish that is part of its character. Its aromas recall black cherry, plum, blackberry and dried fruit, with notes of Mediterranean herbs, tobacco, liquorice and spice. It is often blended with Malvasia Nera, which adds perfume and softness, and in some areas with other varieties such as Primitivo or Montepulciano. It is also the basis of Salento’s rosés, a deeply rooted local tradition: a short period of skin contact gives wines of intense cherry colour, savoury and fruity.

Where it is grown

It is almost exclusive to Apulia and, within the region, to Salento, across the provinces of Lecce, Brindisi and Taranto. It is the main grape of denominations such as Salice Salentino, Squinzano, Brindisi, Copertino, Leverano and Alezio, and is widely used in IGT Salento and Puglia wines, both as a single variety and in blends. Outside this region it is rarely found.

At the table

The reds go with grilled meat, lamb, stews, pasta with ragù and aged cheeses; the rosés suit fish, roast vegetables and Mediterranean cooking in general. Serve the reds at 16–18 °C and the rosés at 10–12 °C.
