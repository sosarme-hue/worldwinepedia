White grape

Rkatsiteli

Georgia’s most widely planted white grape, with high acidity and notable cold resistance. It gives both fresh European-style whites and amber wines with long qvevri maceration, tannic and complex.

Origin

Rkatsiteli is one of Georgia’s oldest varieties and comes from the east of the country, from the Kakheti region, where it is still the dominant grape. Its name is usually translated as “red stem” or “red vine”, after the reddish tone its shoots and stalks take on as they ripen. During the Soviet period it was planted massively in Georgia and other republics of the USSR for its productivity, acidity and hardiness, and for decades it became one of the white varieties with the largest planted area in the world.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive, late-ripening vine that withstands cold winters well and keeps high acidity even at good ripeness. It adapts to very diverse soils. When quality is the aim, yields must be limited, since at high production levels it gives neutral wines. Its good acidity also makes it suitable for sparkling wines and for distilling brandy.

In the glass

Made in the European style, without prolonged skin contact, it gives medium-bodied, fresh and somewhat austere dry whites with aromas of green apple, quince, pear, citrus and white flowers. Made by the traditional Kakhetian method, fermented and macerated for months with skins, seeds and sometimes stems in qvevri, it becomes an amber or orange wine with perceptible tannins and aromas of dried apricot, orange peel, walnuts, black tea, honey and spice. Both styles keep a marked acidity that gives them freshness and ageing potential.

Where it is grown

It is Georgia’s most widely planted variety, above all in Kakheti, where it features in appellations such as Tsinandali, Gurjaani and Napareuli, often alongside Mtsvane Kakhuri. Outside the country it is grown in Moldova, Ukraine, Russia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bulgaria and Romania, largely a legacy of Soviet expansion. In the United States it has been planted for decades in the Finger Lakes region of New York State, and there are also plantings in other countries, such as China. In recent years, international interest in Georgia’s amber wines has given the variety new visibility.
