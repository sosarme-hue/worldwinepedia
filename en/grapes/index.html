Grapes

International and native varieties, with their synonyms, regions and wines.

Black grape

Agiorgitiko

The emblematic red grape of the Peloponnese and the sole variety of the Nemea PDO. It gives deeply coloured wines with generous fruit and soft tannins, from rosés and young reds to oak-aged reds.

Aglianico

The great red grape of southern Italy, very late-ripening, with firm tannins and high acidity; the base of Taurasi and Aglianico del Vulture, wines built for long ageing.

Alfrocheiro

A Portuguese red grape with deep colour, lively red fruit and good acidity, one of the classic varieties of the Dão; in Spain it is grown under other names, such as Bruñal in Arribes and Baboso Negro in the Canary Islands.

Alicante Bouschet

A red-fleshed teinturier grape bred in France in the nineteenth century, known in Spain as Garnacha Tintorera and highly regarded today in the Alentejo.

Baga

The emblematic red grape of Bairrada, with firm tannins and high acidity, making austere long-lived reds as well as rosés and sparkling wines.

Barbera

Piedmont’s most widely planted red grape, with deep colour, high acidity and soft tannins; the grape of Barbera d’Asti, Barbera d’Alba and Nizza.

Blaufränkisch

The great red grape of Burgenland, known as Lemberger in Germany and Kékfrankos in Hungary: deeply coloured reds with fresh acidity, dark fruit and spicy notes.

Bobal

A native red grape from the Utiel-Requena plateau and La Manchuela, deeply coloured and fresh, which has moved from bulk wine to quality wines.

Bonarda

Argentina’s second red grape after Malbec, identical to the Douce Noir of Savoie and California’s Charbono, giving fruity, deeply coloured reds with soft tannins.

Brancellao

A pale, aromatically delicate Galician red grape, the same variety as Portugal’s Alvarelhão, revived in Ribeira Sacra and Ribeiro for light, floral and fresh reds.

Cabernet Franc

A parent of both Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, it gives aromatic, fresh reds and is a leading grape in the Loire and Saint-Émilion.

Cabernet Sauvignon

The quintessential international red grape, born in Bordeaux and grown in almost every wine region in the world, giving structured, long-lived wines.

Canaiolo

A traditional red grape of Tuscany, Sangiovese’s historic partner in Chianti, which brings fruit, softness and colour to blends.

Carignan

A Mediterranean red with good acidity, colour and tannin, called Mazuelo in Rioja and Samsó in Catalonia; Carignan in France and Carignano in Sardinia.

Carmenère

An old Bordeaux red grape that almost vanished in France and survived in Chile, mistaken for Merlot until 1994; it gives deeply coloured reds with soft tannins and notes of pepper and spice.

Castelão

A red grape of southern Portugal, closely tied to the sandy soils of Palmela on the Setúbal Peninsula and long known as Periquita.

Caíño Tinto

A Galician red grape with very high acidity and moderate alcohol, grown on both banks of the Minho, which yields fresh, spicy reds with red fruit; its name links it to other varieties called caíño, such as Caíño Blanco.

Cinsault

A heat-tolerant Mediterranean red that makes light, fragrant wines, essential to Provence rosé and a parent of South Africa’s Pinotage.

Corvina

The main grape of Valpolicella and Bardolino, thin-skinned and lively in acidity, and the backbone of Amarone and Recioto made from dried grapes.

Counoise

A minor red grape of the southern Rhône, permitted in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, which brings freshness, peppery notes, red fruit and moderate colour to blends.

Dolcetto

An early-ripening Piedmontese red giving deeply coloured wines with dark fruit and moderate acidity, mostly meant for drinking young; its benchmark is Dogliani.

Fetească Neagră

The great native red grape of Romania and Moldova, whose name means “black maiden”. It gives deeply coloured reds with aromas of plum, sour cherry and spice and soft tannins.

Frappato

A red grape from south-eastern Sicily, around Vittoria, giving light, fragrant wines with soft tannins; together with Nero d’Avola it makes Cerasuolo di Vittoria.

Gamay

The grape of Beaujolais, giving brightly coloured reds with fresh red fruit and soft tannins; also found in the Loire, Savoie and Switzerland.

Graciano

A low-yielding, high-acid red, traditionally used alongside Tempranillo in Rioja, to which it adds freshness, colour and aroma.

Grenache

A Mediterranean variety that withstands heat and drought, central to Aragon, Navarra and Priorat, the southern Rhône and, as Cannonau, Sardinia.

Hondarrabi Beltza

The red grape of txakoli: a short-cycle Basque variety with high acidity that makes light, fresh red and rosé txakolis and goes in small proportions into some whites.

Juan García

A native red grape of the Arribes del Duero, giving medium-coloured, fresh and aromatic wines, characteristic of the Arribes D.O.

Kadarka

A red grape of Balkan origin, for centuries the most important in Hungary, which makes light-coloured, spicy, aromatic reds and is known in Bulgaria as Gamza.

Kalecik Karası

An Anatolian red grape native to the Kalecik area near Ankara, once on the verge of disappearing. It gives light-coloured wines with red fruit aromas and soft tannins, often compared with Pinot Noir.

Lagrein

A native red grape of Alto Adige, grown mainly around Bolzano, which gives very dark, fruity reds with firm tannins, as well as rosés known as Kretzer.

Lambrusco

A family of red grapes from Emilia-Romagna that gives the famous sparkling and semi-sparkling reds, from dry to sweet, fresh, fruity and lively in acidity.

Listán Negro

The main red grape of the Canary Islands, grown on volcanic soils in often pre-phylloxera vineyards, giving fresh, spicy, red-fruited wines.

Malbec

A red grape from Cahors that found a second home in Mendoza and has become Argentina’s signature variety.

Marselan

A French cross of Cabernet Sauvignon and Grenache, deeply coloured and finely tannic, which has spread across southern France and found great success in China.

Maturana Tinta

A minor red grape revived in Rioja, with deep colour, high acidity and a marked herbal and spicy character, now used in blends and in a few varietal wines.

Mavrud

Bulgaria’s most emblematic native red grape, linked to the Thracian Valley and especially to Asenovgrad. Late-ripening, it gives dark, acidic and tannic wines well suited to ageing.

Mencía

An Atlantic-influenced red from Bierzo and inland Galicia, giving fresh, aromatic, red-fruited wines; in Portugal it is called Jaen.

Merlot

A Bordeaux red giving soft, fleshy, fruity wines, the leading grape of Pomerol and Saint-Émilion and one of the most widely planted varieties in the world.

Monastrell (Mourvèdre)

A late-ripening, sun-loving red grape, the mainstay of Jumilla, Yecla and Alicante and, as Mourvèdre, the heart of Bandol in France.

Mondeuse Noire

The emblematic red grape of Savoie, related to Syrah, giving deeply coloured wines with lively acidity and aromas of black fruit, violet and pepper.

Montepulciano

A central Italian red with deep colour, soft tannins and generous fruit, the grape of Montepulciano d’Abruzzo and Rosso Conero; it has no connection with the Tuscan town of Montepulciano.

Moristel

A native red grape of Somontano, with moderate colour, soft tannins and expressive red fruit, used for young reds, rosés and blends, and for a few varietal wines from old vines.

Nebbiolo

Piedmont’s great red grape, pale in colour but high in acidity and tannin, which gives long-lived Barolo and Barbaresco.

Negroamaro

The great red grape of Salento, in southern Apulia, giving dark, warm reds with a slightly bitter finish; it is also the basis of the area’s traditional rosés.

Nerello Mascalese

The great red grape of Etna, making pale, high-acid wines with fine tannins and volcanic character, often compared with Pinot Noir or Nebbiolo.

Nero di Troia

A red grape from northern Apulia, the star of Castel del Monte, giving deeply coloured wines with firm tannins and good ageing potential.

Nero d’Avola

Sicily’s most widely planted red grape, with deep colour, ripe fruit and good acidity, found in simple wines and in oak-aged reds, and part of Cerasuolo di Vittoria.

País

The Spanish Listán Prieto, one of the first European vines planted in the Americas; in Chile, with old dry-farmed vineyards in the south, it is enjoying a revival with light, rustic reds.

Petit Verdot

A very late-ripening Bordeaux red used in small proportions to add colour, tannin and spice to Médoc blends; in warm climates it also makes varietal wines.

Petite Sirah

The Californian name for Durif, a French cross of Syrah and Peloursin: almost black, very tannic reds with dark fruit and spice and great ageing potential.

Pinot Meunier

A red grape of the Pinot Noir family and one of the three main varieties of Champagne, valued for its fruit, its roundness and its resistance to spring frost.

Pinot Noir

Burgundy’s great red grape, delicate and demanding, giving fine, aromatic reds and one of the core grapes of Champagne.

Pinotage

South Africa’s own red grape, a cross of Pinot Noir and Cinsault, giving deeply coloured wines with dark fruit and smoky notes, and part of the so-called Cape Blend.

Plavac Mali

The main red grape of the Dalmatian coast, an offspring of Crljenak Kaštelanski (Zinfandel). It gives powerful wines, high in alcohol and with firm tannins, on sunny slopes above the Adriatic.

Poulsard

A very thin-skinned red grape from the Jura that gives pale, almost translucent reds, delicate and aromatic, with notes of red fruit, rose and spice.

Prieto Picudo

A native red grape from southern León, deeply coloured and fresh, the basis of the reds and rosés of the León D.O.

Primitivo

A red grape from Puglia, genetically identical to California’s Zinfandel and of Croatian origin, giving powerful wines with very ripe fruit and high alcohol.

Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso

The most widespread native red grape of Friuli, named after the red colour of its stalk, giving deeply coloured wines with lively acidity and a slightly bitter finish.

Rondinella

A red grape from Verona, an offspring of Corvina, which accompanies it in Valpolicella, Amarone and Bardolino, contributing colour, fruit and reliability.

Sagrantino

A red grape from Montefalco, in Umbria, famous for its very dark colour and for being one of the most tannic grapes in the world; it makes long-lived reds and a traditional sweet passito.

Sangiovese

Italy’s most widely planted red grape, the soul of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano.

Saperavi

Georgia’s great red grape, one of the few quality varieties with coloured flesh. It gives very dark, acidic and structured wines, both dry and naturally semi-sweet, and withstands cold well.

Sousón

A red-fleshed grape with very high acidity, the same variety as Portugal’s Vinhão: the backbone of the traditional reds of the Minho and a revived variety in Galicia, where it brings colour, freshness and structure.

Syrah (Shiraz)

A red grape from the northern Rhône (Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie) that, under the name Shiraz, became Australia’s signature variety.

Tannat

Red grape from south-west France, the basis of Madiran, famous for its abundant tannins and deep colour. In Uruguay, where it arrived in the nineteenth century, it has become the country’s signature variety.

Tempranillo

Spain’s great red grape, the backbone of Rioja and Ribera del Duero, and also important in Portugal under the names Tinta Roriz and Aragonez.

Teroldego

The signature red grape of Trentino, grown mainly on the Campo Rotaliano plain, giving deeply coloured, fruity wines with lively acidity.

Tinta Barroca

An early-ripening, sugar-rich red grape from the Douro, one of the recommended varieties for Port, which brings body, softness and ripe fruit to blends; it is also grown in South Africa.

Tinta Negra

The most widely grown red grape on Madeira and the basis of most of its fortified wines: a versatile variety which, thanks to the Madeira production process, can make every style from dry to sweet.

Tinto Cão

An old red grape of the Douro, with very low yields and late ripening, which gives fine, fresh, long-lived wines; it is one of the five recommended varieties for Port.

Touriga Franca

The most widely planted red grape in the Douro, an offspring of Touriga Nacional that brings floral perfume, fruit and elegance to Port and the region’s dry reds.

Touriga Nacional

Portugal’s most prestigious red grape, with small berries, great concentration and floral aromas, a pillar of Port and of the great reds of the Douro and Dão.

Trincadeira

A Portuguese red grape of strong character, spicy and herbal, very prone to rot, which shows at its best in the hot, dry climate of the Alentejo; in the Douro it is called Tinta Amarela.

Trousseau (Merenzao, Bastardo)

A red grape from the Jura that is the same variety as Galicia’s Merenzao and Portugal’s Bastardo; it gives medium-coloured, fresh, spicy reds with more structure than Poulsard.

Xinomavro

The great red grape of northern Greece, the basis of the wines of Naoussa and Amyndeon. Acidic and tannic, often moderate in colour, it gives long-lived reds frequently compared with those of Nebbiolo.

Zweigelt

Austria’s most widely planted red grape, a twentieth-century cross of Blaufränkisch and St. Laurent that gives juicy, cherry-scented reds with soft tannins and good freshness.

Öküzgözü

A red grape from Eastern Anatolia whose name means “ox eye”, after the large size of its berries. It gives fruity wines with lively acidity and moderate tannins, and is traditionally blended with the tannic Boğazkere.

White grape

Airén

A drought-resistant white grape from La Mancha, one of the most widely planted varieties in the world, traditionally used for table wine and distillation.

Albariño

The great Atlantic white grape of Rías Baixas and, as Alvarinho, of Monção e Melgaço in Vinho Verde: fresh, aromatic, saline wines.

Aligoté

Burgundy’s “other” white grape, with lively acidity and citrus aromas, its own village appellation in Bouzeron and the traditional base of kir.

Antão Vaz

The great white grape of the Alentejo, tolerant of heat and drought, which makes medium- to full-bodied whites with tropical and citrus fruit, often blended with Arinto for freshness.

Arinto

A Portuguese white with very high acidity and citrus aromas, the star of Bucelas and grown across almost the whole country, where it brings freshness to blends.

Arneis

A native white grape of Roero, in Piedmont, which came close to disappearing and today gives aromatic, medium-bodied whites with notes of pear, white flowers and almond.

Assyrtiko

Greece’s best-known white grape, native to Santorini, able to keep very high acidity in hot, dry climates. It gives taut, saline, long-lived whites as well as sweet Vinsanto.

Avesso

A Vinho Verde white grape tied to the Baião subregion, next to the Douro, which makes wines with more body and alcohol than typical Vinho Verde, with stone fruit, citrus and fresh acidity.

Boal

One of the four noble white grapes of Madeira, which gives its name to the medium-rich style of this fortified wine: a rich Madeira balanced by lively acidity, with notes of caramel, nuts and spice.

Carricante

The great white grape of Etna, grown at high altitude on the slopes of the volcano, giving wines of very lively acidity, mineral character and notable ageing potential.

Catarratto

One of the most widely planted white grapes in Sicily, traditional in Marsala and Alcamo and a parent of Grillo, giving fresh, savoury wines with a Mediterranean profile.

Chardonnay

The great white grape of Burgundy and Champagne, highly adaptable, faithfully reflecting climate and winemaking, from Chablis to California and Australia.

Chasselas

Switzerland’s great white grape, called Fendant in the Valais and Gutedel in Germany. It gives light whites with moderate acidity and subtle aromas, highly sensitive to the place where they are grown.

Chenin Blanc

A white grape of vibrant acidity from the Loire, able to make long-lived dry, sparkling and sweet wines; it is South Africa’s most planted white variety.

Clairette

An old white grape from southern France, low in acidity with floral and aniseed aromas, found in the whites of the Rhône and Provence, in Clairette du Languedoc and in the sparkling wines of Die.

Colombard

A white grape from south-west France, traditionally distilled for Cognac and Armagnac, which today makes fresh, fruity whites in Gascony, South Africa and California.

Cortese

A white grape from south-eastern Piedmont and the basis of Gavi, giving dry, light whites with lively acidity and aromas of green apple, citrus and almond.

Encruzado

The great white grape of the Dão, with notable structure and balance, well suited to barrel fermentation and lees ageing and able to age for many years.

Falanghina

An ancient white grape from Campania giving fresh, aromatic, savoury wines; under this name two distinct varieties are in fact grown, Falanghina Flegrea and Falanghina Beneventana.

Fernão Pires

One of Portugal’s most widely planted white grapes, aromatic, versatile and early-ripening, called Maria Gomes in Bairrada, which makes everything from young floral whites to sparkling and sweet wines.

Fetească Albă

A traditional white variety of Romania and Moldova, the “white maiden”, known in Hungary as Leányka. It gives delicate, floral whites, dry or with some residual sugar.

Fiano

A white grape from Campania with an oily texture and aromas of flowers, honey and hazelnut, the base of Fiano di Avellino and capable of ageing well.

Folle Blanche

A very high-acid white grape, the historic variety of Cognac and Armagnac before phylloxera, which today makes Gros Plant du Pays Nantais and fine brandies in Armagnac.

Friulano

The signature white grape of Friuli, formerly called Tocai Friulano and identical to Sauvignonasse; it gives medium-bodied wines with a soft texture and a characteristic almond finish.

Furmint

Hungary’s great white grape and the main variety of Tokaj, with very high acidity and a strong susceptibility to noble rot. It gives both extremely long-lived sweet wines and taut, mineral dry whites.

Garganega

A late-ripening white from the Veneto, the base of Soave and Gambellara, with notes of almond, flowers and citrus; in Sicily it is known as Grecanico.

Gewürztraminer

A pink-skinned white with an unmistakable lychee and rose aroma, emblematic of Alsace and also grown in Germany, Austria, Alto Adige and Somontano.

Glera

The grape of Prosecco, itself called Prosecco until 2009: an aromatic, productive white that gives light, fruity sparkling wines, nearly always made by the tank method.

Godello

A Galician white grape from Valdeorras and Bierzo, rescued from near extinction in the 1970s, giving wines with body, freshness and ageing potential.

Graševina (Welschriesling)

A white variety widely grown in central and eastern Europe and the most planted in Croatia. Despite its names it is not related to Riesling; it gives fresh, fruity whites and great botrytised sweet wines.

Greco

A white grape from Campania, the basis of the Greco di Tufo DOCG, giving structured whites with firm acidity and a mineral character, with aromas of peach, pear and almond.

Grenache Blanc

The pale-skinned form of Grenache, giving broad, low-acid whites; it is the signature grape of Terra Alta and is widely grown in the Rhône and Roussillon.

Grillo

A white grape from western Sicily, a cross of Catarratto and Muscat of Alexandria, once a key grape of Marsala and now used above all for fresh, savoury dry whites.

Gros Manseng

A white grape from the French Pyrenees, more productive than Petit Manseng, which makes dry whites with lively acidity and aromas of citrus and tropical fruit in Jurançon and Gascony.

Grüner Veltliner

Austria’s signature white grape and its most widely planted variety: whites with lively acidity, green fruit and a characteristic touch of white pepper.

Hondarrabi Zuri

The white grape of txakoli, grown on the Basque coast, giving light, very fresh wines with marked acidity.

Hárslevelű

A Hungarian white grape, the second variety of Tokaj after Furmint, from which it descends. It brings floral perfume, spice and a rounder texture to both sweet and dry wines.

Listán Blanco

The Canary Islands name for Palomino, the grape of sherry: on the islands it is the most widely planted white grape and, on volcanic soils and ungrafted vines, makes fresh, saline dry whites with a marked Atlantic character.

Loureira (Loureiro)

A highly aromatic Atlantic white grape with floral and bay-leaf notes, grown in Rías Baixas and Ribeiro and, as Loureiro, in Portugal’s Vinho Verde.

Macabeo (Viura)

A versatile white grape, called Viura in Rioja, a base of Cava together with Xarel·lo and Parellada, and also found in French Roussillon as Macabeu.

Malagousia

An aromatic Greek white grape, rescued from near extinction in the twentieth century, which gives perfumed wines of jasmine, peach and citrus with medium body and moderate acidity.

Malvasia

A name shared by a group of varieties, many unrelated, famous since the Middle Ages for sweet, aromatic wines; in Spain the Canary Islands versions stand out.

Marsanne

A white grape from the northern Rhône and the main variety in white Hermitage, giving broad wines of moderate acidity with notes of almond, honeysuckle and pear that turn honeyed with age.

Melon de Bourgogne

The grape of Muscadet, near the mouth of the Loire, making dry, light, saline whites that are often aged on their lees.

Moschofilero

An aromatic, pink-skinned grape native to the Mantinia plateau in the Peloponnese. It gives light, fresh and highly perfumed whites with floral and citrus notes, as well as rosés and sparkling wines.

Muscadelle

An aromatic white grape from south-west France, the third variety in the dry and sweet whites of Bordeaux, and the basis of the celebrated fortified wines of Rutherglen in Australia.

Muscat

A family of highly aromatic grapes, recognisable by their intense aroma of fresh grapes and flowers, giving dry, sparkling and above all sweet wines around the world.

Müller-Thurgau

A nineteenth-century German cross, early-ripening and productive, that became Germany’s most planted grape and gives light, floral whites with gentle acidity.

Palomino

The grape of sherry: neutral as a young wine, it is transformed by ageing under flor or oxidative ageing into finos, manzanillas, amontillados and olorosos.

Parellada

A Catalan white grape from higher, cooler areas, the most delicate and floral of the three traditional Cava varieties.

Pecorino

A white grape from central Italy, between the Marche and Abruzzo, rescued from extinction in the late 20th century, which gives fresh, structured, aromatic wines.

Pedro Ximénez

An Andalusian white grape, queen of Montilla-Moriles, which gives dry finos and olorosos as well as the famous sweet wine made from sun-dried grapes.

Petit Manseng

A white grape from Béarn with tiny, thick-skinned berries, able to build up a lot of sugar without losing acidity, and the basis of the celebrated sweet Jurançon made from grapes shrivelled on the vine.

Petite Arvine

A late-ripening white variety native to the Valais and the emblem of its winegrowing. It gives dry whites with lively acidity and a salty finish, and sweet wines from grapes shrivelled on the vine.

Pinot Blanc

The white mutation of the Pinot family, with discreet aromas and a soft texture, widely grown in Alsace, Germany, Austria and northern Italy for fresh whites and sparkling wines.

Pinot Gris

A greyish-skinned mutation of Pinot that, depending on where it is grown, gives rich, spicy whites (Alsace) or light, crisp ones (Italian Pinot Grigio).

Pošip

A native white grape of the Dalmatian island of Korčula and one of Croatia’s most prestigious, giving full-bodied wines of ripe fruit, almond and Mediterranean herbs.

Rebula (Ribolla Gialla)

A historic white variety from the hills between the Italian Collio and Slovenian Brda. Thick-skinned and high in acidity, it gives everything from light whites and sparkling wines to amber skin-macerated wines.

Riesling

Germany’s great white grape, with vibrant acidity and strong expression of terroir, capable of dry and sweet wines of great longevity.

Rkatsiteli

Georgia’s most widely planted white grape, with high acidity and notable cold resistance. It gives both fresh European-style whites and amber wines with long qvevri maceration, tannic and complex.

Roditis

A widely planted pink-skinned Greek grape that makes light, fresh whites with citrus and melon notes, the basis of white Patras and of much retsina.

Roussanne

A white grape from the northern Rhône and the usual partner of Marsanne, giving aromatic, full-bodied, age-worthy wines with notes of pear, flowers and herbal tea.

Sauvignon Blanc

An aromatic, high-acid white grape, emblem of Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé, which won worldwide fame with the wines of Marlborough, New Zealand.

Savagnin

An ancient white grape of the Traminer family and the emblem of the Jura, where it makes Vin Jaune, aged for years under a veil of yeast, and dry whites with firm acidity.

Semillon

A thin-skinned Bordeaux white, key to the great sweet wines of Sauternes and the dry whites of Graves, and the star of Australia’s Hunter Valley.

Sercial

A white grape of extreme acidity that gives the driest style of Madeira, of great longevity; on the Portuguese mainland it is known as Esgana Cão.

Silvaner

A Central European white grape with a discreet, earthy profile, emblematic of Franken and found in Rheinhessen, Alsace and Alto Adige, that clearly expresses limestone soils.

Torrontés

Argentina’s signature white grape, born in the country from crosses involving Muscat of Alexandria: highly aromatic, floral, dry wines at their best in the high valleys of Salta.

Trebbiano

The name of a family of Italian white grapes; the most widespread, Trebbiano Toscano, is France’s Ugni Blanc, the base of Cognac and Armagnac.

Treixadura

A Galician white grape, the leading variety of Ribeiro wines and known in Portugal’s Vinho Verde as Trajadura; it gives aromatic, rounded wines.

Verdejo

A native Castilian white grape, the heart of the Rueda D.O., giving aromatic, fresh wines with a characteristic slightly bitter finish.

Verdelho

A white grape from Madeira and the Azores that gives the medium-dry style of Madeira as well as fresh, saline still whites; it is also grown in Australia.

Verdicchio

The great white grape of the Marche, the basis of Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi and Verdicchio di Matelica, giving fresh, savoury whites with an almondy finish that are capable of ageing.

Vermentino

A Mediterranean white with citrus and herbal aromas and a saline finish, emblematic of Sardinia, Liguria and Corsica, and known as Rolle in Provence and the Languedoc.

Vernaccia

A historic white grape from San Gimignano, in Tuscany, the basis of Italy’s first DOC; it gives dry, savoury wines with an almondy finish. Other grapes called Vernaccia are different varieties.

Vidal

A white hybrid grape of French origin, very cold-hardy and thick-skinned, which has become the most widely used variety for Canadian icewine, especially in Ontario.

Viognier

An aromatic white from the northern Rhône, the grape of Condrieu, with apricot and floral aromas and a rich texture; it is also co-fermented with Syrah in Côte-Rôtie.

Xarel·lo

A Catalan white grape from Penedès, the most structured of the three traditional Cava varieties; it also makes still whites with good ageing potential.
