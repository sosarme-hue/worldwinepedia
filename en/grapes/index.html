Grapes

International and native varieties, with their synonyms, regions and wines.

Black grape

Bobal

A native red grape from the Utiel-Requena plateau and La Manchuela, deeply coloured and fresh, which has moved from bulk wine to quality wines.

Cabernet Franc

A parent of both Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, it gives aromatic, fresh reds and is a leading grape in the Loire and Saint-Émilion.

Cabernet Sauvignon

The quintessential international red grape, born in Bordeaux and grown in almost every wine region in the world, giving structured, long-lived wines.

Carignan

A Mediterranean red with good acidity, colour and tannin, called Mazuelo in Rioja and Samsó in Catalonia; Carignan in France and Carignano in Sardinia.

Graciano

A low-yielding, high-acid red, traditionally used alongside Tempranillo in Rioja, to which it adds freshness, colour and aroma.

Grenache

A Mediterranean variety that withstands heat and drought, central to Aragon, Navarra and Priorat, the southern Rhône and, as Cannonau, Sardinia.

Juan García

A native red grape of the Arribes del Duero, giving medium-coloured, fresh and aromatic wines, characteristic of the Arribes D.O.

Listán Negro

The main red grape of the Canary Islands, grown on volcanic soils in often pre-phylloxera vineyards, giving fresh, spicy, red-fruited wines.

Malbec

A red grape from Cahors that found a second home in Mendoza and has become Argentina’s signature variety.

Mencía

An Atlantic-influenced red from Bierzo and inland Galicia, giving fresh, aromatic, red-fruited wines; in Portugal it is called Jaen.

Merlot

A Bordeaux red giving soft, fleshy, fruity wines, the leading grape of Pomerol and Saint-Émilion and one of the most widely planted varieties in the world.

Monastrell (Mourvèdre)

A late-ripening, sun-loving red grape, the mainstay of Jumilla, Yecla and Alicante and, as Mourvèdre, the heart of Bandol in France.

Nebbiolo

Piedmont’s great red grape, pale in colour but high in acidity and tannin, which gives long-lived Barolo and Barbaresco.

Pinot Noir

Burgundy’s great red grape, delicate and demanding, giving fine, aromatic reds and one of the core grapes of Champagne.

Prieto Picudo

A native red grape from southern León, deeply coloured and fresh, the basis of the reds and rosés of the León D.O.

Sangiovese

Italy’s most widely planted red grape, the soul of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano.

Syrah (Shiraz)

A red grape from the northern Rhône (Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie) that, under the name Shiraz, became Australia’s signature variety.

Tempranillo

Spain’s great red grape, the backbone of Rioja and Ribera del Duero, and also important in Portugal under the names Tinta Roriz and Aragonez.

White grape

Airén

A drought-resistant white grape from La Mancha, one of the most widely planted varieties in the world, traditionally used for table wine and distillation.

Albariño

The great Atlantic white grape of Rías Baixas and, as Alvarinho, of Monção e Melgaço in Vinho Verde: fresh, aromatic, saline wines.

Chardonnay

The great white grape of Burgundy and Champagne, highly adaptable, faithfully reflecting climate and winemaking, from Chablis to California and Australia.

Godello

A Galician white grape from Valdeorras and Bierzo, rescued from near extinction in the 1970s, giving wines with body, freshness and ageing potential.

Grenache Blanc

The pale-skinned form of Grenache, giving broad, low-acid whites; it is the signature grape of Terra Alta and is widely grown in the Rhône and Roussillon.

Hondarrabi Zuri

The white grape of txakoli, grown on the Basque coast, giving light, very fresh wines with marked acidity.

Loureira (Loureiro)

A highly aromatic Atlantic white grape with floral and bay-leaf notes, grown in Rías Baixas and Ribeiro and, as Loureiro, in Portugal’s Vinho Verde.

Macabeo (Viura)

A versatile white grape, called Viura in Rioja, a base of Cava together with Xarel·lo and Parellada, and also found in French Roussillon as Macabeu.

Malvasia

A name shared by a group of varieties, many unrelated, famous since the Middle Ages for sweet, aromatic wines; in Spain the Canary Islands versions stand out.

Muscat

A family of highly aromatic grapes, recognisable by their intense aroma of fresh grapes and flowers, giving dry, sparkling and above all sweet wines around the world.

Palomino

The grape of sherry: neutral as a young wine, it is transformed by ageing under flor or oxidative ageing into finos, manzanillas, amontillados and olorosos.

Parellada

A Catalan white grape from higher, cooler areas, the most delicate and floral of the three traditional Cava varieties.

Pedro Ximénez

An Andalusian white grape, queen of Montilla-Moriles, which gives dry finos and olorosos as well as the famous sweet wine made from sun-dried grapes.

Riesling

Germany’s great white grape, with vibrant acidity and strong expression of terroir, capable of dry and sweet wines of great longevity.

Sauvignon Blanc

An aromatic, high-acid white grape, emblem of Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé, which won worldwide fame with the wines of Marlborough, New Zealand.

Treixadura

A Galician white grape, the leading variety of Ribeiro wines and known in Portugal’s Vinho Verde as Trajadura; it gives aromatic, rounded wines.

Verdejo

A native Castilian white grape, the heart of the Rueda D.O., giving aromatic, fresh wines with a characteristic slightly bitter finish.

Xarel·lo

A Catalan white grape from Penedès, the most structured of the three traditional Cava varieties; it also makes still whites with good ageing potential.
