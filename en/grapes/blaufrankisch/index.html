Blaufränkisch

The great red grape of Burgenland, known as Lemberger in Germany and Kékfrankos in Hungary: deeply coloured reds with fresh acidity, dark fruit and spicy notes.

Origin

Blaufränkisch is an old Central European variety, grown for centuries in the lands bordering the middle Danube: Austria, Hungary, Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Slovenia and Croatia. Genetic studies point to Gouais Blanc (Weißer Heunisch) as one of its parents. Its name, “blue Frankish”, recalls the medieval distinction between “Frankish” grapes, regarded as superior, and “Hunnish” ones, considered more ordinary. It is in turn one of the parents of Zweigelt, born of its cross with St. Laurent.

Viticulture

It buds early, which exposes it to spring frost, and ripens late, so it needs warm, well-exposed sites to ripen fully. It is vigorous and, if left unchecked, productive. It clearly expresses the soil it grows in, giving finer, spicier wines on slate and schist and broader ones on limestone or clay.

In the glass

It gives deeply coloured reds with high acidity and firm but often fine tannins. The aromas recall dark cherry, blackberry and blueberry, with notes of pepper, spice, herbs and a mineral character. For a long time it was made in simple styles or softened with new oak; today many producers aim for fresher, more precise wines, aged in large casks or tanks, which can age gracefully for years.

Where it is grown

In Austria it is the signature red of Burgenland: Mittelburgenland, nicknamed “Blaufränkischland”, Eisenberg in the south and the slopes of the Leithaberg near Lake Neusiedl all have DAC appellations largely devoted to it. It is also found in Carnuntum, in Lower Austria. In Germany, under the name Lemberger, it is grown mainly in Württemberg. In Hungary, as Kékfrankos, it is one of the most planted red grapes, and in the United States it is found as Lemberger in Washington State and in the Finger Lakes region of New York.

At the table

Its freshness and structure go well with roast meat, game, spiced stews such as goulash, charcuterie and aged cheeses. It is served at 15–17 °C.
