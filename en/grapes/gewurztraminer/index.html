Gewürztraminer

A pink-skinned white with an unmistakable lychee and rose aroma, emblematic of Alsace and also grown in Germany, Austria, Alto Adige and Somontano.

Origin

Gewürztraminer is the aromatic, pink-skinned form of Savagnin or Traminer, one of the oldest varieties of central Europe. Its name is associated with the village of Tramin (Termeno) in Alto Adige, although there is no proof that the variety was born there. The German prefix Gewürz means “spice” and refers to its perfume. The aromatic form became widespread in Alsace and Germany from the nineteenth century onwards.

Viticulture

It buds early, is susceptible to spring frost and viral diseases, and gives low, irregular yields. It prefers cool climates with long, dry autumns, where it can ripen slowly and reach high sugar levels without losing its aroma entirely. Its acidity is low, so in warm climates the wines can turn heavy. The grapes, pink to reddish in colour, give very concentrated musts.

In the glass

It is one of the easiest varieties to recognise blind. Its wines are deep golden, full-bodied, oily in texture and generous in alcohol. The aroma recalls lychee, rose, rosewater, ginger, orange peel and sweet spice. It can be made dry, off-dry or sweet; Alsace produces notable Vendanges Tardives and Sélection de Grains Nobles, sometimes from grapes affected by noble rot.

Where it is grown

Alsace is its great benchmark, and it is one of the four varieties permitted in Alsace Grand Cru wines. In Germany it is grown in the Pfalz and Baden, and in Austria in Styria and Burgenland. In Italy, Alto Adige makes dry, fresh Gewürztraminer. In Spain it stands out in Somontano. Beyond Europe it is found in New Zealand, Oregon, California, Chile and Australia, always in cool areas.

At the table

It pairs well with spicy Asian food, curries, foie gras and washed-rind cheeses such as Munster. Sweet versions go with fruit desserts. Serve at 8–11 °C. Because of its low acidity and generous alcohol, it is best enjoyed well chilled and with food, rather than on its own, so that its rich texture is balanced.
