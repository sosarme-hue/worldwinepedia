Juan García

A native red grape of the Arribes del Duero, giving medium-coloured, fresh and aromatic wines, characteristic of the Arribes D.O.

Origin

Juan García is a traditional variety of the Arribes del Duero, the deep canyon carved by the Duero river along the Spanish–Portuguese border in the provinces of Salamanca and Zamora. Genetic studies have identified it with Mouratón, found in some parts of Galicia.

Viticulture

It is grown on terraces and steep slopes over granite and slate soils, in a Mediterranean-influenced climate within the enclosed valley, milder than that of the surrounding plateau. Many vineyards are old and bush-trained.

In the glass

It gives cherry-red wines of medium intensity, with fresh acidity, soft tannins and moderate alcohol. The aromas recall red fruit (cherry, raspberry), with floral notes, wild herbs and a mineral character associated with the granite and slate soils. Young wines are traditional, although reds with short oak ageing are also made. It is also used for rosé.

Where it is grown

Its cultivation is concentrated in the Arribes D.O., where it is the most characteristic red variety, alongside others such as Rufete and Bruñal. Outside this area it is scarce, limited to a few plantings in Galicia under the name Mouratón. No significant plantings are known outside Spain.

The Arribes landscape, protected as a natural park on the Spanish side and continued in Portugal by the Douro Internacional Natural Park, combines terraces held up by dry-stone walls with olive trees, almond trees and vines. Twentieth-century rural depopulation left many plots abandoned, since the slopes rule out mechanisation, but from the end of that century small wineries and cooperatives have brought old Juan García vineyards back into production. In Fermoselle, one of the main towns of the area, many traditional cellars carved into the rock beneath the houses survive. The denomination covers municipalities in the provinces of Zamora and Salamanca.

At the table

Light and fresh, Juan García reds are versatile: they go with Iberian charcuterie, grilled meat, pulses and local cheeses. Young wines can be served slightly cool, around 14–15 °C, and the rosés at 8–10 °C.
