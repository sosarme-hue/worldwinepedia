Hondarrabi Zuri

The white grape of txakoli, grown on the Basque coast, giving light, very fresh wines with marked acidity.

Origin

Hondarrabi Zuri is the traditional white variety of the Basque Country; its Basque name means “white Hondarribia”, after the border town in Gipuzkoa. Its genetic identity is complex: some studies have found that part of the vines grown under this name are in fact south-western French varieties such as Courbu Blanc or Crouchen. Its red counterpart, Hondarrabi Beltza, is a different variety.

Viticulture

It grows in a very humid, rainy Atlantic climate with mild temperatures and little sunshine, on slopes close to the sea. It was traditionally trained on high pergolas and is now also grown on trellises. The humidity makes vineyard health, against downy and powdery mildew, a constant concern. The grapes ripen with difficulty, giving musts of moderate alcohol and high acidity.

In the glass

It gives pale whites with green hints, light-bodied, with lively acidity, often a slight spritz of carbon dioxide from fermentation, and moderate alcohol. The aromas recall green apple, lemon, grapefruit, fresh herbs and white flowers, with a saline background. It is traditionally poured from a height to liven up its sparkle, and drunk young. In recent decades txakolis aged on lees, sparkling versions and more structured wines have also been made.

Where it is grown

It is the main variety of the three txakoli denominations: Getariako Txakolina in Gipuzkoa, Bizkaiko Txakolina in Bizkaia and Arabako Txakolina in the Ayala valley, Álava. It is also found in some parts of Cantabria and Burgos. Outside the Basque and Cantabrian area it is practically not grown.

Of the three denominations, Getariako Txakolina was the first to be recognised. Besides Hondarrabi Zuri, some of them allow other white varieties, such as Hondarrabi Zuri Zerratia, identified as Petit Courbu, and blending with Hondarrabi Beltza makes it possible to produce rosé txakolis.

At the table

Txakoli is the classic partner for pintxos, anchovies, bonito, grilled fish and the shellfish of the Cantabrian coast. It is served well chilled, at 6–9 °C, and is best drunk young.
