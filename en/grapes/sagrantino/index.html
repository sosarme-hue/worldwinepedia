Sagrantino

A red grape from Montefalco, in Umbria, famous for its very dark colour and for being one of the most tannic grapes in the world; it makes long-lived reds and a traditional sweet passito.

Origin

Sagrantino is tied almost entirely to Montefalco and a few neighbouring communes in the province of Perugia, in central Umbria. Its origin is uncertain. One tradition credits Franciscan friars with bringing it, and another derives the name from the “sacraments”, since the wine was supposedly used in religious celebrations, but neither idea has been proven. What is known is that for centuries it was made mainly as a sweet wine from dried grapes, and that by the mid-20th century its plantings had shrunk to a very small area. Its revival came through the efforts of local producers and the creation of the denomination, which came to include a dry red.

Viticulture

It is a vine with moderate yields and small bunches, with very thick-skinned berries rich in colouring matter and polyphenols. It ripens late, usually in October, and needs good exposure for its tannins to ripen fully. The hills of Montefalco, with soils of clay, silt and calcareous sands and a continental climate with warm summers, offer it suitable conditions. Managing that enormous tannic load, in the vineyard and in the cellar, is the key to balanced wines.

In the glass

The dry red is almost opaque in colour, powerful and highly structured, with abundant tannin and lively acidity. Its aromas recall blackberry, plum, sour cherry and ripe black fruit, with notes of spices such as cinnamon and clove, earth, leather and dried herbs. It usually needs long ageing in wood and bottle before showing at its best, and it can age for many years. The passito, made from grapes dried after harvest, is a sweet, concentrated red in which the tannin balances the sugar.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark area is the Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG, which covers the dry red and the passito, both made exclusively from this variety. It also features, in smaller proportions and alongside Sangiovese, in the reds of the Montefalco Rosso DOC. Outside Umbria its presence is very limited.

At the table

The dry red calls for hearty dishes: game, lamb, roasts, stews and aged cheeses, as well as Umbrian cooking with truffles. The passito goes with nut-based desserts and mature cheeses. Serve the red at 16–18 °C in a large glass.
