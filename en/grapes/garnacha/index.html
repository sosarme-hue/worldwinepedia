Grenache

A Mediterranean variety that withstands heat and drought, central to Aragon, Navarra and Priorat, the southern Rhône and, as Cannonau, Sardinia.

Origin

Most ampelographers place Grenache’s origin in north-eastern Spain, probably Aragon, from where it spread through the territories of the Crown of Aragon to southern France and Sardinia. On the Italian island it is known as Cannonau, and a local origin is also argued there; the question remains open.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous vine that buds early and ripens late, highly resistant to drought and wind, which makes it ideal for hot, dry climates. It is traditionally trained as a free-standing bush vine. It accumulates sugar easily, so its wines reach high alcohol levels, and it is prone to oxidation. It is sensitive to coulure and downy mildew. Old, low-yielding vines give the most concentrated wines.

In the glass

Its wines are paler than their alcohol might suggest, generous and warm, with soft tannins and moderate acidity. Ripe red fruit (strawberry, raspberry, cherry) dominates, with sweet spice, white pepper and Mediterranean herbs. On slate or granite soils and at altitude it gains freshness and tension. It is used for red wines, highly regarded rosés and fortified sweet wines.

Where it is grown

In Spain it stands out in Campo de Borja, Calatayud, Cariñena, Navarra, Priorat, Montsant, Terra Alta, Empordà, Rioja and the Gredos mountains. In France it is the leading grape of Châteauneuf-du-Pape and much of the Côtes du Rhône, as well as Provençal rosés and the vins doux naturels of Roussillon (Banyuls, Rivesaltes). In Italy it is Sardinia’s Cannonau. Outside Europe it is grown in Australia (McLaren Vale, Barossa), the United States (California) and South Africa, often blended with Syrah and Mourvèdre.

At the table

Grenache’s generous fruit and soft tannins make it versatile at the table: rosés suit rice dishes and vegetables, young reds go with stews and grilled meat, and the most concentrated wines with braises and game. Fortified sweet wines are kept for chocolate desserts.
