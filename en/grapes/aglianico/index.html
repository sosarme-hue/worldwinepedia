Aglianico

The great red grape of southern Italy, very late-ripening, with firm tannins and high acidity; the base of Taurasi and Aglianico del Vulture, wines built for long ageing.

Origin

Aglianico is an ancient variety of southern Italy, grown for centuries in Campania and Basilicata. It has traditionally been said to have arrived with Greek colonists and to take its name from Hellenico, but this etymology is disputed and genetic analysis has not confirmed a Greek origin. What is certain is its long presence in the inland areas of Irpinia, Benevento and Monte Vulture.

Viticulture

It buds early and ripens very late: in the high country of Irpinia the harvest can stretch to the end of October or even into November. It needs plenty of light and well-drained soils and does particularly well on volcanic soils. Its long growing season at altitudes of several hundred metres lets it keep high acidity while its thick skins build colour and tannin. It is susceptible to powdery mildew and to rot in wet autumns.

In the glass

It makes deep ruby to garnet wines, full-bodied, with powerful tannins and pronounced acidity, and aromas of black cherry, plum, blackberry, violet, liquorice, spice and earthy or smoky notes. In youth it can be austere and tannic; with oak ageing and several years in bottle it develops towards leather, tobacco, forest floor and cocoa. Its structure and longevity have often earned it comparisons with Nebbiolo.

Where it is grown

In Campania its main appellations are Taurasi DOCG in Irpinia and Aglianico del Taburno DOCG in the province of Benevento; it also goes into other wines of the region, such as Falerno del Massico. In Basilicata, on the slopes of the extinct volcano Vulture, Aglianico del Vulture DOC and Aglianico del Vulture Superiore DOCG are made. It is also grown in Puglia and Molise, and outside Italy there are experimental plantings in Australia and California.

At the table

It calls for hearty dishes: roast lamb and kid, Neapolitan ragù, game, mushrooms and aged cheeses such as caciocavallo. Serve at 16–18 °C, ideally after some aeration.
