Hondarrabi Beltza

The red grape of txakoli: a short-cycle Basque variety with high acidity that makes light, fresh red and rosé txakolis and goes in small proportions into some whites.

Origin

Hondarrabi Beltza is the traditional red variety of the Basque Country and the companion of the white Hondarrabi Zuri in txakoli vineyards. Beltza means “black” in Basque, and the name shares its root with that of the Gipuzkoan town of Hondarribia. Despite the similar name, it is not a red version of Hondarrabi Zuri but a distinct variety. It survived in small family vineyards on the Cantabrian coast and in the Basque interior, and with the creation of the txakoli appellations it became the only leading native red grape of the area.

Viticulture

It is a vine with a short growing cycle, suited to the cool and very rainy Atlantic climate of the Cantabrian coast, where late-ripening varieties struggle to reach maturity. It is traditionally trained on pergolas or high trellises to keep the bunches away from ground moisture and improve ventilation, since fungal disease pressure is high. Its bunches are small to medium and its relatively thick-skinned berries ripen with high acidity and moderate sugar.

In the glass

It makes wines of medium to light ruby colour, light body, fine tannins and very lively acidity. The aroma recalls fresh red fruit (raspberry, redcurrant, cherry), with herbal and vegetal notes and sometimes a light touch of spice. It is used for red txakolis, generally young and for early drinking, and for rosé txakolis, pale in colour and very fresh. In small proportions it also goes into some white txakolis, alongside Hondarrabi Zuri, to add nuance and structure.

Where it is grown

Its area is practically limited to the three txakoli appellations: Getariako Txakolina in Gipuzkoa, Bizkaiko Txakolina in Bizkaia and Arabako Txakolina in northern Álava. In all of them it covers a much smaller area than Hondarrabi Zuri, since the great majority of txakoli is white.

At the table

Red and rosé txakolis go with chargrilled fish, baby squid, salt cod, pintxos, charcuterie and fresh or young sheep’s cheeses. Serve them cool: rosés at 8–10 °C and reds at 12–14 °C.
