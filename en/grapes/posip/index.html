White grape

Pošip

A native white grape of the Dalmatian island of Korčula and one of Croatia’s most prestigious, giving full-bodied wines of ripe fruit, almond and Mediterranean herbs.

Origin

Pošip is a white variety native to Korčula, an island off the Dalmatian coast in southern Croatia. According to local tradition, it was found growing wild in the interior of the island, in the Smokvica area, and from there it was propagated in neighbouring vineyards. Genetic analysis has confirmed that it originated on Korčula itself, as the offspring of two old local varieties from the island. Today it is one of the most highly regarded white grapes in the country and a symbol of the wines of Dalmatia.

Viticulture

It is an early-ripening vine with regular yields, well suited to the Mediterranean climate of hot, dry summers. On Korčula it is grown mainly in the inland fields of Čara and Smokvica, on stony limestone soils of red earth. Because it accumulates sugar easily and can lose acidity in the warmest years, the harvest date is decisive in obtaining balanced, fresh wines.

In the glass

It makes pale golden whites, medium- to full-bodied and often high in alcohol, with acidity that can be fresh or moderate depending on the vineyard and the year. Their aromas recall apricot, peach, figs, citrus and tropical fruit, with notes of almond and Mediterranean herbs. It is mostly made in steel to preserve the fruit, although some producers age it on the lees or in barrel, which adds texture and allows it to develop for a few years.

Where it is grown

Its birthplace and main growing area is Korčula, with the wines of Čara and Smokvica as the reference. It has spread to other parts of central and southern Dalmatia, such as the Pelješac peninsula and other islands and coastal areas, where it adapts well to the warm, bright climate of the Adriatic. Outside Croatia it is hardly grown, so it remains a characteristically Dalmatian variety, closely tied to the wine identity of the Croatian coast.

At the table

It goes with chargrilled fish, shellfish, black cuttlefish risotto, octopus, olive-oil-based cooking and ewe’s-milk cheeses from the Dalmatian coast. Serve at 9–12 °C.
