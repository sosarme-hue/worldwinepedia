Catarratto

One of the most widely planted white grapes in Sicily, traditional in Marsala and Alcamo and a parent of Grillo, giving fresh, savoury wines with a Mediterranean profile.

Origin

Catarratto is a native white grape of Sicily, grown for centuries mainly in the west of the island, in the provinces of Trapani, Palermo and Agrigento. It is one of the most widely planted grapes in Sicily. Two types were traditionally distinguished, Catarratto Bianco Comune and Catarratto Bianco Lucido, the latter with berries carrying less bloom and looking shinier; DNA analysis has shown that they belong to the same variety. The same studies have confirmed that Catarratto is one of the parents of Grillo, born of its cross with Muscat of Alexandria.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous and very productive vine, well adapted to the hot, dry climate of western Sicily. Its abundance long made it a grape for large volumes: bulk wine, concentrated must and base wines for Marsala. When yields are controlled and it is grown on hills of some altitude, on clay, limestone or sandy soils, it keeps good acidity and gives wines of higher quality. It ripens mid to late season.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow whites, light to medium in body, fresh and savoury. Its aromas recall lemon, apple, pear and white flowers, with notes of Mediterranean herbs and almond and often a saline touch. Most is drunk young, although versions from high vineyards or aged on lees gain texture and complexity. In Marsala it provides the base and freshness, and on Etna it accompanies Carricante in the volcano’s whites.

Where it is grown

It is grown almost exclusively in Sicily. It is the main variety of the Alcamo DOC, one of the grapes authorised in the Marsala DOC and a frequent component of Etna Bianco. It is also made as a single variety or in blends, often with Inzolia or Chardonnay, in the Sicilia DOC and in IGT Terre Siciliane wines. Outside the island its presence is minimal.

At the table

It goes with fish, shellfish, fried dishes, pasta with vegetables or seafood, salads and Sicilian cooking in general. Serve at 8–10 °C.
