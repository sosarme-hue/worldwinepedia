País

The Spanish Listán Prieto, one of the first European vines planted in the Americas; in Chile, with old dry-farmed vineyards in the south, it is enjoying a revival with light, rustic reds.

Origin

País is the same variety as Listán Prieto, an old Spanish grape that today survives mainly in the Canary Islands. Taken to the Americas by Spanish conquistadors and missionaries in the sixteenth century, it was one of the first European vines grown on the continent and for centuries the basis of sacramental and everyday wine. It received different names depending on the place: País in Chile, Criolla Chica in Argentina, Negra Corriente in Peru and Mission in California, where it was spread by the Franciscan missions. It is also a parent of several South American criolla grapes, such as Torrontés Riojano.

Viticulture

It is a rustic, vigorous, drought-resistant vine, which allowed it to thrive for centuries without irrigation. In southern Chile there are many old, dry-farmed bush vines, often a century old or more and ungrafted, since phylloxera never reached the country. It usually gives large bunches and generous yields.

In the glass

It gives pale reds, light- to medium-bodied, with moderate acidity and sometimes rustic tannins. The aromas recall red fruit (strawberry, raspberry, cherry), with earthy, herbal and floral notes. For a long time it went into simple bulk wines and pipeño, a popular young, unfiltered wine traditionally sold in barrels or demijohns. Since the 2010s many Chilean producers have revived it with careful winemaking, often using carbonic maceration or minimal intervention, producing light, fresh, highly drinkable reds as well as rosés and sparkling wines.

Where it is grown

In Chile it remains one of the red varieties with the largest planted area, concentrated in the south: Maule, Itata and Bío-Bío, where small-scale family dry farming predominates. In Argentina, as Criolla Chica, it covers a small area; in the United States, Mission almost disappeared from California, although some old vines survive. In the Canary Islands, as Listán Prieto, it is grown in small quantities, often interplanted with other varieties.

At the table

Its light reds go with charcuterie, empanadas, white meat, simple stews and Chilean country cooking. They are served slightly cool, at 13–15 °C.
