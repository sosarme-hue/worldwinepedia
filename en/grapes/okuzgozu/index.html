Öküzgözü

A red grape from Eastern Anatolia whose name means “ox eye”, after the large size of its berries. It gives fruity wines with lively acidity and moderate tannins, and is traditionally blended with the tannic Boğazkere.

Origin

Öküzgözü is a native red variety of Eastern Anatolia, originating in the province of Elazığ, around the old town of Harput and the valleys of the upper Euphrates. Its name means “ox eye” in Turkish and refers to its large, round, dark berries. It is one of the most important Turkish red grapes for winemaking and, together with Boğazkere, forms one of the country’s most traditional blending pairs.

Viticulture

In its area of origin it is grown at altitude, in a harsh continental climate with very cold winters and hot, dry summers, on stony and clay soils. It is a productive vine with large bunches and juicy berries, which needs yield control to give concentrated wines. It ripens relatively late. In recent decades several wineries have also planted it in the Aegean region and in Thrace, far from its traditional area, with results in a somewhat different style.

In the glass

It gives ruby wines of medium intensity, medium body, marked acidity and moderate tannins. Its profile is above all fruity, with aromas of cherry, raspberry, plum, blackberry and pomegranate, sometimes with notes of spice, chocolate and dried herbs. Thanks to its freshness and fruit it can be drunk young, although barrel-aged versions gain complexity. Blended with Boğazkere, from Diyarbakır and far more tannic, Öküzgözü brings fruit, acidity and softness, while Boğazkere provides structure and colour; this combination is the basis of some of Turkey’s best-known reds.

Where it is grown

Its main area is still Elazığ, with plantings also in neighbouring provinces of Eastern Anatolia such as Malatya. Many wineries in the west of the country buy grapes in this region and transport them to their facilities to be vinified. It has also spread to the Aegean region, Turkish Thrace and Central Anatolia. Outside Turkey its presence is practically nil. It is made as a varietal, blended with Boğazkere or with international varieties, and also as rosé.
