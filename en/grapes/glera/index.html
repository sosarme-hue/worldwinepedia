Glera

The grape of Prosecco, itself called Prosecco until 2009: an aromatic, productive white that gives light, fruity sparkling wines, nearly always made by the tank method.

Origin

Glera comes from north-eastern Italy. Its historic name, Prosecco, is linked to the village of the same name near Trieste in Friuli-Venezia Giulia, although the variety became established above all in the hills of Treviso in the Veneto. In 2009, when the Prosecco DOC was created and Conegliano Valdobbiadene was raised to DOCG status, the grape was officially renamed Glera, an old synonym, so that “Prosecco” would be protected as a geographical name and could not be used as a variety name in other countries.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine ripening mid to late, with large bunches and thin-skinned golden-yellow berries. It is susceptible to some fungal diseases and to drought. In the hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene it is grown on very steep slopes, often with a great deal of manual work, while on the plains of the DOC cultivation is more mechanised and yields are higher.

In the glass

It makes light-bodied wines with moderate to lively acidity and modest alcohol, with aromas of pear, green apple, melon, white peach and acacia and wisteria blossom. To preserve that fruit, most Prosecco is made by the Martinotti or Charmat method, with the second fermentation in sealed pressure tanks. There are spumante, frizzante and still versions, from Brut Nature to Dry, as well as Col Fondo, refermented in bottle and left undisgorged.

Where it is grown

Almost all of it is in the Veneto and Friuli-Venezia Giulia, within the large Prosecco DOC and the Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco and Asolo Prosecco DOCGs. Within Valdobbiadene, the hill of Cartizze is considered the most prestigious site, and “Rive” designates wines from specific communes or hamlets. Outside Italy it is grown in Australia, particularly in Victoria’s King Valley, where it was introduced by families of Italian descent.

At the table

It is a classic aperitif and the base of cocktails such as the Spritz and the Bellini, and it suits starters, light fish dishes, shellfish and vegetable cooking. Serve at 6–8 °C.
