Dolcetto

An early-ripening Piedmontese red giving deeply coloured wines with dark fruit and moderate acidity, mostly meant for drinking young; its benchmark is Dogliani.

Origin

Dolcetto is a traditional variety of southern Piedmont, grown for centuries in the hills of the Langhe and Monferrato and in neighbouring Liguria. Its name, “little sweet one”, does not refer to the wine, which is dry, but to the sweet taste of its grapes, which were once also eaten at the table.

Viticulture

It ripens one or two weeks ahead of Barbera and well before Nebbiolo, so it can be grown at higher altitudes and on cooler exposures where those varieties would not ripen. It is a delicate vine, susceptible to fungal diseases and frost, with a tendency to drop its bunches. It prefers clay-limestone soils. Its natural acidity is low for a Piedmontese red, while its colour and tannins are considerable.

In the glass

It makes deep ruby wines with purple glints, medium-bodied, with moderate acidity and noticeable but soft tannins. Its aromas recall black cherry, plum, blackberry, violet and liquorice, with a slightly bitter, almond-like finish that is very characteristic of the variety. It is usually made with short macerations and no oak for drinking in its first few years, although the best Dogliani can take some bottle age.

Where it is grown

Its stronghold is Piedmont, with Dogliani DOCG, Diano d’Alba DOCG, Dolcetto di Ovada Superiore DOCG and the DOCs Dolcetto d’Alba, Dolcetto d’Asti and Dolcetto d’Acqui, among others. In Liguria, around Pornassio, it is known as Ormeasco. Its area has shrunk in recent decades as many Langhe growers have replaced it with the more highly valued Nebbiolo or Barbera. Outside Italy there are plantings in Australia and the United States (California and Oregon).

At the table

It is Piedmont’s everyday red, pairing with antipasti, cured meats, fresh pasta, risotto, white meats and young cheeses. Serve at 14–16 °C. Its soft acidity and gentle fruit also make it an easy companion for simple everyday meals, from vegetable dishes to pizza.
