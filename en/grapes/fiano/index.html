Fiano

A white grape from Campania with an oily texture and aromas of flowers, honey and hazelnut, the base of Fiano di Avellino and capable of ageing well.

Origin

Fiano is an old Campanian variety, grown for centuries in the hills of Irpinia in the province of Avellino. It has often been linked to the vitis apiana mentioned by Latin authors, a grape whose sweet bunches attracted bees, although this identification is unproven. By the mid-twentieth century very little was grown, and its revival owes much to a few Irpinia wineries that championed local varieties.

Viticulture

It is a vine of medium vigour and low yields, with small bunches and thick-skinned berries. It ripens between late September and October. It does well on the volcanic, clay and limestone soils of Irpinia, where vineyards lie at altitudes of several hundred metres, with cool nights that preserve acidity. It is susceptible to some fungal diseases, but its thick skin gives it a degree of resistance to rot.

In the glass

It makes straw-yellow to golden whites, medium- to full-bodied, with an oily texture and fresh acidity. Its aromas recall white flowers, pear, quince, citrus, honey, toasted hazelnut and herbs, over a mineral or smoky background. Young, it can be reserved, but with a few years in bottle it gains complexity and develops notes of honey, nuts and spice. It can be made in tank, with lees ageing or with some oak.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark appellation is Fiano di Avellino DOCG in Irpinia, with highly regarded communes such as Lapio. It is also grown in the Cilento and other parts of Campania, as well as in Puglia, Basilicata and Sicily, where IGT wines are made. Outside Italy it has been well received in Australia, especially in McLaren Vale and other South Australian regions, for its ability to keep its freshness in warm climates.

At the table

It suits fish in sauce, shellfish, pasta with vegetables or seafood, white meats and semi-hard cheeses. Serve at 10–12 °C.
