Macabeo (Viura)

A versatile white grape, called Viura in Rioja, a base of Cava together with Xarel·lo and Parellada, and also found in French Roussillon as Macabeu.

Origin

Macabeo is an old variety from north-eastern Spain, probably originating in Catalonia or Aragon, from where it spread along the Ebro valley and into French Roussillon. In Rioja and Navarra it is called Viura.

Viticulture

It buds late, which protects it from spring frost, and ripens late. It is productive, with large, compact bunches that are susceptible to grey rot and downy mildew. It adapts to warm, dry climates and, with controlled yields, gives wines of more character. Its musts tend to oxidise, so they need care in the winery.

In the glass

Young Macabeo wines are pale, light- to medium-bodied, with moderate acidity and discreet aromas of white fruit, flowers and almond. Its neutral character makes it very well suited to sparkling wine: in Cava it contributes freshness and fruit and is one of the three traditional varieties. In Rioja, as Viura, it gives both young whites and traditional whites with long barrel ageing and great ageing potential, showing notes of nuts, honey and wax. Well made, it can develop remarkable complexity.

Where it is grown

It is one of Spain’s most widely planted white varieties. It stands out in Penedès and the Cava D.O., in Rioja and Navarra, Costers del Segre, Conca de Barberà, Somontano, Cariñena, Calatayud, Campo de Borja, La Mancha and Valencia. In France, as Macabeu, it is grown in Roussillon and Languedoc, where it goes into still whites and vins doux naturels such as Rivesaltes. Outside these two countries it is rarely found.

Viura is the most widely planted white variety in Rioja, and in Rioja whites it is often blended with Malvasía Riojana and Garnacha Blanca. In Roussillon it is a regular component of the region’s dry white blends.

At the table

Young Macabeo whites go with appetisers, salads, fish and vegetables, and the sparkling wines it forms part of suit a wide range of dishes. Long-aged Rioja Viuras call for fish in sauce, poultry and aged cheeses.
