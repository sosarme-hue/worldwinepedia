Loureira (Loureiro)

A highly aromatic Atlantic white grape with floral and bay-leaf notes, grown in Rías Baixas and Ribeiro and, as Loureiro, in Portugal’s Vinho Verde.

Origin

Loureira is a variety from north-western Iberia, traditional in the Miño (Minho) valley and, in Portugal, in the Lima valley, where it is known as Loureiro. Its name is associated with the bay laurel (loureiro in Galician and Portuguese), because of an aroma reminiscent of the plant.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive variety suited to the humid, mild Atlantic climate. It is traditionally trained on pergolas or other high systems that improve airflow around the bunches. It is susceptible to downy mildew and rot, which calls for attention in rainy years. It prefers granite, sandy and well-drained soils.

In the glass

It gives pale yellow whites, light- to medium-bodied, with high acidity and moderate alcohol. Its distinctive feature is aromatic intensity: white flowers (orange blossom, acacia), bay leaf, citrus (lemon, lime), green apple and peach. It is best drunk young. In Galicia it is usually blended with Albariño, Treixadura or Caíño Blanco, to which it adds perfume and freshness, although varietal wines also exist. In Portugal many single-variety Loureiro Vinhos Verdes are made.

Where it is grown

In Spain it is grown in the Rías Baixas D.O., especially in Val do Salnés and O Rosal, and in Ribeiro. In Portugal, as Loureiro, it is one of the most important white varieties of Vinho Verde, particularly in the Lima, Cávado and Ave sub-regions. Outside these areas it is very rarely grown.

The Rías Baixas regulations list it among the preferred white varieties, together with Albariño, Treixadura and Caíño Blanco, and in Galicia it is also known as Loureira Blanca or Marqués. It is one of the characteristic grapes of the O Rosal sub-zone, on the lower Miño, where it is usually blended with Albariño. It should not be confused with Loureira Tinta, a different red variety. In Vinho Verde, the Loureiros of the Lima valley are among the most perfumed whites of the region, and the variety is often blended with Arinto, known there as Pedernã, and with Trajadura.

At the table

Its perfume and acidity make it ideal as an aperitif and with shellfish, grilled fish, salads and dishes with herbs or citrus; it also goes well with mildly spiced Asian dishes. It is served at 7–9 °C.
