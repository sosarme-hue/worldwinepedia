Treixadura

A Galician white grape, the leading variety of Ribeiro wines and known in Portugal’s Vinho Verde as Trajadura; it gives aromatic, rounded wines.

Origin

Treixadura is a traditional variety of the Miño (Minho) valley, long grown in the Ribeiro area of Ourense and on the Portuguese bank of the river, where it is called Trajadura. Like other Galician varieties it declined during the twentieth century in favour of more productive vines, and was revived from the 1980s, when Ribeiro turned back to its native grapes.

Viticulture

It ripens mid-season. It is grown on slopes and terraces over decomposed granite soils, in river valleys with an Atlantic climate under continental influence. It is susceptible to botrytis and downy mildew in wet years.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow whites, medium-bodied, with more moderate acidity than Albariño and good alcohol. Its aromas recall ripe apple, pear, peach and tropical fruit, with floral and citrus notes. It is rarely bottled on its own: in Ribeiro it is usually blended with Albariño, Godello, Loureira or Torrontés, to which it adds body and fruit; in Vinho Verde it is traditionally blended with Loureiro and Alvarinho. Some producers age it on its lees for greater complexity.

Where it is grown

Its main area is the Ribeiro D.O., where it is the most characteristic white variety. It is also grown in Monterrei, Ribeira Sacra and the Condado do Tea sub-zone of Rías Baixas. In Portugal, as Trajadura, it is found mainly in the Vinho Verde region, particularly in the Monção e Melgaço sub-region. Outside these areas it is grown only on a very small scale.

Ribeiro has a long winemaking history: in the Middle Ages monasteries such as San Clodio promoted vine growing, and its wines were shipped by sea to other European countries. Today Treixadura is the basis of many of the denomination’s most highly regarded whites, and it also goes into Ribeiro tostado, a traditional sweet wine made from grapes dried after the harvest.

At the table

Ribeiro whites based on Treixadura go well with Galician fish and seafood cooking, octopus, empanadas and young cheeses such as tetilla. They are served chilled, at 8–10 °C.
