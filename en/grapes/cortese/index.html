Cortese

A white grape from south-eastern Piedmont and the basis of Gavi, giving dry, light whites with lively acidity and aromas of green apple, citrus and almond.

Origin

Cortese is a white grape from south-eastern Piedmont, grown for centuries in the provinces of Alessandria and Asti, on the hills that stretch towards the Apennines and the border with Liguria. In a region dominated by red grapes, it became the leading quality white variety, above all thanks to the Gavi area, whose wines long found a natural market in nearby Genoa, where they accompanied its fish cookery. In the 20th century Gavi established itself as one of Italy’s best-known whites.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous and fairly productive vine, so yields must be controlled to obtain wines with concentration. It ripens mid-season, around mid to late September, and retains high acidity. It is susceptible to some fungal diseases, especially downy mildew, and to rot in wet years. In Gavi it grows on hills with marl and clay soils, sometimes reddish in colour, and benefits from air currents coming from the sea across the Apennines.

In the glass

It gives pale yellow whites with greenish tints, light to medium in body, dry and with lively acidity. Its aromas are delicate: green apple, lemon, grapefruit, white peach, white flowers and herbs, with a slightly almondy finish and often a saline note. Most is made in tank and drunk young, although the best Gavi, especially from selected vineyards or aged on lees, gains complexity after a few years in bottle. Sparkling wines are also made.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark denomination is the Gavi DOCG, also called Cortese di Gavi, which requires the variety alone. It is also grown in other Piedmontese DOCs such as Cortese dell’Alto Monferrato and Colli Tortonesi, in Oltrepò Pavese in Lombardy, and in Veneto, where under the name Bianca Fernanda it forms part of Bianco di Custoza, near Lake Garda.

At the table

It is a natural partner for fish, shellfish and crustaceans, pasta with pesto and Ligurian cooking, as well as vegetables and aperitifs. Serve at 8–10 °C.
