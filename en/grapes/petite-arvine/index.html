Petite Arvine

A late-ripening white variety native to the Valais and the emblem of its winegrowing. It gives dry whites with lively acidity and a salty finish, and sweet wines from grapes shrivelled on the vine.

Origin

Petite Arvine is a variety of the Valais, where it has been grown for centuries and is considered the most representative grape of the canton’s winegrowing heritage. It should not be confused with Grande Arvine, another local variety of lesser quality that has now almost disappeared. For much of the twentieth century its area was very small, but since the end of that century it has grown considerably thanks to the interest of producers and consumers.

Viticulture

It is a demanding vine, with medium budburst and late ripening, that needs the warmest, best-exposed plots to ripen fully. It is sensitive to wind and to some diseases, and its yields must be controlled to obtain concentrated wines. The sunny slopes on the right bank of the Rhône, around Fully, Leytron, Chamoson and Sion, are among its favoured areas. Its ability to keep its acidity even in very ripe grapes allows it to produce both balanced dry and sweet wines.

In the glass

The dry wines are pale yellow, medium-bodied, with lively acidity and a characteristic finish often described as salty. Their aromas recall grapefruit, rhubarb, citrus, wisteria and white flowers, with notes of exotic fruit in warm vintages. Overripe or vine-shrivelled grapes, sometimes affected by noble rot, are used for sweet wines in which the acidity balances the sugar and which develop notes of honey, quince and candied fruit. The dry wines are enjoyed young, but the best develop well for several years.

Where it is grown

The Valais holds the vast majority of plantings. Outside Switzerland the variety is established in the Aosta Valley in Italy, where it is made under the Valle d’Aosta Petite Arvine designation. There are also small experimental plantings in other cool-climate countries.

At the table

The dry wines go well with lake and sea fish, shellfish, herb-based dishes, mountain cheeses and mildly spiced Asian food; the sweet ones with foie gras, blue cheeses and fruit desserts. Serve the dry wines at 8-10 °C.
