Cabernet Sauvignon

The quintessential international red grape, born in Bordeaux and grown in almost every wine region in the world, giving structured, long-lived wines.

Origin

Cabernet Sauvignon arose in the Bordeaux region, probably in the seventeenth or eighteenth century, from a spontaneous cross between Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc. This parentage was demonstrated in 1997 through DNA analysis carried out at the University of California, Davis. From Bordeaux it spread around the world and is now among the most widely planted wine grape varieties.

Viticulture

It buds and ripens late, with small bunches and small, thick-skinned berries, which explains its colour and high tannin. It resists rot and winter frost well but needs enough heat to ripen; in cool climates or at high yields, marked vegetal notes from pyrazines appear. It prefers warm, well-drained soils, such as the gravels of the Médoc.

In the glass

It gives deeply coloured wines with firm tannins, good acidity and a medium to full body. Typical aromas are blackcurrant (cassis), blackberry and black cherry, with notes of green pepper, mint, eucalyptus or cedar depending on the climate. It has a great affinity with oak barrels, which add toast, vanilla and spice, and remarkable ageing potential, during which notes of tobacco, leather and cigar box emerge. It is often blended with Merlot and Cabernet Franc.

Where it is grown

In France it is the backbone of the great reds of Bordeaux’s Left Bank (Médoc, Graves). In the United States it made Napa Valley famous, and it is key in Chile, Australia (Coonawarra, Margaret River), South Africa (Stellenbosch) and Argentina. In Italy it features in the so-called Super Tuscans, and in Spain it is grown in Penedès, Navarra, Somontano, Costers del Segre, Ribera del Duero and La Mancha, among other areas. It is also found in Portugal, Austria and New Zealand.

At the table

Its firm tannins find their best counterpoint in grilled red meat, lamb and aged cheeses. Young, powerful wines benefit from aeration, and long-aged ones are served at around 17–18 °C.
