Grenache Blanc

The pale-skinned form of Grenache, giving broad, low-acid whites; it is the signature grape of Terra Alta and is widely grown in the Rhône and Roussillon.

Origin

Grenache Blanc is a colour mutation of red Grenache, with which it shares its genetic profile. Like its parent it comes from north-eastern Spain and spread across the western Mediterranean, especially Catalonia, Aragon and southern France.

Viticulture

It inherits Grenache’s hardiness: it is vigorous, copes well with drought and wind, and ripens late. It accumulates sugar readily and tends to lose acidity if picked late, so the harvest date is decisive for balance. Its musts oxidise easily, which calls for care in the winery.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow wines, medium- to full-bodied, with generous alcohol and moderate acidity. The nose shows white and stone fruit (pear, peach), floral notes, fennel and herbs, and often a slightly saline background on limestone soils. It responds well to barrel fermentation and ageing on lees, which add volume and texture. It is used on its own or in blends, for example with Macabeo, Roussanne or Marsanne.

Where it is grown

In Spain its stronghold is Terra Alta, in Tarragona, where whites are made with a specific mention of the variety; it is also found in Priorat, Montsant, Empordà, Costers del Segre, Alella, Aragon and Navarra. In France it is one of the most important white varieties of the south: it is part of white Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Côtes du Rhône, and of the wines of Roussillon and Languedoc, where it also goes into vins doux naturels. There are also plantings in California and Australia.

At the table

Thanks to its body and texture, Grenache Blanc pairs well with fish in sauce, seafood rice, poultry and semi-cured cheeses. Barrel-aged versions can take richer dishes. It is served cool, but not too cold, so that its aromas can be appreciated.
