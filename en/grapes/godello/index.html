Godello

A Galician white grape from Valdeorras and Bierzo, rescued from near extinction in the 1970s, giving wines with body, freshness and ageing potential.

Origin

Godello is a variety from north-western Iberia, traditional in the Sil valley in Galicia and in Bierzo. In Portugal it is known as Gouveio and grown in the Douro. After phylloxera and replacement by more productive varieties, very few vines remained by the mid-twentieth century. In the 1970s the Revalagar project in Valdeorras revived the variety from the surviving vines, and its area has grown steadily ever since.

Viticulture

It buds and ripens early, with small, compact bunches, and is susceptible to powdery mildew and botrytis. It is often grown on slopes and terraces over slate and granite soils, in areas with an Atlantic climate under continental influence, drier and warmer in summer than the Galician coast.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow whites, medium- to full-bodied, with good acidity and a broad, silky texture. The aromas recall apple, pear, peach and citrus, with notes of herbs, white flowers and a mineral character associated with slate. Its structure suits lees and barrel ageing, which add complexity (nuts, toasty notes), and some wines develop well in bottle for years. Because of its volume and finesse it is sometimes compared with Burgundian Chardonnay.

Where it is grown

It is the main white variety of the Valdeorras D.O. and is also grown in Monterrei, Ribeira Sacra, Ribeiro and Bierzo, where it has gained prominence. In Portugal, as Gouveio, it goes into the white wines of the Douro and Trás-os-Montes. Outside the peninsula it is still very rarely grown.

The name Valdeorras is traditionally linked to the gold the Romans mined in the area, and the revival of Godello is often cited as one of the clearest examples of the rescue of a native variety in Spain. In the Douro, Gouveio goes into both still white wines and white Port.

At the table

Its body and freshness suit white fish, shellfish, rice dishes, poultry and soft cheeses. Barrel-aged Godellos go well with fish in sauce and white meat. It is served at 9–12 °C.
