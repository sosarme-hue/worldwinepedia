Arneis

A native white grape of Roero, in Piedmont, which came close to disappearing and today gives aromatic, medium-bodied whites with notes of pear, white flowers and almond.

Origin

Arneis is a white grape from Piedmont, grown for centuries on the hills of Roero, on the left bank of the Tanaro river, opposite the Langhe. In Piedmontese dialect, arnèis describes a difficult or somewhat roguish person, a name usually linked to how temperamental it is to grow. For a long time it was planted alongside Nebbiolo, sometimes to soften its reds and sometimes to lure birds away from the red bunches, hence its old nickname Nebbiolo Bianco. By the 1960s and 1970s its cultivation had become marginal, but a few producers in Roero and the Langhe chose to make it as a single-variety wine and saved it.

Viticulture

It is a vine of moderate vigour and irregular yields, susceptible to powdery mildew, which ripens relatively early, at the beginning of September. One of its challenges is that its acidity drops quickly at the end of ripening, so the timing of the harvest is decisive for keeping freshness. It does particularly well in the loose, sandy soils of Roero, of marine origin, which favour fine, fragrant wines.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow whites of medium body and moderate acidity, with a soft, rounded texture. Its aromas recall pear, apple, white peach and white flowers, with notes of chamomile, herbs and a characteristic almond finish. Most are made in stainless steel to preserve the fruit and are drunk young, although there are more complex versions aged on lees or in wood, as well as some sparkling wines and passiti.

Where it is grown

Its main area is the Roero DOCG, which includes Roero Arneis, and it is also grown in the Langhe, where it is made under the Langhe DOC. Outside Italy it has been planted on a small scale in Australia and the United States.

At the table

It goes well with aperitifs, vegetable starters, freshwater and sea fish, pasta with vegetables, white meat and fresh cheeses. Serve at 8–10 °C.
