Graciano

A low-yielding, high-acid red, traditionally used alongside Tempranillo in Rioja, to which it adds freshness, colour and aroma.

Origin

Graciano is a northern Spanish variety, historically recorded in La Rioja and Navarra. Genetic studies have shown it to be the same grape as Tintilla de Rota from Cádiz, Portugal’s Tinta Miúda and Sardinia’s Cagnulari. In France it was known as Morrastel, a name sometimes applied to Monastrell in Spain, which has caused confusion.

Viticulture

It buds late and ripens very late, with low and irregular yields, which brought it close to extinction in the twentieth century, as it was less profitable than other varieties. It is susceptible to downy mildew and needs warm years to ripen fully. From the last decades of the twentieth century, growers in Rioja and Navarra revived it for its quality.

In the glass

It gives very deeply coloured wines with high acidity, marked tannins and moderate alcohol. It is highly aromatic: dark fruit (blackberry, blueberry), floral, balsamic and spicy notes, sometimes with an herbal or minty touch. Its acidity and structure give it great ageing potential. Blended with Tempranillo it adds freshness and longevity; as a varietal wine it is intense and distinctive.

Where it is grown

Its main area is Rioja, where it is one of the authorised red varieties, though grown on a small area. It is also found in Navarra and other northern areas, and as Tintilla de Rota in the Jerez region, where it has traditionally been used for sweet wines. In Portugal it appears as Tinta Miúda in the Lisboa region, and in Sardinia as Cagnulari. There are small plantings in Australia and California.

In Rioja, together with Tempranillo, Garnacha and Mazuelo, it is part of the traditional blend of many Reservas and Gran Reservas, although in small proportions, as a complement that adds acidity, colour and aroma.

At the table

Intense and fresh, Graciano wines go well with red meat, lamb, game and spiced stews, as well as aged cheeses. Thanks to their acidity they also suit dishes with tomato or pepper sauces. They are served at 16–18 °C.
