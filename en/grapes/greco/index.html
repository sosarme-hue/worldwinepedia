Greco

A white grape from Campania, the basis of the Greco di Tufo DOCG, giving structured whites with firm acidity and a mineral character, with aromas of peach, pear and almond.

Origin

Greco is a white grape from Campania, grown for centuries in Irpinia, in the province of Avellino. Its name has fuelled the idea that it arrived with the Greek colonists who settled in southern Italy in antiquity, but this is an unconfirmed tradition. DNA analysis has shown that it is the same variety as Asprinio, grown near Aversa, north of Naples. It should not be confused with other grapes bearing the Greco name, such as Greco Bianco from Calabria or the red Greco Nero, which are different varieties.

Viticulture

It is a vine of moderate vigour and late ripening, with compact bunches and fairly thick-skinned berries. It needs careful yield control and is susceptible to powdery and downy mildew. Its classic area is the hills of Tufo and the neighbouring communes, several hundred metres above sea level, where the soils combine clay, limestone and volcanic material. Tufo was home to sulphur mines for a long time, and the presence of this element in the soil is often linked to the mineral character of its wines, although that link has not been proven.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow to golden whites, medium- to full-bodied, with firm acidity and a structure unusual in a white wine, sometimes with a slight phenolic touch on the palate. Its aromas recall peach, apricot, pear, citrus and almond, with notes of herbs, flint and honey. The best examples age well, developing hints of nuts and hydrocarbons over time. Sparkling wines are also made.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark denomination is the Greco di Tufo DOCG, which covers Tufo and several nearby communes in the province of Avellino. It is also grown elsewhere in Campania for DOC and IGT wines and, under the name Asprinio, in the Aversa DOC. Outside Campania it has little presence.

At the table

It goes with fish in sauce, shellfish, crustaceans, pasta with seafood, white meat and semi-mature cheeses. Serve at 10–12 °C.
