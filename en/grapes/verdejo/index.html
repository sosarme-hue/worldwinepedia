Verdejo

A native Castilian white grape, the heart of the Rueda D.O., giving aromatic, fresh wines with a characteristic slightly bitter finish.

Origin

Verdejo is native to the Duero plateau and has been grown for centuries in the Rueda area, across the provinces of Valladolid, Segovia and Ávila. Its distant origins are not documented with certainty. Its name comes from the greenish tint of its berries. It should not be confused with Portugal’s Verdelho or Italy’s Verdello, which are different varieties.

Viticulture

It is well adapted to the extreme continental climate of the plateau, with cold winters, hot summers and sharp differences between day and night, and to stony soils of rounded pebbles as well as poor, sandy, well-drained ones. It is susceptible to powdery mildew. Its musts oxidise easily, so many wineries harvest at night so that the grapes arrive cool at the winery.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow whites with green hints, medium-bodied, with fresh acidity and a somewhat creamy texture. The aromas combine white and tropical fruit, citrus and very characteristic herbal notes (fennel, freshly cut grass, bay leaf), with a slightly bitter finish considered typical of the variety. Besides young wines, whites aged on lees or fermented in barrel are made, with more volume and ageing potential. Historically, Rueda also produced fortified wines with oxidative ageing, such as Dorado.

Where it is grown

Its cultivation is concentrated in the Rueda D.O., where it is the main variety and where it made a strong comeback from the 1970s, after decades of decline in favour of more productive varieties. It is also grown in other parts of Castilla y León, such as Cigales, Toro and León, and in Castilla-La Mancha. Outside Spain there are small plantings, for example in Australia.

At the table

Young Verdejos go well with appetisers, salads, vegetables, fish and rice dishes, and their herbal edge suits asparagus and dishes with aromatic herbs. Aged versions can take poultry and fish in sauce. It is served at 8–10 °C.
