Hárslevelű

A Hungarian white grape, the second variety of Tokaj after Furmint, from which it descends. It brings floral perfume, spice and a rounder texture to both sweet and dry wines.

Origin

Hárslevelű is a native Hungarian variety whose name means “linden leaf”, referring to the shape of its leaves. It has been grown for centuries in Tokaj alongside Furmint, and genetic studies indicate that it descends from Furmint, with which it shares much of its history. In Slovakia, in the Slovak part of Tokaj, it is known as Lipovina, and in German-speaking countries it has been called Lindenblättrige.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine that ripens somewhat earlier than Furmint and generally reaches high sugar levels with more moderate acidity. Its berries can be affected by Botrytis cinerea and shrivel, although it is usually considered somewhat less prone to noble rot than Furmint. It adapts well to volcanic and loess soils. Because of its aromatic character and texture, in Tokaj it is often used to complement Furmint in blends, both sweet and dry.

In the glass

It gives medium- to full-bodied wines, with a softer, more unctuous texture than Furmint and generally less piercing acidity. Its aroma is markedly floral, with notes of linden blossom, honey, spices such as cinnamon or clove, pear, peach and sometimes herbs. In sweet wines it brings perfume, aromatic finesse and roundness; in dry wines it can give expressive whites that age well. With age it takes on honeyed and nutty tones.

Where it is grown

Its main area is Tokaj, where it is the second most important variety. Elsewhere in Hungary it is grown in regions such as Somló, the Villány and Siklós area and the Debrő area, in the foothills of the Mátra mountains, traditionally known for its Hárslevelű wines. Outside Hungary its presence is limited mainly to the Slovak part of Tokaj and to small plantings in other countries.

In recent years several Tokaj producers have begun bottling it as a dry single-vineyard varietal, which has helped reveal a character of its own, different from Furmint’s, more aromatic and broader, which used to be hidden in blends.
