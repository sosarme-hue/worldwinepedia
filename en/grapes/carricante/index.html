Carricante

The great white grape of Etna, grown at high altitude on the slopes of the volcano, giving wines of very lively acidity, mineral character and notable ageing potential.

Origin

Carricante is a native white grape of Sicily, grown for centuries on the slopes of Etna, in the east of the island. Its name is linked to the Sicilian verb meaning “to load”, a reference to its abundant crops, which filled the carts at harvest time. For a long time it was a grape of local importance, interplanted in the vineyards with other varieties, but the revival of Etna as a wine region from the late 20th century onwards has made it one of Italy’s most highly regarded white grapes.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine, so yield control is essential to obtain quality wines. It ripens late, often in October, and keeps very high acidity, with a notable proportion of malic acid. It is grown mainly on the eastern side of the volcano, the most exposed to the influence of the Ionian Sea and to rainfall, in high-altitude vineyards that can exceed a thousand metres. The soils are volcanic, sandy and mineral-rich, formed by the breakdown of lava flows and ash. It was traditionally trained as bush vines on chestnut stakes, often on terraces held up by volcanic stone walls.

In the glass

It gives pale yellow whites with greenish tints, light to medium in body, with cutting acidity and a marked mineral, saline character. Its aromas recall lemon, green apple, pear and orange blossom, with notes of aniseed, herbs and flint. Young, it can seem austere, but with years in bottle it gains complexity and develops hints of honey and hydrocarbons that are often compared to those of Riesling.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark area is the Etna DOC, where it is the basis of Etna Bianco, often blended with Catarratto, and of Etna Bianco Superiore, reserved for the commune of Milo and requiring a higher minimum proportion of Carricante. It has also been planted elsewhere in Sicily, though on a small scale. Outside the island it is hardly grown.

At the table

It goes with raw or grilled fish, shellfish, crustaceans, pasta with seafood and fresh cheeses. Serve at 8–10 °C, and bottle-aged wines a little warmer.
