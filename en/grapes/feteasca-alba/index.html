White grape

Fetească Albă

A traditional white variety of Romania and Moldova, the “white maiden”, known in Hungary as Leányka. It gives delicate, floral whites, dry or with some residual sugar.

Origin

Fetească Albă is an old variety from the historic region of Moldavia, between north-eastern present-day Romania and the Republic of Moldova, from where it spread to Transylvania and other parts of the Carpathian basin. Like its namesake, the red Fetească Neagră, its name comes from fată, “girl”, and means “white maiden”; in Hungary it is known as Leányka, which has the same meaning, and in German as Mädchentraube. It should not be confused with Fetească Regală, a different white variety that appeared in Transylvania in the twentieth century and is now also widely planted in Romania.

Viticulture

It is a vine of medium vigour and relatively early ripening, well adapted to continental climates and reasonably resistant to winter cold. Its bunches are medium-sized and its berries thin-skinned, which makes it somewhat prone to rot in wet autumns; in certain areas, such as Cotnari, it can be affected by noble rot. At high yields its wines are neutral, so quality depends on controlling production and choosing well-exposed slopes.

In the glass

It gives pale yellow whites with green highlights, light to medium body and moderate to lively acidity. Its aromatic profile is delicate: white flowers, vine blossom, peach, apricot, citrus and apple, sometimes with a touch of almond or honey. Traditionally it was often made medium-dry or medium-sweet, a style still appreciated in Romania and Moldova, but today dry, fresh, aromatic versions are also common. It is also used as a base for sparkling wines and, in the best conditions, for late-harvest wines. Most of its wines are drunk young.

Where it is grown

It is one of the most widespread whites in Romania, above all in Romanian Moldavia, in areas such as Cotnari, Iași and Huși, and on the Târnave plateau in Transylvania. In the Republic of Moldova it is present in all three of the country’s geographical indications. In Hungary, under the name Leányka, it is grown in areas such as Eger, where light, aromatic whites are made.

At the table

The dry wines go with white fish, salads, vegetables and fresh cheeses; the medium-dry ones with lightly spiced dishes and not-too-sweet desserts. Serve at 8-10 °C.
