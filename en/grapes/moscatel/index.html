Muscat

A family of highly aromatic grapes, recognisable by their intense aroma of fresh grapes and flowers, giving dry, sparkling and above all sweet wines around the world.

Origin

The name Muscat covers a family of related varieties that share a very characteristic aroma, due to their high content of terpene compounds such as linalool and geraniol. They are among the oldest known grapes in the Mediterranean. The two most important are Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains, considered the finest, and Muscat of Alexandria, which DNA analysis shows to be its offspring. There are also pink- and red-skinned forms, as well as the more recent Muscat Ottonel.

Viticulture

Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains has small berries and ripens early to mid-season; it is susceptible to powdery mildew and rot. Muscat of Alexandria, with large berries, needs warm climates and is also used as a table grape and for raisins. Both adapt well to Mediterranean climates and are suited to sun-drying or late harvesting.

In the glass

The common feature is aromatic expression: fresh grapes, orange blossom, rose, white flowers, citrus, peach and, in aged sweet wines, honey, raisin and nuts. The grapes are used for aromatic dry wines, light sweet sparkling wines such as Moscato d’Asti, fortified vins doux naturels, late-harvest wines and wines from dried grapes.

Where it is grown

In Spain, Muscat of Alexandria stands out in Málaga, Valencia and Alicante, and Muscat à Petits Grains in Navarra; there are also Muscats in Cádiz, Tarragona, the Canary Islands and elsewhere. In France it is the basis of the vins doux naturels of Beaumes-de-Venise, Frontignan and Rivesaltes, and dry Muscats are made in Alsace. In Italy, as Moscato, it gives Moscato d’Asti and Asti and, as Zibibbo, Passito di Pantelleria. Portugal is famous for Moscatel de Setúbal, and in Austria and Germany it is known as Muskateller. Outside Europe, notable examples are Rutherglen Muscat in Australia, the Muscats of South Africa, where Muscat of Alexandria is called Hanepoot, and those of Chile and Argentina, partly used for pisco distillation in Chile. It is also grown in the United States.
