White grape

Parellada

A Catalan white grape from higher, cooler areas, the most delicate and floral of the three traditional Cava varieties.

Origin

Parellada is native to Catalonia and has traditionally been grown in the higher parts of Penedès and in the inland districts of Tarragona and Lleida. Together with Macabeo and Xarel·lo it forms the classic trio of varieties used to make Cava.

Viticulture

It buds and ripens late and is productive, with large bunches and thin-skinned berries. It needs cool, high-altitude sites to keep its acidity and aromatic finesse; in warm climates its wines turn out flat. In Penedès it is grown mainly in the Alt Penedès and the highest areas. It is susceptible to powdery mildew and grey rot.

In the glass

It gives very pale wines, light-bodied, with fresh acidity and moderate, often low, alcohol. Its aromas are delicate: white flowers, green apple, white stone fruit and citrus. In Cava it adds finesse, freshness and aromatic elegance, complementing the body of Xarel·lo and the fruit of Macabeo. It is also used for young, light, aromatic still whites meant for early drinking, and in blends with varieties such as Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc.

Where it is grown

It is grown almost exclusively in Catalonia. The main areas are Penedès and the Cava D.O., Conca de Barberà and Costers del Segre, and it is also found in Tarragona and the Catalunya D.O. Outside Catalonia it is very rarely found.

Its importance in Catalonia is closely tied to the history of Cava. After phylloxera devastated Penedès at the end of the nineteenth century, many vineyards were replanted with white varieties just as Catalan traditional-method sparkling wines, made in Sant Sadurní d’Anoia since the 1870s, were taking off. That boom consolidated the trio of Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada, which still defines the classic style of Cava today, although it now shares the stage with other authorised varieties such as Chardonnay. Producers continue to value it for the finesse and lightness it brings to blends.

At the table

Its light, aromatic whites suit aperitifs, salads, vegetables, white fish and simple shellfish dishes. They are served chilled, at 7–9 °C, and are best drunk young to enjoy their freshness.
