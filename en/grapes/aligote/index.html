Aligoté

Burgundy’s “other” white grape, with lively acidity and citrus aromas, its own village appellation in Bouzeron and the traditional base of kir.

Origin

Aligoté is a Burgundian variety recorded since at least the eighteenth century. DNA analysis has shown it to be a natural cross between Pinot and Gouais Blanc, which makes it a sibling of Chardonnay, Gamay and Melon de Bourgogne. It once covered more of Burgundy than it does today, but it was pushed onto less favoured sites as Chardonnay took over the best slopes.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine of medium ripening, fairly resistant to winter cold though susceptible to grey rot. It keeps high acidity even when fully ripe. It is grown mainly in the marginal areas of the Côte d’Or and in the Côte Chalonnaise, the Hautes-Côtes and the Mâconnais. There are two traditional types of clone, known as Aligoté vert and Aligoté doré; the latter is considered of higher quality.

In the glass

It makes pale, dry whites, light- to medium-bodied with vibrant acidity, with aromas of lemon, grapefruit, green apple and white flowers and occasionally hazelnut or herbal notes. It is usually made without oak and drunk young, although in recent years several Burgundy producers have been making old-vine Aligoté, aged on lees or in barrel, with more depth and ageing potential.

Where it is grown

In Burgundy it produces the regional AOC Bourgogne Aligoté and the village appellation Bouzeron in the Côte Chalonnaise, the only Burgundy appellation devoted to this grape. It is also used in Crémant de Bourgogne. Canon Félix Kir, mayor of Dijon, popularised the mix of Aligoté and crème de cassis that bears his name. Outside France, Aligoté is widespread in Eastern Europe, especially Moldova, Ukraine, Romania, Bulgaria and Russia, and there are small plantings in Switzerland and elsewhere.

At the table

Its freshness suits Burgundy-style snails, goat’s cheeses, shellfish, simply cooked fish and gougères. Serve at 8–10 °C. Old-vine and barrel-aged versions show best slightly less cold.
