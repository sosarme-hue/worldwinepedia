White grape

Albariño

The great Atlantic white grape of Rías Baixas and, as Alvarinho, of Monção e Melgaço in Vinho Verde: fresh, aromatic, saline wines.

Origin

Albariño is a variety from north-western Iberia, on both banks of the Miño (Minho) river, which separates Galicia from Portugal. Its exact origin is disputed between southern Galicia and the Portuguese area of Monção e Melgaço. A popular tradition credits its arrival to Cistercian monks from Burgundy, but this idea has no scientific support.

Viticulture

It grows in a humid, mild Atlantic climate, so it is traditionally trained on high pergolas, which help air circulate around the bunches and reduce the fungal diseases to which it is susceptible (downy and powdery mildew). Its berries are small and thick-skinned, giving it resistance to humidity and aromatic richness. It prefers granite and sandy soils that are acidic and well drained.

In the glass

It gives pale lemon-yellow whites with green hints, high acidity, a medium body and an often saline finish. The aromas are intense: stone fruit (peach, apricot), citrus (grapefruit, lemon), apple, white flowers and sometimes herbal notes. It is traditionally drunk young, but lees ageing and selections from old vines give fuller wines that develop well in bottle for several years. Sparkling wines and some barrel-fermented wines are also made.

Where it is grown

In Spain it is the main grape of the Rías Baixas D.O., with sub-zones such as Val do Salnés, O Rosal, Condado do Tea, Soutomaior and Ribeira do Ulla, and it is also found in other Galician areas such as Ribeiro. In Portugal, as Alvarinho, it is the signature variety of the Monção e Melgaço sub-region of Vinho Verde and has spread to other regions. Beyond the peninsula it is grown in the United States (California, Oregon), Australia, New Zealand and Uruguay.

At the table

It is a classic partner for fish and shellfish: oysters, clams, octopus, grilled fish and light Asian cooking. It is served chilled, at 8–11 °C; lees-aged versions can be served a little warmer.
