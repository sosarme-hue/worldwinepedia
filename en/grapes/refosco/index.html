Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso

The most widespread native red grape of Friuli, named after the red colour of its stalk, giving deeply coloured wines with lively acidity and a slightly bitter finish.

Origin

Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso is a red grape from north-eastern Italy, grown for centuries in Friuli Venezia Giulia and eastern Veneto. Its full name, meaning “Refosco with the red stalk”, refers to the colour taken on by the bunch stem as the grapes ripen, a feature that makes it easy to recognise in the vineyard. The distinction matters, because several different grapes from north-eastern Italy and the Istrian peninsula are known as Refosco, such as Refosco Nostrano or di Faedis and Terrano, which genetic studies distinguish from it. Of them all, Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso is the most widely planted and the one usually referred to simply as Refosco.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, late-ripening vine, usually harvested in October. Its skin is fairly thick and it resists rot relatively well, making it suitable for Friuli’s climate, with its often rainy autumns. It adapts both to the gravel plains of Grave and Aquileia, where it gives fruitier, lighter wines, and to the hills of the Colli Orientali, with marl and sandstone soils, which give more structured wines.

In the glass

It gives deep purple-ruby reds, medium-bodied, with lively acidity, firm tannins and a very characteristic slightly bitter finish. Its aromas recall blackberry, plum, sour cherry and raspberry, with notes of violet, herbs, almond and spice. Young versions are fresh and fruity; those made from hillside grapes and aged in wood have more structure and can age well. Some wines are also made from partially dried grapes.

Where it is grown

Its main area is Friuli, in denominations such as Friuli Grave, Friuli Colli Orientali, Friuli Aquileia, Friuli Latisana and Friuli Isonzo, as well as the Friuli DOC. In eastern Veneto it is grown in areas such as Lison-Pramaggiore and Piave. Outside Italy its presence is limited.

At the table

It goes with grilled red meat, game, cured meats, stews, polenta with meat and aged cheeses; its acidity helps it balance rich dishes. Serve at 16–18 °C.
