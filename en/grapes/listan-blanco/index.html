White grape

Listán Blanco

The Canary Islands name for Palomino, the grape of sherry: on the islands it is the most widely planted white grape and, on volcanic soils and ungrafted vines, makes fresh, saline dry whites with a marked Atlantic character.

One grape, two names

Listán Blanco and Palomino are the same variety. In the Jerez area it is known as Palomino or Palomino Fino and is the basis of sherry and Manzanilla; in the Canary Islands, where it arrived with Andalusian settlers and merchants after the Castilian conquest, it kept the old name of listán, which was also used in Andalusia. It should not be confused with Listán Negro, a distinct Canarian variety, nor with Listán Prieto. This entry focuses on its life in the Canaries; its role in sherry is covered under Palomino.

Viticulture in the Canaries

Phylloxera never reached the archipelago, so many vines are planted on their own roots and some vineyards are very old. Listán Blanco grows on volcanic soils, from coastal areas up to high altitudes on the slopes of Tenerife, under the influence of the trade winds and the sea of clouds. In the Orotava Valley the cordón trenzado survives, a system in which the canes are braided into long cords raised on forked props. It is a vigorous and productive vine which, unlike in Jerez, is grown in the Canaries to make table wines.

In the glass

On its own it is a grape of discreet aroma and moderate acidity, but in the islands’ cool, high vineyards it makes light to medium-bodied dry whites, with notes of apple, citrus, herbs and white flowers and a saline or mineral streak often attributed to the volcanic soils. Besides young whites, there are wines aged on lees, wines fermented in oak and, in some cases, sparkling wines.

Where it is grown

It is the most widely planted white grape in the Canary Islands. It dominates on Tenerife, in the Tacoronte-Acentejo, Valle de La Orotava, Ycoden-Daute-Isora, Valle de Güímar and Abona appellations, and is also found on La Palma, Gran Canaria, El Hierro and Lanzarote. As Palomino it is also grown in Andalusia, Galicia and Castile and León, and in other countries.

At the table

Canarian Listán whites go with fresh fish, wrinkled potatoes with mojo sauce, goat’s cheeses and shellfish. Serve at 8–11 °C.
