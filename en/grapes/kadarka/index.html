Kadarka

A red grape of Balkan origin, for centuries the most important in Hungary, which makes light-coloured, spicy, aromatic reds and is known in Bulgaria as Gamza.

Origin

Kadarka is a variety of Balkan origin. Its exact birthplace is debated, but it is usually placed in the area of Lake Skadar, between Montenegro and Albania, from which its name is thought to derive. From the Balkans it spread through the Danube basin and for a long time became the most widely grown red grape in Hungary. It is traditionally linked with the history of Bikavér, the famous ‘bull’s blood’ of Eger and Szekszárd, although today these blends are based mainly on other varieties, such as Kékfrankos.

Viticulture

It is a demanding vine. It ripens late and needs long, warm autumns; its bunches are compact and its berries thin-skinned, which makes it very prone to rot in wet years. Its ripening can also be uneven within the same bunch. These difficulties explain why many growers replaced it with easier varieties in the twentieth century, and its area shrank considerably. In recent decades, the selection of quality clones and interest in historic varieties have driven its revival.

In the glass

It makes light- to medium-coloured reds, light or medium-bodied, with soft tannins and lively acidity. Their aromas recall sour cherry, raspberry and strawberry, with very characteristic notes of spice, pepper, paprika and herbs. The best versions combine delicacy and complexity and can develop well for a few years.

Where it is grown

In Hungary it is found mainly in Szekszárd, its main stronghold, in Hajós-Baja and in Eger, where it may form part of Bikavér or be bottled on its own. In Bulgaria it is known as Gamza and is grown in the north of the country, near the Danube. In Romania, under the name Cadarcă, it was historically important, and it is also grown in Serbia, especially in Vojvodina. Outside this part of central Europe and the Balkans its presence is very scarce.

At the table

Its spicy reds go very well with Hungarian dishes flavoured with paprika, such as goulash or pörkölt, as well as charcuterie, poultry and grilled meat. Serve at 14–16 °C.
