Moristel

A native red grape of Somontano, with moderate colour, soft tannins and expressive red fruit, used for young reds, rosés and blends, and for a few varietal wines from old vines.

Origin

Moristel is a traditional red variety of Upper Aragón, linked above all to the Somontano district at the foot of the Pyrenees in the province of Huesca. Together with Parraleta it is one of the two native red grapes that distinguish this appellation. Its name has led to confusion with Monastrell, but they are different varieties. During the twentieth century, with the restructuring of Somontano’s vineyards and the arrival of international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Chardonnay, its area shrank considerably, although old vineyards survive and some wineries have revived them.

Viticulture

It is a moderately productive vine with compact bunches and thin-skinned berries that ripens mid-season. It adapts to Somontano’s continental climate, with cold winters, hot summers and a Pyrenean influence that cools the nights, and to its brown, calcareous, loamy soils. Its main difficulty in the winery is its tendency to oxidise: the must and the wine easily lose colour, so it needs careful handling during winemaking and ageing.

In the glass

It makes wines of moderate ruby colour, light to medium body, soft tannins and balanced acidity. Its aroma is fruity and direct, with strawberry, raspberry and cherry, sometimes with floral notes and a touch of pepper. Because of these qualities it has traditionally been used for young reds and rosés, and as a component in blends with Tempranillo, Garnacha or the French varieties, to which it adds freshness and fruit. Some wineries make varietal wines from old vines, occasionally with a short period in barrel, that aim to express local character.

Where it is grown

It is concentrated in the Somontano DO, in the province of Huesca, and is very rare elsewhere. Its presence, always minor compared with the international varieties and Tempranillo, has stabilised thanks to producers’ interest in setting their wines apart with grapes native to the area.

At the table

Its light, fruity wines go with charcuterie, roast vegetables, rice dishes, white meat and Aragonese dishes such as roast young lamb (ternasco). Serve at 14–16 °C.
