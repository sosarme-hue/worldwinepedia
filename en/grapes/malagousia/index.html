Malagousia

An aromatic Greek white grape, rescued from near extinction in the twentieth century, which gives perfumed wines of jasmine, peach and citrus with medium body and moderate acidity.

Origin

Malagousia is a Greek white variety native to the west of the country, from the area of Nafpaktos in Aetolia-Acarnania, on the shores of the Gulf of Corinth. By the middle of the twentieth century it was on the verge of disappearing, with only a few scattered vines surviving. Its revival began thanks to researchers at the University of Thessaloniki, who collected and studied the plant material, and to producers in Greek Macedonia, especially at Epanomi near Thessaloniki, who planted it and demonstrated its quality. Today it is one of the most highly regarded aromatic white grapes of Greece.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous vine of medium ripening, with medium-sized bunches. It adapts to warm climates and a variety of soils, although in very hot areas it tends to lose acidity if picked late, so the harvest date is decisive in keeping freshness. Protecting the must from oxidation is also important to preserve its perfume.

In the glass

It makes highly aromatic whites, with notes of jasmine and other white flowers, peach, apricot, pear, citrus, tropical fruit and herbs such as basil or mint. On the palate they have medium body, a soft texture and moderate acidity. Mostly young wines fermented in steel are made, although some producers use skin contact, lees ageing or barrel fermentation to give it more volume. It is often blended with Assyrtiko, which adds acidity and structure.

Where it is grown

Since its revival it has spread across much of mainland Greece: it is especially important in Greek Macedonia, it is grown in Attica, Thessaly and the Peloponnese, and it has returned to its area of origin in western Greece. It is almost always made as a dry white, usually labelled with the name of the variety, often under regional protected geographical indications. Outside Greece it is hardly grown, so it remains an essentially Greek variety.

At the table

It goes with Mediterranean cooking, herb salads, grilled fish, shellfish, lemony dishes and light Asian food. Serve at 8–11 °C.
