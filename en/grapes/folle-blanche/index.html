Folle Blanche

A very high-acid white grape, the historic variety of Cognac and Armagnac before phylloxera, which today makes Gros Plant du Pays Nantais and fine brandies in Armagnac.

Origin

Folle Blanche is an old variety from western France, bound up with the history of grape brandy. For centuries it was the main grape of the Charentes and Gascony for distilling Cognac and Armagnac, because it gave light wines, low in alcohol and very high in acidity, ideal for producing fine, aromatic spirits. Its name, which means ‘mad’, is traditionally explained by its vigour and abundant production.

Viticulture

After the phylloxera crisis at the end of the nineteenth century, French vineyards had to be replanted on American rootstocks. Once grafted, Folle Blanche became even more vigorous and productive, and its very compact bunches became extremely susceptible to grey rot. For that reason in Cognac it was almost entirely replaced by the hardier Ugni Blanc, the French name for Trebbiano. It remains a delicate variety today, needing careful viticulture and climates that favour good air circulation around the bunches.

In the glass

As wine, it makes dry, light, pale whites with very marked acidity, discreet aromas of lemon, green apple and white flowers, and mineral or saline notes. As with Muscadet, some Gros Plant is aged on its lees until bottling, which gives it a little more texture and a slight yeasty character. They are wines to drink young. Distilled, it gives delicate, floral and fruity spirits, prized for their finesse.

Where it is grown

In the Pays Nantais, at the mouth of the Loire, it is known as Gros Plant and gives its name to the Gros Plant du Pays Nantais appellation, which produces very fresh dry whites, neighbours of Muscadet. In Armagnac it survives in a small but valued proportion for spirits of great finesse, often distilled separately, and in Cognac it remains present on a marginal scale. Outside France it is very rarely grown.

At the table

Gros Plant is a classic partner for oysters, raw shellfish, mussels and simple fish from the Atlantic coast. Serve well chilled, at 7–9 °C. Folle Blanche Armagnacs are enjoyed at the end of a meal.
