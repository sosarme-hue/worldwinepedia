Savagnin

An ancient white grape of the Traminer family and the emblem of the Jura, where it makes Vin Jaune, aged for years under a veil of yeast, and dry whites with firm acidity.

Origin

Savagnin is one of the oldest European varieties. Genetic studies identify it with Traminer and have shown that it is related, as a parent or direct offspring, to numerous grapes of France and central Europe. Gewürztraminer is its aromatic, pink-skinned form. It should not be confused with Sauvignon Blanc, despite the similar name. Its emblematic territory is the French Jura, where it is also called Naturé.

Viticulture

It is an early-budding, late-ripening vine with small bunches and moderate yields. In the Jura it is planted mainly on grey and bluish marls, on well-exposed slopes. It keeps high acidity even when it reaches good ripeness, an essential quality for wines that undergo long ageing.

In the glass

In the Jura it makes two great styles. The ‘ouillé’ whites, that is, with the barrels topped up to prevent oxidation, are taut wines with notes of apple, citrus, fresh walnut and spice. Vin Jaune, by contrast, is aged in barrels that are not topped up for at least five years, under a veil of yeast similar to that of fino Sherry. The result is a dry, powerful and very long-lived wine, with aromas of walnut, curry, spice, toasted almond and apple, bottled in the traditional 62 cl clavelin. The Château-Chalon appellation produces only Vin Jaune. Savagnin also goes into Vin de Paille and Crémant du Jura.

Where it is grown

Besides the Jura appellations, such as Arbois, L’Étoile, Château-Chalon and Côtes du Jura, it is grown in Switzerland, where in the Valais it is known as Heida or Païen and makes high-altitude dry whites. Under the name Traminer it is found in central Europe. In Australia it has gained ground, partly because vineyards planted as Albariño turned out in fact to be Savagnin.

At the table

Vin Jaune is the classic partner for Comté cheese and for Bresse chicken with morels, and is served at about 14–16 °C; the ouillé whites go with freshwater fish and poultry.
