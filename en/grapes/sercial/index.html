White grape

Sercial

A white grape of extreme acidity that gives the driest style of Madeira, of great longevity; on the Portuguese mainland it is known as Esgana Cão.

Origin

Sercial is a Portuguese white grape that on the island of Madeira gives its name to the driest style of the island’s famous fortified wine. On the mainland it is known as Esgana Cão, literally “dog strangler”, a name that refers to its almost aggressive acidity. Together with Verdelho, Boal and Malvasia, it is one of the four traditional noble white varieties of Madeira.

Viticulture

It is a late-ripening vine with low, irregular yields, susceptible to powdery mildew and rot. On Madeira it is grown mainly in the island’s highest and coolest areas, on terraces (poios) of volcanic basalt soil, often on low pergolas called latadas. Even when fully ripe it keeps very high acidity and moderate sugar levels. Its area is small and has fallen considerably since phylloxera, when it was partly replaced by Tinta Negra.

In the glass

Sercial Madeira is the driest and lightest of the varietal styles: pale amber to golden, with aromas of citrus, orange peel, almond, walnut, spice and smoky or iodine notes, and a cutting acidity that balances the alcohol and oxidation. Like all Madeira it undergoes heating and oxidative ageing, either in estufas or slowly in casks in the lodges (the canteiro system). Thanks to that process and its acidity, the best Sercial can age for decades, even more than a century.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark area is the island of Madeira, within the Madeira DOP. On the mainland it is grown in small quantities as Esgana Cão in Bucelas, where it is blended with Arinto, and in the Douro, where it is used in some white Ports and still wines. It is practically absent outside Portugal.

At the table

Sercial Madeira is an excellent aperitif and goes well with consommés, soups, nuts, olives, shellfish and aged cheeses. Serve lightly chilled, at 10–12 °C.
