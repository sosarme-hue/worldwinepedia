Cinsault

A heat-tolerant Mediterranean red that makes light, fragrant wines, essential to Provence rosé and a parent of South Africa’s Pinotage.

Origin

Cinsault (also spelt Cinsaut) is an old variety from southern France, probably from the Languedoc or Provence. During the twentieth century it was widely planted in the French Mediterranean and in North Africa, especially Algeria and Morocco, for its productivity and drought resistance. In South Africa it was long known as Hermitage, and it was there, in 1925, that Professor Abraham Perold crossed it with Pinot Noir to create Pinotage.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous and very productive vine that copes well with heat and drought and adapts to poor soils. If yields are not controlled the wines are dilute, but old bush vines produce grapes of high quality. Its berries are large, thin-skinned and fleshy, which is why it has also been used as a table grape. It is susceptible to some fungal diseases and to mites.

In the glass

It makes lightly coloured wines with light body, soft tannins and moderate acidity, with aromas of strawberry, raspberry, cherry, flowers and gentle spice. That delicacy makes it ideal for fresh, fragrant rosés and as a blending partner that adds perfume and finesse to more powerful varieties such as Grenache, Syrah and Carignan. Old vines can give light but very expressive reds.

Where it is grown

In France it is found in Languedoc-Roussillon, Provence and the southern Rhône, and it is one of the varieties permitted in Châteauneuf-du-Pape. In Provence it plays a leading role in rosé. In Lebanon it is part of many blends from the Bekaa Valley. South Africa still has old vineyards that have inspired renewed interest in the variety, and in Chile’s Itata Valley there are old dry-farmed plantings. It is also grown in Australia and California.

At the table

Its rosés suit Mediterranean cooking, salads, grilled fish and dishes with olive oil and herbs; its light reds go with cured meats and white meats. Serve rosés at 8–10 °C and reds at 13–15 °C.
