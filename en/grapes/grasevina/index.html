Graševina (Welschriesling)

A white variety widely grown in central and eastern Europe and the most planted in Croatia. Despite its names it is not related to Riesling; it gives fresh, fruity whites and great botrytised sweet wines.

Origin

The origin of this variety remains unclear. Its German name, Welschriesling, contains the adjective welsch, which in older German referred to anything foreign or Latin, fuelling theories of an Italian or French origin, although there is no conclusive evidence. Most specialists place it somewhere in central Europe or the Danube basin, where it has been grown for centuries. What is clear is that, despite its names (Welschriesling, Riesling Italico, Laški Rizling, Olaszrizling), it is not related to Rhine Riesling. In Croatia it is called Graševina and is the country’s most widely planted variety.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive, late-ripening vine that retains its acidity well and adapts to continental climates with long autumns. It needs soils that are not too dry and is susceptible to some fungal diseases. At high yields it gives light, neutral wines, which explains its reputation as a grape for everyday wine; with controlled yields and good slopes, by contrast, it produces whites of character. Its thin skin and late ripening make it suitable for noble rot in humid areas such as the surroundings of Lake Neusiedl in Austria.

In the glass

The dry wines are pale yellow with green highlights, light to medium-bodied with fresh acidity. Their aromas recall green apple, pear, citrus, meadow flowers and herbs, with a very characteristic slightly bitter almond finish. Late-harvest versions and botrytised wines, such as the Trockenbeerenauslese of Austria’s Burgenland or Croatian ice wines and selected late harvests, offer notes of honey, apricot, candied fruit and spice, supported by acidity.

Where it is grown

In Croatia it is the leading white, above all in Slavonia and the Danube region, with areas such as Kutjevo. In Slovenia, under the name Laški Rizling, it is the most widespread white in the eastern Podravje region. In Austria it is grown mainly in Burgenland and Styria; in Hungary, as Olaszrizling, around Lake Balaton and in other regions. It is also found in Romania, Serbia and northern Italy, where it is known as Riesling Italico.

At the table

The dry wines go with river fish, poultry, pork, paprika-based dishes and fresh cheeses; the sweet wines with foie gras, blue cheeses and fruit desserts. Serve the dry wines at 8-10 °C.
