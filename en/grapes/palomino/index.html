White grape

Palomino

The grape of sherry: neutral as a young wine, it is transformed by ageing under flor or oxidative ageing into finos, manzanillas, amontillados and olorosos.

Origin

Palomino is an Andalusian variety, linked for centuries to the area of Jerez de la Frontera, Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María. The form that dominates vineyards today is Palomino Fino, which displaced other variants after phylloxera. In the Canary Islands it is known as Listán Blanco, and in South Africa it was traditionally called Fransdruif.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine that ripens mid-season and is well adapted to heat and drought. In the Jerez area it is grown above all on albariza, a white soil rich in calcium carbonate that stores winter rainfall and releases it during the summer. It is susceptible to downy mildew and some pests.

In the glass

Its must is low in acidity and neutral in aroma, which makes it of little interest for young wines but ideal for ageing. In Jerez it is fermented to dryness and fortified with grape spirit. Aged under flor, a layer of yeast that protects the wine from oxygen, it gives fino and, in Sanlúcar, manzanilla: pale, dry, pungent wines with notes of almond, yeast, bread and salinity. With oxidative ageing it gives oloroso, amber to mahogany in colour, with aromas of walnut, leather and spice; amontillado and palo cortado combine both kinds of ageing. The wines are traditionally aged in the criaderas and solera system. Still whites are also made, with growing attention to individual vineyards in recent years.

Where it is grown

Its main area is the Jerez-Xérès-Sherry and Manzanilla-Sanlúcar de Barrameda D.O., where it is the dominant variety, and it is also found in Condado de Huelva, Montilla-Moriles and Málaga. As Listán Blanco it is the most planted white grape in the Canary Islands. It is also grown in Galicia (Valdeorras, Ribeiro, Monterrei), on the northern plateau and in Portugal. Beyond the peninsula there are plantings in South Africa, Australia and California, historically used partly for fortified wines and distillation.
