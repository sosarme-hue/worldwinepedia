Airén

A drought-resistant white grape from La Mancha, one of the most widely planted varieties in the world, traditionally used for table wine and distillation.

Origin

Airén is native to central Spain and linked above all to La Mancha, although it has also been grown in Andalusia under the name Lairén. For much of the twentieth century it was the wine grape with the largest vineyard area in the world, thanks to the vast extent of La Mancha’s vineyards, although that area has shrunk in recent decades through grubbing-up and conversion to other varieties.

Viticulture

Its main virtue is hardiness: it copes very well with drought, intense summer heat and the cold of the plateau’s continental winters. It is traditionally grown as dry-farmed bush vines with wide spacing, on poor limestone and clay soils. It is productive and relatively resistant to diseases such as powdery mildew, helped by the dry climate.

In the glass

Its wines are pale, light-bodied, with moderate acidity and a fairly neutral aroma. For a long time it went into everyday wines and, to a large extent, into the distillation of grape spirit and of base spirits for brandy, including Brandy de Jerez. With temperature-controlled fermentation and well-timed harvesting, fresh, fruity young whites are made, with notes of apple, banana, citrus and white flowers. Some producers work with old vines to obtain wines of more character.

Where it is grown

It is concentrated in Castilla-La Mancha, especially in the La Mancha and Valdepeñas D.O.s, where it is the most planted variety, and in neighbouring areas such as Ribera del Júcar and Manchuela, as well as Madrid and Murcia. As Lairén it has been grown in Andalusia, in Córdoba and Málaga. Outside Spain it is practically not grown.

At the table

Young Airén whites are simple, refreshing wines, suitable as an aperitif and with salads, vegetables, fish and tapas. They are served well chilled, at 6–8 °C, and are best drunk within the year.
