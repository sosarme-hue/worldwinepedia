Trebbiano

The name of a family of Italian white grapes; the most widespread, Trebbiano Toscano, is France’s Ugni Blanc, the base of Cognac and Armagnac.

Origin

In Italy the name Trebbiano covers several different white varieties, not always related to each other. The most important is Trebbiano Toscano, probably native to central Italy and grown for centuries. This grape spread to France, where it is called Ugni Blanc and, in the Cognac region, also Saint-Émilion. Other members of the family include Trebbiano Romagnolo, Trebbiano Spoletino and Trebbiano Abruzzese, whereas the so-called Trebbiano di Soave or di Lugana has turned out to be genetically identical to Verdicchio.

Viticulture

Trebbiano Toscano is a very vigorous, productive vine that buds late, which helps it avoid spring frost, and also ripens late. It gives large bunches and high yields and keeps high acidity even in warm climates. These qualities explain why it is one of the most widely planted white grapes in the world, although its aromatic profile is neutral unless yields are limited.

In the glass

It makes pale, light-bodied wines with fresh acidity and moderate alcohol, with discreet aromas of lemon, apple, pear, almond and white flowers. Its neutrality and acidity make it ideal for distillation: in Cognac and Armagnac it is used for low-alcohol, high-acid base wines that are distilled and aged in oak. Some producers in Abruzzo and Umbria make more complex, age-worthy wines from varieties of the family.

Where it is grown

In Italy it is found across almost all of the centre of the country: Tuscany, Umbria, Lazio, Emilia-Romagna, Abruzzo and the Marche. It was part of the traditional Chianti recipe, which allowed white grapes, and is used in Tuscan Vin Santo, Orvieto, Frascati and the Trebbiano d’Abruzzo DOC, among other appellations. In France, as Ugni Blanc, it is the dominant grape of Cognac and Armagnac and also makes dry whites in Gascony and Provence. In Portugal it is known as Talia, and it is also grown in Argentina and Australia.

At the table

Simple Trebbiano whites go with aperitifs, fish, salads and vegetable dishes. Serve at 8–10 °C.
