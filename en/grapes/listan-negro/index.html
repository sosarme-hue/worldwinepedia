Listán Negro

The main red grape of the Canary Islands, grown on volcanic soils in often pre-phylloxera vineyards, giving fresh, spicy, red-fruited wines.

Origin

Listán Negro is the most characteristic red variety of the Canary Islands, where it has been grown for centuries. It should not be confused with Listán Prieto, a different Canarian variety, which travelled to the Americas with the colonists and is known there as País or Mission. Nor is it a colour variant of Listán Blanco, the Canarian name for Palomino.

Viticulture

Phylloxera never reached the Canary Islands, so many vines are grown ungrafted and some vineyards are very old. Listán Negro grows on volcanic soils, from sea level up to considerable altitudes, under the influence of the trade winds. In the Orotava Valley on Tenerife, the traditional cordón trenzado (braided cordon) system survives, in which the canes are woven together into long cordons. It is a vigorous vine with regular yields.

In the glass

It gives medium-coloured reds, light- to medium-bodied, with fresh acidity and moderate tannins. The aromas combine red fruit (raspberry, cherry), notes of pepper and herbs and a character often described as volcanic or smoky. It is frequently made by carbonic maceration to produce very fruity young reds, but also in oak-aged wines and rosés. It is often blended with Negramoll, Tintilla or Listán Prieto.

Where it is grown

It is the most planted red grape in the archipelago and is found in all of its denominations of origin, especially those of Tenerife: Tacoronte-Acentejo, Valle de La Orotava, Ycoden-Daute-Isora, Valle de Güímar and Abona. It is also grown on Lanzarote, La Palma, Gran Canaria, El Hierro and La Gomera, and is covered by the Islas Canarias D.O.P. Outside the Canaries it is practically not grown at all.

In recent decades, several Tenerife producers have focused on single-plot and old-vine wines that have drawn international attention to volcanic Canary Island reds.

At the table

Young Listán Negro reds suit Canarian cooking well: goat and black pork, rabbit in salmorejo, papas with mojo sauce and island cheeses. Being light, they can be served slightly cool, around 14–15 °C.
