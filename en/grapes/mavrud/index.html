Black grape

Mavrud

Bulgaria’s most emblematic native red grape, linked to the Thracian Valley and especially to Asenovgrad. Late-ripening, it gives dark, acidic and tannic wines well suited to ageing.

Origin

Mavrud is a native red variety of southern Bulgaria, grown for centuries in the Thracian Valley and associated above all with the town of Asenovgrad, at the foot of the Rhodopes, and with the area around Plovdiv. Its name comes from the Greek word mavro, “black”, referring to the dark colour of its grapes and wines. A Bulgarian folk legend tells that when Khan Krum ordered the vineyards to be uprooted in the ninth century, the bravery of a young man named Mavrud, attributed to the wine he had drunk, persuaded the ruler to spare this vine, although it is a traditional tale with no documentary basis.

Viticulture

It is a late-budding, late-ripening vine that needs long, warm summers and dry autumns to complete its cycle, conditions it finds in the Thracian Valley. Its bunches and berries are small and thick-skinned, and its yields are modest. In cool years it can struggle to ripen its tannins. During the socialist era it lost ground to more productive international varieties, but since the late twentieth century many wineries have replanted it and bottled it as a quality varietal.

In the glass

It gives wines of very intense red colour, medium- to full-bodied, with marked acidity and abundant tannins, sometimes slightly rustic when young. Its aromas recall blackberry, plum, black cherry and mulberry, with notes of violet, spice, pepper and, with barrel ageing, tobacco, cocoa and leather. Thanks to its acidity and structure it ages well in bottle. It is made as a varietal, with or without oak ageing, and also blended with varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or Syrah, to which it brings freshness and a local character.

Where it is grown

It is grown almost exclusively in Bulgaria, in the Thracian Valley: around Asenovgrad, Plovdiv, Perushtitsa, Pazardzhik and Stara Zagora, and in other areas in the south of the country. Asenovgrad is its benchmark appellation. Outside Bulgaria it is very rarely found. Together with Melnik, it is one of the varieties that best represent the identity of Bulgarian wine.
