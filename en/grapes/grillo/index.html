Grillo

A white grape from western Sicily, a cross of Catarratto and Muscat of Alexandria, once a key grape of Marsala and now used above all for fresh, savoury dry whites.

Origin

Grillo is a white grape from Sicily, particularly associated with the province of Trapani and the Marsala area at the western tip of the island. DNA analysis has confirmed that it is a cross between Catarratto Bianco and Muscat of Alexandria, known in Sicily as Zibibbo. The cross is attributed to the Sicilian ampelographer Antonio Mendola, who is said to have bred it in the 19th century. After phylloxera it was widely planted around Marsala, because it easily reached the high sugar levels sought by producers of this fortified wine. It later lost ground to more productive varieties, and in recent decades it has undergone a strong revival as a grape for dry whites.

Viticulture

It is a vine well adapted to the heat and drought of the Sicilian summer, a valuable quality in the west of the island. It accumulates sugar quickly, so for fresh dry wines the harvest date is decisive: picked early it retains acidity and aroma, while a later harvest gives more powerful, alcoholic wines. It is grown mainly on the hills and plains near the coast, often on clay, limestone or sandy soils, and benefits from sea breezes.

In the glass

As a dry wine it gives straw-yellow whites, medium-bodied, fresh and savoury. Its aromas recall citrus, grapefruit, apple and white flowers, with notes of Mediterranean herbs, tropical fruit, almond and often a saline touch. Versions from riper harvests or aged on lees are broader and more structured. In Marsala it contributes body and alcohol and is usually combined with Catarratto and Inzolia.

Where it is grown

It is almost exclusive to Sicily. It is made as a single-variety wine under the Sicilia DOC and in other denominations in the west of the island, and it remains one of the grapes authorised in the Marsala DOC. Outside Sicily its presence is minimal.

At the table

It goes with grilled fish, shellfish, fish couscous, pasta with sardines and vegetables, and Sicilian cooking in general. Serve at 8–10 °C.
