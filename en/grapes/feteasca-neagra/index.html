Black grape

Fetească Neagră

The great native red grape of Romania and Moldova, whose name means “black maiden”. It gives deeply coloured reds with aromas of plum, sour cherry and spice and soft tannins.

Origin

Fetească Neagră is an old variety from the historic region of Moldavia, now divided between north-eastern Romania and the Republic of Moldova. Its name derives from fată, “girl”, and is usually translated as “black maiden”. Although it shares its name with Fetească Albă and Fetească Regală, which are white, no direct relationship with them has been demonstrated. During the twentieth century its area declined in favour of French varieties, which were more productive and easier to sell, but since the end of that century producers in both countries have revived it as a mark of identity.

Viticulture

It is a vine of medium vigour with medium to late ripening, needing warm, well-exposed plots to reach good phenolic ripeness. It withstands winter cold and drought reasonably well but is susceptible to some fungal diseases, and if yields are pushed it gives light, inexpressive wines. Its quality depends greatly on site: the slopes of Dealu Mare in Romania and some areas in the south of the Republic of Moldova are among the most highly regarded. It often accumulates a good deal of sugar, so its wines can be high in alcohol.

In the glass

It gives deep ruby or purplish reds, medium- to full-bodied, with generally soft tannins and moderate acidity. Its aroma recalls ripe plum, sour cherry, blackberry and blackcurrant, with spicy notes, hints of raisins or prunes and, at times, a smoky or cocoa touch. Barrel ageing, common for top wines, adds structure and notes of vanilla and toast. It is bottled as a varietal or blended with Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or other varieties, to which it contributes fruit and softness. Some rosés are also made from it.

Where it is grown

It is grown mainly in Romania, in areas such as Dealu Mare, Drăgășani, Dobrogea and the hills of Romanian Moldavia, and in the Republic of Moldova, especially in the south and centre of the country. Outside these two countries its presence is very limited.

At the table

It pairs with roast meats, spiced stews, cured sausages, traditional Romanian dishes such as sarmale (stuffed cabbage leaves) and semi-hard cheeses. Serve at 16-18 °C.
