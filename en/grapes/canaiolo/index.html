Canaiolo

A traditional red grape of Tuscany, Sangiovese’s historic partner in Chianti, which brings fruit, softness and colour to blends.

Origin

Canaiolo, or Canaiolo Nero, is a red grape from central Italy, grown in Tuscany for centuries. For a long time it was one of the region’s most important grapes, in some areas even more so than Sangiovese. In the 19th century, the Chianti formula attributed to Baron Bettino Ricasoli, which based the wine on Sangiovese, gave it a supporting role to soften the blend. Over the 20th century, however, its plantings shrank considerably, as Chianti moved towards a higher proportion of Sangiovese or towards international varieties. It should not be confused with Canaiolo Bianco, a different white grape.

Viticulture

It is a vine of moderate vigour and mid-season ripening, with medium-sized bunches and fairly tough-skinned berries. It was traditionally considered well suited to drying, which made it useful for governo, the old Tuscan technique of adding must from dried grapes to the wine to trigger a second fermentation. It adapts to the hillside soils of central Tuscany, with clays, marls and galestro, where it is usually planted alongside Sangiovese.

In the glass

It gives ruby reds, light to medium in body, with soft tannins and moderate acidity. Its aromas recall red cherry, raspberry, plum and violet, with notes of herbs and gentle spice. In blends it adds fruit, roundness and colour, and tempers the acidity and austerity of Sangiovese. Single-variety wines are uncommon, although some producers have begun to bottle it on its own to show its character.

Where it is grown

It is grown mainly in Tuscany, where it is permitted as a complementary variety in Chianti, Chianti Classico, Vino Nobile di Montepulciano and other denominations. It is also found in Umbria, particularly in the Torgiano area, and to a lesser extent in other regions of central Italy. Outside Italy its presence is negligible.

At the table

It goes well with Tuscan cured meats, pasta with ragù, roast and grilled meat, pulses and semi-mature cheeses. Serve at 15–17 °C.
