White grape

Silvaner

A Central European white grape with a discreet, earthy profile, emblematic of Franken and found in Rheinhessen, Alsace and Alto Adige, that clearly expresses limestone soils.

Origin

Silvaner comes from Central Europe. DNA analysis has shown that it is a natural cross between Savagnin (Traminer) and Österreichisch Weiss, an old, almost vanished Austrian variety, which points to an origin in the Danube basin. From there it reached Germany, where tradition places its first documented planting in Franken, in the village of Castell, in the mid-seventeenth century. For much of the twentieth century it was one of Germany’s most widely planted grapes, until Müller-Thurgau and other varieties took over much of its ground.

Viticulture

It buds early, making it susceptible to spring frost, and ripens mid-season, earlier than Riesling. It is productive: high yields give neutral, dilute wines, while moderate crops on good soils produce whites of character. It prefers limestone and marl soils and is credited with expressing the character of the site clearly.

In the glass

It gives pale whites, medium-bodied, with moderate to lively acidity and subtle rather than exuberant aromas: green apple, pear, melon, citrus, hay, herbs and earthy or mineral notes. Its aromatic restraint is precisely what sets it apart and makes it so valued at the table. Most wines are dry, although late-harvest sweet wines also exist. The best, from hillside vineyards, can develop for several years in bottle.

Where it is grown

In Germany it is the signature variety of Franken, where it grows mainly on shell limestone (Muschelkalk) and Keuper soils along the Main, and where its wines are traditionally bottled in the distinctive flattened Bocksbeutel. Rheinhessen has the largest planted area in the country, and it is also found in the Pfalz. In Alsace, as Sylvaner, it is a traditional variety, and the Zotzenberg grand cru is the only one in the region that permits it. In Italy it is grown in Alto Adige, especially the Valle Isarco (Eisacktal), and in Switzerland, in the Valais, it is called Johannisberg.

At the table

It is a classic partner for white asparagus, a springtime favourite in Franken, and goes well with freshwater fish, vegetables, salads, white meat and fresh cheeses. It is served at 8–11 °C.
