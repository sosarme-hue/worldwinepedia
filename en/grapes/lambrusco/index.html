Lambrusco

A family of red grapes from Emilia-Romagna that gives the famous sparkling and semi-sparkling reds, from dry to sweet, fresh, fruity and lively in acidity.

Origin

Lambrusco is not a single grape but a family of red varieties native to Emilia-Romagna and southern Lombardy, in the Po Valley. Its name comes from the Latin labrusca, the term classical authors used for the wild vine that grew along the edges of fields. The main ones are Lambrusco Salamino, Lambrusco Grasparossa, Lambrusco di Sorbara, Lambrusco Maestri and Lambrusco Marani; genetic studies have confirmed that they are distinct varieties rather than mere clones.

Viticulture

They are generally vigorous, productive vines, traditionally trained high on the fertile Po plain, although they are also grown in the hills. Sorbara has delicate flowering and is prone to coulure, so it is often planted alongside Lambrusco Salamino as a pollinator. Grasparossa, grown in the hills of Castelvetro, owes its name to the red colour its stalks take on in autumn.

In the glass

Lambrusco wines are reds or rosés with bubbles, frizzante or spumante, with lively acidity and moderate alcohol. Sorbara makes pale, very aromatic wines with notes of violet, strawberry and raspberry; Grasparossa darker, more tannic and structured wines with blackberry and black cherry; Salamino sits in between, with abundant red fruit. The second fermentation usually takes place in a sealed tank, although there are also bottle-fermented versions made by the traditional or ancestral methods. Dry, off-dry and sweet styles are made.

Where it is grown

Its main areas are in the provinces of Modena and Reggio Emilia, with DOCs such as Lambrusco di Sorbara, Lambrusco Grasparossa di Castelvetro, Lambrusco Salamino di Santa Croce, Modena and Reggiano, and in the province of Mantua in Lombardy. Lambrusco Maestri has also spread beyond Italy, notably to Argentina, and there are small plantings in Australia.

At the table

Its acidity and bubbles balance the richness of Emilian food: cured meats such as mortadella and culatello, stuffed pasta, lasagne, cotechino and Parmigiano Reggiano. Serve chilled, at 10–14 °C depending on style.
