Syrah (Shiraz)

A red grape from the northern Rhône (Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie) that, under the name Shiraz, became Australia’s signature variety.

Origin

Despite legends linking it to the Persian city of Shiraz or to Syracuse, DNA analysis published in the late 1990s showed that Syrah comes from the Rhône Valley and is the result of a natural cross between Dureza, a red variety from the Ardèche, and Mondeuse Blanche, from Savoie.

Viticulture

It buds and ripens mid-season and is vigorous, needing yield control to give concentrated wines. It adapts both to cool granite slopes and to warm climates, although it loses acidity if overripe. It is sensitive to extreme drought and to chlorosis on limestone soils, and its shoots are fragile in the wind.

In the glass

Style varies greatly with climate. In cooler areas such as the northern Rhône it gives medium- to full-bodied wines with fine tannins and aromas of blackberry, violet, black olive, black pepper and smoky or meaty notes. In warm climates such as Australia’s Barossa the wines are more opulent and alcoholic, with ripe dark fruit, chocolate, liquorice and sweet spice. It ages well and is often blended with Grenache and Mourvèdre, or co-fermented with a small proportion of white Viognier, as is traditional in Côte-Rôtie.

Where it is grown

In France its benchmark areas are the northern Rhône appellations (Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie, Cornas, Saint-Joseph, Crozes-Hermitage); it is also widespread in the southern Rhône and Languedoc. Australia, where it is called Shiraz, is the other great centre, with Barossa, McLaren Vale, the Hunter Valley and the Clare Valley. It is also grown in South Africa, the United States (California, Washington), Chile, Argentina, New Zealand (Hawke’s Bay) and Italy (Tuscany, Sicily). In Spain it has spread mainly in Castilla-La Mancha, Jumilla, Catalonia and Aragon.

At the table

Spicy cool-climate Syrahs go well with grilled meat, herb-crusted lamb and game; the more opulent warm-climate Shiraz calls for barbecue and spiced dishes. They are served at 16–18 °C.
