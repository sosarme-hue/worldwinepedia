Baga

The emblematic red grape of Bairrada, with firm tannins and high acidity, making austere long-lived reds as well as rosés and sparkling wines.

Origin

Baga is a traditional variety of north-central Portugal, above all Bairrada, the region between the Atlantic coast and the Buçaco and Caramulo ranges, north of Coimbra. It is also grown in the Dão and other parts of the Centro region. Its name simply means “berry”. During the twentieth century it was Bairrada’s dominant grape, and in recent decades some local producers have helped restore its reputation.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive, late-ripening vine with compact bunches and small, thick-skinned berries. That compactness makes it prone to grey rot, a significant problem in Bairrada’s Atlantic climate, where autumn rains can coincide with the harvest. Yield control and the choice of picking date are therefore decisive. It prefers clay-limestone soils, considered the region’s best for this variety.

In the glass

When fully ripe it makes deeply coloured, medium-bodied reds with very firm tannins and high acidity, with aromas of plum, sour cherry, blackcurrant, herbs, tobacco and smoky or earthy notes. In youth they can be hard and austere, but the best develop for many years in bottle, gaining complexity and finesse. Its acidity also makes it well suited to rosés and to traditional-method sparkling wines, a historic speciality of Bairrada.

Where it is grown

Its main area is the Bairrada DOC, where it remains the most characteristic red grape, although the regional rules now allow many other varieties. It is also found in the Dão and elsewhere in the Centro region, such as the Beiras. It is practically unknown outside Portugal.

At the table

Bairrada’s signature dish, roast suckling pig (leitão), is traditionally served with local sparkling wines or reds, whose acidity cuts through the fat. The reds also suit red meat, game and aged cheeses. Serve reds at 15–17 °C and sparkling wines at 6–8 °C. Mature reds benefit from being decanted an hour or so before serving.
