Avesso

A Vinho Verde white grape tied to the Baião subregion, next to the Douro, which makes wines with more body and alcohol than typical Vinho Verde, with stone fruit, citrus and fresh acidity.

Origin

Avesso is a white variety native to northern Portugal, closely associated with the Baião subregion, at the south-eastern end of the Vinho Verde region, on the banks of the Douro river and on the border with the Douro wine region. It has traditionally been grown there, and most of its area is still concentrated there. Its name means “reverse” or “opposite” in Portuguese and is often linked to its character, different from that of the other Vinho Verde grapes, although there is no reliable explanation.

Viticulture

Baião is the warmest and driest part of Vinho Verde: it is shielded from Atlantic influence by the inland mountains and has hotter summers than the coast. Avesso adapts well to these conditions and to the granite soils of the area. It ripens mid to late season and benefits from warmth to complete its cycle, and its grapes reach higher potential alcohol than other varieties of the region without losing fresh acidity.

In the glass

Its wines depart from the light, tart and sometimes slightly spritzy style associated with Vinho Verde. They are medium- to full-bodied whites with a rich texture and somewhat higher alcohol, with aromas of stone fruit (peach, apricot), citrus, apple, floral notes and sometimes nuts. Their balanced acidity gives freshness and some ability to develop in bottle. Varietal wines are made, some aged on lees or in oak, and it is also blended with Arinto, Loureiro or Alvarinho.

Where it is grown

Almost all of it is grown in the Baião subregion and neighbouring parts of Vinho Verde, such as Amarante and Paiva. Outside this area it is very rare. Although it remains a minor variety, interest in fuller-bodied whites and native grapes has made its wines known beyond the region.

At the table

Its whites go with baked fish, salt cod, shellfish, white meat and semi-mature cheeses. Serve at 9–11 °C.
