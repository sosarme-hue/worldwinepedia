Brancellao

A pale, aromatically delicate Galician red grape, the same variety as Portugal’s Alvarelhão, revived in Ribeira Sacra and Ribeiro for light, floral and fresh reds.

Origin

Brancellao is a traditional red variety of Galicia, long grown along the banks of the Miño and the Sil. DNA analysis has confirmed that it is the same grape as Alvarelhão from northern Portugal, known by different names on either side of the border. Its Galician name is most commonly explained as a reference to the light hue of its wines. For much of the twentieth century it was reduced to scattered vines in old vineyards, interplanted with other varieties, until interest in native grapes led to its revival.

Viticulture

It is a low- and irregular-yielding vine, prone to fungal disease, especially downy and powdery mildew, which partly explains why it was abandoned. Its bunches are medium-sized and its thin-skinned berries give musts with little colour. It adapts well to the slate and granite slopes of the Sil and Miño canyons, where it is grown on terraces, and to the granite soils of Ribeiro. It ripens mid-season and keeps a fresh acidity.

In the glass

Its wines are pale ruby, light-bodied, with soft tannins and moderate alcohol. The aroma, delicate and highly expressive, combines red fruit such as strawberry and cherry with floral notes of rose and violet and hints of herbs and spice. It is often blended with Mencía, to which it brings finesse and perfume, or with Caíño, Sousón and Merenzao, but small-volume varietal wines are also made, notable for their transparency.

Where it is grown

In Spain it is found mainly in Ribeira Sacra and Ribeiro, where it is one of the preferred red varieties, and to a lesser extent in other parts of Galicia. In Portugal, as Alvarelhão, it is grown in the Minho, Trás-os-Montes, the Douro, where it traditionally went into some Ports and rosé wines, and the Dão. Everywhere its area is small.

At the table

Its lightness makes it a good partner for white meat, charcuterie, mushrooms, rice dishes and full-flavoured fish. Serve it slightly cool, at 13–15 °C.
