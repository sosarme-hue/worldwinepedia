Alfrocheiro

A Portuguese red grape with deep colour, lively red fruit and good acidity, one of the classic varieties of the Dão; in Spain it is grown under other names, such as Bruñal in Arribes and Baboso Negro in the Canary Islands.

Origin

Alfrocheiro, also called Alfrocheiro Preto, is a Portuguese red variety associated above all with the Dão region, where it is part of the traditional mix of varieties alongside Touriga Nacional, Tinta Roriz and Jaen. Its exact origin is unclear. DNA analysis has shown that it is the same variety as several Spanish vines known by local names, among them Bruñal in Arribes and Baboso Negro in the Canary Islands, and that it has been a parent of other Iberian varieties.

Viticulture

It is a vine of moderate vigour and yield, with small to medium bunches and relatively thin-skinned berries. It ripens early to mid-season and retains good acidity, which is useful in warm climates. It is susceptible to powdery mildew and rot, so it needs care in wet years. In the Dão it adapts to the granite soils of the plateau, at some altitude and sheltered by the mountain ranges that surround the region, and in the south it has been planted in the Alentejo for its ability to keep freshness in the heat.

In the glass

It makes deeply coloured wines with fine tannins and lively acidity, and an aroma marked by ripe red and black fruit: strawberry, raspberry, blackberry and cherry, sometimes with spicy and floral notes. Its balance allows it to give both young, fruity wines and reds with some ageing potential. In the Dão it is used mainly in blends, to which it adds colour, fruit and freshness, although varietal wines appreciated for their vivacity are also made.

Where it is grown

Its main area is the Dão, and it is also found in the Alentejo, the Tejo, Bairrada and other Portuguese regions. In Spain, as Bruñal, it is a minor and highly regarded variety in the Arribes DO, on the Portuguese border, and as Baboso Negro it is grown in small quantities on several of the Canary Islands. Everywhere its area is modest.

At the table

Its reds go with roast meat, kid, charcuterie, mushrooms and firm cheeses. Serve at 15–17 °C.
