Mencía

An Atlantic-influenced red from Bierzo and inland Galicia, giving fresh, aromatic, red-fruited wines; in Portugal it is called Jaen.

Origin

Mencía is a variety from the north-west of the Iberian Peninsula. For a long time it was thought to be related to Cabernet Franc, a hypothesis that DNA analysis has ruled out. It has, however, been shown to be identical to Portugal’s Jaen from the Dão; both regions claim it as their own and the question remains unresolved.

Viticulture

It buds and ripens early to mid-season, with small to medium bunches. It does well on slate and granite soils and on slopes, often on very steep terraces, as in the Ribeira Sacra. It is susceptible to downy and powdery mildew and to botrytis, which matters in a humid climate like Galicia’s. Its wines can lose acidity if the grapes become overripe.

In the glass

It produces purple-red wines, light- to medium-bodied, with lively acidity and fine tannins. It is very aromatic: fresh red and black fruit (raspberry, cherry, redcurrant, blackberry), floral violet notes, herbal hints and a mineral character associated with slate. It was traditionally made to be drunk young, but old vines and careful winemaking have produced wines with structure and ageing potential.

Where it is grown

Its main area is Bierzo, in León, where it is the dominant variety. In Galicia it is the leading red grape of the Ribeira Sacra, Valdeorras and Monterrei, and it is also found in Ribeiro. There are plantings in Asturias (Cangas) and in Arribes, in the province of Salamanca. In Portugal, as Jaen, it is grown mainly in the Dão, where it goes into red blends alongside Touriga Nacional.

In Bierzo, the denomination has adopted a classification by origin inspired by the Burgundian model, which distinguishes regional wines, village wines, paraje (named-site) wines and classified vineyards, many of them made from old hillside Mencía vines.

At the table

Its freshness and fine tannins make Mencía a good partner for north-western Spanish cooking: botillo sausage, octopus, empanadas, small game and mushrooms. Young wines can be served slightly cool, around 14–15 °C.
