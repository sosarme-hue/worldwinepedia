White grape

Encruzado

The great white grape of the Dão, with notable structure and balance, well suited to barrel fermentation and lees ageing and able to age for many years.

Origin

Encruzado is a white variety native to the Dão, the mountainous region of north-central Portugal surrounded by the Estrela, Caramulo and Buçaco ranges. Its cultivation has hardly spread beyond this area, and for a long time it was just one of the many white grapes planted together in traditional field blends. Since the late twentieth century, research into local varieties and the production of single-varietal wines have established it as one of Portugal’s great white grapes.

Viticulture

It is a vine of medium vigour, moderate yields and medium ripening. It adapts well to the poor, sandy granite soils of the Dão, at altitudes of roughly 400 to 700 metres, where the climate combines cold, wet winters with hot, dry summers. It is susceptible to powdery and downy mildew, and its juice oxidises easily, so it needs careful handling in the winery.

In the glass

It makes straw-yellow whites, medium- to full-bodied with balanced acidity, without the overtly aromatic character of some other varieties. Its aromas recall citrus, pear, peach, white flowers, resin and hazelnut, with a mineral note often attributed to the granite soils. It takes well to barrel fermentation and lees ageing, which add texture and complexity without masking the fruit; its structure and balance have often prompted comparisons with white Burgundy. Over time it develops notes of honey, nuts and toast.

Where it is grown

Practically all of it is in the Dão DOC, where it is made both on its own and blended with other white grapes of the region, such as Malvasia Fina, Bical and Cerceal-Branco. It is very limited in other Portuguese regions and almost unknown abroad.

At the table

It suits fish in sauce, baked salt cod, poultry, white meats, mushrooms and soft cheeses such as Serra da Estrela. Serve at 10–12 °C. Older bottles are best served slightly warmer to reveal their complexity.
