Primitivo

A red grape from Puglia, genetically identical to California’s Zinfandel and of Croatian origin, giving powerful wines with very ripe fruit and high alcohol.

Origin

For a long time the origin of Primitivo and of California’s Zinfandel was a mystery. From the 1990s, DNA analysis showed that they are the same variety, and in 2001 a team led by the researcher Carole Meredith identified it with an old Croatian grape from the Dalmatian coast, Crljenak Kaštelanski, historically known as Tribidrag. In Puglia it has been recorded since the eighteenth century; its name, “the first one”, refers to its early ripening.

Viticulture

It buds and ripens early but unevenly: green, ripe and raisined berries can sit side by side on the same bunch. As a result it accumulates sugar easily and its wines often reach high alcohol levels. In Puglia it is traditionally bush-trained on red clay and limestone soils in a hot, dry Mediterranean climate. It is prone to rot when it rains at harvest and can give a second crop on secondary shoots.

In the glass

It makes dark ruby wines, full-bodied, with soft tannins and moderate acidity, and aromas of blackberry, ripe plum, black cherry, jam, dried fig, sweet spice and pepper. They often exceed 14% abv. It can be made in young, fruity styles, as oak-aged reds or as naturally sweet wines from overripe grapes. In California, as Zinfandel, it is also used for slightly sweet rosés known as White Zinfandel.

Where it is grown

In Italy its main area is Puglia, with the Primitivo di Manduria DOC, the Primitivo di Manduria Dolce Naturale DOCG and the Gioia del Colle DOC, as well as many IGT Puglia wines. As Zinfandel it is one of California’s signature reds, with old vineyards in areas such as Sonoma, Lodi and Paso Robles. In Croatia it is being revived under the names Tribidrag and Crljenak Kaštelanski. It is also grown in Australia and South Africa.

At the table

It suits chargrilled meat, barbecue, spiced stews, pasta with rich sauces and aged cheeses. Sweet versions pair with dark chocolate and blue cheeses. Serve dry wines at 16–18 °C.
