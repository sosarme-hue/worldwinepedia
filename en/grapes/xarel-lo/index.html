White grape

Xarel·lo

A Catalan white grape from Penedès, the most structured of the three traditional Cava varieties; it also makes still whites with good ageing potential.

Origin

Xarel·lo is native to Catalonia and historically linked to Penedès. It is known as Pansa Blanca in Alella, north of Barcelona, and as Cartoixà in parts of Tarragona. There is also a pink-skinned mutation, Xarel·lo Vermell, grown in small quantities.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous vine that buds and ripens mid- to late season, with large bunches and thick-skinned berries. It adapts well to the Mediterranean climate of Penedès and its limestone and clay soils. It is susceptible to powdery mildew. Its thick skin gives it some resistance and contributes to the structure of its wines.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow wines with body, good acidity and medium alcohol. The aromas recall white fruit (apple, pear), citrus, fennel and Mediterranean herbs, with an earthy or mineral character. In Cava it is the variety that adds body, structure and the capacity for long ageing, compared with the freshness of Macabeo and the delicacy of Parellada. In recent decades still Xarel·lo whites have multiplied, often from old vines, aged on lees, in barrel or in amphorae, and they develop well over several years.

Where it is grown

It is grown almost exclusively in Catalonia. Its main area is Penedès, where it is one of the most planted varieties, and the Cava D.O., as well as the sparkling wines of Corpinnat and Clàssic Penedès. It is also found in Alella, as Pansa Blanca, in Tarragona and in Conca de Barberà. Outside Catalonia it is very rarely grown.

Both the Corpinnat collective brand and the Clàssic Penedès category of the Penedès D.O., devoted to long-aged sparkling wines, require organically grown grapes, and in both Xarel·lo is usually the predominant variety. Its ageing capacity has made it the mainstay of many of the longest-aged Catalan sparkling wines.

At the table

Xarel·lo whites, with body and freshness, go with fish, rice dishes, grilled vegetables such as calçots, poultry and semi-cured cheeses. Long-aged sparkling wines, in which it often predominates, pair well with shellfish and fairly intense dishes.
