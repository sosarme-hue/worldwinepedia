Touriga Franca

The most widely planted red grape in the Douro, an offspring of Touriga Nacional that brings floral perfume, fruit and elegance to Port and the region’s dry reds.

Origin

Touriga Franca, known until a few decades ago as Touriga Francesa, is a variety from the Douro Valley. Despite its old name, it has no connection with France. DNA analysis has shown it to be a natural cross between Touriga Nacional and Marufo, another old red grape of northern Portugal. Its name was officially changed to avoid confusion about its origin.

Viticulture

Unlike its parent Touriga Nacional, it is a reliable, productive vine with medium-sized, compact bunches, which explains why it is the most widely planted red grape in the Douro region. It ripens mid-season, tolerates heat well and prefers warm, south-facing slopes, though it adapts to other aspects too. It is relatively disease-resistant and yields consistently from year to year, qualities that are highly valued on the Douro’s demanding terraced slopes.

In the glass

It makes deeply coloured wines, medium- to full-bodied, with firm but fine tannins and good acidity. It is one of the most aromatic Douro varieties: rose, violet, red and black fruit such as raspberry, blackberry and cherry, with notes of herbs and spice. It is usually blended with Touriga Nacional, which brings power and structure, while Touriga Franca adds perfume, fruit and elegance; this pairing forms the basis of many Ports and the finest dry Douro reds.

Where it is grown

Its territory is almost exclusively northern Portugal, above all the demarcated Douro region, across its three subregions of Baixo Corgo, Cima Corgo and Douro Superior, and to a lesser extent Trás-os-Montes. It is grown less often in other Portuguese regions, and its presence outside Portugal is very limited.

At the table

Douro reds with Touriga Franca go with chargrilled meat, roast kid, game stews and aged cheeses; Ruby and Late Bottled Vintage Ports pair with chocolate and red-fruit desserts. Serve the reds at 16–18 °C.
