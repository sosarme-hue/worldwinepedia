Kalecik Karası

An Anatolian red grape native to the Kalecik area near Ankara, once on the verge of disappearing. It gives light-coloured wines with red fruit aromas and soft tannins, often compared with Pinot Noir.

Origin

Kalecik Karası is a native Turkish red variety, originating in the Kalecik district of Ankara province, beside the Kızılırmak river. Its name simply means “the black one of Kalecik”. By the mid-twentieth century its cultivation had shrunk until it was almost reduced to a few old vineyards, but from the 1970s researchers at the Faculty of Agriculture of Ankara University undertook selection and conservation work that allowed it to be revived. Since then it has become one of Turkey’s most highly regarded varieties.

Viticulture

In its area of origin it is grown in a valley with a continental climate, with cold winters and hot, dry summers, on alluvial and stony soils near the river. It requires careful vineyard management and yield control to give wines with concentration and well-defined aromas. Its cultivation has spread to other regions of Turkey, where it adapts to varied climates, although producers consider that it gives its finest wines in relatively cool areas.

In the glass

It gives light ruby wines, light- to medium-bodied, with soft tannins and moderate to lively acidity. Its aromas recall strawberry, raspberry, cherry and redcurrant, sometimes with floral, candy and gentle spice notes; with ageing, hints of leather and forest floor appear. Because of its lightness and fruity profile it is often compared with Pinot Noir or Gamay. It is made mainly as a young red or with moderate barrel ageing, and also as rosé and sparkling wine.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark area is Kalecik and, more broadly, the Ankara region in Central Anatolia, where several wineries work with their own vineyards or with grapes from local growers. It is also grown in Cappadocia, in the Aegean region and in Turkish Thrace. Outside Turkey it is practically non-existent. It is one of the varieties that best illustrate Turkish wineries’ renewed interest in their native grapes, and many of them include a varietal Kalecik Karası in their range.
