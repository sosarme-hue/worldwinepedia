Frappato

A red grape from south-eastern Sicily, around Vittoria, giving light, fragrant wines with soft tannins; together with Nero d’Avola it makes Cerasuolo di Vittoria.

Origin

Frappato is a red grape from Sicily, grown for centuries in the south-east of the island, especially in the province of Ragusa and around the town of Vittoria. Its exact origin is unknown, and the meaning of its name is also unclear. For a long time it was used almost exclusively in blends, but in recent decades it has attracted attention as a single-variety wine, offering a light, aromatic profile that contrasts with the image of powerful, warm Sicilian reds.

Viticulture

It is a vine of moderate vigour, adapted to the warm, dry climate of south-eastern Sicily, although it needs some freshness to retain its aromas. Around Vittoria it grows mainly on a plateau of moderate altitude, with reddish sandy soils over limestone, which drain well and favour fine, fragrant wines. It ripens mid-season and tends to give lightly coloured must, so its wines are pale. It was traditionally trained as bush vines, although trellising now predominates.

In the glass

It gives light ruby reds, light to medium in body, with soft tannins, good acidity and marked aromatic expression. Its aromas recall strawberry, raspberry, red cherry and pomegranate, with floral notes of violet and rose and touches of white pepper and herbs. It is a wine meant mainly for drinking young, often slightly chilled, although some more serious versions develop well for a few years. In Cerasuolo di Vittoria it contributes perfume and freshness, while Nero d’Avola brings colour, body and structure.

Where it is grown

Its main area is south-eastern Sicily. It is one of the two grapes of Cerasuolo di Vittoria, the island’s only DOCG, together with Nero d’Avola, and it is made as a single-variety wine in the Vittoria DOC. It is also used in Sicilia DOC and IGT wines. Outside Sicily it is hardly grown.

At the table

Thanks to its lightness it goes well with cured meats, pasta with tomato, tuna and other full-flavoured fish, pizza, roast vegetables and white meat. Serve at 14–16 °C, a little cooler than most reds.
