Falanghina

An ancient white grape from Campania giving fresh, aromatic, savoury wines; under this name two distinct varieties are in fact grown, Falanghina Flegrea and Falanghina Beneventana.

Origin

Falanghina is a white grape from Campania, in southern Italy, grown for centuries around Naples and in the interior of the region. Its name is usually derived from falanga, a word for the poles or stakes that supported the vines. It has often been linked to the wines of Roman Campania, such as the famous Falernian, but this connection is an unproven hypothesis. Genetic studies have shown that two distinct varieties are grown under the name Falanghina: Falanghina Flegrea, native to the Campi Flegrei west of Naples, and Falanghina Beneventana, from Sannio in the province of Benevento. In the late 20th century it was a little-known grape, and since then it has become one of the most widespread white varieties in Campania.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous vine with regular yields, ripening mid to late season and keeping good acidity. In the Campi Flegrei it grows on loose, sandy volcanic soils in which phylloxera struggles to thrive, so there are still vineyards planted ungrafted, on their own roots. In Sannio the vineyards lie on inland hills, with clay and limestone soils and a more continental climate.

In the glass

It gives straw-yellow whites, medium-bodied, fresh and savoury. Its aromas recall citrus, apple, pear, peach and white flowers, with touches of tropical fruit and herbs and, in volcanic areas, a mineral, saline note. Most is drunk young, although the best versions gain complexity after a few years in bottle. Sparkling wines and sweet wines from dried grapes are also made.

Where it is grown

Its main denominations are Falanghina del Sannio, with subzones such as Taburno, Guardia Sanframondi, Sant’Agata dei Goti and Solopaca, and Campi Flegrei. It is also the basis of white Falerno del Massico, in northern Campania, and is widely used in IGT wines. Outside Campania little is grown.

At the table

It goes well with fish, shellfish, pasta with seafood, buffalo mozzarella, vegetables and pizza. Serve at 8–10 °C.
