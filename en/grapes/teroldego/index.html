Teroldego

The signature red grape of Trentino, grown mainly on the Campo Rotaliano plain, giving deeply coloured, fruity wines with lively acidity.

Origin

Teroldego is a native red grape of Trentino, in northern Italy, recorded in the region for centuries. Its birthplace is the Campo Rotaliano (or Piana Rotaliana), a triangular plain at the confluence of the Noce and Adige rivers, between the communes of Mezzocorona, Mezzolombardo and San Michele all’Adige. DNA analysis indicates that it is a parent of Lagrein, the great red grape of neighbouring Alto Adige, and that it is related to Marzemino, another traditional Trentino variety. The origin of its name is not known for certain.

Viticulture

It is a vigorous, productive vine, so limiting yields is essential to obtain concentrated wines. In the Campo Rotaliano it grows on deep alluvial soils of gravel, sand and pebbles brought down by the Noce, very free-draining and good at storing the day’s heat. The plain is surrounded by mountains that shelter it from cold winds, allowing the grapes to ripen well in an Alpine setting. It is traditionally trained on the Trentino pergola, although vertical trellising has also spread.

In the glass

It gives deeply coloured, purple-ruby reds with good acidity, moderate, soft tannins and a medium to full body. Its aromas recall black cherry, blackberry, raspberry and plum, with notes of violet, almond, spice and sometimes herbs or tar. Young wines are fruity and easy to drink, while more ambitious, oak-aged versions age well and gain complexity. A rosé is also made.

Where it is grown

Its benchmark denomination is the Teroldego Rotaliano DOC, which covers the Campo Rotaliano plain. It is also grown in other parts of Trentino for DOC and IGT wines, and in small quantities in other Italian regions. Outside Italy its presence is very limited.

At the table

It goes with grilled red meat, game, cured meats, polenta, stews and aged mountain cheeses. Younger, fruitier versions also suit lighter dishes, such as pasta with meat or mushroom sauces. Serve at 16–18 °C.
