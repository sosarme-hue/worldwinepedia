07 / Culture & learning

Learning to look, smell and taste.

Tasting

Primary, secondary and tertiary aromas

The classic classification of wine aromas by origin —the grape, winemaking and ageing— and how to use it to describe and understand what you perceive in the glass.

Blind tasting

What blind tasting is, why professionals and enthusiasts use it, how deductive reasoning works and how to organise a blind tasting at home.

Food pairing

Cooking with wine

Wine is a classic kitchen ingredient: it brings acidity, aroma and depth to stews, sauces, marinades and desserts. This article explains what happens when it is cooked, how to choose it and how much truth there is in the idea that the alcohol evaporates completely.

Food pairing with sparkling wines

Sparkling wines are far more than a wine for toasts: their acidity and bubbles make them exceptionally versatile at the table. This article explains why, and how to choose according to style, sweetness and ageing.

History & culture

Champagne and the traditional method

How a still wine from northern France became the world's most famous sparkling wine, what truth there is in the Dom Pérignon legend and how a wine with second fermentation in bottle is made.

The Judgment of Paris, 1976

The 1976 blind tasting in Paris in which California wines came out ahead of great French wines, and its influence on how the world viewed New World wine.
