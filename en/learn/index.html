Accessible articles to understand wine: science, vineyard, tasting, food pairing, history and practical guides.

Oenology & science

Acidity and pH in wine

Acids give wine freshness and longevity, while pH affects its colour, stability and resistance to spoilage. Which acids grapes contain, how they are measured and how growers and winemakers manage them.

Amphorae, tinajas and qvevri

Clay vessels have accompanied wine since its beginnings. How they affect fermentation and ageing, which traditions have survived in Georgia, Spain and Portugal, and why they have returned to many modern wineries.

Residual sugar and sweetness levels

The sugar left in a wine after fermentation determines whether it is dry, medium or sweet. How it is retained, how it is perceived and what label terms mean for still and sparkling wines.

Botrytis and noble rot

The same fungus, Botrytis cinerea, can ruin a harvest or give rise to some of the world's most celebrated sweet wines. Everything depends on the weather, the grape and the moment it strikes.

Fining, stabilisation and filtration

The operations that leave wine clear and stable before bottling: settling and racking, fining agents, the prevention of tartrate and protein deposits, and the different types of filtration.

Biological ageing and flor

Some wines age beneath a layer of yeast that floats on their surface and protects them from oxygen. This is how Andalusian fino and manzanilla, the vin jaune of the Jura and other singular wines are born.

Barrel ageing and types of oak

Why wine is aged in oak barrels, what the wood contributes depending on its species, origin, toast and age, and how vessel size and length of ageing make a difference.

How white wine is made

White wine is made by fermenting juice separated from the skins; its quality depends on preserving fruit, preventing oxidation and controlling temperature precisely.

How rosé wine is made

Rosé is made from red grapes whose juice spends only a short time in contact with the skins; the length of that contact and the method chosen determine its colour, aroma and structure.

How red wine is made

From bunch to bottle: how fermenting the must in contact with the skins extracts colour, tannins and aromas, and which winemaking decisions shape the style of a red wine.

Alcoholic fermentation and yeasts

How yeasts turn grape sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide, which species are involved and why fermentation shapes so much of a wine's character.

Malolactic fermentation

The second transformation many wines undergo: bacteria convert malic acid into lactic acid, softening acidity and bringing stability and new nuances.

Carbonic maceration

A red winemaking method in which whole bunches, held in an atmosphere of carbon dioxide, begin fermenting inside each berry. It produces very fruity wines, light in tannin and intended to be drunk young.

Sparkling wine production methods

An overview of the main ways of putting bubbles into wine: the traditional method, the Charmat or Martinotti method, the ancestral method, the transfer method and carbonation, with their technical and stylistic differences.

Polyphenols, colour and tannins

Anthocyanins, tannins and other phenolic compounds explain a wine's colour, its astringency and much of how it evolves over time.

The chemistry of wine aroma

From the terpenes of Muscat to the peppery note of Syrah: which molecules explain the primary, secondary and tertiary aromas of wine.

Sulphites and sulphur dioxide

Sulphur dioxide is the most widely used preservative in winemaking, protecting wine from oxidation and microorganisms. What it is, how it works, why labels mention sulphites and what a wine with no added sulphites means.

Sweet wines: production methods

Late harvest, drying, freezing the grapes, noble rot and fortification: the different ways of concentrating sugar or keeping it in the wine, and the great styles that result from each.

Orange wines and skin contact

Orange wines are made from white grapes vinified as if they were red, fermenting on their skins. The result is an amber colour, a tannic texture and a distinctive aromatic profile.
