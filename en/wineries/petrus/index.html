Petrus

A small Pomerol estate on a mound of blue clay, whose wine, made almost entirely from Merlot, is one of the most famous in Bordeaux.

Petrus lies on the Pomerol plateau, on the Right Bank of Bordeaux. Its small vineyard occupies a slightly raised mound where a very compact blue clay, rich in smectite, comes to the surface, surrounded by gravel soils. This clay, which retains water evenly and cracks in summer, is considered the key to the wine’s character.

In the 19th century the wine already had a good local reputation, but its international fame came in the 20th century. The owner Madame Edmond Loubat, who gradually acquired the estate from the 1920s onwards, made it known in the British and American markets. After the Second World War the Libourne merchant Jean-Pierre Moueix became its exclusive distributor and later co-owner. His son Christian Moueix ran the winemaking for decades, and today the property belongs to the branch of the family headed by Jean-François Moueix.

Pomerol has no official classification system, so Petrus appears on no list, but its prestige and scarcity place it among the most expensive and sought-after wines in the world. The name, which refers to Saint Peter, appears on the label beside an image of the apostle holding the keys of heaven; the estate no longer uses the word “Château”.

The vineyard is planted almost entirely with Merlot, and the wine is made with great care, with a fast hand harvest, often in the afternoon to avoid dew, and ageing in barrel. Only one wine is produced: the estate does not sell a second wine.
