Château Angélus

Saint-Émilion estate of the de Boüard de Laforest family, whose name evokes the Angelus bells and whose wines are marked by a high proportion of Cabernet Franc.

Château Angélus lies at the foot of the southern slope of Saint-Émilion, west of the town. Its name comes from a plot from which one could hear at the same time the bells of three nearby churches ringing the Angelus, the morning, noon and evening prayer. That is why the bell is the estate’s emblem and appears on its golden label, and a carillon rings in its tower.

The property belongs to the de Boüard de Laforest family, who have been in Saint-Émilion for several generations. From the 1980s Hubert de Boüard de Laforest undertook a thorough modernisation of the vineyard and the winemaking, and the estate rose through the Saint-Émilion classification until it reached the rank of Premier Grand Cru Classé “A” in 2012. Today it is run by his daughter, Stéphanie de Boüard-Rivoal.

In 2021 the family announced that Angélus would not apply for the 2022 revision of the classification, a decision also taken by other leading Saint-Émilion estates, so its wines are now sold as Saint-Émilion Grand Cru without any mention of classification.

The vineyard lies on clay and limestone soils on the upper part and on sand and clay at the foot of the slope, and is planted with Merlot and a very high proportion of Cabernet Franc for the area. As well as the grand vin, the estate makes the second wine Carillon d’Angélus. Its style combines richness, ripe fruit and freshness.
