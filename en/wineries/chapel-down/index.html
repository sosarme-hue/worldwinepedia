Winery

Chapel Down

One of England’s largest wine producers, based in Tenterden (Kent). It makes traditional-method sparkling wines and still wines, and is particularly known for its Bacchus whites.

Chapel Down is based in Tenterden, in the Kent Weald, on an estate where vines were planted at the end of the 1970s, in the early days of modern English viticulture. The company adopted the Chapel Down name in the 1990s and has since grown to become one of the country’s largest producers, both in vineyard area and in volume of wine. Its development has gone hand in hand with the rise of English wine, to which it has helped give visibility on the domestic market.

The winery works with its own and leased vineyards in Kent, among them Kit’s Coty, on the chalk slopes of the North Downs near Aylesford, regarded as its most prestigious site, and with grapes from partner growers in south-east England. The main varieties are Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier for sparkling wines, and Bacchus, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir for still wines.

The range covers non-vintage, vintage and rosé traditional-method sparkling wines, led by Chapel Down Brut, and a series of still wines in which Bacchus holds a prominent place: Chapel Down has been one of the wineries that has done most to make this aromatic variety known as a characteristic English style. The wines from the Kit’s Coty vineyard, both sparkling and still Chardonnay, represent the most ambitious part of its production. The overall style is fresh, fruity and marked by acidity.

Visits & wine tourism

The Tenterden winery welcomes visitors with guided tours of the vineyard and winery, tastings and a shop.
