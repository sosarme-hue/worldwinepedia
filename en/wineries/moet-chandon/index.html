Moët & Chandon

Champagne house founded in 1743 in Épernay by Claude Moët, now part of the LVMH group and one of the most widely distributed houses in the world.

Moët & Chandon was founded in 1743 by Claude Moët, a wine merchant from Épernay. His grandson Jean-Rémy Moët expanded the business considerably in the late 18th and early 19th centuries and enjoyed a close relationship with Napoleon Bonaparte, who visited the house several times. In the 19th century Pierre-Gabriel Chandon, Jean-Rémy’s son-in-law, joined the management and the firm took the name Moët & Chandon.

The house is based on the Avenue de Champagne in Épernay, beneath which run kilometres of galleries dug into the chalk, where the bottles rest during ageing. It is one of the largest vineyard owners in Champagne, with plots spread across the appellation’s main areas, and works with the three classic varieties: Pinot Noir, Pinot Meunier and Chardonnay.

Its best-known wine is Moët Impérial, a non-vintage brut created in 1869, alongside Rosé Impérial, the medium-dry Nectar versions and the Grand Vintage vintage champagnes. Dom Pérignon, the prestige cuvée born within the house, is now marketed as a separate brand. Since the merger that created LVMH, Moët & Chandon has been part of the Moët Hennessy group.

The house style aims for a fruity, approachable champagne with fine bubbles and a fresh profile, designed to be consistent from bottle to bottle thanks to the blending of many base wines and reserve wines.

Visits & wine tourism

It offers guided tours of its underground cellars in Épernay, with a tasting, by prior booking.
