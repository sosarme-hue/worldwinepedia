Marchesi Antinori

Florentine family involved in wine since 1385, one of Italy’s most influential houses and the creator of Tignanello, one of the first great “Super Tuscans”.

The Antinori family’s history in wine dates back to 1385, when Giovanni di Piero Antinori joined the Arte dei Vinattieri, the guild of Florence’s wine merchants. Since then the business has remained in the hands of the same family for more than twenty generations; its historic seat is Palazzo Antinori, in the centre of Florence.

In the second half of the twentieth century, under the leadership of Piero Antinori, the house played a decisive role in the renewal of Tuscan wine. Tignanello, made from 1971 onwards with Sangiovese and a small share of Cabernet, aged in barriques and without the white grapes then required by Chianti regulations, became the model for the so-called Super Tuscans.

Today the family owns estates in Tuscany’s main areas, such as Tenuta Tignanello in Chianti Classico, Pian delle Vigne in Montalcino and Guado al Tasso in Bolgheri, as well as properties in Umbria, Piedmont (Prunotto) and Apulia (Tormaresca), among others. In 2012 it opened a winery built into the hillside at Bargino, in the municipality of San Casciano Val di Pesa, which has become its production centre in Chianti Classico.

The range runs from widely available wines, such as Peppoli Chianti Classico, to prestige labels like Tignanello and Solaia. The house style seeks to combine the identity of Sangiovese with precise, modern winemaking.

Visits & wine tourism

The Antinori nel Chianti Classico winery, at Bargino, offers guided tours with tastings, by prior booking.
