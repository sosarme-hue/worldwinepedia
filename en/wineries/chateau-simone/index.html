Château Simone

Family property in Meyreuil, near Aix-en-Provence, the historic reference of the small Palette appellation, making long-lived reds, whites and rosés.

Château Simone lies in Meyreuil, a few kilometres east of Aix-en-Provence, and is the best-known property of Palette, one of the smallest appellations in Provence. It has belonged to the Rougier family since the 19th century, and several generations have succeeded one another at the head of the estate.

The vineyards occupy the slopes of the Montaigu massif above the river Arc, on stony limestone soils. Many plots face north, an unusual orientation in Provence which, together with the influence of the surrounding pine woods, creates a relatively cool microclimate. The vines, often old, mix numerous varieties, as is traditional in Palette.

The house makes a red based on Grenache, Mourvèdre and Cinsault, a white dominated by Clairette and a rosé, as well as a second wine. The wines are aged in the old cellars cut into the rock beneath the house.

Château Simone's style is classic and low-intervention: fresh, complex wines, far from the image of Provence as a land of light rosés, and with remarkable ageing potential, the rosé included.

The Palette appellation, recognised in 1948, covers just a few dozen hectares in the communes of Meyreuil, Le Tholonet and Aix-en-Provence, and Château Simone accounts for a significant share of its area. Its rules require longer minimum ageing than is usual in Provence.
