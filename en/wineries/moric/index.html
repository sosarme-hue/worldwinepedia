Moric

Project of winegrower Roland Velich in Burgenland, devoted to old-vine Blaufränkisch from Lutzmannsburg and Neckenmarkt in a style that favours finesse over power.

Moric is the personal project of Roland Velich, who comes from a Burgenland winegrowing family and launched it in the early 2000s. His aim was to show the potential of Blaufränkisch, Austria’s great native red variety, known in Germany as Lemberger and in Hungary as Kékfrankos, as a grape capable of producing fine, long-lived wines that express their origin, at a time when many of the region’s reds sought above all concentration, ripeness and new oak.

To this end Velich focused on old vineyards in Mittelburgenland, particularly in the villages of Lutzmannsburg and Neckenmarkt, near the Hungarian border. The plots in the two villages have different soils and exposures, and the project vinifies their grapes separately to show those differences. Many of these vines are several decades old and give low yields.

Winemaking is minimal-intervention, with spontaneous fermentations, gentle extraction and ageing in used barrels and larger casks, so as not to mask the fruit or the expression of the soil. The range includes a regional Blaufränkisch, village wines from Lutzmannsburg and Neckenmarkt and old-vine wines from each of them. Moric’s style, with fine tannins, marked acidity and spicy and floral notes, has had a notable influence on the development of Austrian reds.
