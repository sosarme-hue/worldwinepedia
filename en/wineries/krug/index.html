Winery

Krug

Champagne house founded in Reims in 1843 by Joseph Krug, known for its Grande Cuvée, a blend of many wines from different harvests fermented in small oak casks.

Krug was founded in Reims in 1843 by Joseph Krug, born in Mainz, who had previously worked at another Champagne house. His idea was to make the best possible champagne every year regardless of the conditions of the harvest, by combining wines from many plots and different years. This philosophy is reflected in the Grande Cuvée, the house’s main wine, which is conceived not as a simple non-vintage champagne but as a prestige cuvée.

The house remained in the hands of the Krug family for generations, and since 1999 it has been part of the LVMH group; members of the family have remained involved with it. Krug works with its own vineyards and with growers throughout the region, and keeps a large library of reserve wines from many harvests.

One of the house’s distinctive features is that all its base wines are fermented in small used oak casks, which give no oaky flavour but a slight oxygenation. Each plot is vinified separately, giving the team hundreds of different wines to blend.

As well as Grande Cuvée, Krug makes a Rosé, a vintage champagne, Krug Vintage, and two single-plot wines: Clos du Mesnil, from Chardonnay, and Clos d’Ambonnay, from Pinot Noir. For some years now, each bottle has carried an edition number or a code that reveals the story of its blend. Krug champagnes are marked by richness, complexity and ageing potential.
