Ridge Vineyards

Californian winery founded in 1962, famous for its Monte Bello Cabernet Sauvignon from the Santa Cruz Mountains and for its old-vine Zinfandels from Sonoma, such as Lytton Springs and Geyserville.

Ridge was founded in 1962 by a group of engineers from the Stanford Research Institute who bought a property on the crest of the Santa Cruz Mountains above Cupertino, where vines had been planted in the late nineteenth century. There the Monte Bello wine was born, a Cabernet Sauvignon-based red that became one of the great Californian classics. In 1969 Paul Draper joined, leading the winemaking for decades and defining the house style.

Draper championed traditional, low-intervention winemaking inspired by nineteenth-century practice: fermentations with native yeasts, the use of air-dried American oak and a priority on vineyard quality. Ridge also became famous for its old-vine Zinfandels from Sonoma, such as Lytton Springs, in Dry Creek Valley, and Geyserville, in Alexander Valley, vineyards partly planted more than a century ago in which Zinfandel grows alongside Petite Sirah, Carignan and Mataro (Mourvèdre).

The winery has two homes: the historic Monte Bello estate in the Santa Cruz Mountains and the Lytton Springs winery near Healdsburg, built to sustainable standards. Ridge is also known for its transparency, since it lists on its labels the ingredients and methods used in making each wine.

Although Ridge wines do not always carry a variety name on the label, since many are field blends inherited from historic plantings, the house has become a reference for understanding California’s pre-Prohibition vineyard heritage.

Visits & wine tourism

It welcomes visitors at both its homes, Monte Bello and Lytton Springs, with tastings.
