Tenuta San Guido

The Incisa della Rocchetta family estate in Bolgheri, home of Sassicaia, the Cabernet-based red that brought international fame to the Tuscan coast.

Tenuta San Guido stretches between the Tyrrhenian Sea and the hills of Bolgheri, on the coast of the province of Livorno. The estate belongs to the Incisa della Rocchetta family and is known both for its wines and for breeding racehorses, as well as for a protected wildlife reserve.

In the 1940s Marchese Mario Incisa della Rocchetta, an admirer of the great wines of Bordeaux, planted Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc on a stony plot of the estate. For years the wine was drunk within the family, until it was first sold commercially with the 1968 vintage. Its name, Sassicaia, refers to the stones (“sassi”) in the soil.

The success of Sassicaia showed the potential of an area that until then had no tradition of great reds, and paved the way for the wines of Bolgheri. Over time the Bolgheri Sassicaia appellation became a DOC in its own right, an exceptional case in Italy as it corresponds to a single estate.

Under the leadership of Nicolò Incisa della Rocchetta, the founder’s son, and his descendants, the estate added two more wines: Guidalberto, from Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, and Le Difese, from Cabernet Sauvignon and Sangiovese, both more approachable and meant to be drunk earlier.
