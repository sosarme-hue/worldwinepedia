Vinícola Salton

Family winery in Bento Gonçalves, in the Serra Gaúcha, founded in 1910 by descendants of Italian immigrants and one of Brazil’s largest sparkling wine producers.

Salton’s history goes back to the arrival in southern Brazil, at the end of the nineteenth century, of immigrants from northern Italy, who introduced viticulture to the hills of the state of Rio Grande do Sul. In 1910 the Salton brothers, sons of one of those immigrants, founded a company in Bento Gonçalves devoted to making wine and other drinks. The firm has remained in the hands of the family for more than a century.

The Serra Gaúcha, Brazil’s main winegrowing area, is a hilly region with a humid, rainy climate, in which grapes tend to ripen with high acidity and moderate sugar levels. These conditions are particularly well suited to sparkling wines, which have become the speciality of Brazilian wine. Salton draws on its own vineyards and on a network of growers in the area, and it also works with grapes from other parts of the state, such as the Campanha, on the border with Uruguay.

The house is best known for its espumantes, made both by the traditional method, with a second fermentation in bottle, and by the tank method. Among them, the Moscatel espumante, sweet, light and aromatic, stands out as one of Brazil’s most popular styles. Its range also includes still red and white wines from varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Tannat and Chardonnay. The modern winery in Bento Gonçalves is open to visitors, who can see the production process and taste the range.
