Domaine William Fèvre

Chablis domaine and house, one of the largest owners of grand cru vineyards in the appellation, belonging since 1998 to the Henriot house.

Domaine William Fèvre bears the name of William Fèvre, a Chablis grower from a family with a long tradition in the area, who in the second half of the 20th century assembled a major holding of vineyards in the best sites of the appellation. Fèvre was also a prominent voice in defence of the Chablis terroir, particularly against the extension of the production area onto soils other than the traditional ones.

In 1998 the domaine was acquired by the Champagne house Henriot, which already owned the Burgundy house Bouchard Père & Fils. The new owners introduced changes in the vineyard and cellar, including a more moderate use of new oak, which had characterised the wines of the previous period.

Today William Fèvre is one of the largest owners of grands crus in Chablis, with plots in most of them, including Les Clos, Bougros, Vaudésir, Valmur, Les Preuses and Blanchot, and also owns many premiers crus, such as Vaillons, Montée de Tonnerre, Fourchaume and Montmains. The house distinguishes the domaine wines, made from grapes from its own vineyards, from those of its négociant business, sold in particular under the name Champs Royaux.

All the wines are made from Chardonnay. The premiers and grands crus are fermented and aged partly in used barrels and partly in tank, with the aim of preserving the purity and mineral tension typical of the Kimmeridgian marl soils of Chablis.
