Bodegas Muga

Family winery in Haro founded in 1932, known for fermenting and ageing its wines entirely in wood and for running its own cooperage.

Bodegas Muga was founded in 1932 by Isaac Muga Martínez and Aurora Caño, both from families involved in wine in Haro. In 1971 the winery moved to its current home in the Station District, a stone building next to the railway tracks. The company is still family-owned and is run by descendants of the founders.

The house works with its own vineyards and those of trusted growers on the foothills of the Obarenes Mountains, around Haro, in the Rioja Alta area. The main varieties are the region’s traditional ones: Tempranillo, Grenache, Mazuelo (Carignan) and Graciano, plus Viura and Malvasía for the whites.

Muga’s most distinctive feature is the use of wood at every stage: it ferments in oak vats, ages in barrels and large casks, and runs its own cooperage, where its coopers build and repair barrels of oak from different origins. It also keeps traditional practices such as egg-white fining and gravity racking.

Its range runs from young rosés and whites to reservas and gran reservas, such as Prado Enea, alongside more modern-style wines like Torre Muga and Aro, and sparkling wines under the Conde de Haro label. The house style combines the structure of classic Rioja with well-defined fruit.

Visits & wine tourism

It offers guided tours of the winery and its cooperage, with a tasting, by prior booking.
