Château Climens

Barsac first growth, in the Sauternes area, which makes its noble-rot sweet wines exclusively from Sémillon.

Château Climens lies in Barsac, one of the five communes of the Sauternes area, on the left bank of the Garonne. Barsac wines may be sold under the commune’s own appellation or under that of Sauternes. The estate stands on one of the highest parts of the Barsac plateau, on red sand and gravel soils over a subsoil of fissured limestone.

In the 1855 classification it was recognised as a Premier Cru Classé among the sweet wines of Sauternes and Barsac. In 1971 it was bought by Lucien Lurton, a member of a well-known family of Bordeaux estate owners, and from the early 1990s it was run by his daughter Bérénice Lurton, who converted the vineyard to biodynamic viticulture. In 2022 the estate changed hands.

One of Climens’s distinctive features is that its vineyard is planted solely with Sémillon, whereas most estates in the area combine this variety with Sauvignon Blanc and sometimes Muscadelle. The autumn mists formed where the Ciron meets the Garonne favour the development of Botrytis cinerea, noble rot, and the harvest is done by hand in several passes, picking only the grapes at the desired stage.

As well as the grand vin, the estate makes a second sweet wine, Cyprès de Climens. The house style is associated with sweet wines of great freshness and balance, in which acidity offsets the richness in sugar.
