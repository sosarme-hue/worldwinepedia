Maison Patriarche

Beaune négociant house founded in 1780, known for its vast underground cellars beneath a former convent and for a range covering many of Burgundy’s appellations.

Maison Patriarche is one of the historic négociant houses of Beaune, the wine capital of the Côte d’Or. It was founded in 1780 by Jean-Baptiste Patriarche and, like other houses in the town, devoted itself from the outset to selecting, ageing and selling wines from the different villages of Burgundy.

Its headquarters occupy the former convent of the Visitandines, a 17th-century building in the heart of Beaune. Beneath it run vaulted galleries that rank among the most extensive cellars in the region, where millions of bottles and barrels rest. These cellars have become one of the town’s most visited sites for wine lovers.

As a négociant, Patriarche buys grapes, musts and wines from growers throughout Burgundy and vinifies or ages them in its own facilities. Its range is very broad, from regional wines such as Bourgogne and Crémant de Bourgogne to village appellations and premiers crus of the Côte de Nuits and the Côte de Beaune, as well as Chablis. Reds are made from Pinot Noir and whites from Chardonnay, in line with the rules of each appellation.

The house style is classic and approachable, aimed at faithfully expressing the character of each village, and its catalogue offers a straightforward way to discover the diversity of Burgundian terroirs, from the mineral whites of the north to the reds of the Côte d’Or.

Visits & wine tourism

The historic cellars beneath the former Visitandines convent, in the centre of Beaune, are open to visitors and include a tasting.
