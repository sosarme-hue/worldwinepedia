Brancott Estate

Successor to Montana, the company that planted Marlborough's first commercial vineyards in 1973 and made there the first Sauvignon Blancs that brought the region fame.

Brancott Estate is the current name of the brand that for decades was known as Montana, one of the companies that transformed New Zealand wine. In 1973 Montana planted vines in the Brancott Valley, in Marlborough, in the north-east of the South Island, when the region was mostly sheep pasture and almost nobody believed in its viticultural potential. It was the beginning of the region's first commercial vineyards.

In the late 1970s the company made its first Sauvignon Blancs there, with intense aromas and lively acidity, which surprised people with their character and opened the way to a style that would travel around the world. Within a few decades Marlborough became the largest wine region in the country and Sauvignon Blanc its emblematic variety.

The brand now belongs to the Pernod Ricard group, which adopted the name Brancott Estate for its export wines in memory of that first vineyard. The range extends from widely distributed wines, such as the Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc, to single-vineyard and more ambitious selections, alongside Pinot Noir, Pinot Gris, Chardonnay and sparkling wines.

The house style seeks to express the aromatic profile typical of Marlborough: tropical and citrus fruit, herbaceous notes and refreshing acidity, with winemaking mostly in stainless steel in order to preserve freshness.

Visits & wine tourism

It has a visitor centre with tasting room and restaurant in Marlborough, near Blenheim.
