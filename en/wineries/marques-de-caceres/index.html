Marqués de Cáceres

Winery in Cenicero founded in 1970 by Enrique Forner, who brought Bordeaux-inspired winemaking techniques to Rioja; it is now one of the best-known Rioja brands outside Spain.

Marqués de Cáceres was founded in 1970 in Cenicero, in Rioja Alta, by Enrique Forner, a member of a wine family of Valencian origin who had spent several years in Bordeaux, where he had managed wine estates. That experience gave the winery the trait that made its name in its early years: the application in Rioja of Bordeaux winemaking principles, with advice from the French oenologist Émile Peynaud.

In contrast to the model then prevailing, based on very long ageing in used barrels, the winery opted for temperature-controlled fermentation, more careful grape selection and oak ageing designed to preserve fruit. That approach, which seems commonplace today, was new in the Rioja of the 1970s and helped to renew the region’s style.

The company is still owned by the Forner family. It works with its own vineyards and with grapes from growers around Cenicero and other parts of Rioja Alta, where Tempranillo, Garnacha and Graciano predominate. Its range includes Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva reds, top wines such as Gaudium, and whites and rosés. The house also makes wines in other Spanish appellations, such as Rueda and Ribera del Duero.

Thanks to its wide international distribution, its labels, especially the Crianza, have become a highly recognisable reference for Rioja in shops and restaurants in many countries.
