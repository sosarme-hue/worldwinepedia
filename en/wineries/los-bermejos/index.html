Bodegas Los Bermejos

Lanzarote winery known for its volcanic Malvasía and Listán Negro wines from vines grown in pits dug into volcanic ash.

Bodegas Los Bermejos is one of the best-known wineries in the Lanzarote D.O., on the easternmost island of the Canary archipelago. Its range, focused on the island's traditional varieties, has helped make Lanzarote's volcanic wines known beyond the Canaries.

A unique vineyard

Lanzarote's viticulture is among the most unusual in the world. After the great eruptions of the eighteenth century, much of the island was covered by a layer of volcanic ash and lapilli, known locally as picón. Farmers learned to dig pits through this layer down to the fertile soil, plant one or a few vines at the bottom and protect each pit with a small semicircular stone wall against the trade winds. The picón retains moisture from the night dew and limits evaporation, allowing vines to grow with very little rain. Yields are extremely low and all work is done by hand. As phylloxera never reached the Canaries, the vines are planted ungrafted.

Varieties and wines

The emblematic variety is volcanic Malvasía, from which Los Bermejos makes dry, semi-sweet and naturally sweet whites, as well as sparkling wines. The winery also works with Listán Negro, the main red grape of the Canaries, making fresh reds, including one by carbonic maceration, and rosés. The house style seeks freshness, the aromatic expression of the varieties and the saline, mineral character attributed to the volcanic soil.
