Winery

Barone Ricasoli

Historic house of the castle of Brolio, in Gaiole in Chianti, linked to Baron Bettino Ricasoli, who in the nineteenth century proposed the grape formula for Chianti.

The Ricasoli family has owned the castle of Brolio, in the commune of Gaiole in Chianti, since the Middle Ages, and it is one of the oldest families associated with wine in Italy. The castle, on a hill with wide views over southern Chianti, was for centuries a strategic point between Florence and Siena.

The family’s most famous figure is Baron Bettino Ricasoli (1809-1880), a statesman who became Prime Minister of the Kingdom of Italy. At Brolio he spent many years experimenting with the local varieties and eventually proposed a composition for Chianti wine based mainly on Sangiovese, with contributions of Canaiolo and Malvasia. This “formula” influenced later Chianti regulations, although current Chianti Classico rules allow wines made from Sangiovese alone and no longer require white grapes.

After a period in which the brand was in the hands of outside groups, the family regained control of the company in the 1990s under the leadership of Francesco Ricasoli. Since then, work has been done to study the soils and replant the vineyards surrounding the castle.

The range includes Brolio Chianti Classico, several Gran Selezione wines such as Castello di Brolio and single-vineyard bottlings, as well as IGT reds. The castle and its gardens are open to visitors.

Visits & wine tourism

The castle of Brolio and its gardens can be visited; the winery offers tastings and guided tours by appointment.
