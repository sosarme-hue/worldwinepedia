Winery

Nyetimber

A pioneer of English traditional-method sparkling wine, founded in West Sussex in 1988 with the three classic Champagne varieties. It makes its wines exclusively from grapes grown in its own vineyards in southern England.

Nyetimber’s history as a winery began in 1988, when the American couple Stuart and Sandy Moss bought the estate at West Chiltington (West Sussex) and planted Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier with the aim of making traditional-method sparkling wine. At the time it was a risky venture, because hybrid varieties and German crossings dominated in England. The estate’s name is very old and already appears, in a different form, in the eleventh-century Domesday Book. The first vintages, from the early 1990s, showed that southern England could produce quality sparkling wine, and Nyetimber became a reference point for the producers who followed.

After several changes of ownership, it was acquired in 2006 by the Dutch businessman Eric Heerema, who expanded the vineyards considerably. Today the house farms several estates in West Sussex, Hampshire and Kent, many on chalk or greensand soils, and works only with its own grapes. Winemaking is led by Cherie Spriggs, together with Brad Greatrix.

The range focuses on traditional-method sparkling wines: the Classic Cuvée, the Chardonnay Blanc de Blancs, a rosé, a demi-sec, and single-vineyard and prestige wines such as Tillington Single Vineyard and the 1086 cuvée. The house style seeks precision, freshness and long ageing on the lees, which brings complexity.
