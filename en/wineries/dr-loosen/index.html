Weingut Dr. Loosen

Family estate in the Middle Mosel run by Ernst Loosen, with old vines on some of the region’s best slopes, such as Erdener Prälat and Wehlener Sonnenuhr.

The Loosen family has been involved in Mosel winegrowing for more than two centuries. Ernst Loosen took over the estate in the late 1980s and has since made it one of the international benchmarks for German Riesling, with vineyard work aimed at low yields and minimal-intervention winemaking.

The estate owns vines in several of the most highly regarded vineyards of the Middle Mosel: the Erdener Treppchen and Erdener Prälat, on red and grey slate; the Ürziger Würzgarten, with red soils of slate and volcanic sandstone; and the Wehlener Sonnenuhr, Graacher Himmelreich and Bernkasteler Lay, on blue slate. A large share of these vines are old, many of them ungrafted, benefiting from the resistance of slate soils to phylloxera.

The range runs from entry-level wines such as Dr. L Riesling, made from grapes grown in slate vineyards across the region, to single-vineyard wines in every Prädikat category, from Kabinett to Trockenbeerenauslese, as well as dry Rieslings from the best sites. The house style aims to express the differences between soils and slopes, with slow fermentations and ageing in tanks and traditional wooden casks. Ernst Loosen has also developed projects outside the Mosel, including the J. L. Wolf estate in the Pfalz and the Eroica collaboration with Chateau Ste. Michelle in Washington State.
