Château Lynch-Moussas

Fifth growth of the 1855 classification in Pauillac, owned by the Castéja family and located in the western sector of the appellation.

Château Lynch-Moussas is a fifth growth in the 1855 classification, located in the Pauillac appellation, in the western sector of the commune, further from the Gironde estuary, near Château Batailley. Its name recalls the Lynch family, of Irish origin, who owned the estate in the 18th century and also gave their name to Lynch-Bages.

In the 19th century the property was divided, and the part that kept the name Lynch-Moussas passed in the 20th century to the Castéja family, linked to the Bordeaux wine trade. From the late 1960s Émile Castéja undertook the restoration of the vineyard and buildings, which had fallen into poor condition, and today the estate is run by his son Philippe.

The vineyard, surrounded by woodland, lies on gravel soils planted mainly with Cabernet Sauvignon, with a share of Merlot. Successive investments in the vineyard and winery have gradually refined the style.

Lynch-Moussas wines have the classic Pauillac profile — blackcurrant, cedar, firm structure — in a somewhat lighter and more approachable register than the commune's great growths. The estate also makes a second wine, Les Hauts de Lynch-Moussas. This fruitier second wine is made from plots and lots not selected for the grand vin and is intended to be drunk earlier, while the grand vin is kept for longer ageing.
