Giuseppe Quintarelli

Small family winery in Negrar, in Valpolicella Classica, whose Amarone, aged for years in large casks, is one of Italy’s most revered wines.

The winery lies in the hills of Negrar, at the heart of Valpolicella Classica, north of Verona. Its fame is linked to Giuseppe Quintarelli (1927-2012), known as “Bepi”, who inherited the small family estate and made it a benchmark of Italian wine without changing its artisanal scale.

Quintarelli worked with very traditional methods: long periods of drying the grapes in lofts, slow fermentations and many years of ageing in large oak casks, often far longer than the rules require. His wines were released only when he considered them ready, and in vintages he did not judge good enough for Amarone, the grapes went into other wines. The labels, written in a calligraphic style, have become a symbol of the house.

Besides Amarone della Valpolicella Classico, the winery makes a Recioto, a Valpolicella Classico Superiore refermented in contact with the skins of dried grapes, the Rosso del Bepi red and wines from dried grapes of varieties such as Cabernet, among them Alzero. The base varieties are the area’s traditional ones, such as Corvina, Corvinone and Rondinella, together with others planted on the estate.

Since Giuseppe’s death the winery has been run by his family, in particular his daughter Fiorenza and his grandsons, who maintain the same approach.
