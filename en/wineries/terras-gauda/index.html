Bodegas Terras Gauda

Winery in O Rosal, in southern Rías Baixas, known for its white blends of Albariño, Loureira and Caíño Blanco, and for its role in reviving the latter variety.

Bodegas Terras Gauda is located in O Rosal, the subzone of the Rías Baixas Denominación de Origen at the south-western tip of Galicia, in the valley of the river Miño, facing Portugal and close to its mouth on the Atlantic. It was founded in the late 1980s, in the same years in which the appellation was created, and has become one of the reference wineries of this subzone.

While single-varietal Albariño dominates in the Val do Salnés, in O Rosal it is traditional to blend Albariño with other local varieties, especially Loureira and Caíño Blanco. Terras Gauda has made that tradition its hallmark, and is recognised for helping to revive Caíño Blanco, a variety that had almost disappeared, late-ripening and high in acidity, which brings tension and structure to the blend.

The vineyards stretch over slopes facing the Miño, often terraced, on granitic soils with some clay. The climate is Atlantic, though somewhat warmer than in Salnés thanks to the shelter of the valley. The house’s best-known wine is Terras Gauda O Rosal, a blend of the three varieties whose proportions vary with the vintage. The range also includes Abadía de San Campio, a single-varietal Albariño, and wines with longer lees ageing.

The winery’s style seeks aromatic, fresh whites with the complexity contributed by the different varieties.
