Château Gloria

Saint-Julien estate created from 1942 onwards by Henri Martin with plots bought from neighbouring classified growths; although it is not in the 1855 classification, it is a benchmark of the appellation.

Château Gloria is a unique case in the Médoc. It did not exist as such in 1855, so it is not part of that year’s official classification, but its vineyard is made up largely of plots that once belonged to classified estates in Saint-Julien. Its creator, Henri Martin, from a family of coopers and growers in the village, began in 1942 to buy vines from neighbours such as Léoville Poyferré, Gruaud Larose, Beychevelle and Saint-Pierre, until he had assembled a sizeable property.

Henri Martin was a leading figure in 20th-century Bordeaux wine: for many years he was mayor of Saint-Julien and he chaired various professional bodies, among them the Conseil Interprofessionnel du Vin de Bordeaux. In 1982 he also acquired Château Saint-Pierre, a fourth growth in Saint-Julien.

After his death the estate passed to his daughter and his son-in-law, Jean-Louis Triaud, and it is now run by their descendants, who also manage Saint-Pierre under the name Domaines Henri Martin.

Gloria’s vineyard lies on gravel and is planted mainly with Cabernet Sauvignon, with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. The estate also makes the second wine Château Peymartin. Gloria has earned a reputation as a classic, reliable Saint-Julien, valued by enthusiasts for offering the character of the appellation at a lower price than many classified growths.
