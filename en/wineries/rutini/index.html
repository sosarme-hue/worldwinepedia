Rutini Wines

Mendoza winery founded in 1885 by the Italian immigrant Felipe Rutini, with a modern winery in Tupungato and a wine museum at its former home in Maipú.

The history of Rutini begins in 1885, when Felipe Rutini, an Italian immigrant who had arrived in Mendoza, founded a winery in Coquimbito, in the department of Maipú. The company, long known as Bodega La Rural, grew over the twentieth century and became one of the province’s traditional houses. Its former premises in Maipú house a wine museum with a large collection of tools, presses and objects linked to the winemaking history of Mendoza.

Around the turn of the century the house redirected its production towards the Uco Valley, where it already owned vineyards, and built a modern winery in Tupungato, at the foot of the Andes. The altitude of the area, its cool nights and its stony, alluvial soils show in wines of good acidity and deep colour. The company also works with vineyards in other parts of Mendoza.

Under the Rutini brand it makes varietal wines from Malbec, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Chardonnay and other grapes, as well as the traditional Cabernet-Malbec, a blend closely tied to the history of the house, and top-end wines such as Felipe Rutini or Apartado. The winery also produces the Trumpeter line, fruity in style and affordably priced, which is widely distributed on the international market. The house style is classic, with ageing in oak barrels and wines conceived for the table, and it remains one of the most familiar names in Argentine wine.

Visits & wine tourism

The wine museum at its former premises in Maipú is open to visitors.
