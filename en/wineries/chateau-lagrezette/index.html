Château Lagrezette

Renaissance castle in the Lot Valley near Cahors, whose vineyard is devoted mainly to Malbec in the Cahors appellation.

Château Lagrezette is in Caillac, a few kilometres from Cahors, in the Lot Valley in south-west France. The castle, medieval in origin and remodelled during the Renaissance, overlooks a vineyard on the river terraces. In 1980 the property was bought by Alain Dominique Perrin, known for his long career in luxury jewellery, who restored the building and undertook the replanting of the vineyard and the renovation of the winery.

The Cahors appellation is the historic home of Malbec, known locally as Côt or Auxerrois, which must make up at least 70% of the appellation’s reds. Traditionally associated with very deeply coloured “black wines”, Cahors has evolved towards reds with ripe fruit and more polished tannins. Lagrezette works along these lines, with concentrated, barrel-aged wines in which Malbec is almost always the sole or main variety.

The range has several tiers: from wines for earlier drinking to the château’s grand vin and single-plot cuvées such as Le Pigeonnier, one of the best-known reds of the appellation. Under the “By Lagrezette” label the house also makes wines outside the appellation, sold as Vin de France.

Visits & wine tourism

The castle and its cellars can be visited; the estate offers tours and tastings.
