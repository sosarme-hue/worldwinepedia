Rombauer Vineyards

Family winery in the Napa Valley founded in 1980 by Koerner and Joan Rombauer, best known for its rich, creamy Carneros Chardonnay.

Rombauer Vineyards was founded in 1980 by Koerner and Joan Rombauer, who had settled in the Napa Valley in the early 1970s. The winery was built on a wooded hillside north of St. Helena, beside the Silverado Trail, and from the beginning it was a family business; today it is run by their descendants. The family is related to Irma Rombauer, author of the celebrated American cookbook The Joy of Cooking.

The house owes much of its renown to its Carneros Chardonnay, from the cool area at the southern end of the Napa Valley and Sonoma County, beside San Pablo Bay. It is a white in an opulent style, fermented and aged in barrel and put through malolactic fermentation, which has found a very wide audience in the United States and has become one of the most sought-after Californian Chardonnays in shops and restaurants.

As well as Chardonnay, Rombauer makes Sauvignon Blanc, Zinfandel, Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon, from its own vineyards and from growers in the Napa Valley, Los Carneros and other parts of California, such as the Sierra Foothills for Zinfandel. The winery’s overall style favours ripe fruit, a broad texture and softness, very much in line with one strand of the Californian winemaking tradition, and it has kept that style consistent over the decades.

Visits & wine tourism

It welcomes visitors for tastings at its St. Helena winery, by prior reservation.
