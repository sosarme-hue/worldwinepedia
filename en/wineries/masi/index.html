Masi Agricola

The Boscaini family’s company, established in Valpolicella since 1772, specialising in the appassimento technique and known for its Costasera Amarone and for Campofiorin.

Masi takes its name from the Vaio dei Masi, a small valley in Valpolicella where the Boscaini family bought its first vineyards in 1772. The company has remained in the same family ever since and is based in Gargagnago, in the municipality of Sant’Ambrogio di Valpolicella, in the classic zone.

The house has made appassimento, the drying of grapes after harvest, the core of its identity. The grapes are laid on racks or in crates in ventilated lofts for several weeks or months, so that they lose water and concentrate sugars and aromas. This technique is used to make Amarone and Recioto della Valpolicella, and the company has set up its own technical team to study it.

In 1964 Masi launched Campofiorin, a red in which wine from fresh grapes is refermented on partially dried grapes, a technique akin to ripasso that the house has described as “double fermentation”. The wine became a benchmark among reds from the Verona area.

Besides its estates in Valpolicella and other parts of the Veneto, the company is associated with the estate of the Counts Serego Alighieri, descendants of Dante, and has a project in Argentina. Its best-known wines include Costasera Amarone, Campofiorin and its Recioto.
