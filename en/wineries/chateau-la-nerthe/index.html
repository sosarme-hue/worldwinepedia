Château La Nerthe

One of the oldest and most renowned estates in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, farmed organically, with vineyards in a single block around the château.

Château La Nerthe is one of the historic estates of Châteauneuf-du-Pape, in the southern Rhône valley between Avignon and Orange. Its existence as a wine property has been documented for centuries, and in the 18th century it was already among the first in the village to bottle its wine and make it known beyond the region, at a time when selling in bulk was still the norm.

In the 19th century, after the phylloxera crisis, one of its owners, Commandant Joseph Ducos, made a detailed study of the local varieties and advocated combining several grapes to balance the wine, an idea that influenced the definition of the appellation, which today authorises a long list of varieties. Since 1985 the estate has belonged to the Richard family, which has restored the château and modernised the winery.

The vineyard forms a large continuous block around the building, something unusual in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, and is farmed organically. The soils combine the famous rounded pebbles, sands and clays. Grenache dominates, accompanied by Syrah, Mourvèdre and other traditional varieties, while the whites are made from varieties such as Roussanne, Clairette and Grenache Blanc.

The house style seeks a balance between the sun-drenched richness of Grenache and the freshness and structure brought by the other varieties. Besides the red and white Châteauneuf-du-Pape, La Nerthe makes special cuvées such as the Cuvée des Cadettes, from old vines.
