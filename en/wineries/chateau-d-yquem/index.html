Château d’Yquem

The most famous estate in Sauternes and the only Premier Cru Supérieur in the 1855 classification, renowned for its sweet wines made from nobly rotten grapes.

Château d’Yquem stands on a hill in the commune of Sauternes, south of Bordeaux. In 1785, through marriage, the estate passed to the Lur-Saluces family, who kept it for more than two centuries. In the 1855 classification of the white wines of Sauternes and Barsac it was the only one ranked Premier Cru Supérieur. Since 1999 it has been majority-owned by the LVMH group.

The vineyard is planted with Sémillon, the dominant variety, and Sauvignon Blanc. The local microclimate, with autumn mists formed where the Ciron meets the Garonne, encourages the development of Botrytis cinerea, noble rot, which concentrates the sugars and acids in the grapes.

Harvesting is done by hand in several successive passes through the vineyard, picking only the bunches or berries at the right stage of botrytisation. The wine is fermented and aged in barrel for a long period. In vintages where quality is not judged sufficient, the château does not release its wine.

As well as the great sweet wine, Yquem makes a dry white, “Y” (Ygrec). Yquem’s wines are known for their ageing potential, which can extend over many decades.

The estate’s vineyard lies in a single block surrounding the château.
