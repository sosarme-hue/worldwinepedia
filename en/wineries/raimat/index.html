Winery

Raimat

Large wine estate near Lleida, acquired in 1914 by Manuel Raventós of the Codorníu family, and a pioneer of the Costers del Segre D.O.

Raimat's story begins in 1914, when Manuel Raventós, of the family that owned Codorníu, bought a vast estate of arid, saline land north-west of Lleida, crowned by an old castle. With the arrival of water from the Aragon and Catalonia Canal and long work to improve the soils, the estate was turned into a farm in which the vineyard takes centre stage. A village, also called Raimat, grew up around it.

Architecture

The historic winery was designed by the architect Joan Rubió i Bellver, a disciple of Gaudí, and is cited as one of the first buildings in Spain built in reinforced concrete. The estate's name is associated with the legend of the bunch of grapes and the hand (raïm and mà in Catalan) shown on the castle's coat of arms.

Vineyard and style

The climate is dry and continental, with sharp temperature swings and little rain, which favours healthy viticulture. Raimat was among the first Spanish wineries to plant varieties such as Chardonnay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, and played a prominent role in the birth of the Costers del Segre D.O. In recent years the estate has committed to organic viticulture and sustainability. Its range includes fruity, approachable whites, rosés and reds, as well as base wines for sparkling wine.

Visits & wine tourism

The estate offers visits to the winery and vineyard, with a tasting of its wines.
