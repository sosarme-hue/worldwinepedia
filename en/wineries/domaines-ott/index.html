Domaines Ott

Provençal house founded by Marcel Ott in the early 20th century, a pioneer of quality rosé and owned by Louis Roederer since 2004.

Marcel Ott, an agronomist of Alsatian origin, arrived in Provence in the late 19th century and in 1912 bought Château de Selle, in Taradeau, inland in the Var département. From that estate the family built Domaines Ott, which over time added two more properties: Clos Mireille, by the sea near La Londe-les-Maures, and Château Romassan, in the Bandol appellation.

Domaines Ott was among the first producers to champion Provence rosé as a quality wine, at a time when it was considered a simple wine. The house is also known for the shape of its bottle, inspired by amphorae, designed in the 1930s and now its trademark.

The main varieties are Mediterranean: Grenache, Cinsault, Syrah and Mourvèdre for the rosés and reds, and Sémillon and Rolle (Vermentino) for the whites, among others. The rosés are made by direct pressing or brief maceration, aiming for a pale colour and a delicate profile.

In 2004 the Ott family sold a majority stake to Champagne Louis Roederer, and family members remained involved in management. As well as the wines from its three estates, the house sells the more affordable BY.OTT range.

The wines of the three estates are sold under each property’s name and reflect their differences in soil and climate, between inland Var and the Mediterranean coast.
