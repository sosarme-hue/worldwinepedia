Maison Louis Latour

Family-owned Burgundy house with vineyards in Aloxe-Corton since the late 18th century, closely linked to the Grand Cru Corton-Charlemagne.

The Latour family has owned vineyards in Aloxe-Corton since the late 18th century, and the house takes 1797 as its reference date. In the 19th century the family added a négociant business, based in Beaune, to its activity as growers. In 1891 it acquired Château Corton Grancey in Aloxe-Corton, whose 18th-century winery is considered one of the first in Burgundy designed to work by gravity.

Louis Latour is one of the main landowners on the hill of Corton, in both the red Grand Cru Corton and the white Corton-Charlemagne, which is one of its best-known wines. The house is still family-owned and runs its own cooperage, which makes some of the barrels in which its wines are aged.

As well as wines from the Côte d’Or, Latour makes wines from the Mâconnais and the Côte Chalonnaise, and it pioneered the planting of Chardonnay in the Ardèche, in southern France, in the late 1970s.

The house style stands out above all in its Chardonnay whites, broad in texture and carefully aged, and in classically styled Pinot Noir reds.

The house remains in the hands of the Latour family, which has run it for generations. Aloxe-Corton, where the château stands, is one of the best-known villages of the Côte de Beaune.
