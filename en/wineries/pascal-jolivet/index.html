Winery

Pascal Jolivet

Upper Loire house founded in 1987, specialising in Sauvignon Blanc from Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé.

Pascal Jolivet, from a family involved in the wine trade, founded his own house in 1987, a recent date by local standards. Since then it has become one of the most widely available names in the Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé appellations, which face each other on the two banks of the Loire in central France.

The house combines its own vineyards with grapes from local growers, across the different soil types typical of the Upper Loire: the limestone marls known as terres blanches, the limestone called caillottes, and flint (silex). The latter is particularly associated with Pouilly-Fumé and some parts of Sancerre, and is often linked to mineral, smoky notes.

Jolivet advocates low-intervention winemaking, with fermentations using indigenous yeasts and ageing on lees, and works mainly in stainless-steel tanks to preserve the fruit and freshness of Sauvignon Blanc. As well as the whites, it makes red and rosé Sancerre from Pinot Noir and single-plot wines.

The house style is characterised by clean, aromatic whites with marked acidity, true to the classic profile of Loire Sauvignon.

In the Upper Loire, the whites of Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé are made exclusively from Sauvignon Blanc, while the reds and rosés of Sancerre come from Pinot Noir. The climate, under continental influence, with cold winters and mild summers, favours wines with lively acidity and fresh aromas.
