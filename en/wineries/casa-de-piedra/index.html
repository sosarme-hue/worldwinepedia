Winery

Casa de Piedra

Winery founded in 1997 by the winemaker Hugo D’Acosta in San Antonio de las Minas, in the southern Valle de Guadalupe. It is one of the wineries that shaped the renewal of artisan wine in Baja California, with its red Vino de Piedra as its emblem.

Casa de Piedra is the personal project of the winemaker Hugo D’Acosta, one of the most influential figures in contemporary Mexican wine, and his brother Alberto. After studying oenology in Europe and working as a winemaker in Baja California, Hugo D’Acosta founded the winery in 1997 in San Antonio de las Minas, at the southern end of the Valle de Guadalupe, towards Ensenada. The building, made of local stone, gives the house its name and reflects its commitment to architecture integrated into the landscape.

The winery makes a limited production from grapes from selected vineyards in Baja California, in a semi-arid Mediterranean climate and on poor, sandy soils of granitic origin. Its philosophy seeks wines that reflect the character of the valley, with ripe fruit but also freshness and measured oak ageing. Besides his work at Casa de Piedra, Hugo D’Acosta set up a workshop-school in the valley, known as La Escuelita, which has served to train new winemakers and has made it easier for numerous small projects to emerge, so his influence in the region extends well beyond his own winery.

The house’s most emblematic wine is Vino de Piedra, a barrel-aged red blend of Tempranillo and Cabernet Sauvignon, regarded as one of the benchmark reds of the Valle de Guadalupe. The winery also makes Piedra del Sol, a Chardonnay white, and other wines in small quantities. The style combines the ripeness of the Baja California climate with a pursuit of structure, balance and ageing potential.
