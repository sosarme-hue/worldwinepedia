Jacob's Creek

Australian brand born in the Barossa, where Johann Gramp planted his first vines in 1847 beside Jacob's Creek. Today it is one of the most widely distributed Australian wine labels in the world.

The history of Jacob's Creek dates back to 1847, when the Bavarian immigrant Johann Gramp planted vines on the banks of a small Barossa stream called Jacob's Creek, near the town of Rowland Flat. The family business that grew out of that vineyard, long known as Orlando, became over the years one of the major producers of South Australia.

The Jacob's Creek brand was launched in the 1970s with a blended red designed to offer a reliable, approachable Australian wine. Its success was swift, first in Australia and then in export markets such as the United Kingdom, and it did a great deal to introduce the fruit-forward, straightforward style of Australian wine to drinkers abroad. Today the brand belongs to the Pernod Ricard group.

The best-known range is Classic, made up of affordable varietals and blends produced from grapes grown in different parts of south-eastern Australia, which makes it possible to keep a consistent style from one vintage to the next. Alongside it there are more ambitious lines with a more precise regional origin, such as Barossa reds or Rieslings and sparkling wines from cooler areas, and the Double Barrel series, which finishes its ageing in barrels that previously held whisky.

The house style as a whole aims for clean, ripe fruit, balance and easy drinking, which explains why its wines are among Australia's best sellers in supermarkets and online shops in many countries.

Visits & wine tourism

The Jacob's Creek visitor centre in the Barossa offers tastings and activities around the history of the brand.
