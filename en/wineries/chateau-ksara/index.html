Château Ksara

The oldest winery of modern Lebanon, founded in 1857 by the Jesuits near Zahlé, in the Bekaa Valley. It is known for its underground ageing galleries and a wide range of reds, whites and rosés.

Château Ksara was founded in 1857 by the Jesuit order on land at Ksara, near the town of Zahlé, in the central Bekaa. The priests planted varieties of French origin, among them Cinsault, and began making dry wine at a time when local production was mainly geared towards sweet wine for religious use and towards distillation. Their work is regarded as the beginning of modern viticulture in Lebanon.

One of the winery’s best-known features is its underground galleries, a network of caves of ancient origin that the Jesuits fitted out and extended, and which naturally maintain a stable temperature and humidity for ageing wine. In the 1970s the Jesuits sold the winery to a group of Lebanese investors, who have run it as a private company ever since.

Ksara works with its own vineyards and those of partner growers in different parts of the Bekaa. Its range is broad, from young everyday wines to aged reds in a Bordeaux or Mediterranean mould, such as the red that bears the house name or the Réserve du Couvent, as well as whites such as the Blanc de Blancs, rosés and some sweet wines. The winery also produces arak. Thanks to its history and size, it is one of the benchmarks of Lebanese wine both on the home market and for export.

Visits & wine tourism

The winery welcomes visitors, who can tour its underground ageing galleries and taste its wines.
