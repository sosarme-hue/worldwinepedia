Winery

Bodega Norton

Historic Luján de Cuyo winery founded in 1895 by the British engineer Edmund James Palmer Norton. Since 1989 it has belonged to the Austrian Swarovski family.

Bodega Norton owes its name to Edmund James Palmer Norton, a British engineer who came to Mendoza in connection with railway construction and in 1895 planted vineyards and founded a winery in Perdriel, on the banks of the Mendoza River. It was one of the first wineries in the area to back French varieties. During the twentieth century the company passed through several hands, and in 1989 it was acquired by Gernot Langes-Swarovski, of the Austrian family known for crystal, who undertook the modernisation of the facilities and the renewal of the vineyards. His son Michael Halstrick later ran the company.

The winery has its own vineyards in various parts of Luján de Cuyo, such as Perdriel and Agrelo, and in the Uco Valley, which allows it to make both widely distributed young wines and more ambitious reds from old vines. Its range is broad: it includes varietals, Malbec reservas, Bordeaux-style blends, single-plot wines, traditional-method sparkling wines and a late-harvest white that is very popular in Argentina.

The house style seeks to combine the ripe fruit typical of Luján de Cuyo with a measured use of oak. Norton is one of the best-known Argentine wineries abroad and one of the historic benchmarks of Mendoza Malbec.

Visits & wine tourism

The winery offers tours and tastings, usually by prior booking.
