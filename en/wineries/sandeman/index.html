Winery

Sandeman

House founded in London in 1790 by the Scot George Sandeman, famous for its caped and hatted “Don” figure and now part of the Portuguese group Sogrape.

Sandeman was founded in London in 1790 by George Sandeman, a young Scot who began trading in Port and Sherry. The firm soon established itself in Portugal and Spain, and for more than two centuries it was one of the great exporting houses for both fortified wines. The Sandeman family ran the company for several generations.

The house is known throughout the world for its image: the silhouette of “The Don”, a figure dressed in a Portuguese student’s cape and a wide-brimmed Spanish-style hat, alluding to the two countries of its wines. This logo, created in the 1920s, is regarded as one of the first great brand symbols in the world of wine.

Since 2002 Sandeman has belonged to Sogrape, Portugal’s largest family-owned wine group. Its ageing lodges stand on the banks of the Douro in Vila Nova de Gaia, opposite the city of Porto, and in the Douro valley the house works with Quinta do Seixo, near Pinhão, where a significant share of its wines is made.

The range covers every style of Port: rubies such as Fine Ruby and Founders Reserve, age-dated tawnies of 10, 20, 30 and 40 years, Late Bottled Vintage, Vintage and whites. The house style aims for approachable, well-defined wines in the everyday categories and careful ageing in the age-dated tawnies.

Visits & wine tourism

The lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia welcome visitors with guided tours and tastings, and Quinta do Seixo, in the Douro, also has a visitor centre.
