Winery

M. Chapoutier

Tain-l’Hermitage house dating back to 1808, a major landowner on the hill of Hermitage and a pioneer of biodynamics and of braille labels.

The Chapoutier house has its roots in 1808 in Tain-l’Hermitage, on the banks of the Rhône opposite Tournon. The family gradually acquired vineyards on the hill of Hermitage until it became one of its main landowners. In 1990 Michel Chapoutier took over and transformed the house: he converted the vineyards to biodynamic farming and gave priority to single-plot wines.

Since 1996 Chapoutier has printed its labels in braille, in tribute to Maurice de la Sizeranne, a former owner of one of its Hermitage plots and a champion of education for blind people; his name is borne by the red Hermitage Monier de la Sizeranne.

The range covers the great appellations of the northern Rhône — Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph, Côte-Rôtie, Condrieu — as well as Châteauneuf-du-Pape, with La Bernardine, and other southern areas. Outside the Rhône, the house has vineyards in Roussillon, Alsace and Australia.

The reds are based on Syrah in the north and Grenache in the south, and the Hermitage whites on Marsanne. The house style seeks to express the soil of each plot, with wines of precise fruit and good structure.

The hill of Hermitage, granitic in its western part and with more varied soils towards the east, is divided into highly reputed lieux-dits, and Chapoutier makes several single-plot wines from them.

Visits & wine tourism

It has a tasting room and shop in Tain-l’Hermitage.
