Tasca d'Almerita

Sicilian family that has owned the Regaleali estate, in the interior of the island, since the nineteenth century, producing wines from several areas, from Etna to the island of Salina.

Tasca d’Almerita’s history is bound up with Regaleali, a large farming estate in the interior of Sicily, between the provinces of Palermo and Caltanissetta, which the Tasca family acquired in the nineteenth century. For a long time the property was devoted to mixed farming, and wine gained importance over the twentieth century until it became the main activity.

Regaleali lies at a considerable altitude for Sicily, with clay and limestone hills and a marked difference between day and night temperatures, conditions that favour fresh wines for the latitude. Native varieties such as Nero d’Avola, Perricone, Catarratto and Inzolia are grown there, alongside international varieties. The estate falls under the Contea di Sclafani DOC.

Over the years the family has extended its presence to other parts of the island: Tascante on Etna; Capofaro on the island of Salina, devoted to Malvasia; the island of Mozia, off Marsala, where it grows Grillo; and Sallier de La Tour, near Palermo.

Its best-known wines include Rosso del Conte, a red from old Nero d’Avola vines at Regaleali, the Lamùri Nero d’Avola and the whites and rosés of the Regaleali line.
