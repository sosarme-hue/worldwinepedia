Weingut Dr. Bürklin-Wolf

Large family estate in the Mittelhaardt, in the Pfalz, with vines in the finest vineyards of Forst, Deidesheim and Ruppertsberg, farmed biodynamically and classified according to its own system.

Bürklin-Wolf is one of the historic estates of the Pfalz and one of the largest family-owned wine estates in Germany. It is based in Wachenheim, in the Mittelhaardt, and has passed down the generations within the same family; it is now run by Bettina Bürklin-von Guradze and her family. During the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the house was one of the small group of great Mittelhaardt estates that brought international fame to Pfalz Riesling.

Its vineyards include plots in some of the region’s most highly regarded sites: Kirchenstück, Jesuitengarten, Pechstein and Ungeheuer in Forst; Hohenmorgen and Kalkofen in Deidesheim; and Gaisböhl in Ruppertsberg. From the 1990s the estate adopted its own Burgundy-inspired classification of its vineyards, with the designations G.C. (Grand Cru) and P.C. (Premier Cru), based on an old Bavarian classification from the nineteenth century, when the Pfalz belonged to Bavaria.

In the first decade of the twenty-first century the estate converted to biodynamic viticulture, of which it is now one of the leading exponents in Germany. Production focuses on dry Riesling, with wines that aim to express the character of each soil, from sandstone and limestone to the basalt of the Pechstein. To a lesser extent it also makes Rieslings with sweetness and some wines from other varieties.
