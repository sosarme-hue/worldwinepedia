Yatir Winery

A winery created in the early 2000s together with the Carmel winery at the southern end of the Judean Hills, on the edge of the Negev. Its high-altitude vineyards around the Yatir Forest yield highly concentrated reds, among them Yatir Forest.

Yatir Winery was born in the early 2000s as a project involving the historic Carmel winery, with the aim of making high-quality wines from vineyards at the southern end of the Judean Hills, where they meet the Negev desert. The winery was built next to the archaeological site of Tel Arad, an area with abundant remains of ancient winemaking.

The vineyards are spread around the Yatir Forest, a large man-made forest planted in a semi-arid area. They lie at high altitudes for the region, which brings cool nights and a marked difference between day and night temperatures, while the scant rainfall makes irrigation necessary. The soils combine loess, limestone and stony areas. These conditions, unusual for a quality vineyard, produce thick-skinned grapes of deep colour and great concentration.

The range focuses on reds. The main wine is Yatir Forest, a barrel-aged blend based on Cabernet Sauvignon, and the winery also makes other reds and some whites under the Yatir name. The wines are kosher. The house style combines ripe fruit and power with the freshness brought by altitude. The creation of Yatir showed that the high areas in the south of the country, long considered too arid for quality wine, could produce wines of character, and it encouraged further plantings in similar sites.
