Domaine de Villaine

Domaine in Bouzeron, in the Côte Chalonnaise, founded by Aubert and Pamela de Villaine and known for its Aligoté whites from the Bouzeron appellation.

Domaine de Villaine, long known as Domaine A. et P. de Villaine, is based in Bouzeron, a small village in the north of the Côte Chalonnaise, in Burgundy. It was founded in 1971 by Aubert de Villaine, for decades co-director of the Domaine de la Romanée-Conti, and his American wife Pamela, who settled in this then little-regarded area.

The domaine became a benchmark for the Côte Chalonnaise. It was among the first in the area to adopt organic farming and championed the potential of the Aligoté variety, particularly its form known as aligoté doré, on the slopes of Bouzeron. This work contributed to the creation of the Bouzeron appellation, the only village appellation in Burgundy devoted to Aligoté.

Besides Bouzeron, the domaine makes whites and reds from Chardonnay and Pinot Noir in the Bourgogne Côte Chalonnaise, Rully and Mercurey appellations, with single-plot wines reflecting the diversity of the area’s limestone and clay soils. For years day-to-day management has been in the hands of Pierre de Benoist, Aubert de Villaine’s nephew.

The house style is characterised by precise, fresh wines without artifice, which prioritise the expression of terroir over oak or overripeness, and which often reward a few years of patience in the cellar.
