Harveys

Historic firm of British origin, founded as a wine merchant in Bristol in the late eighteenth century, famous for the sweet sherry Harveys Bristol Cream.

Harveys is one of the best-known names in sherry in the English-speaking world. The firm was born in Bristol, in south-west England, in the late eighteenth century as a wine import and trading business, and took its name from the Harvey family, who ran it for generations. For centuries Bristol was one of the main ports through which sherry entered England, and the city developed a tradition of merchants' own blends.

Bristol Milk and Bristol Cream

Bristol merchants sold a sweet sherry known as Bristol Milk. According to the story told by the house, in the nineteenth century a lady visitor tasted a new, richer blend and remarked that if the other wine was milk, this one was the cream; hence the name Bristol Cream, which would become one of the best-selling sherries in the world. For a long time Harveys bought and blended wines from various Jerez bodegas.

In Jerez

In the twentieth century the firm acquired its own bodegas and soleras in Jerez de la Frontera, where its wines are made today. After passing through several international groups, the brand now belongs to the Bodegas Fundador group. Besides Bristol Cream, the range includes finos, amontillados, olorosos and Pedro Ximénez, as well as very long-aged wines. The wines are aged by the traditional criaderas and solera system, and the sweet blends combine dry wines with Pedro Ximénez.
