Tyrrell's

Family winery in the Hunter Valley founded in 1858 by Edward Tyrrell. It is one of the great references for Hunter Semillon and was a pioneer of Chardonnay in Australia.

Edward Tyrrell, an English immigrant, obtained land at Pokolbin, in the Lower Hunter, in 1858. He built a slab hut that still survives and planted his first vines, and in the following years the family began making wine. For generations the winery sold much of its production in bulk to other companies, until, in the mid-twentieth century, Murray Tyrrell drove bottling under the family name and direct sales at the winery.

Tyrrell’s has been one of the houses that have done most to define the classic style of Hunter Semillon: picked early, made without oak and able to age for decades. Its Vat 1 Hunter Semillon is one of the best-known examples. In the early 1970s the winery released Vat 47 Chardonnay, regarded as one of the first varietal Chardonnays sold in Australia, at a time when the variety was almost unknown in the country.

The family keeps very old vineyards in the Hunter, some planted in the nineteenth century, which yield single-vineyard Shiraz and Semillon. The winery also owns vines in other regions, such as McLaren Vale and Heathcote. Tyrrell’s remains an independent company run by descendants of the founder, with Bruce Tyrrell and his children at the helm. Many of its Semillons are released after several years in bottle to show their development.

Visits & wine tourism

The Pokolbin winery has a cellar door and offers guided tours.
