Winery

Viña Almaviva

Winery born of a partnership between Concha y Toro and Baron Philippe de Rothschild, with vineyards in Puente Alto, in Alto Maipo. It makes a single grand vin, Almaviva, whose first vintage was 1996.

Almaviva is the result of an alliance between two great wine houses: Chile’s Concha y Toro and Bordeaux’s Baron Philippe de Rothschild, owner of Château Mouton Rothschild. The agreement was signed in 1997, with the aim of creating a top-flight Chilean wine combining the terroir of Alto Maipo with the tradition of the great Bordeaux châteaux. The first vintage was 1996.

The name comes from Count Almaviva, a character in Beaumarchais’s The Marriage of Figaro, later immortalised in Mozart’s opera, and the label shows the wine’s name in the author’s handwriting. The design also features a motif inspired by the kultrún, the Mapuche ceremonial drum, as a tribute to Chile’s indigenous culture.

The vineyards are in Puente Alto, on an alluvial terrace of the Maipo River at the foot of the Andes, with stony, well-drained soils and nights cooled by mountain air. Mainly Cabernet Sauvignon is grown there, accompanied by Carmenère, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot and Merlot. The winery, of contemporary architecture, was designed to make a single grand vin in the manner of a Bordeaux château, based on careful plot selection. Over time a second wine has been added, Epu, whose name means “two” in the Mapuche language. Much of Almaviva is sold through the Bordeaux négociant network.
