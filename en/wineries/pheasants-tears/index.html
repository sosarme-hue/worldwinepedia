Winery

Pheasant's Tears

A winery in Sighnaghi, Kakheti, founded in 2007 by the American painter John Wurdeman and the Georgian grower Gela Patalishvili. It makes all its wines in qvevri, from numerous native varieties.

Pheasant's Tears was born in 2007 out of the friendship between John Wurdeman, an American painter living in Georgia, and Gela Patalishvili, a grower from a Kakheti family with a long winemaking tradition. Their aim was to preserve and promote traditional Georgian winemaking in qvevri and the great diversity of the country’s native varieties, at a time when many of them were in decline. The winery’s name comes from a Georgian folk tale according to which only an exceptional wine could make a pheasant weep with joy.

The winery is based in Sighnaghi, a small hilltop town overlooking the Alazani valley. It works with its own vineyards and those of local growers in Kakheti and also makes wines from grapes grown in other regions of Georgia. All its wines are fermented and aged in buried qvevri, with native yeasts and minimal intervention.

Its wines include an amber Rkatsiteli made with long skin maceration and a red Saperavi, as well as wines from lesser-known varieties such as Kisi, Mtsvane, Chinuri, Tavkveri and Shavkapito. The winery has played a notable part in the international spread of qvevri wines and Georgian amber wines, and has encouraged the revival of minority varieties. In Sighnaghi it runs a restaurant devoted to Georgian cuisine, where its wines can be tasted.

Visits & wine tourism

In Sighnaghi the winery has a restaurant where its wines can be tasted with Georgian food.
