Dr. Konstantin Frank

Family winery founded in 1962 on the shores of Keuka Lake by the scientist Konstantin Frank, who showed that the European vine could be grown in the cold climate of the north-eastern United States.

Konstantin Frank was born in 1899 into an ethnic German community in the Odessa region, in present-day Ukraine, and trained as a scientist specialising in viticulture. There he gained experience in growing vines in conditions of very cold winters. He emigrated to the United States in the early 1950s and worked at the agricultural experiment station in Geneva, New York, linked to Cornell University.

At that time it was believed that Vitis vinifera could not survive north-eastern winters, and the Finger Lakes vineyards were planted with American varieties and hybrids. Frank argued the opposite: the problem was not the cold itself but the lack of suitable rootstocks and appropriate growing practices. With the support of Charles Fournier of the Gold Seal winery, he planted European vines on the slopes of Keuka Lake, and in 1962 he founded his own winery, Vinifera Wine Cellars, near Hammondsport.

His successes with Riesling, Chardonnay, Gewürztraminer and the Georgian Rkatsiteli encouraged many other producers and changed the course of viticulture in the region and in the eastern United States. The winery remains in the hands of the Frank family, which has added traditional-method sparkling wines under the Château Frank name. Riesling, in dry and semi-dry versions, remains the core of the range.

Konstantin Frank died in 1985, but his name remains associated with the birth of vinifera viticulture in the east of the country.

It has a tasting room overlooking Keuka Lake.
