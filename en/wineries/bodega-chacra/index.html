Bodega Chacra

Small winery in Río Negro, Patagonia, founded in 2004 by Piero Incisa della Rocchetta. It is devoted mainly to old-vine Pinot Noir, farmed according to biodynamic principles.

Bodega Chacra was founded in 2004 by Piero Incisa della Rocchetta, a member of the Italian family that owns Tenuta San Guido, the Bolgheri estate that makes Sassicaia. During a trip to Patagonia he discovered in Mainqué, in the Upper Río Negro Valley, an ungrafted Pinot Noir vineyard planted in 1932 that had survived the abandonment of many vineyards in the region. Captivated by its potential, he bought the estate and set about restoring it.

In Argentina the word chacra refers to a small farm. The winery works with several plots of different ages, all in the Mainqué area, and farms its vineyards according to the principles of biodynamic agriculture, without herbicides or synthetic products. The wines are made with minimal intervention, fermentation with indigenous yeasts and ageing in used barrels or other vessels that do not mark the wine with oak aromas.

Its best-known wines bear names that refer to the vineyards of origin: Treinta y Dos, from the plot planted in 1932; Cincuenta y Cinco, from a vineyard dating from 1955; and Barda, made from younger vineyards. The winery has also made Patagonian Chardonnay in collaboration with the Burgundy producer Jean-Marc Roulot. Chacra has played an important role in restoring the prestige of Patagonian Pinot Noir.
