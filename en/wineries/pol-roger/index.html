Champagne Pol Roger

Family Champagne house founded in Épernay in 1849, independent and famous for its link with Winston Churchill, to whom it dedicates its prestige cuvée.

Pol Roger was founded in 1849 by Pol Roger, a young merchant from Aÿ who settled in Épernay. His descendants, who added the founder’s first name to their surname, have run the house ever since, and Pol Roger remains one of the few large Champagne houses still independent and in the hands of the founding family.

The house is based on the Avenue de Champagne in Épernay, beneath which run several kilometres of galleries dug into the chalk, some at great depth, where low temperatures favour a slow second fermentation and a fine mousse. Pol Roger still practises hand riddling of its bottles.

Its relationship with Winston Churchill is famous. The British statesman, a great lover of champagne, enjoyed a close friendship with Odette Pol-Roger, and after his death in 1965 the house added a black band as a sign of mourning to the labels of its brut destined for the United Kingdom. In 1984 it launched the Sir Winston Churchill cuvée, with the 1975 vintage, designed to reflect the style the statesman enjoyed.

The range also includes the Brut Réserve, known as “White Foil” from the colour of its capsule, a Rosé, a Blanc de Blancs and a vintage brut. The house style is associated with fine, elegant and very consistent champagnes.
