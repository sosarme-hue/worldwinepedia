Weingut Gunderloch

Estate in Nackenheim, on Rheinhessen’s Roter Hang, founded in 1890 and known for its Rieslings from the Nackenheimer Rothenberg vineyard, on red Permian soils.

The estate was founded in 1890 by Carl Gunderloch, a banker from Mainz, who acquired vineyards in Nackenheim on the banks of the Rhine. It has remained in the hands of his descendants ever since. In the last decades of the twentieth century Agnes and Fritz Hasselbach brought the house to international prominence, at a time when Rheinhessen was known mainly for high-volume wines; it is now run by their son Johannes Hasselbach.

The vineyards lie on the Roter Hang, the strip of steep slopes stretching between Nackenheim and Nierstein, whose red soil of Permian clay and sandstone (Rotliegend) is unique in the region. The estate’s most emblematic vineyard is the Nackenheimer Rothenberg, facing south-east above the Rhine, beside which lie other Nierstein sites such as Pettenthal and Hipping. Riesling is practically the only variety in the best vineyards.

Gunderloch makes Riesling in a wide range of styles: dry single-vineyard wines, such as the Grosses Gewächs from the Rothenberg and Pettenthal; lightly sweet wines, such as the Jean-Baptiste Kabinett; and, in favourable years, nobly sweet wines, which played a decisive part in the house’s reputation. The wines are characterised by ripe fruit, spicy notes and a mineral accent attributed to the red soils.
