Recanati Winery

Israeli winery founded in 2000 in the Hefer Valley, known for its reds from Mediterranean varieties such as old vine Carignan.

Recanati Winery was founded in 2000 by Lenny Recanati, an Israeli businessman, in the Hefer Valley, on the coastal plain between Tel Aviv and Haifa. The winery was created at a time of profound renewal in Israeli wine, when a growing number of producers were committing to quality and to better viticulture.

Like many wineries in the country, Recanati does not depend on a single vineyard but works with plots in different areas, particularly in Upper Galilee and the Judean Hills, where altitude brings freshness. In its early years it stood out for reds and whites from international varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Chardonnay, but over time it has become one of the leading wineries in the revival of the Mediterranean varieties grown in Israel.

Its best-known wine in that line is the Wild Carignan from the Reserve range, made from old, dry-farmed bush vines, a heritage that for decades was used for simple wines. The house also works with Syrah, Petite Sirah and Marselan, and has helped to spark interest in some of the region's indigenous varieties. Its wines are certified kosher, like most Israeli wines.

Recanati's style seeks expressive, balanced reds with the ripe fruit typical of a Mediterranean climate but also freshness and a measured use of oak, reflecting the diversity of Israel's wine landscapes.
