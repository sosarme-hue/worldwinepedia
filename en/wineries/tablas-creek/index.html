Tablas Creek Vineyard

Paso Robles winery founded in 1989 by the Perrin family of Château de Beaucastel and the importer Robert Haas, devoted to Rhône varieties grown from plant material brought from France.

The Perrin family, owners of Château de Beaucastel in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, and Robert Haas, their importer in the United States, spent years looking for a place in California with conditions similar to those of the southern Rhône. In 1989 they bought a property in the hills west of Paso Robles, in what is now the Adelaida District AVA, attracted by its calcareous soils, its warm climate of sunny days and cool nights, and its rainfall pattern.

Instead of using the plant material available in California, they imported cuttings of Beaucastel’s varieties, which had to go through a long quarantine before they could be propagated. The winery set up its own nursery and has since spread these vines among many other American producers, helping to widen the presence of varieties such as Mourvèdre, Grenache, Counoise, Roussanne and Picpoul Blanc.

The range is built around Rhône-inspired blends: Esprit de Tablas, a red with a prominent share of Mourvèdre, and Esprit de Tablas Blanc, based on Roussanne, along with wines such as Côtes de Tablas and Patelin de Tablas, the latter also made with grapes from other local growers. The vineyard is farmed organically, and the winery has stood out for the detailed information it shares about its work.

The project is regarded as one of the most influential in spreading Rhône varieties in the United States and in establishing Paso Robles as a leading region for them.

Visits & wine tourism

It has a tasting room on the estate and offers vineyard tours.
