Weingut Knoll

Family estate in Unterloiben, in the Wachau, known for its Grüner Veltliner and Riesling from terraced sites such as the Loibenberg and the Schütt, and for its label depicting Saint Urban.

The Knoll family has grown vines in Unterloiben, at the eastern end of the Wachau near Dürnstein, for generations. From the second half of the 20th century, Emmerich Knoll made the estate one of the region’s benchmarks, and today it is run by the next generation of the family, who maintain a style very faithful to tradition.

The Wachau is a stretch of the Danube valley in Lower Austria, a UNESCO World Heritage site, where vines are grown on terraces held up by dry-stone walls over gneiss and other crystalline rocks, with loess deposits in the lower areas. Knoll owns parcels in some of the most highly regarded vineyards of the Loiben area, such as the Loibenberg, the Schütt and the Kellerberg, as well as in the Pfaffenberg, which lies in the Kremstal.

The house mainly makes Grüner Veltliner and Riesling in the three Wachau categories — Steinfeder, Federspiel and Smaragd — as well as sweet wines in some vintages. Winemaking is traditional, with fermentation in tank and in large old wooden casks.

Its labels, with a baroque image of Saint Urban, patron saint of winegrowers, have barely changed in decades and have become a hallmark of the estate. Knoll’s wines are known for their purity, concentration and great ageing potential.
