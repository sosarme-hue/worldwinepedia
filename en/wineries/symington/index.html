Symington Family Estates

Family group of Scottish origin, one of the leading Port producers, owner of historic houses such as Graham’s, Dow’s, Warre’s and Cockburn’s and of many quintas in the Douro.

The Symington family’s history in Portugal begins in 1882, when the Scot Andrew James Symington arrived in Porto to work in the wine trade. Over time the family became partners in historic houses such as Warre’s, regarded as the oldest of the British Port firms, and Dow’s, and in the following decades it steadily extended its presence: it acquired Graham’s in 1970 and Cockburn’s in 2010. Today the company is still in the hands of members of several generations of the family.

The group is one of the largest vineyard owners in the Douro. Each brand keeps its flagship quintas, such as Quinta do Bomfim, near Pinhão, linked to Dow’s; Quinta da Cavadinha, linked to Warre’s; and Quinta dos Malvedos, linked to Graham’s. In 1989 it added Quinta do Vesuvio, a large historic estate in the Douro Superior that gives its name to its own Ports and still wines.

As well as Ports in every style, from rubies and age-dated tawnies to Vintage, Symington makes still Douro wines, such as the Altano range and those of Quinta do Vesuvio. The company has promoted research programmes in viticulture and climate change adaptation, and it is certified as a B Corp. Each brand’s house keeps its own style, so that, for example, Dow’s Vintage Ports are usually described as drier and Graham’s as richer and sweeter.

Visits & wine tourism

Several of its houses welcome visitors, including the Graham’s and Cockburn’s lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia and Quinta do Bomfim in Pinhão, by prior booking.
