Château Cheval Blanc

Great Saint-Émilion estate on the border with Pomerol, famous for the high proportion of Cabernet Franc in its wine.

Château Cheval Blanc lies at the north-western edge of the Saint-Émilion appellation, next to the border with Pomerol. The estate was assembled in the 19th century by the Fourcaud-Laussac family, who kept it for more than a century. In 1998 it was acquired by Bernard Arnault and Belgian businessman Albert Frère.

Its soils combine gravel and clay, which is unusual in Saint-Émilion and closer to those of Pomerol. Cheval Blanc’s most distinctive feature is the importance of Cabernet Franc, which in many vintages makes up more than half of the blend, completed with Merlot. In 2011 a new winery designed by architect Christian de Portzamparc was inaugurated.

For decades it was one of the Premiers Grands Crus Classés “A” of Saint-Émilion, together with Château Ausone. In 2021 the estate decided not to apply for the revision of the classification, so its wines have since been sold without that mention.

As well as the grand vin, the estate makes the second wine Le Petit Cheval. The Cheval Blanc style combines rich fruit, freshness and aromatic finesse, with great ageing potential.

Its owners are also involved in projects outside Bordeaux, such as Cheval des Andes in Argentina.
