Veramonte

Casablanca Valley winery created in the 1990s by Agustín Huneeus. Known for its cool-climate whites and Pinot Noir, it is part of the Spanish group González Byass.

Veramonte was created in the 1990s by Agustín Huneeus, a Chilean businessman with a long career in the wine industry in both Chile and California, where he is known for his projects in the Napa Valley. Huneeus was one of the first to invest on a large scale in the Casablanca Valley, then a very new wine area, convinced that its cool climate, influenced by the Pacific Ocean, would make it possible to produce very fresh whites.

The winery was built in the eastern part of the valley, next to the road linking Santiago with Valparaíso, and its vineyards extend across the surrounding hills. Over time the Huneeus family also developed the Ritual brand, focused on Casablanca wines, and converted its vineyards to organic farming, with practices such as cover crops and reduced treatments.

Since the mid-2010s Veramonte has been part of the Spanish wine group González Byass, known for its sherries, which has maintained the winery’s focus on cool-climate wines. The range includes Casablanca Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, as well as reds made from grapes from other Chilean valleys, such as Colchagua. Veramonte was one of the brands that made Casablanca whites known internationally.
