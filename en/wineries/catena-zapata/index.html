Winery

Bodega Catena Zapata

Family winery in Mendoza founded in 1902 by the Italian immigrant Nicola Catena. Under Nicolás Catena Zapata it pioneered high-altitude Malbec and the international rise of Argentine wine.

The Catena story begins in 1902, when Nicola Catena, who had emigrated from Italy, planted his first Malbec vineyard in Mendoza. For several decades the family made wine for the domestic market, like so many wineries of the time. The great change came with Nicolás Catena Zapata, the founder’s grandson, who, after getting to know Californian winegrowing closely in the 1980s, decided to steer the company towards quality wines able to compete on international markets.

One of his best-known contributions was the exploration of altitude. In addition to its estates in Luján de Cuyo and Maipú, the family planted vineyards at greater altitude in the Uco Valley, among them the Adrianna vineyard in Gualtallary, planted in the early 1990s at about 1,500 metres. This work helped show that Malbec could produce fresh, complex and long-lived wines in Mendoza.

The main winery, in Agrelo, is a pyramid-shaped building inspired by Mayan architecture, surrounded by vineyards and overlooking the Andes. Today the company is run by the family, with Laura Catena, Nicolás’s daughter, as a leading figure; she also founded the Catena Institute of Wine, devoted to research into high-altitude viticulture and Malbec plant selection. The range extends from varietal wines to estate and single-plot reds.

Visits & wine tourism

The winery offers tours and tastings, usually by prior booking.
