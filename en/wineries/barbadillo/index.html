Bodegas Barbadillo

Family house in Sanlúcar de Barrameda founded in 1821, closely associated with manzanilla and known for Solear and its seasonal en rama manzanilla releases.

Bodegas Barbadillo was founded in 1821 by Benigno Barbadillo, who returned from Mexico and settled in Sanlúcar de Barrameda, at the mouth of the Guadalquivir. The house is still a family business, and its bodegas are spread across Sanlúcar's upper town, around the castle of Santiago.

Sanlúcar and manzanilla

The humidity and Atlantic breezes that reach Sanlúcar help the flor —the layer of yeast that protects the wine from oxidation— stay alive and thick all year round. This biological ageing gives manzanilla, a dry wine made from Palomino, lighter and more saline than Jerez fino, which may only be aged in Sanlúcar. Barbadillo is one of the houses that have done most to spread this style.

Wines

Its best-known manzanilla is Solear. The house was also a pioneer in marketing manzanilla en rama, bottled straight from the cask with minimal filtration, in seasonal releases. The range includes manzanilla pasada, amontillados, olorosos, palo cortados and Pedro Ximénez, as well as very old wines. Barbadillo also makes still white wines, among them the popular Castillo de San Diego, from the local area.

Visits & wine tourism

The Sanlúcar bodegas offer guided tours with tastings, and the house has a museum devoted to manzanilla.
