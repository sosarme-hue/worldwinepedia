Famille Hugel

Family-owned house in Riquewihr dating back to 1639, one of the best known in Alsace and a driving force behind the rules for Vendanges Tardives and Sélection de Grains Nobles.

The Hugel family has been established in Riquewihr, one of the best-preserved medieval villages in Alsace, since 1639, when Hans Ulrich Hugel settled there during the Thirty Years’ War. The house is still in the same place and is run by members of the family, which has worked in it for more than twelve generations.

Hugel combines its own vineyard, with plots on the best slopes of Riquewihr, including the Grand Cru Schoenenbourg, with grapes bought from growers it has worked with for a long time. It makes the classic Alsace varieties, above all Riesling, Gewurztraminer and Pinot Gris, in a generally dry style.

Jean Hugel, a leading figure of the house in the 20th century, played an important role in defining the Vendanges Tardives (late harvest) and Sélection de Grains Nobles (selection of nobly rotten berries) designations, officially regulated in the 1980s, categories in which Hugel is a benchmark producer.

The range is organised into several tiers, from the Classic wines and the Gentil blend up to wines from the family’s best vineyards and late-harvest sweet wines. Hugel’s yellow labels are highly recognisable.

Riquewihr, surrounded by vineyards, is one of the most visited villages on the Alsace Wine Route.
