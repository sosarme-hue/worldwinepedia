Yalumba

Winery founded in 1849 at Angaston by Samuel Smith and still in the hands of his descendants. It is one of Australia’s oldest family houses and a pioneer of Viognier in the country.

Yalumba was founded in 1849 by Samuel Smith, an English brewer who had emigrated to South Australia and bought land near Angaston, at the eastern edge of the Barossa Valley. The name comes from an Aboriginal language and is usually translated as “all the land around”. The company has remained in the hands of the founding family, now bearing the name Hill-Smith, and is regarded as one of the country’s oldest family wineries. The stone winery, with its clock tower, is one of the most recognisable buildings in the Barossa.

Yalumba stands out for some activities that are unusual for a winery: it runs its own cooperage, where barrels are made from air-dried staves, and a vine nursery that supplies plants to growers across the country. In the 1980s it was one of the first companies to plant Viognier in Australia, in the Eden Valley, and has played a decisive role in spreading this variety.

The range is very broad, extending from high-volume wines to old-vine Barossa reds. Among the best known is The Signature, a blend of Cabernet Sauvignon and Shiraz that is dedicated each vintage to a person linked to the company. Yalumba also makes wines from Coonawarra and other regions, as well as sparkling and fortified wines.

Visits & wine tourism

Yalumba has a cellar door at its historic winery in Angaston, in the Barossa.
