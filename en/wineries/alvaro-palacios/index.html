Winery

Álvaro Palacios

Álvaro Palacios's winery in Gratallops, one of the leading names in the revival of Priorat in the late 1980s, focused on old-vine Garnacha and Carignan grown on llicorella slate.

Álvaro Palacios, from a Rioja winemaking family (Palacios Remondo, in Alfaro), arrived in Priorat in the late 1980s. Together with other pioneers, including René Barbier, he set out to revive a district of steep vineyards that had been in decline for decades following phylloxera and rural depopulation. That small group of projects transformed the area's image and helped Priorat later become a Qualified Designation of Origin.

Vineyards

The estate works around Gratallops, on the characteristic llicorella, a dark, crumbly slate that forces roots to dig deep and gives very low yields. Many plots are farmed on terraces or steep slopes where mechanisation is almost impossible. The leading varieties are Garnacha and Carignan (Cariñena), often from old bush vines.

Style and wines

The range is tiered along lines similar to Burgundy: a regional wine (Camins del Priorat), a village wine from Gratallops (Gratallops Vi de la Vila), single-vineyard wines such as Finca Dofí and Les Aubaguetes, and the celebrated L'Ermita, from a north-facing slope of old Garnacha. Over the years the house style has moved towards fresher, more transparent wines, with less emphasis on oak and more expression of the soil. Álvaro Palacios also leads projects in Bierzo (Descendientes de J. Palacios, with his nephew Ricardo Pérez) and at the family winery in Rioja Oriental.
