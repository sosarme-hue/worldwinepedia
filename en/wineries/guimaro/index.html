Winery

Guímaro

Family winery in the Amandi subzone of Ribeira Sacra, run by Pedro Manuel Rodríguez, whose Mencía reds from terraced vineyards above the Sil are a benchmark for the appellation.

Guímaro is the project of the Rodríguez family in the municipality of Sober, in the province of Lugo, within the Amandi subzone, one of the five into which the Ribeira Sacra Denominación de Origen is divided. Like many families in the area, the Rodríguez grew vines and made wine for their own consumption and for local sale, until in the early 1990s they began bottling under their own label. The winery is run by Pedro Manuel Rodríguez, who has championed work with old vineyards and single-plot wines.

Amandi lies on the slopes that fall towards the river Sil, one of the most spectacular vineyard landscapes in Europe, with stone terraces built over centuries on very steep gradients. The soils are slate and granite, and the southern exposure of many plots favours ripening in a climate of transition between Atlantic and continental influences. Vineyard work is almost always manual, in what is locally known as heroic viticulture.

Mencía is the main variety, accompanied in the old vineyards by other traditional varieties such as Brancellao, Merenzao and Sousón. The house’s best-known wine is the red Guímaro, a young, fresh Mencía. The range also includes a Godello white and single-plot reds such as Camiño Real and Finca Meixemán, made from old vines, in some cases fermented with stems.

Guímaro’s style seeks reds with red fruit, freshness and the mineral imprint of slate.
