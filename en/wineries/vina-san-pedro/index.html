Viña San Pedro

One of Chile’s historic wineries, founded in 1865 in Molina, in the Curicó Valley, and known internationally for its Gato Negro brand.

Viña San Pedro was founded in 1865 by the Correa Albano brothers in Molina, in the Curicó Valley, in central Chile, south of Santiago. It is one of the oldest wineries in the country and, over the course of the twentieth century, became one of its largest producers and exporters. Its Molina vineyards, on a broad plain between the Andes and the Coastal Range, are among the most extensive in Chile.

The house is best known for Gato Negro, a brand of affordable varietal wines with a black cat on the label, which is sold in many countries and is one of the most widely distributed Chilean wine brands in the world. Alongside it, the winery produces other lines such as 35 Sur, the more ambitious 1865 series, and top-end wines such as Cabo de Hornos, a Cabernet Sauvignon red named after the southernmost tip of the American continent.

Viña San Pedro belongs to a larger Chilean wine group that brings together several wineries, and it works with its own vineyards and those of growers in different valleys, from Elqui in the north to areas in the south of the country. Its range thus covers the main varieties grown in Chile, such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Carmenère, Merlot, Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay, in a style that runs from fruity everyday wines to oak-aged reds intended for cellaring, which together make it one of the best-known names in Chilean wine abroad.
