Maison Louis Jadot

Beaune house founded in 1859, one of Burgundy’s major négociants-éleveurs, with its own vineyards from the Côte de Nuits to Beaujolais.

Louis Henry Denis Jadot founded the house in Beaune in 1859, building on vineyards the family already owned, including plots in the Beaune Premier Cru Clos des Ursules. His successors developed the négociant-éleveur business, which consists of buying grapes or wine from growers and then making, ageing and bottling them under the house name, combined with the farming of an ever-larger estate vineyard.

In 1985 the house was acquired by the Kopf family, owners of the American importer Kobrand, who kept the management and technical team in place. For more than four decades winemaking was led by Jacques Lardière, a well-known figure in Burgundy. The house is based in the former Couvent des Jacobins in Beaune, a name used for one of its most widely available ranges.

Jadot makes wines from a large number of appellations, from regional Bourgogne up to Grands Crus of the Côte de Nuits and Côte de Beaune, as well as wines from the Mâconnais and Beaujolais, where it owns Château des Jacques in Moulin-à-Vent.

Its style aims to express the character of each appellation clearly, with well-structured Pinot Noir reds and precise Chardonnay whites.

It is one of the Burgundy houses with the strongest presence in export markets.
