Au Bon Climat

Winery founded in 1982 by Jim Clendenen in Santa Barbara County, a reference for Burgundian-style Californian Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, with acidity and restrained alcohol.

Jim Clendenen discovered wine during a stay in France and, after working several harvests in Burgundy and other regions, founded Au Bon Climat in 1982, initially with Adam Tolmach. The name, which in French means something like “in the good climate”, refers both to Santa Barbara’s cool climate and to the word climat, which in Burgundy designates a delimited vineyard parcel.

From the beginning Clendenen championed a style that ran counter to the Californian trend towards maximum ripeness: wines with marked acidity, moderate alcohol and French oak ageing intended to accompany rather than dominate the fruit, with an eye to the table and to ageing. For years the winery worked in facilities on the Bien Nacido vineyard, in the Santa Maria Valley, one of the area’s historic sources of grapes, alongside other projects such as Bob Lindquist’s Qupé.

Au Bon Climat makes Pinot Noir and Chardonnay with the Santa Barbara County appellation, as well as single-vineyard wines, and Clendenen also created other labels, among them Clendenen Family Vineyards, with which he explored different varieties and styles. Since Jim Clendenen’s death in 2021, the winery has continued under his family and team, faithful to the style that made it known.

His influence was considerable among Central Coast producers seeking a fresher, more restrained style.
