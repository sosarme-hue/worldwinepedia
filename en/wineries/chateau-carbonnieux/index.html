Château Carbonnieux

Château in Léognan, Pessac-Léognan, classified in Graves for both its red and its white and owned by the Perrin family since the mid-20th century.

Château Carbonnieux is one of the oldest estates in the Graves area, south of Bordeaux. It lies in the commune of Léognan, within the Pessac-Léognan appellation, and its wine history goes back several centuries; in the 18th century it belonged to the Benedictine monks of the abbey of Sainte-Croix in Bordeaux, who helped build the reputation of its wines, especially the whites.

In 1956 the estate was bought by the Perrin family, who still run it. In the Graves classification, Carbonnieux is listed as a cru classé for both its red and its white wine, a double distinction shared by only a few properties.

The vineyard lies on the gravel typical of the area, mixed with clay and limestone in some sectors. For the reds, mainly Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot are grown, with some Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot; for the whites, Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon.

Carbonnieux is known in particular for its white, fresh and aromatic in style, with barrel ageing that gives it texture and ageing potential. Its red, classic in profile, combines fruit and finesse. The estate also makes a second wine, La Croix de Carbonnieux, in red and white.
