Château Talbot

Saint-Julien fourth growth owned by descendants of the Cordier family since the early 20th century, which also makes a white wine, Caillou Blanc.

Château Talbot is one of the largest estates in the Saint-Julien appellation, in the Médoc. By tradition its name is associated with the English constable John Talbot, who died in 1453 at the Battle of Castillon, the last major engagement of the Hundred Years’ War, although the historical connection between the man and the property is not precisely documented.

In the 1855 classification it was recognised as a Quatrième Grand Cru Classé. In the early 20th century it was bought by Désiré Cordier, a wine merchant, and it has remained in the hands of his descendants ever since; in recent decades the Bignon-Cordier family has run the estate and renovated the cellars.

The vineyard, in a single block in the centre of the appellation, lies on gravel and is planted mainly with Cabernet Sauvignon, with Merlot and small areas of Petit Verdot and Cabernet Franc. A small part is given over to white varieties, Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon, used to make Caillou Blanc du Château Talbot, which is sold under the Bordeaux appellation, as Saint-Julien covers only red wines.

As well as the grand vin, the estate produces the second wine Connétable de Talbot. The Talbot style is usually described as classic, with ripe fruit, a certain roundness and, with age, the cedar and tobacco notes typical of Saint-Julien. It is a widely distributed classified Bordeaux, appreciated for the balance between style and price within its category.
