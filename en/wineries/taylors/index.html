Winery

Taylor’s (Taylor, Fladgate & Yeatman)

Port house founded in 1692, one of the oldest in the trade, known for its Vintage Ports, for popularising Late Bottled Vintage and for its Quinta de Vargellas in the Douro Superior.

Taylor’s was founded in 1692 by the English merchant Job Bearsley, placing it among the oldest Port houses still trading. Its company name, Taylor, Fladgate & Yeatman, comes from the surnames of partners who joined the firm in the nineteenth century. Today it belongs to The Fladgate Partnership, an independent group that also includes other Port houses such as Fonseca and Croft.

The house owns vineyards in the Douro, most notably Quinta de Vargellas, in the Douro Superior, acquired in the late nineteenth century, which gives its name to single-quinta Vintage Ports in undeclared years. Other important properties are Quinta de Terra Feita and Quinta do Junco, in the Pinhão river valley.

Taylor’s is known for its Vintage Ports, firm and structured in style, and for introducing innovations that shaped the category: in 1934 it launched Chip Dry, presented as the first dry white Port, and in 1970 it released one of the first Late Bottled Vintage Ports, a style that brought the character of a single harvest to a ready-to-drink wine. Its range also includes age-dated tawnies, from 10 to over 40 years old, and single-harvest Ports. Its ageing lodges are in Vila Nova de Gaia, across the river from the city of Porto.

Visits & wine tourism

Its lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia offer tours with tastings.
