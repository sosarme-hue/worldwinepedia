Weingut Prager

Family estate in Weißenkirchen, in the Wachau, known for its Riesling and Grüner Veltliner from terraced sites such as Achleiten and Klaus and for Toni Bodenstein’s geological work.

Weingut Prager is in Weißenkirchen, one of the central wine villages of the Wachau, on the banks of the Danube. In the second half of the 20th century, Franz Prager was one of the growers who built the reputation of the region’s dry wines, and he took part in creating the Vinea Wachau association, which established the Steinfeder, Federspiel and Smaragd categories.

Since the late 1980s the estate has been run by his son-in-law Toni Bodenstein, together with his wife Ilse, of the Prager family. Bodenstein, trained in the natural sciences, has paid particular attention to the geology of the Wachau’s vineyards and the influence of different rock types — gneiss, amphibolite, marble — on the character of the wines. He has also planted vines at higher altitudes than is usual in the region, on the parcel known as Wachstum Bodenstein.

The house’s vines are spread over some of the most highly regarded terraces in and around Weißenkirchen, such as the Achleiten, the Klaus, the Steinriegl and the Kaiserberg. The estate mainly makes Riesling and Grüner Veltliner in the Federspiel and Smaragd categories.

The Prager style is marked by dry wines of great precision that seek to express the geological origin of each parcel, with good concentration and ageing potential. The house is particularly known for its Rieslings.
