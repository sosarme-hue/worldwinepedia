Suertes del Marqués

Winery in the Orotava Valley, in northern Tenerife, making single-plot wines from old Listán Negro and Listán Blanco vines trained in braided cordon.

Suertes del Marqués is a winery in the Orotava Valley, on the northern side of the island of Tenerife. In the early twenty-first century it moved from selling grapes to making its own wines and became one of the names that brought Canary Island wines to international attention. The word suerte refers in the Canaries to a plot of land, and the winery organises much of its range around these plots.

The valley and the braided cordon

The Orotava Valley runs down from the slopes of Mount Teide towards the Atlantic Ocean. The vineyards lie on volcanic soils at very varied altitudes and are influenced by the trade winds, which soften the climate and bring humidity. One of the area's peculiarities is the braided cordon (cordón trenzado): the vines are trained into long arms that are plaited together and extend several metres along supports, a traditional growing method of the valley. Many vines are very old and, as elsewhere in the Canaries, ungrafted, because phylloxera never reached the islands.

Wines and style

The winery works mainly with Listán Negro and Listán Blanco, along with other local varieties such as Tintilla and Vijariego. It makes village wines, such as the red 7 Fuentes, and single-plot wines made in small quantities. The style seeks freshness, lightness and volcanic character, with fermentations using native yeasts and a moderate use of oak.
