Domaine Marcel Lapierre

Family domaine in Villié-Morgon, in Beaujolais, whose founder, Marcel Lapierre, was one of the pioneers of natural wine in France.

Marcel Lapierre took over the family vines in Villié-Morgon, in the heart of the Morgon cru, in the early 1970s. In the early 1980s, influenced by the thinking of the chemist and négociant Jules Chauvet, he decided to give up herbicides and synthetic products in the vineyard and to keep additives in the cellar to a minimum, including sulphur dioxide. His work made him one of the founding figures of the natural wine movement.

Together with other Morgon growers, such as Jean Foillard, Guy Breton and Jean-Paul Thévenet, he formed the group that the American importer Kermit Lynch dubbed the “Gang of Four”, which helped restore the prestige of the Beaujolais crus at a time when the region was associated above all with Beaujolais Nouveau.

Marcel Lapierre died in 2010. Since then the domaine has been run by his children Mathieu and Camille Lapierre, who have kept to his principles: organic farming, late hand harvesting to obtain ripe grapes, whole-bunch maceration, indigenous yeasts and very limited use of sulphur.

The house’s best-known wine is its Morgon, made from old-vine Gamay. The range also includes Raisins Gaulois, a light Vin de France red, and other local wines. They are reds of lively fruit, soft texture and great expressiveness.
