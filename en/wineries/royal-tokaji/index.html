Royal Tokaji

A winery founded in 1990 in Mád, one of the first private ventures in Tokaj after the fall of communism, known for its Tokaji Aszú from historic classified vineyards.

Royal Tokaji was born in 1990, just as Hungary was emerging from the communist regime, through a partnership between a group of foreign investors, among them the British wine writer Hugh Johnson, and local Tokaj growers. It was one of the first private companies in the region after decades in which production had been concentrated in a large state winery, and it helped bring the wines of Tokaj back to the attention of international markets.

It is based in Mád, one of the region’s historic villages, and works with vineyards on some of the slopes classified as first growths since the eighteenth century. They include Mézes Mály, in Tarcal, and Betsek, Szent Tamás and Nyulászó, in Mád, whose names appear on the labels of its single-vineyard wines. The main varieties are Furmint and Hárslevelű, together with Sárgamuskotály.

The range centres on Tokaji Aszú, made from hand-selected botrytised berries, in both blended and single-vineyard versions, and on Eszencia in vintages that allow it. It also produces late-harvest sweet wines and dry Furmint whites. Its style seeks to balance concentrated sweetness with Furmint’s characteristic acidity, and its Aszú wines are aged for a long time in casks in the house’s underground cellars.
