Winery

d'Arenberg

Family winery in McLaren Vale, founded in 1912 by Joseph Osborn and now run by his great-grandson Chester Osborn. It is known for its old-vine Shiraz and Grenache and for its unusual wine names.

In 1912 Joseph Osborn, an Adelaide businessman, bought a property with a vineyard in McLaren Vale. For years the family sold its grapes and wine in bulk to other companies, until his grandson Francis Osborn, known as d’Arry, who took over the property at a very young age, launched the d’Arenberg brand in 1959, in tribute to the surname of his mother, Helena d’Arenberg. The label, with its diagonal red stripe, became an emblem of the house.

Since the mid-1980s the chief winemaker has been Chester Osborn, the fourth generation of the family. The winery has kept traditional methods: many reds are fermented in open vats, with the cap plunged by hand or by foot treading, and pressed in old basket presses. It works with old vines of Shiraz, Grenache and Mourvèdre on different McLaren Vale soils and has experimented with numerous Mediterranean varieties. Its wines often bear humorous or curious names, such as The Dead Arm, which refers to a vine trunk disease, or The Footbolt.

In 2017 the winery opened the d’Arenberg Cube, a striking multi-storey building inspired by a Rubik’s cube, which houses a tasting room, a restaurant and spaces devoted to wine and art, and has become one of the region’s tourist attractions.

Visits & wine tourism

The d’Arenberg Cube, in McLaren Vale, houses the tasting room, a restaurant and exhibition spaces open to the public.
