Silver Oak Cellars

Winery founded in 1972 by Justin Meyer and Ray Duncan that devotes itself exclusively to Cabernet Sauvignon, aged in American oak, with one wine from Napa Valley and another from Alexander Valley.

Silver Oak was born in 1972 from a partnership between Justin Meyer, who had learned the wine trade in Napa, and the businessman Raymond Twomey Duncan. From the outset they made an unusual decision: to make just one kind of wine, Cabernet Sauvignon, and to age it exclusively in American oak barrels rather than the French oak preferred by most of their neighbours. Another hallmark was the long ageing before release, in barrel and then in bottle, with the aim of offering wines ready to drink when sold.

The winery makes two Cabernet Sauvignons: one with the Napa Valley appellation, made in Oakville, and another with the Alexander Valley appellation, in Sonoma, which has its own winery near Healdsburg. Both share the house style, with ripe fruit and notes of vanilla, coconut and spice from the American oak, and differ according to the character of each valley: more structured from Napa, softer and fruitier from Alexander Valley.

After Justin Meyer’s departure, the Duncan family took charge of the company, which has also created other wineries, such as Twomey Cellars, devoted to Pinot Noir and Merlot. Silver Oak has invested in sustainable architecture for its newer facilities, both in Oakville and in Alexander Valley.

Its loyalty to a single style, maintained over decades, has made Silver Oak one of the most recognisable Cabernet Sauvignon brands in California, widely found in restaurants.

Visits & wine tourism

It offers tastings and tours at its wineries in Oakville and Alexander Valley.
