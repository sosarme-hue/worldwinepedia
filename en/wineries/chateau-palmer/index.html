Château Palmer

Margaux third growth named after the British general Charles Palmer, known for an unusually high proportion of Merlot for the Médoc.

Château Palmer lies in the Margaux appellation, in the commune of Cantenac, next to Château Margaux. At the beginning of the 19th century the estate was bought by Charles Palmer, a British general who had served in the Napoleonic Wars, who enlarged the vineyard and gave it his name. Financial difficulties forced him to sell, and in the mid-19th century it passed to the Pereire bankers, who built the present château with its slate-roofed towers.

In the 1855 classification it was recognised as a Troisième Grand Cru Classé, although its reputation has often exceeded its rank. Since the 1930s it has belonged to a group of Bordeaux and Dutch merchant families, among them the Sichel and Mähler-Besse families.

The vineyard lies on the gravels of the Margaux plateau. One of its distinctive features is its proportion of Merlot, very high for the Médoc and similar to that of Cabernet Sauvignon, together with a little Petit Verdot. In recent decades the estate has adopted biodynamic viticulture and keeps its own herd to produce manure.

As well as the grand vin, Palmer makes Alter Ego, conceived as a distinct wine rather than a simple second wine, and has developed other experimental projects. Its black and gold label is one of the most recognisable in Bordeaux. The house style is associated with aromatic, silky, deep wines that combine the elegance of Margaux with a generous texture.
