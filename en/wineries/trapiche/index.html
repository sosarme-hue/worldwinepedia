Winery

Trapiche

One of the oldest and largest wineries in Argentina, with origins in Mendoza in 1883. It is part of Grupo Peñaflor and makes a broad range, from everyday wines to single-vineyard Malbecs.

Trapiche’s origins go back to 1883, when wine was made on an estate in Godoy Cruz, Mendoza, known as El Trapiche. Over the twentieth century the brand grew to become one of the most widely distributed in the country and became part of Grupo Peñaflor, one of Argentina’s largest wine groups, which also owns wineries such as El Esteco.

Trapiche works with its own vineyards and with a large network of growers in Mendoza’s main areas, from Maipú and Luján de Cuyo to the Uco Valley and eastern Mendoza. This diversity allows it to make a very broad range: high-volume young wines, oak-aged varietals such as the Oak Cask line, more ambitious reds such as Broquel and Iscay, and a series of single-vineyard Malbecs which for years has named on the label the growers who farm those estates.

One of the symbols of the house is its historic winery in Coquimbito, Maipú, an early twentieth-century building surrounded by vineyards and restored by the company, which retains elements of the industrial architecture of the period. Trapiche has played an important role in spreading Argentine Malbec on international markets.

Given its size and long history, the winery is also a good reflection of the evolution of the Mendoza wine industry, from the table wine of the twentieth century to today’s wines of origin.

The winery offers tours and tastings, usually by prior booking.
