St. Andrea

Eger winery run by György Lőrincz, one of the producers who have renewed Egri Bikavér with blends of Burgundian and Bordeaux inspiration.

St. Andrea is a family winery in the Eger region of northern Hungary, in the foothills of the Bükk mountains. It is run by the winemaker György Lőrincz, who has become one of the region’s leading names and has played a decisive part in restoring the prestige of Egri Bikavér, the “bull’s blood” of Eger.

Egri Bikavér is a blended red whose rules require several varieties, with Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch) as the usual backbone, alongside others such as Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Pinot Noir or Kadarka. During the socialist era the name became associated with high-volume wines of uneven quality; producers such as St. Andrea have worked to give it back its character as a terroir wine, with lower yields and careful winemaking.

The winery makes several versions of Bikavér from different vineyards, among them Merengő, Hangács and Áldás, as well as white blends under the Egri Csillag designation and single-vineyard wines. Its style seeks elegance and freshness rather than power, with a measured use of oak.

Eger has a relatively cool climate for Hungary, with volcanic soils of rhyolite and tuff, as well as clays and loess, which favour reds of lively acidity and fine tannins. Viticulture at St. Andrea pays particular attention to soil management and grape selection.
