Robert Mondavi Winery

Winery founded in 1966 in Oakville by Robert Mondavi, a key figure in the modernisation of Californian wine and the spread of quality varietal wines.

Robert Mondavi, the son of Italian immigrants, worked for years at the family’s Charles Krug winery in St. Helena. In 1966, following family disagreements, he founded his own winery in Oakville, the first large winery built in Napa since the end of Prohibition. The building, in a style inspired by the Californian missions, was designed by the architect Cliff May and became a recognisable image of the valley.

Mondavi argued tirelessly that California could produce wines comparable to the great European ones, and introduced practices then uncommon in the region, such as cool fermentation and ageing in French oak barrels. In 1968 he gave the name Fumé Blanc to his oak-aged Sauvignon Blanc, a term later adopted by other producers. He was also a great promoter of wine at the table, of wine tourism and of food culture.

The heart of the winery is the To Kalon vineyard in Oakville, planted in the nineteenth century by Hamilton W. Crabb and the source of its most ambitious Cabernet Sauvignons. Mondavi also forged international partnerships, among them Opus One with Baron Philippe de Rothschild. In 2004 the company was acquired by Constellation Brands. Robert Mondavi died in 2008.

Today the winery makes a range extending from Napa Valley wines to selections from Oakville and the To Kalon vineyard. Its summer concerts and culinary activities carry on the educational mission of its founder, who saw wine as part of a way of life bound up with food and culture.

Visits & wine tourism

The winery welcomes visitors in Oakville, with tours and tastings.
