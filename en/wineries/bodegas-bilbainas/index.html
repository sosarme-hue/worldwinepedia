Bodegas Bilbaínas

Century-old winery in Haro’s Station District, founded in 1901 and part of the Raventós Codorníu group, best known for its Viña Pomal brand.

Bodegas Bilbaínas was founded in 1901 in Haro by Santiago de Ugarte, a businessman from Bilbao —hence the name—, who acquired the premises of a French wine merchant house established in the town at a time when phylloxera had devastated the vineyards of France and many négociants were looking for wine in Rioja. It stands in the Station District, alongside other historic wineries that took advantage of the arrival of the railway to ship their wines to Bilbao and abroad.

For much of the twentieth century it was one of Rioja’s major exporting houses. In 1997 it became part of the Catalan group Codorníu, now Raventós Codorníu, which has renovated its facilities while preserving its old underground cellars and stone buildings.

The winery has extensive vineyards of its own around Haro, in Rioja Alta, planted with Tempranillo, Garnacha, Graciano and Mazuelo (Carignan), as well as white varieties. Its best-known brand is Viña Pomal, on the market for more than a century, with classic-style Crianzas, Reservas and Gran Reservas. Other labels of the house are La Vicalanda and Viña Zaco.

Bodegas Bilbaínas was also a pioneer of traditional-method sparkling wine in Rioja. Its style combines the tradition of oak ageing with ripe, expressive fruit.
