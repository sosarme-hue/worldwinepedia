Paul Jaboulet Aîné

Historic northern Rhône house founded in 1834 in Tain-l’Hermitage, famous for its Hermitage La Chapelle and owner of vineyards in the main appellations of the northern valley.

Paul Jaboulet Aîné was founded in 1834 by Antoine Jaboulet in Tain-l’Hermitage, the town at the foot of the hill of Hermitage on the left bank of the Rhône. The house takes its name from one of his sons, Paul, the eldest (“aîné”). For more than a century and a half the business remained in the hands of the Jaboulet family, who made it one of the benchmarks of the northern Rhône, both as a vineyard owner and as a négociant.

In 2006 the house was bought by the Frey family, who also own Château La Lagune in the Médoc, and who undertook a renovation of the winery and of the work in the vineyard. Jaboulet owns parcels in Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Cornas, Saint-Joseph and Condrieu, among other appellations, and rounds out its range with southern Rhône wines made from bought-in grapes.

Its best-known wine is Hermitage La Chapelle, named after the small chapel at the top of the hill of Hermitage, which belongs to the house. Alongside it stand the white Hermitage Chevalier de Sterimberg and the Crozes-Hermitage Domaine de Thalabert, one of the historic estates of that appellation. At the most accessible end of the range, the Côtes du Rhône Parallèle 45 is one of the most widely distributed Rhône wines.

The house style for its reds is built on Syrah, with black fruit, spicy notes and, in the top bottlings, a structure designed for ageing.

Visits & wine tourism

The house has a reception and tasting space in the Tain-l’Hermitage area; check conditions on its website.
