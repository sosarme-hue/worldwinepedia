Winery

Kanonkop

Family estate on the slopes of the Simonsberg, in Stellenbosch, regarded as one of the great references for Pinotage and also known for its Bordeaux blend Paul Sauer.

Kanonkop lies on the lower slopes of the Simonsberg mountain, between Stellenbosch and Paarl. Its name comes from a small hill, or kop, from which a cannon was fired in the seventeenth century to alert farmers to the arrival of ships in Table Bay, their cue to go and sell their produce. The estate has belonged to the same family since the early twentieth century. One of its owners was Paul Sauer, a South African government minister, whose name is now borne by the house’s leading red; the property then passed to his descendants in the Krige family, who still run it.

For decades the grapes were sold to other companies, until in the 1970s the estate began bottling its own wines. Its winemakers have played a leading role in raising the standing of Pinotage, the variety created in South Africa in 1925, at a time when many producers regarded it as lacking prestige. Kanonkop keeps old bush-trained Pinotage vines, the source of its old-vine wine, and has shown that this grape can give structured, long-lived reds.

The winery keeps traditional winemaking methods: the reds ferment in open concrete tanks, where the cap of skins is punched down by hand at regular intervals, and are aged in French oak barrels. Besides Pinotage and Paul Sauer, a blend of Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Merlot, it makes a Cabernet Sauvignon, a blend called Kadette and a rosé.

Visits & wine tourism

The estate has a tasting room open to the public on the slopes of the Simonsberg.
