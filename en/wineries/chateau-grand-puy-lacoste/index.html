Château Grand-Puy-Lacoste

Fifth growth of Pauillac in the 1855 classification, owned by the Borie family since 1978 and known for a classic style dominated by Cabernet Sauvignon.

Château Grand-Puy-Lacoste lies in Pauillac, in the Médoc, on one of the gravel rises in the interior of the appellation known as Grand-Puy; locally, the word puy means a small hill. The two estates bearing this name, Grand-Puy-Lacoste and Grand-Puy-Ducasse, were originally part of the same property.

In the 1855 classification, Grand-Puy-Lacoste was ranked among the fifth growths. In 1978 it was bought by the Borie family, also associated with Château Ducru-Beaucaillou in Saint-Julien, who undertook the renovation of the vineyard and winery. Since then the estate has been run by the branch of François-Xavier Borie.

The vineyard, in a single block around the château, lies on deep gravel and is planted mainly with Cabernet Sauvignon, completed by Merlot and a small proportion of Cabernet Franc.

Its style is considered a very classic expression of Pauillac: reds with black fruit, cedar and graphite notes, firm tannins and great ageing potential. The estate also makes a second wine, Lacoste-Borie, which is more approachable in youth.

The estate lies inland, west of the town of Pauillac, away from the riverside plots along the estuary where some of the first growths are found. This position, on deep, well-drained gravel, contributes to the firm character and freshness of its wines in cooler vintages.
