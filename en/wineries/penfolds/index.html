Winery

Penfolds

Historic house founded in 1844 at Magill, near Adelaide, and now with its main winery in the Barossa. It is famous for Grange and for its tradition of blending grapes from several regions.

Penfolds was founded in 1844 by the English doctor Christopher Rawson Penfold and his wife Mary at Magill, on the outskirts of Adelaide, where they planted vines next to their house, called The Grange. In the early days the wine was prescribed as a tonic for patients, but the company soon became one of Australia’s major producers of fortified wines. The Magill Estate vineyard still survives, now surrounded by the city’s residential suburbs.

A decisive moment came in the 1950s, when chief winemaker Max Schubert, after a trip to Bordeaux, set out to create a great age-worthy red from Australian Shiraz. The first experimental vintage of Grange was 1951; at first the company’s management received the wine with scepticism, but over time it became the country’s most famous red. Penfolds also developed a system of wines identified by “Bin” numbers, which still organises much of its range.

The house philosophy has traditionally been based on blending grapes from different regions and vineyards of South Australia to achieve a consistent style, with concentrated fruit and oak ageing, often in American oak. Over time it has also introduced single-region wines, such as those from the Barossa. Its main winery is at Nuriootpa, in the Barossa Valley. Today it is part of the Treasury Wine Estates group.

Visits & wine tourism

Penfolds welcomes visitors at Magill Estate, in Adelaide, and at its Barossa cellar door, with a range of tasting experiences.
