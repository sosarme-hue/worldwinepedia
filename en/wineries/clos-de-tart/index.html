Clos de Tart

Monopole Grand Cru in Morey-Saint-Denis, created by Cistercian nuns in the Middle Ages and owned since 2017 by Artémis Domaines, the Pinault family group.

Clos de Tart is one of Burgundy’s oldest and most famous vineyards. Located in Morey-Saint-Denis, in the Côte de Nuits, it is a monopole: the entire walled vineyard belongs to a single owner, something exceptional among Burgundy’s Grands Crus.

Its origins go back to the twelfth century, when it was acquired by the Cistercian nuns of the abbey of Notre-Dame de Tart, who gave it its name and kept it until the French Revolution. Sold then as national property, it passed through few hands: the Marey-Monge family and, from 1932, the Mommessin family. In 2017 it was acquired by Artémis Domaines, the Pinault family group, which also owns Château Latour.

The vineyard, recognised as a Grand Cru in 1939, occupies a slope of varied clay-limestone soils. One of its peculiarities is that the vine rows run north to south, across the slope, which favours even sun exposure and limits erosion. The estate picks and vinifies the different sectors of the clos separately.

Besides the Clos de Tart Grand Cru, it makes a second wine from the younger vines and some Morey-Saint-Denis wines. The house style combines depth, finesse and great ageing potential.
