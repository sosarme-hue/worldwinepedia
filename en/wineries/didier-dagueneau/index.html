Domaine Didier Dagueneau

Pouilly-Fumé domaine in the Loire, founded by Didier Dagueneau and now run by his son Louis-Benjamin, famous for single-parcel Sauvignon Blancs such as Silex.

Didier Dagueneau began making wine in Saint-Andelain, in the Pouilly-Fumé appellation, in the early 1980s. From a family of local growers, he became known for his nonconformist character and his exacting standards in vineyard and cellar, at a time when much of local production was geared towards simple wines. Low yields, careful viticulture and parcel-by-parcel vinification were the pillars of his work.

Didier Dagueneau died in 2008 in a light-aircraft accident. Since then the domaine has been run by his son Louis-Benjamin Dagueneau, who has maintained the level and stylistic line of the house, with the help of his sister Charlotte.

The vineyard, planted with Sauvignon Blanc, is divided into parcels with different soils, among them the flint soils that give their name to the domaine’s most famous wine, Silex. Other labels are Pur Sang, Buisson Renard and Blanc Fumé de Pouilly, the appellation’s alternative name. The wines are fermented and aged in barrels of various sizes, including elongated ones known as “cigares”, without the oak dominating.

The family also makes sweet and dry wines in Jurançon, in south-west France, at the Les Jardins de Babylone estate. The Dagueneau style is marked by purity, tension and an ageing potential uncommon in Sauvignon Blanc.
