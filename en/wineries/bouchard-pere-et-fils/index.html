Bouchard Père & Fils

Beaune house founded in 1731, with its cellars in the old Château de Beaune and one of the largest vineyard holdings in the Côte d’Or.

Bouchard Père & Fils was founded in 1731 by Michel Bouchard, a cloth merchant who settled in Beaune and went into the wine trade. His descendants developed the business and, after the French Revolution, bought many vineyards that had belonged to the Church and the nobility, including plots in some of the finest premiers crus of Beaune.

In the early 19th century the family acquired the Château de Beaune, a former 15th-century fortress whose bastions house the company’s cellars. A collection of old bottles from the house is kept there, some of them dating back to the 19th century.

In 1995 the company was acquired by the Henriot family, owners of the Champagne house of the same name, who invested in a new winery and in more precise vineyard management. The Bouchard domaine has one of the largest vineyard areas in the Côte d’Or, with plots in grands crus such as Chevalier-Montrachet, Corton-Charlemagne, Le Montrachet and Chambertin and in many premiers crus in Beaune, Volnay, Meursault and other villages.

The house’s most emblematic vineyard is the Vigne de l’Enfant Jésus, in the Beaune Grèves premier cru. As well as its domaine wines, Bouchard has a large négociant business, with regional wines such as the La Vignée range. It makes classic-style reds from Pinot Noir and whites from Chardonnay, which seek to express the character of each terroir.
