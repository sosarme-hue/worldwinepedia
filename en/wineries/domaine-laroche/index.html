Domaine Laroche

Chablis house with origins in the mid-19th century, based in the historic Obédiencerie and with vineyards in all the main tiers of the appellation, from Petit Chablis to Grand Cru.

Domaine Laroche is one of the best-known houses in Chablis, Burgundy’s northernmost wine area, devoted exclusively to Chardonnay. The Laroche family began growing vines in Chablis in the mid-19th century, and from the 1960s Michel Laroche considerably expanded the estate and turned it into an internationally recognised name. Today it is part of the French wine group AdVini.

The house is headquartered in the Obédiencerie, a group of historic buildings in the centre of Chablis linked to the monks of the abbey of Saint-Martin de Tours, who took refuge in Chablis with the relics of Saint Martin in the Middle Ages. Its cellars hold a large old wooden press, one of the symbols of the house.

Laroche’s vineyards cover the appellation’s different tiers: Petit Chablis, Chablis, several premiers crus such as Fourchaumes and Vaudevey, and grand cru plots, including a notable presence in Les Blanchots. The soils are the Kimmeridgian marls and limestones characteristic of the area, with fossils of tiny oysters.

The house style seeks the purity and mineral tension typical of Chablis. Tank ageing predominates in the basic wines, while the premiers and grands crus may be partly aged in barrel, always with a moderate use of oak.

Visits & wine tourism

<p>The house welcomes visitors at the Obédiencerie, in the centre of Chablis, with tours of the historic cellars and tastings of its range.</p>
