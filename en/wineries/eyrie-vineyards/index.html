The Eyrie Vineyards

A pioneering Willamette Valley winery founded by David and Diana Lett, who in the 1960s planted the area’s first Pinot Noir and the first Pinot Gris in the United States.

David Lett studied viticulture at the University of California, Davis and, against prevailing opinion, concluded that Pinot Noir would give its best results in a climate cooler than California’s. In the mid-1960s he settled in Oregon and, together with his wife Diana, planted a vineyard in the Dundee Hills, in the Willamette Valley: these were the area’s first Pinot Noir vines, along with Chardonnay, Pinot Gris and other varieties. The original vineyard, known as the Original Vines, is still in production.

Lett also planted what is considered the first Pinot Gris vineyard in the United States, a variety that would in time become Oregon’s most widely planted white. To make his wines he set up the winery in a former industrial building in McMinnville. In the late 1970s his Pinot Noir attracted attention at comparative tastings in France, which helped to make the region known and to draw other producers, among them the Drouhin family.

Eyrie’s style is characterised by wines light in colour, with lively acidity and moderate alcohol, made with minimal intervention and able to evolve over many years. After David Lett’s death in 2008, his son Jason Lett took over, maintaining farming without synthetic products and the philosophy of the house.

Because of its founding role, The Eyrie Vineyards holds a central place in the history of Oregon wine.
