Château Léoville Barton

Saint-Julien second growth owned since 1826 by the Barton family, of Irish origin, known for classic Cabernet Sauvignon-based reds.

Château Léoville Barton is one of the three châteaux that emerged from the division of the old Léoville estate in Saint-Julien, together with Léoville Las Cases and Léoville Poyferré. It was ranked as a second growth in the 1855 classification and has belonged since 1826 to the Barton family, wine merchants of Irish origin established in Bordeaux since the eighteenth century. A few years earlier, in 1821, the family had acquired the neighbouring Château Langoa Barton. The family is also linked to the history of the Bordeaux wine trade through the négociant house Barton & Guestier, which bears its name together with that of its historic partners.

Léoville Barton has no château building of its own: the wine is made and aged at Langoa Barton’s facilities, although the two vineyards are vinified separately. The property was run for decades by Anthony Barton and is now in the hands of his daughter Lilian Barton-Sartorius and the family’s new generation.

The vineyard lies on the gravel typical of Saint-Julien, with a majority of Cabernet Sauvignon, completed by Merlot and Cabernet Franc. The house is known for a classic style, untouched by fashion, with structured wines of precise fruit and cedar notes that need years in bottle and age for decades. It also makes a second wine, La Réserve de Léoville Barton.
