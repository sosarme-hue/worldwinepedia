Veuve Clicquot Ponsardin

Reims house founded in 1772 and shaped by Barbe-Nicole Ponsardin, the “widow Clicquot”, credited with the riddling table; famous for its yellow label.

The house was founded in Reims in 1772 by Philippe Clicquot. His son François married Barbe-Nicole Ponsardin, who was widowed in 1805, at the age of 27, and chose to take charge of the business at a time when it was very unusual for a woman to run a company. This is the origin of the brand name, Veuve (“widow”) Clicquot.

Madame Clicquot is credited with two important innovations in the history of champagne: the riddling table (pupitre), devised around 1816 to gather the sediment in the neck of the bottle and obtain a clear wine, and the first known blended rosé champagne, made in 1818 by adding red wine to white. She also opened up export markets, particularly in Russia.

The house keeps its bottles in old chalk quarries dug beneath Reims, the crayères. Its style relies on Pinot Noir, which gives structure to its blends, completed with Pinot Meunier and Chardonnay and a significant proportion of reserve wines.

Its flagship wine is the Brut Carte Jaune, recognisable by its yellow label, alongside the Rosé, the vintage wines and the prestige cuvée La Grande Dame, dedicated to the founder. Veuve Clicquot belongs to the LVMH group.

Today it is one of the best-known champagne brands in the world.

Visits & wine tourism

It offers guided tours of its crayères in Reims, with a tasting, by prior booking.
