Chateau Ste. Michelle

The best-known winery in Washington State, based in Woodinville near Seattle, with vineyards in the Columbia Valley. It has been decisive in making Washington Riesling and reds known.

The origins of Chateau Ste. Michelle go back to wine companies set up in Washington after the end of Prohibition, which for years made mainly wines from American varieties and fortified wines. In the 1960s the company began to focus on Vitis vinifera and adopted the Ste. Michelle name; the winemaker André Tchelistcheff, a key figure in Napa, advised it during that period. In the 1970s the present headquarters was built in Woodinville, a French-inspired building surrounded by gardens, west of the Cascades.

Although the winery is near Seattle, the grapes come from vineyards east of the mountains, in the Columbia Valley AVA, where the semi-arid, sunny climate allows even ripening. The company farms its own vineyards in areas such as Cold Creek and Canoe Ridge, and makes a broad range of whites and reds, from widely distributed wines to single-vineyard selections.

Ste. Michelle is especially known for its Riesling, of which it has become one of the largest producers in the world. Since the late 1990s it has made Eroica, a Riesling created in collaboration with Ernst Loosen of the Dr. Loosen estate in the Mosel. For decades the company was the core of a larger group, Ste. Michelle Wine Estates, which brought together other wineries in Washington, Oregon and California and contributed decisively to the development of wine in the American Northwest.

Visits & wine tourism

The Woodinville headquarters welcomes visitors, with tastings and tours of the winery and gardens.
