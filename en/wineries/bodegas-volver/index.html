Bodegas Volver

Winery making old-vine wines in several areas of central and south-eastern Spain, known for Volver (La Mancha) and Tarima (Alicante).

Bodegas Volver is a Spanish winery that, rather than focusing on a single appellation, makes wines in several areas of central and south-eastern Spain from old, often undervalued vineyards. Its name, meaning “to return”, alludes to the idea of going back to traditional varieties and to old vines that were frequently grubbed up or used for bulk wine.

Areas and varieties

In La Mancha, Spain's largest wine region, the winery works with old Tempranillo vines —known there as Cencibel— grown in an extreme continental climate, with very hot summers, cold winters and calcareous soils. In Alicante, in the dry, high-altitude inland areas, it focuses on Monastrell, the red variety of the Levante, often from old bush vines. The winery has also made wines in other nearby appellations, such as Jumilla.

Wines and style

Its best-known wines are Volver, an old-vine Tempranillo from La Mancha aged in barrel, and Tarima, a range of Monastrell wines from Alicante that includes young, fruity reds and older-vine versions. Many of its wines have been distributed mainly in export markets, particularly the United States. The house style offers ripe fruit, concentration and modest prices, with the aim of showing the value of little-known vineyards.
