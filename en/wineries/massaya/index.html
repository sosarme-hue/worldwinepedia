Winery

Massaya

Lebanese winery founded in 1998 by the brothers Ramzi and Sami Ghosn in the Bekaa Valley, with the initial involvement of French partners from Bordeaux and the Rhône.

Massaya is the project of the brothers Ramzi and Sami Ghosn, whose family owned land at Tanaïl, in the Bekaa Valley. After the years of the Lebanese civil war, during which they lived abroad, the brothers returned to the family estate and in 1998 launched the winery, with the aim of making modern wines that would reflect the character of Lebanon.

At the outset they were joined by renowned French partners from Saint-Émilion and Châteauneuf-du-Pape, whose experience influenced the house's orientation towards Bordeaux and Mediterranean varieties.

The Bekaa is a high plateau between the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon mountain ranges, with hot, dry summers, nights kept cool by the altitude, and cold winters. These conditions allow regular ripening and healthy grapes. Massaya grows and selects Cinsault, Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre and Cabernet Sauvignon, among other varieties, and makes reds, rosés and whites. Its range extends from fresh, fruity wines for early drinking to barrel-aged reds such as the Gold Reserve, its most ambitious wine.

In addition to the Bekaa winery, the family has developed a wine tourism venue in the Lebanese mountains. Massaya's style combines the generosity of Mediterranean fruit with the freshness brought by altitude, and the house has helped to renew the image of Lebanese wine abroad.

Visits & wine tourism

The Tanaïl estate welcomes visitors and offers tastings and meals.
