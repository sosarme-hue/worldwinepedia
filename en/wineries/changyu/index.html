Changyu

The oldest modern winery in China, founded in 1892 in Yantai, Shandong province, by the businessman Zhang Bishi. Today it is one of the country’s largest wine groups, with estates in several regions, including Ningxia.

Changyu was founded in 1892 in the port city of Yantai, on the Shandong peninsula, by Zhang Bishi, a Chinese businessman who had made his fortune in South-East Asia. Zhang imported European vines, hired foreign technicians and built underground cellars for ageing wine, in what is regarded as the birth of the modern wine industry in China. From the outset, the company also made brandy and other drinks besides wine.

During the twentieth century the company went through the country’s profound political and economic changes and came under state ownership. With the economic opening, Changyu became a large listed company and one of China’s leading wine producers. In Yantai it preserves its historic cellars, alongside which a museum devoted to wine culture has been created.

Since the early twenty-first century the group has developed a network of estates or “châteaux” in different Chinese wine regions, often in collaboration with foreign partners. They include Château Changyu-Castel, in Yantai, created with the French Castel group, and Château Changyu Moser XV, in Ningxia, developed with the Austrian producer Lenz Moser. The group also makes ice wine in the north-east of the country and has acquired wineries in other countries. Its range runs from mass-market wines to more ambitious estate reds, with a strong emphasis on Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Gernischt (Carménère).
