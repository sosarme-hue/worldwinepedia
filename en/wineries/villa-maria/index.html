Villa Maria

Company founded in 1961 in Auckland by George Fistonich, of Dalmatian descent, which became one of New Zealand’s largest producers, with a major presence in Marlborough.

Villa Maria was born in 1961, when George Fistonich, the son of Dalmatian immigrants, leased a small vineyard at Mangere, in south Auckland, and began making wine on a small scale. Like many families of Croatian origin settled in the area, he started with modest production, but in the following decades the company grew to become one of the great names in New Zealand wine. In recognition of his contribution to the industry, Fistonich was knighted.

With the rise of Sauvignon Blanc, Villa Maria developed a strong presence in Marlborough, where it owns vineyards and a winery, and it also works in Hawke’s Bay, particularly in the Gimblett Gravels area, and in other regions. Its range is organised into several tiers, such as Private Bin, Cellar Selection, Reserve and single-vineyard wines. As well as Sauvignon Blanc, it makes Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling and Bordeaux-style and Syrah reds.

The company was one of the first in New Zealand to adopt the screwcap across the board in the early 2000s, a decision that was much debated at the time and was eventually followed by most of the industry. It has also promoted sustainable and organic viticulture programmes. In 2021 the family sold the company, which passed to another New Zealand wine group.
