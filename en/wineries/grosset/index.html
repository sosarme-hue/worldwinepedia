Winery

Grosset

Small Clare Valley winery founded in 1981 by Jeffrey Grosset. It is a benchmark for dry Australian Riesling with its Polish Hill and Springvale wines.

Jeffrey Grosset founded his winery in 1981 at Auburn, at the southern end of the Clare Valley, after working as a winemaker for other Australian companies. From the outset he focused on dry Riesling and on the idea that each vineyard could express a character of its own. His two best-known wines come from two sub-areas of the valley with very different soils: Polish Hill, with poor soils over slate and shale, and Springvale, at Watervale, with terra rossa over limestone. The comparison between the two has become a classic example of the influence of soil on Riesling.

Grosset played a prominent part in the collective decision by Clare producers in 2000 to bottle their Rieslings under screwcap, an initiative that transformed the way bottles are sealed in Australia and New Zealand. The winery farms its vineyards organically and makes its wines with limited intervention, seeking purity and precision.

As well as the Rieslings, the range includes Gaia, a Bordeaux-style blend based on Cabernet Sauvignon from a high-altitude vineyard in Clare, and wines from the Adelaide Hills, such as a Chardonnay and a Pinot Noir. It is a small company with limited production, much of which is sold in Australia.
