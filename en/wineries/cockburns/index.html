Cockburn’s

Port house founded in 1815 by the Scot Robert Cockburn, creator of Special Reserve and owned by the Symington family since 2010.

Cockburn’s was founded in 1815 by Robert Cockburn, a Scottish merchant who settled in Porto after the Napoleonic Wars. Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries it became one of the most prestigious British Port houses, with a long tradition of Vintage Port and a major presence in the United Kingdom market.

The house was also one of the pioneers in developing viticulture in the Douro Superior, the easternmost and driest part of the region, home to Quinta dos Canais, its flagship estate on the banks of the river. Traditional Douro varieties such as Touriga Nacional and Touriga Franca are grown there.

In 1969 Cockburn’s launched Special Reserve, a ruby-style Port with somewhat longer wood ageing than basic rubies. Its success helped to establish the Reserve category, today one of the best-selling categories of Port. After passing through several international groups, the brand was acquired in 2010 by the Symington family, which also owns Graham’s, Dow’s and Warre’s and has restored the firm, relatively dry style that characterised the house’s Vintage Ports.

Cockburn’s lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia keep a working cooperage of their own, something rare today, where the casks and vats in which the wines age are repaired and maintained.

Visits & wine tourism

The lodges in Vila Nova de Gaia offer guided tours, including the cooperage, and tastings of its Ports.
