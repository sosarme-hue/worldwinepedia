Tenuta di Trinoro

Estate in the Val d’Orcia, southern Tuscany, created by Andrea Franchetti and devoted to reds from Bordeaux varieties such as Cabernet Franc and Merlot.

Tenuta di Trinoro lies near Sarteano, in the province of Siena, in an isolated part of the Val d’Orcia with no earlier tradition of prestigious winemaking. It was created in the early 1990s by Andrea Franchetti, who planted vineyards on stony, clay hills with the idea of making Tuscan reds inspired by the great wines of Bordeaux’s Right Bank. Franchetti, who died in 2021, also founded the Passopisciaro estate on Mount Etna in Sicily.

Instead of Sangiovese, Tuscany’s dominant variety, Trinoro chose the Bordeaux varieties: Cabernet Franc, Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Petit Verdot. The vineyards were planted at high density and harvesting is usually late, in search of full ripeness. The wines are made under the Toscana IGT designation.

The range includes the estate’s main wine, which bears its name, the second wine Le Cupole and a series of single-plot wines, the ‘Campi’, each from a specific vineyard and often from a single variety, such as Campo di Tenaglia and Campo di Magnacosta, from Cabernet Franc, and Palazzi, from Merlot. These are reds of great concentration, ripe fruit and dense texture, with a marked personality within the Tuscan scene and a slow evolution in bottle, which rewards patient cellaring.
