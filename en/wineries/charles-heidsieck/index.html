Champagne Charles Heidsieck

Reims Champagne house founded in 1851 by Charles-Camille Heidsieck, known for its generous use of reserve wines and its cellars in ancient chalk quarries.

Champagne Charles Heidsieck was founded in Reims in 1851 by Charles-Camille Heidsieck, a member of a family already involved in the region’s wine trade. Its founder travelled several times to the United States to promote his wines, which earned him the nickname “Champagne Charlie”. The house is distinct from Piper-Heidsieck, another brand born of the same family, although the two shared owners for part of their recent history.

Its wines age in the crayères of Reims, ancient chalk quarries dug in Gallo-Roman times and converted into cellars, whose constant temperature and humidity favour slow ageing. These underground galleries are part of the wider ensemble recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Champagne.

The Charles Heidsieck style is associated with rich, complex champagnes with notes of nuts and pastry. One of its distinctive features is the use of a high proportion of reserve wines in its Brut Réserve, combined with long ageing on the lees, giving a riper, more enveloping profile than many non-vintage champagnes.

The range includes Brut Réserve, Rosé Réserve, a Blanc de Blancs, vintage champagnes and a prestige cuvée, Blanc des Millénaires, made from Chardonnay and known for its long ageing in the cellar before release.
