Winery

Torbreck

Barossa winery founded in 1994 by David Powell, specialising in reds and whites from Rhône varieties grown on old vines.

Torbreck was founded in 1994 by David Powell, who had worked as a lumberjack in Scotland; the winery's name comes from a Scottish forest, Torbreck, near Inverness. Back in the Barossa, Powell became interested in plots of dry-grown old vines, many of them threatened with being pulled up at a time when grapes were worth little, and he reached agreements with their owners to bring them back into production.

The winery is located at Marananga, in the western Barossa, and specialises in the varieties of the Rhône Valley: Shiraz, Grenache and Mataro (Mourvèdre) for the reds, and Viognier, Marsanne and Roussanne for the whites. Its style aims for concentrated, expressive wines with ripe, deep fruit, but also texture and ageing potential.

The range is tiered. At its base are wines such as Woodcutter's, whose name recalls the founder's past, and the Rhône-inspired blend The Steading. At the top are the single-site and very old vine reds, among them RunRig, a Shiraz co-fermented with a small percentage of Viognier in the manner of Côte-Rôtie, and other limited-production wines such as The Laird and Descendant.

Torbreck has become one of the benchmark names of the modern Barossa and has helped to increase the value placed on the region's heritage of old vines, one of the oldest in the world thanks to the historical absence of phylloxera in South Australia.
