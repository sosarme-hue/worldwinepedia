Cotnari

The historic winery of the Cotnari appellation, in Iași county, heir to the former state farm and devoted to the area’s four traditional varieties.

The Cotnari company, based in the village of the same name in Iași county, in north-eastern Romania, is the oldest and best-known producer in the Cotnari appellation. It derives from the large state wine farm that managed these vineyards during the communist period and which, after 1989, became a private company. Its history is bound up with the winegrowing tradition of Cotnari, which goes back to the Middle Ages and is associated with the Moldavian prince Stephen the Great.

The winery farms extensive vineyards on the hills around Cotnari, on soils of limestone origin, in a continental climate with long, sunny autumns. It is devoted almost exclusively to the area’s four traditional white varieties: Grasă de Cotnari, Tămâioasă Românească, Fetească Albă and Frâncușă. In favourable years, autumn mists allow noble rot to develop, concentrating the sugar in the grapes, above all in Grasă.

Its production ranges from dry wines, such as those from Frâncușă, to medium-sweet and sweet wines from Grasă and Tămâioasă, sold under the appellation’s ripeness terms. The winery keeps a large collection of old vintages in its cellars, which show the ageing potential of Cotnari’s sweet wines. Its wines are very popular in Romania and are part of the traditional image of Romanian wine.
