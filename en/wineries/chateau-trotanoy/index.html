Château Trotanoy

Pomerol estate owned by Établissements Jean-Pierre Moueix since 1953, known for dense, long-lived Merlot-based wines from clay and gravel soils.

Château Trotanoy is one of the historic estates of Pomerol, the small right-bank Bordeaux appellation famous for its Merlot-based reds. It lies on the western part of the Pomerol plateau, somewhat apart from the cluster of properties surrounding Pétrus, on slightly raised ground.

In 1953 it was bought by Jean-Pierre Moueix, the Libourne négociant who played a decisive role in Pomerol’s international reputation, and it has since belonged to Établissements Jean-Pierre Moueix, now run by his family. The firm also manages other benchmark right-bank properties, and Trotanoy has traditionally been regarded as one of its most important wines. Pomerol has no official classification, but Trotanoy is usually counted among the appellation’s great names.

The vineyard, modest in size, lies on soils of dense clay and gravel that harden in summer and retain water at depth. It is planted almost entirely with Merlot, with a small proportion of Cabernet Franc. Harvesting is by hand and each plot is vinified with meticulous care.

Trotanoy is known for a concentrated, deep and highly structured style that can seem closed in youth and needs years in bottle to reveal its richness and complexity. With age it develops very characteristic notes of truffle, leather and spice.
