Winery

Wirra Wirra

McLaren Vale winery founded in 1894 by Robert Strangways Wigley and re-established in 1969 by Greg and Roger Trott, known for reds such as Church Block and RSW Shiraz.

Wirra Wirra was founded in 1894 by Robert Strangways Wigley, an eccentric figure of Adelaide society who settled in McLaren Vale and built a stone winery there. After his death in the early twentieth century, the property gradually fell out of use and the buildings ended up in ruins.

In 1969 the cousins Greg and Roger Trott bought the estate and set about rebuilding it, bringing the old winery back to life. Greg Trott, a much-loved figure in the region, gave the house a light-hearted spirit and a strong attachment to McLaren Vale that is still reflected in the names and stories of many of its wines. One of the estate's symbols is a large bell known as The Angelus, which also gives its name to one of its reds.

McLaren Vale, south of Adelaide, benefits from the influence of Gulf St Vincent, which tempers the summer heat. In this setting, Wirra Wirra makes mainly red wines from Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon and Grenache, as well as some whites. Among its best-known wines are Church Block, a blend of Cabernet Sauvignon, Shiraz and Merlot made since the early years of the re-establishment; RSW Shiraz, whose name pays tribute to the founder; and The Angelus, a Cabernet Sauvignon.

The winery practises environmentally attentive viticulture, with particular attention to the health of soil and vines, and aims for reds with generous fruit, balance and good ageing potential.
