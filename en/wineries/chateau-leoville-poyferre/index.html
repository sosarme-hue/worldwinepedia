Château Léoville Poyferré

Second growth of the 1855 classification in Saint-Julien, born from the division of the former Léoville estate and owned by the Cuvelier family since 1920.

Château Léoville Poyferré lies in the village of Saint-Julien, at the heart of the Médoc. Its history is tied to the great Léoville estate, one of the largest and most prestigious in the Médoc in the eighteenth century, which after the French Revolution was divided into three properties: Léoville Las Cases, Léoville Barton and Léoville Poyferré. The last of these takes its name from the Baron de Poyferré, who owned it in the nineteenth century.

In the 1855 classification, all three Léovilles were ranked among the second growths. In 1920 the Cuvelier family, wine merchants from northern France, acquired Léoville Poyferré, and it has remained in their hands ever since. From the late 1970s Didier Cuvelier led a major modernisation of the vineyard and cellar; today a new generation of the family runs the estate.

The vineyard is divided into plots on the gravels of the Saint-Julien terrace, some of them neighbouring those of Léoville Las Cases and close to Pauillac. It is planted mainly with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, with small proportions of Petit Verdot and Cabernet Franc.

The house style is characterised by reds with generous fruit and a velvety texture, with the structure and longevity typical of great Saint-Juliens. The estate also makes a second wine, Pavillon de Poyferré.
