Mas de Daumas Gassac

Estate in the Gassac valley, in Aniane, created by Aimé and Véronique Guibert in the 1970s, a pioneer of great age-worthy Languedoc wines outside the appellation system.

Mas de Daumas Gassac lies in the Gassac valley, near Aniane, north of Montpellier, in the Hérault department. Aimé Guibert and his wife Véronique bought the property in the early 1970s with no initial intention of making wine. It was the geographer Henri Enjalbert who pointed out to them the quality of the site’s soils, deep, well-drained reddish deposits, and encouraged them to plant vines.

On the advice of the Bordeaux oenologist Émile Peynaud, the Guiberts planted mainly Cabernet Sauvignon, a variety then unusual in the area, together with many other vines of different origins. The first vintage was 1978. As their varieties and approach did not fit the rules of the local appellations, the wines were sold as country wines, today under the Saint-Guilhem-le-Désert protected geographical indication. Even so, the estate soon gained a reputation that led to comparisons with great wines from other regions.

The vineyard is spread over numerous small parcels surrounded by garrigue and woodland, which the family has preserved as part of the estate’s ecosystem. Since Aimé Guibert’s death in 2016, his sons have run the property.

The best-known wines are the Mas de Daumas Gassac red, based on Cabernet Sauvignon, and the white, a blend of numerous varieties. They are long-lived wines that can seem austere in their youth.
