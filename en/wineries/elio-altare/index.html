Elio Altare

Family winery in La Morra, in the Langhe, one of the leading figures in the renewal of Barolo in the 1980s, now run by the Altare family.

The Elio Altare winery is located in the frazione of Annunziata, in the commune of La Morra, one of the classic Barolo production areas in the hills of the Langhe, in Piedmont. The family had farmed vines in the area for generations, and it was Elio Altare who, from the late 1970s, transformed the estate and made it a reference name.

After seeing Burgundian practices at first hand, Elio Altare chose to lower vineyard yields, shorten macerations and age the wine in French oak barriques instead of the large traditional casks. These choices, which sparked intense debate in the region, placed him among the so-called Barolo Boys, the group of producers who promoted a fruitier, more approachable style of Nebbiolo. Over time the house has refined that approach, seeking above all precision and the expression of each vineyard.

The estate, now run by the Altare family, makes a blended Barolo and single-vineyard Barolos, including one from Cannubi, as well as Barbera d’Alba, Dolcetto and Langhe wines such as Arborina and La Villa. The house style is characterised by reds of pure fruit, polished tannins and great elegance, which can be enjoyed earlier than many traditional Barolos without losing ageing potential.
