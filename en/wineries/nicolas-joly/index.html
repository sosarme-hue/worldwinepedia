Nicolas Joly – Clos de la Coulée de Serrant

Joly family estate in Savennières, in the Loire, sole owner of the Coulée de Serrant appellation and one of the pioneers of biodynamic viticulture in France.

The Clos de la Coulée de Serrant is a Chenin Blanc vineyard in Savennières, in Anjou, on a schist slope that drops towards the Loire. According to tradition, its first vines were planted by Cistercian monks in the 12th century, and the site still preserves old buildings. It is one of the few French appellations owned entirely by a single proprietor: the AOC Savennières-Coulée-de-Serrant is, in effect, a monopoly of the Joly family.

The family acquired the property in the 1960s. Nicolas Joly, who took over in the late 1970s after a career in finance, converted the vineyard to biodynamic farming in the 1980s and became one of the leading advocates of the method in the wine world, through books, lectures and the producers’ association he promoted. Today the estate is run by his daughter Virginie Joly.

Besides the Clos de la Coulée de Serrant, the family makes two other Savennières wines: Clos de la Bergerie, in the Savennières-Roche-aux-Moines appellation, and Les Vieux Clos. All are made exclusively from Chenin Blanc, with indigenous yeasts and minimal intervention in the cellar.

The house’s wines are dry whites of great structure and ageing potential, sometimes with botrytis notes depending on the vintage, which the family recommends opening well ahead or decanting.
