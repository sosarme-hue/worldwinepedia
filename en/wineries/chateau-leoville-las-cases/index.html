Château Léoville Las Cases

Saint-Julien second growth owned by the Delon family, whose walled Grand Enclos borders Château Latour.

Château Léoville Las Cases comes from the old Léoville estate, one of the largest in the Médoc in the 18th century, which after the French Revolution was divided into three properties: Léoville Las Cases, Léoville Barton and Léoville Poyferré. The part kept by the family of the Marquis de Las Cases is today the largest of the three.

In the 1855 classification it was recognised as a Deuxième Grand Cru Classé. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the Delon family gradually acquired shares in the estate, and several generations have run it since then; Michel Delon and later his son Jean-Hubert Delon gave it a reputation that often places it among the finest of the second growths.

The heart of the property is the Grand Enclos, a vineyard surrounded by a wall and marked by a famous stone gateway topped by a lion, which appears on the label. It lies in the north of Saint-Julien, close to the estuary and bordering Château Latour, on soils of gravel and sand over clay. It is planted mainly with Cabernet Sauvignon, with Merlot and Cabernet Franc.

As well as the grand vin, the family makes Clos du Marquis, from its own plots outside the Grand Enclos, and the second wine Le Petit Lion. The Delons also own Château Potensac, in the Médoc, and Château Nénin, in Pomerol. The Las Cases style is associated with concentrated, rigorous wines with very long ageing potential.
