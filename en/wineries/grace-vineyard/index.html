Grace Vineyard

Family winery founded in 1997 at Taigu, in China's Shanxi province, one of the pioneers of quality wine in China.

Grace Vineyard was founded in 1997 by the Hong Kong businessman C. K. Chan in the Taigu district of Shanxi province, in northern China. At that time Chinese wine was dominated by large state-owned companies and by wines of modest quality, and Grace was one of the first private wineries to set out to make quality wine from its own vineyards.

His daughter, Judy Chan, took over the running of the winery in the early 2000s and made it one of the best-known names in Chinese wine, both on the domestic market and among enthusiasts abroad. Under her leadership the company broadened its range and also developed vineyards in Ningxia, the north-western region that has established itself as one of the most promising in the country.

The Shanxi vineyards lie in an area with a continental climate, with cold, dry winters and warm summers, in which the wide temperature range between day and night favours freshness in the grapes. Grace grows mainly Bordeaux varieties, such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc, along with others such as Chardonnay.

Its best-known wines include the Deep Blue range and the Chairman's Reserve, its most ambitious red, a barrel-aged blend of Bordeaux varieties. The winery is a good example of the rapid evolution of quality Chinese wine in recent decades.
