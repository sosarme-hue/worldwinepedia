Château Latour

First growth of Pauillac, in the Médoc, named after an old defensive tower, whose heart is the L’Enclos vineyard beside the Gironde estuary.

Château Latour lies at the southern end of the Pauillac appellation, bordering Saint-Julien and close to the Gironde estuary. Its name recalls a fortified tower from the time of the Hundred Years’ War; the round tower shown on the label, however, is a later building, a former dovecote. In the 17th and 18th centuries the estate was linked to the de Ségur family, who also owned Lafite, and its wines were already highly prized in England.

In the 1855 classification it was recognised as a Premier Grand Cru Classé, alongside Lafite, Margaux and Haut-Brion. In the 20th century it passed through the hands of British investors and, since 1993, it has belonged to François Pinault through his Artémis group.

The heart of the property is L’Enclos, a plot of deep gravel over a clay and marl subsoil surrounding the buildings, from which most of the grand vin comes. Cabernet Sauvignon clearly dominates the plantings, alongside Merlot and small areas of Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. The estate has gradually converted to organic viticulture.

As well as the grand vin, Latour makes Les Forts de Latour and a third wine, Pauillac de Latour. In 2012 the château announced that it would stop selling its wines en primeur and would instead release them when it judged them ready to drink. Its style is associated with powerful, deep wines with very long ageing potential.
