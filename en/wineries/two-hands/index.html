Two Hands Wines

Winery founded in 1999 by Michael Twelftree and Richard Mintz, focused on Shiraz and on showing the differences between Australia's regions.

Two Hands was founded in 1999 by Michael Twelftree and Richard Mintz, two partners who came from the world of business rather than from winegrowing families. Their starting idea was at once simple and ambitious: to make the best possible Shiraz from different Australian regions and to show, wine by wine, how origin shapes the character of the variety. The winery is located at Marananga, in the Barossa, where the company also owns vineyards.

In its early years, Two Hands worked mainly with fruit bought from growers in areas such as the Barossa, McLaren Vale, Clare, Padthaway and Heathcote, vinifying each source separately. This logic is reflected in the way the range is organised. The Picture Series, with light-hearted names such as Gnarly Dudes and Angels' Share, offers expressive, approachable wines; the Garden Series, whose wines are named after family gardens, such as Bella's Garden, presents one Shiraz per region so that they can be compared; above them are the single-vineyard wines and the top-tier reds.

The house's first wines were associated with a very ripe, powerful style typical of the Barossa at the turn of the century. Over time the winery has evolved towards greater freshness, with somewhat earlier picking and a more measured use of new oak, without giving up intensity of fruit.

Two Hands has thus become one of the most visible names of Australian Shiraz on international markets and a good introduction to the country's regional diversity.
