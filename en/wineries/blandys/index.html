Blandy’s

Madeira house founded in 1811 by John Blandy and still owned by his family, one of the best-known producers of the island’s fortified wine.

The history of Blandy’s begins in 1811, when the Englishman John Blandy settled on Madeira and founded a trading business that soon focused on the island’s wine. Over the nineteenth century the family extended its activities into shipping, coal and other sectors without giving up wine, and it came through the severe crises caused by powdery mildew and phylloxera in Madeira’s vineyards. Blandy’s is today one of the few Madeira wine houses still owned by the founding family, now in its seventh generation.

In the twentieth century several of the island’s houses joined together in what would become the Madeira Wine Company, of which Blandy’s is part. For some decades the company had the Symington family as a partner, until the Blandys regained full control.

The house makes Madeira in all the traditional styles: age-dated Sercial, Verdelho, Boal and Malmsey, such as its 5, 10 and 15-year-old wines, single-harvest Colheitas and long-aged Frasqueira wines, as well as younger blends made mainly from Tinta Negra. Its finest wines are aged by the canteiro method, in casks kept in warm lodges. Its historic home, Blandy’s Wine Lodge, in the centre of Funchal, occupies part of a former Franciscan monastery.

Like all Madeiras, its wines are marked by very lively acidity and by great resistance to oxidation once the bottle has been opened, so they keep well for a long time.

Visits & wine tourism

Blandy’s Wine Lodge, in Funchal, offers guided tours with tastings.
