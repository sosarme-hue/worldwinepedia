Viña Santa Rita

Historic Maipo Valley estate founded in 1880 by Domingo Fernández Concha in Alto Jahuel. The property retains a nineteenth-century manor house, a park and a museum of pre-Columbian art.

Viña Santa Rita was founded in 1880 by Domingo Fernández Concha, a Chilean businessman and politician, in Alto Jahuel, near Buin, in the Maipo Valley. Like other great estates of the time, it imported vines and technical know-how from France. The estate’s cellars predate the winery and are linked to an episode of Chilean independence: according to tradition, in 1814, after the Battle of Rancagua, a group of 120 patriot soldiers took refuge in them, protected by the owner of the hacienda, Paula Jaraquemada. That episode gives its name to the 120 range, one of the house’s best known.

Since 1980 the winery has belonged to the Claro family’s business group, which drove its modernisation and expansion into other valleys. The group also owns Viña Carmen, one of Chile’s oldest wineries. Santa Rita’s range includes everyday wines, reservas such as Medalla Real and top-end reds such as Casa Real, a Maipo Cabernet Sauvignon, as well as wines from other valleys.

The Alto Jahuel estate retains the manor house, now a hotel, a historic park laid out in the nineteenth century and the Museo Andino, which houses a collection of pre-Columbian art assembled by the owning family. The winery welcomes visitors and is one of the wine tourism destinations closest to Santiago.

Visits & wine tourism

The estate offers tours of its historic cellars and park, tastings, a restaurant, a hotel and access to the Museo Andino, usually by booking.
