Winery

Weingut Robert Weil

Estate in Kiedrich, in the Rheingau, founded in 1875 and devoted almost entirely to Riesling, with the Kiedricher Gräfenberg vineyard as its emblem.

The estate was founded in 1875 by Dr Robert Weil, who had been a professor in Paris and returned to Germany after the Franco-Prussian War. He settled in Kiedrich, a Rheingau village set slightly back from the river at the foot of the Taunus, and acquired vineyards on its slopes. Since the late 1980s the Japanese group Suntory has held a majority stake in the company, while management has remained in the hands of the Weil family, headed by Wilhelm Weil.

The vineyards are concentrated in Kiedrich’s three great sites: the Gräfenberg, a steep south-west-facing slope with phyllite and slate soils, the Turmberg and the Klosterberg. Practically the entire vineyard area is planted with Riesling. The work is based on moderate yields and hand harvesting in several passes, which allows grapes to be selected according to the style of each wine.

Robert Weil makes both dry Rieslings, from the estate wine to the single-vineyard Grosses Gewächs, and wines with sweetness in the Prädikat categories. Its nobly sweet wines and Eisweins, made only in certain years, have brought the house great renown. The style combines the ripe fruit typical of the Rheingau with firm acidity and remarkable precision. The estate is a member of the VDP producers’ association and organises its range according to the association’s vineyard classification.
