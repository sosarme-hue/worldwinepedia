Champagne Louis Roederer

Family-owned Reims house dating back to 1776, creator of Cristal and owner of an extensive vineyard of its own in Champagne.

The house’s origins go back to 1776, when a champagne trading firm was established in Reims. Louis Roederer inherited it in 1833 and gave it his name. Under his management the house bought vineyards, which was unusual among négociants at the time, and opened up export markets, above all Russia.

In 1876 the house created a special champagne for Tsar Alexander II, bottled in clear glass: Cristal. After the Russian Revolution it was no longer made for the court and, years later, it was launched on the general market, becoming one of the best-known prestige cuvées.

Louis Roederer remains a family-owned, independent company, run by the Rouzaud family. One of its hallmarks is its extensive estate vineyard, with plots in the Montagne de Reims, the Vallée de la Marne and the Côte des Blancs, which supplies much of the grapes it uses. Some of the plots are farmed organically and biodynamically.

Besides Cristal, the range includes the non-vintage brut Collection, which replaced the former Brut Premier in 2021, vintage wines, blanc de blancs and rosés. The family group also owns other estates, such as Domaines Ott in Provence and Château Pichon Longueville Comtesse de Lalande in Pauillac.

It is one of the few large Champagne houses that remain independent and family-owned.
