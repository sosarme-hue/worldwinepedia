Château de Beaucastel

Perrin family estate in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, known for growing all thirteen permitted varieties and for the weight of Mourvèdre in its red.

Château de Beaucastel lies in the commune of Courthézon, at the northern end of the Châteauneuf-du-Pape appellation. The estate was acquired in the early 20th century by the family from which today’s Perrin family descends. Jacques Perrin, from the mid-20th century, and then his sons Jean-Pierre and François, gave Beaucastel its international reputation. Today it is run by the following generations of the family.

The vineyard, whose soils are largely covered with the characteristic rounded pebbles, has been farmed without synthetic chemicals for decades. Beaucastel is known for keeping all thirteen varieties historically permitted in Châteauneuf-du-Pape planted and, above all, for the importance it gives to Mourvèdre, which in its red carries a weight similar to that of Grenache, unlike most producers in the appellation.

As well as red and white Châteauneuf-du-Pape — the latter dominated by Roussanne — the estate makes the Côtes du Rhône Coudoulet de Beaucastel, from neighbouring vineyards. The family also runs Famille Perrin, with wines from various southern Rhône appellations.

Beaucastel’s reds are structured, with notes of black fruit, spice and garrigue, and remarkable ageing potential.

Coudoulet de Beaucastel is often presented as the château’s younger sibling.
