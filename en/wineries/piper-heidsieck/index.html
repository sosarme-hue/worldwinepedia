Champagne Piper-Heidsieck

Reims Champagne house founded in 1785 by Florens-Louis Heidsieck, recognisable by its red label and its deeply coloured Rosé Sauvage.

Piper-Heidsieck originates from the house founded in Reims in 1785 by Florens-Louis Heidsieck, a merchant of German origin. After his death the firm passed to his nephews and split into several branches, from which other houses bearing the Heidsieck name also descend. In the 19th century Henri-Guillaume Piper became a partner in the house, and his surname was joined to Heidsieck to form the present name.

Throughout its history the house cultivated an international image linked to the world of entertainment, and its champagne has been associated with cinema for decades. Its red label is one of the most recognisable on shop shelves.

Since 2011 Piper-Heidsieck has belonged, together with Charles Heidsieck, to the French family group EPI. Its winemaking facilities are on the outskirts of Reims, and the house works with grapes from many villages in the region, a significant share of them Pinot Noir.

The range is based on the Cuvée Brut, the house’s non-vintage champagne, alongside Rosé Sauvage, a very deeply coloured rosé, vintage champagnes and other versions such as a demi-sec. The house style is characterised by fruity, fresh, direct champagnes, in which Pinot Noir brings structure and Chardonnay finesse. Charles Heidsieck, its sister house within the same group, shares part of the history of the Heidsieck family in Reims.
