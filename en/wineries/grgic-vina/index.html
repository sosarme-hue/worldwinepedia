Winery

Grgić Vina

A winery founded in 1996 in Trstenik, on the Pelješac peninsula, by Miljenko “Mike” Grgich, the Croatian winemaker who built his career in the Napa Valley. It makes mainly Plavac Mali and Pošip.

Grgić Vina is the Croatian winery of Miljenko “Mike” Grgich (Grgić in Croatian), one of California’s best-known winemakers. Born in 1923 in Desne, in southern Dalmatia, into a winegrowing family, he studied oenology in Zagreb and emigrated in the 1950s, first to Canada and then to California. In the Napa Valley he worked at several leading wineries, including Chateau Montelena, and in 1977, together with Austin Hills, he founded the Grgich Hills winery. He died in 2023.

After Croatian independence Grgich returned to his homeland and in 1996 founded Grgić Vina in Trstenik, a small harbour on the Pelješac peninsula in Dalmatia. His aim was to apply the experience he had gained in California to the native varieties of the Croatian coast. Grgich was also interested in the origins of Zinfandel and supported the research that led to its identification with Dalmatian Crljenak Kaštelanski.

The winery makes mainly two wines. The Plavac Mali comes from vineyards on the Pelješac peninsula, grown on stony slopes facing the sea, and is aged in barrel. The Pošip is a white from this variety, native to the neighbouring island of Korčula, medium-bodied with aromas of ripe fruit. Both reflect a style that combines the ripeness of the Dalmatian sun with careful, modern winemaking, and the winery has helped to make Croatian wines known, particularly in the United States.
