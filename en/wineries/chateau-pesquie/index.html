Château Pesquié

Family estate in Mormoiron, at the foot of Mont Ventoux, a reference for the Ventoux appellation in the southern Rhône Valley.

Château Pesquié is in Mormoiron, in the Vaucluse department, on the southern slopes of Mont Ventoux. The property, with an eighteenth-century manor house surrounded by plane trees, belongs to the Chaudière family, who in the last decades of the twentieth century began bottling their own wines and gave the estate a profile reaching well beyond the region.

The Ventoux appellation, part of the southern Rhône family of wines, is shaped by the influence of the mountain: altitude and the closeness of the Ventoux bring cool nights and wide differences between day and night temperatures, which help preserve acidity and aromatic freshness in the grapes despite the Mediterranean climate. The estate’s soils combine clay, limestone and sand.

The main red varieties are Grenache and Syrah, alongside other Mediterranean grapes; for whites, varieties such as Viognier, Roussanne and Clairette are grown. The range runs from entry-level wines such as “Les Terrasses” to old-vine cuvées such as “Quintessence”, and is completed by geographical-indication wines under the “Le Paradou” brand. The house style aims for wines that are ripe yet fresh and show the character of the Ventoux.
