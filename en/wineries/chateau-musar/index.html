Château Musar

Family winery founded in 1930 by Gaston Hochar, the best-known Lebanese winery internationally. At Ghazir, north of Beirut, it makes long-aged wines of very personal style from grapes grown in the Bekaa and on Mount Lebanon.

Château Musar was founded in 1930 by Gaston Hochar, who set up the winery at Ghazir, a town above the coast north of Beirut, in an old castle known as Mzar, from which the house takes its name. From 1959 his son Serge Hochar took charge of winemaking and, for over half a century, became the figure associated with the winery and one of the leading ambassadors of Lebanese wine. Since his death in 2014, the Hochar family has continued to run the house.

One of the best-known episodes in its history is that the winery kept making wine throughout the civil war of 1975 to 1990, despite enormous difficulties in transporting grapes from the Bekaa to Ghazir. That perseverance helped to make Lebanese wine known in international markets, particularly in the United Kingdom.

The red grapes come mainly from vineyards in the Bekaa, while the whites are made from the native varieties Obaideh and Merwah, from high-altitude vineyards. The house follows a philosophy of minimal intervention in the vineyard and the cellar, and is known for releasing its main wines several years after the harvest, following long ageing in barrel and bottle. Its style, full of personality and able to evolve over decades, has always divided opinion and inspired great loyalty among its admirers. Besides the red, white and rosé Château Musar, the winery makes younger ranges such as Hochar Père et Fils and Musar Jeune.
