Sogrape

Mateus Rosé

Rosé

Slightly sparkling rosé created by Sogrape in 1942, recognisable by its flattened flask-shaped bottle and its label showing the Mateus palace, one of the best-selling Portuguese wines in the world.

Mateus Rosé was created in 1942 by Fernando van Zeller Guedes, the founder of Sogrape, with the idea of making a Portuguese wine designed from the outset for export. Its flattened bottle, inspired by the canteens of First World War soldiers, and its label, which shows the baroque Mateus palace near Vila Real, made it one of the most recognisable wines in the world. During the second half of the twentieth century it was one of Europe’s most internationally successful wines.

It is made from Portuguese red varieties, among them Baga, Rufete, Tinta Barroca and Touriga Franca, in proportions that vary with the vintage. After a brief maceration on the skins to obtain the pink colour, the must ferments at low temperature to preserve fruity aromas. The wine keeps a slight sweetness and a gentle spritz, that is, a small amount of carbon dioxide that gives it freshness.

It is a young rosé, meant to be drunk within the year, without ageing.

Tasting profile

Bright pink colour. Aromas of strawberry, raspberry and red fruit sweets. Light, fresh and slightly sweet on the palate, with a gentle effervescence.

Food pairing

Aperitif, salads, spicy Asian cuisine, pizza, summer dishes

Serving

6-8 °C, white wine glass
